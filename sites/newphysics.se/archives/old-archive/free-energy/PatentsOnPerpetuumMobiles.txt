I don't know of any patent granted for a 'perpetual motion device', but
there is a patent for an "ELECTROSTATIC ENERGY FIELD POWER GENERATING 
SYSTEM". It was granted to William W. Hyde, 1685 Whitney, 
Idaho Falls Id. 83402 on Jan. 30, 1990 under US. Pat. No. 4,897,592.

Hyde does not explicitely mention he invented a perpetuum mobile, but the 
claim seems to be 'hidden' in the patent text on page 5:

   [...] since there is no net change in capacitance thereby enabling the 
   system to convert energy with a reduced input of mechanical energy and 
   high efficiency, as evidenced by minimal loss of charge on the 
   electrodes. It was therefore found that working embodiments of the 
   present invention require less than ten percent of the electrical output 
   energy for the mechanical input.

Although you need a second energy input to maintain an electical field,
this seems to be neglectible since the field is static.

I wonder whether anyone could comment on this. You can get the patent text
and the corresponding technical drawings via ftp at

	phoenix.oulu.fi (130.231.240.17) as /pub/free_energy/HYDEPTNT.ZIP
                                    and /pub/free_energy/HYDEPTNT.doc
