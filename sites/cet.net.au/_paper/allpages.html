Ericksonian hypnosis - a 3 day intensive


in Melbourne


simply learning a natural approach to solutions in hypnosis

with Rob McNeilly


In this 3 day intensive, CET offers a unique opportunity to learn the principles and applications of one of the most rapidly growing treatment models in contemporary psychotherapy. The scope of the program is comprehensive, and presents the essential elements in an elegantly accessible way.
The 3 day intensive is open to practitioners of all levels of experience who are passionate about their work.

simply learning a natural approach to solutions in hypnosis

simple

hypnosis has been understood as complex, special, even dangerous, requiring unique restrictions. In this programme we will explore simple, elegant and respectful ways of connecting clients with their own unique styles of experience.

learning

There will be a minimum of theory and explanations, focusing instead on demonstrations and recurrent practice so that participants will have sufficient skills to begin to use their learning in their clinical practice immediately.

a natural approach

Erickson spoke of the everyday trance as a natural experience that can be enhanced in a clinical situation. By utilising individual clients’ individual skills, a fail-safe method will be made obvious and readily useable.

solutions

Ericksonian hypnosis creates a wonderful context for a trusting therapeutic relationship, helping to restore self-trust in individual clients by reconnecting them with their natural resourcefulness.

hypnosis

When we begin with the principle that hypnosis is an experience that involves focus of attention and absorption in that experience, this natural ability can be readily accessed by clients and incorporated in any clinician’s practice.

3 day intensive

In this 3 day workshop, we will enjoy sharing learning:
creating a mood of connection to enhance rapport,
exploring natural links between everyday happenings
and the process of inviting a client into the experience of hypnosis,
establishing easy and natural relationships between specific client resources and their solutions.

Diploma and Advanced Diploma of 

Solution Oriented Counselling


Overview

When Milton Erickson began working, inventing and experimenting with therapy in the 1930’s there was little to choose from in terms of different therapy styles. In the1960's, there were about 60 different therapy models, and today there are more than 250!!!! It is humbling to note that despite the variety; virtually all of the research data finds roughly equivalent results for all models. And perhaps we only need to look at the world to see that that is so. In fact it is the similarities rather than differences between the models that account for the effectiveness of psychotherapy.

The Solution Oriented Approach honours these similarities, acknowledges that many of the 250 have their roots in Erickson’s work and in a refreshing answer to today’s consumerism, attempts to simplify and unravel the confusion.

Michael Lambert’s 1992 research points to the existence of four factors common to all forms of therapy that contribute to change; 1: extra therapeutic [40%]; 2: Client – therapist relationship [30%]; 3: placebo, hope, and/or expectancy [15%]; and 4: structure, model, and/or technique used[15%]. As humbling as this is we can see that by creating a trusting, legitimizing relationship, observing and listening to the client and reconnecting them to their life outside therapy, utilising expectancy and trusting in our own individual therapeutic creativity we can maximise our effectiveness. But how do we observe and listen in order to most effectively provide a context for the missing learning?

This is the core of Erickson’s work specifically and the Solution Oriented approach generally.

This diploma offers more than just information and techniques. It was developed by Rob McNeilly and grounded in his 25 years of teaching and learning including personally from Milton Erickson, Humberto Maturana, Julio Ollala and Rafael Eccheveria to name a few. It is a rare opportunity to have the ideas of such people distilled into one approach with Rob’s uniquely creative, playful and illuminating style. Following 10 years working in General Practice with the inherent frustrations of the medical model that appear common to doctors, counsellors, psychologists and allied health workers alike, it was a relief to discover a different way. As a student of Rob’s I was impressed by the refreshing learning experience he offered that usefully allowed me to change how I observed and how I could use what I saw to facilitate learning and change in a client. As a teacher of this approach I have developed a passionate interest in the effectiveness of this way of learning, in students and clients alike.

What you will learn
    •    A solid foundation of Solution Oriented language forms.
    •    Micro-observational skills and what to do with these observations.
    •    Techniques for engaging even the most difficult clients.
    •    Utilisation of the emotions and body in effecting change.
    •    Familiarity and trust in your own creative process.
    •    How to confidently adapt these ideas into your own style.

In the advanced diploma this learning will be extended by exploration of
  • listening
  • creating expectancy
  • generating trust
  • shifting unhelpful beliefs
  • self care


Course Details

The diploma involves 16 workshop days over 8 months and the advanced diploma 10 workshop days over 10 month with lecture, supported by written notes, discussion, but the emphasis is on actual demonstrations and the opportunity for you to practice recurrently in pairs or triads. This diploma values experiential learning over acquisition of information such that you will feel confident to incorporate these ideas into your work immediately.

Assignments, relevant to the actual clinical utilisation of the learning offer valuable opportunities for reflection. Unlimited free supervision and personal therapy is available throughout the course and for 1 year following completion of the diploma. All diplomas can be repeated free of charge at any time in the future.

The opportunity for supervised clinical experience will be available in some locations for diploma graduates through a low cost community clinic and provide valuable experience for future employment opportunities.

THE MILTON H ERICKSON INSTITUTE OF TASMANIA

20th August, 2007


Milton H Erickson MD was one of the most influential therapists of the 20th Century. He changed the direction of therapy from classifying pathology to exploring resources – from seeking out understandings of causes for problems to looking with each individual for their own individual solutions.

This solution approach is not just positive thinking. It is a 180 degree shift in direction. While some traditional methods are still stuck in the old mold, many other schools of therapy have followed his lead.

The Milton H Erickson Institute of Tasmania is delighted to provide learning opportunities to health professionals who are interested to support their clients to take charge of their lives again; to regain their self-respect and autonomy; to get out from under the burden of suffering and labelling which can only add to their difficulties.

This Institute is the 130th around the world to honour Erickson’s massive contribution and to offer his approach to counsellors, psychologists, social workers, doctors and other interested health professionals.

The Ericksonian approach assumes that problems come when we become disconnected from our strengths and abilities, and solutions appear when the individual dignity of each client is respected, assuming their resourcefulness, and supporting them to reconnect with their own unique strengths and abilities.

The Milton H Erickson Institute of Tasmania offers training programmes in association with CET [The Centre of Effective Therapy] in

    solution oriented counselling,
    solution oriented family therapy and
    solution oriented hypnosis.

These programmes can lead on the national accreditation through PACFA [Psychotherapy & Counselling Federation of Australia], and participants can join a supportive community for ongoing supervision and learning, as well as assistance and support in moving into a career in private practice.

You are invited to explore details of our programmes, our resources, or contact us.

You are invited to join our learning community!

Solution Oriented Family Therapy

a 3 day intensive

with Dr Gabrielle Peacock & Dr Robert McNeilly



In this 3 day workshop we will explore simple, elegant methods to create a solution mood, and generate new ways for families to be together. The focus will be on those elements beyond technique that are common to all therapies that account for effectiveness, and we will explore how we as therapists can actively generate these essential ingredients of therapy.

This workshop will be of interest to anyone wanting to work with children and their families, or to family therapists looking for a forum to extend their work in a supported way. It provides an opportunity for hands on learning around family therapy, and even those working with families will benefit from this experiential workshop which provides an opportunity to practice.

This approach creates a context for a family to emerge from a group of individuals while exploring the moves that make this possible. These moves are foundational to this work. Managing the mood, joining, and creating connections to unite a family group will be recurrently emphasized, and this in turn will create the possibility to practice with the family that emerges.

This interactive workshop emphasises demonstration and practice over lecture presentation so that participants can expect to be able to use the learning from this learning experience in their practice immediately, and confidently.

Diploma and Advanced Diploma of 

Solution Oriented Counselling


simply learning a natural approach to solutions in counselling

Background

This diploma offers more than just information and techniques. It was developed by Rob McNeilly and grounded in 25 years of teaching and learning including personally from Milton Erickson, Humberto Maturana, Julio Ollala and Rafael Eccheveria to name a few. It is a rare opportunity to have the ideas of such people distilled into one approach with Rob’s uniquely creative, playful and illuminating style. It was extended by Gabrielle's 10 years experience in General Practice with the inherent frustrations of the medical model that appear common to doctors, counsellors, psychologists and allied health workers alike. She was relieved to discover a different way. As a student of Rob’s she was impressed by the refreshing learning experience he offered that usefully allowed her to change how she observed and  use what she saw to facilitate learning and change in a client. As a teachers of this approach Rob & Gabrielle share a passionate interest in the effectiveness of this way of learning for students and clients also.

Overview

When Milton Erickson began working, inventing and experimenting with therapy in the 1930’s there was little to choose from in terms of different therapy styles. In the 1960's, there were about 60 different therapy models, and today there are more than 250!!!! It is humbling to note that despite the variety, virtually all of the research data finds roughly equivalent results for all models. And perhaps we only need to look at the world to see that that it is the similarities rather than differences between the models that account for the effectiveness of psychotherapy.

The Solution Oriented Approach honours these similarities, acknowledges that many of the 250 have their roots in Erickson’s work and in a refreshing answer to today’s consumerism, attempts to simplify and unravel the confusion.

Lambert's research in 1992 and Wampole's in 2001 add statistical evidence supporting the core of Erickson’s work specifically and the Solution Oriented approach generally - the importance of
    creating a trusting, legitimizing relationship,
    observing and listening to the client and reconnecting them to their life outside therapy,
    creating expectancy and
    trusting in our own individual therapeutic creativity
to maximise our effectiveness.

This diploma offers a unique opportunity to learn the principles and applications of one of the most rapidly growing treatment models in contemporary psychotherapy. The scope of the program is comprehensive, and presents the essential elements in an elegantly accessible way and is open to practitioners of all levels of experience who are passionate about their work.

simple

There is an explosion of counselling models and approaches, each new model claiming to be the best.  By exploring each individual's experiences, we can have a simpler and more direct access for the client to their own unique resources.

In this programme we will explore simple, elegant and respectful ways of connecting clients with their own unique resources.

learning

Traditionally learning has been based on theory and the property of a select few experts. By contrast, these diplomas have a minimum of theory and explanations, focusing instead on demonstrations and recurrent practice so that participants will have sufficient skills to begin to use their learning in their clinical practice immediately.

a natural approach

Counselling has been assumed to be a special, different, even weird experience. Erickson spoke of problems being everyday experiences that get stuck, and so can also get unstuck in a helpful way. By utilising individual clients’ individual skills, overlooked skills can be disclosed, re-connected, and found to be readily available, leading to a fail-safe method that will become obvious to the client and so, readily useable.

solutions

Rather than problem solving, the solution approach draws on a client's innate resourcefulness, re-connects them with these resources, and because they come from the client, they can be easily accessible. Solution counselling creates a wonderful context for a trusting therapeutic relationship, helping to restore self-trust in individual clients to allow this process to be realised.

counselling

Traditional counselling relies on theories and manualised protocols which are applied to clients in a "fit where it touches" approach, creating resistance and limited compliance. When we begin with the principle that any problem has a solution waiting to emerge, this natural progression can be readily accessed by all clients and incorporated in any clinician’s practice.

What you will learn

  • The evolution of counselling throughout recent history.    
  • A method of creating genuine rapport.
  • Questions to connect clients with their overlooked resources.
  • Conversations to engage clients in their counselling process.
  • How to create possibilities even in the most "resistant" or "chronic" situations.
  • How to include moods and emotions in solutions
  • How to design strategies
  • Positive reframing
  • How to apply this approach to:
    • anxiety, panic & depression
    • post trauma
    • childhood abuse
    • pain management
    • couples work
    • family relations
    • performance enhancement
    • coaching
    • any other clinical situation

In the advanced diploma this learning will be extended by
  •     reviewing the diploma experiences
  •     practicing advanced counselling approaches
  •     listening for solutions
  •     creating expectancy
  •     generating genuine trust
  •     shifting unhelpful beliefs
  •     individualising self care

Course Details

The diploma involves 14 workshop days over 8 months and the advanced diploma 10 workshop days over 10 month with lecture, supported by written notes and discussion. Assignments, relevant to the actual clinical utilisation of the learning offer valuable opportunities for reflection and any diploma can be repeated free of charge at any time in the future. The emphasis is on live demonstrations and the opportunity for you to practice recurrently in pairs or triads. These diplomas value experiential learning over acquisition of information so that you will feel confident to incorporate these ideas into your work immediately.

Summary

These diplomas provide learning opportunities which are individualised, practical, relevant, easily understood, readily translatable into clinical practice, all in a safe environment of respectful support.

Through this series we would like to introduce curious learners to the ideas of some of the most influential minds of our time. We have chosen to begin this series by introducing you to our most valued teachers and hope to extend this to contributions from members of our growing learning community.

Milton H Erickson MD 1901-1980 was one of the most influential therapists of the 20th
Century. He changed the direction of therapy from classifying pathology to exploring
resources – from seeking out understandings of causes for problems to looking with each individual for their own individual solutions. He was an explorer in therapy, still learning, exploring and teaching right up till his death. His own personal struggles with polio and post polio syndrome provided real life experience and personal exploration with the hypnotic experience. he believed that people didn’t come to therapy to change an unchangeable past, but with some dissatisfaction with the present and a wish for a better future. Rob spent time with Erickson towards the end of his life and brings his personal connection to this introduction of the man behind the purple suit.

Humberto Maturana is a Chillean neurobiologist who has radically transformed our understanding of the processes of life and cognition. He has become famous as a leading exponent of modern systems thinking and is the author of several books, including the bestseller written with Francisco J Varella, The Tree of Knowledge. Some of his more interesting reflections include his time in Chile under the Pinochet dictatorship. He was interested to understand the dictatorship, and how, as in Nazi Germany, how people gradually go blind, and what the causes of such perceptual deprivation were. the experience provided a real life opportunity to observe observing. Maturana came to Melbourne several times in the 90’s and Rob attended his lectures. We would like to introduce you to our take on the man, and share something of what he is about. Later in the series we will look in more depth at some of his important contributions.

Heinz von Foerster 1911-2002 has been variously dubbed a physicist, mathematician, cybernetician, but he preferred to call himself a ‘curiologist’. He would also admit to being Viennese! A colleague and friend of Humberto Maturana, Von Foerster is credited with ceaselessly pointing out the unavoidable biases and blind spots on the part of the observers, who approach the object of description that they perceive to be independent of themselves. He trained as a physicist in Vienna, worked as a magician throughout the war years and wrote his first book Memory: a quantum physical study, which attracted the pioneers of American cybernetics and led to an invitation for him to join them in 1949. He joined a rich and fertile group of scientists who came together in the 50’s at the invitation of the Macy Foundation, names such as John Von Neumann, the inventor of the computer, anthropologists Margaret Mead, Gregory Bateson, as well as neuropsychiatrist Warren McCulloch and others. His clarity and whimsy bring a rare mood to looking at otherwise difficult ideas. We never met him, the closest we get is a video of conversations between him and Maturana on ‘truth and trust’  yet we have developed a great fondness and gratitude to the man and his contributions. As with Maturana we would like to introduce you to the man, and later in the series we will look at some of his important contributions

Distance programmes

with Dr. Robert McNeilly MBBS & Dr Gabrielle Peacock MBBS FRACGP

Principles

The learning experience of these programmes is both unique and everyday. The uniqueness is in its almost total lack of theory and formal structure; the everydayness is in the way the learning is personal and relates to any individual and their everyday experiences.

For millennia learning has been the prerogative of the few, usually wealthy or privileged. Only over the last half century has this begun to change. Academic circles can still maintain a sense of elitism, and while the approach of this programme does not have any argument with academia, it has a radically different focus.

Because these programmes give precedence to experience over theory, curiosity over fixed explanations, and individual differences over categorising into groups or classes, participants can expect to be more like themselves and less like any of those who may already be seen as expert in the field. Previous participants comment that their individuality is enhanced – personal and professional – and their individual style of working becomes more obvious as the programme unfolds.

Structure

All programmes include recorded lectures, an e-book, videos of demonstrations, written assignments, supervision, and most important of all, practice. Ultimately it is what participants learn and take into their clinical experience that will be crucial. Anything that is heard, read, observed or discussed, including personal growth, will become relevant as it incorporated in life or work.

At the beginning of each programme, notes and written assignments are distributed by email, also relevant links to lectures and demonstrations. Participants can then progress though their learning experience at their own pace.

All participants are encouraged to form study groups with others as an important component of the learning process, many people reporting that they are the most relevant. Individual supervision is another important component, whether face to face, email, phone, peer supervision or some combination of these alternatives. Written assignments add to the breadth of the learning and there is often an expression of surprise when they are discovered to be enjoyable as well as useful.

Most people report that 2 to 3 hours a week is more than enough to cover the individual work outside any workshops and study group experiences.

Because of the solid foundations of the approach, there are many options for individual learning styles and outcomes, and participants can expect to develop confidence in their own individual style of learning and working.

Accreditation

These programmes are accredited by CET, as evidence shows overwhelmingly that external accreditation has no correlation with effectiveness of learning or application.

Face to face Workshops

There will be local face to face workshops organised depending on numbers and participants are welcome to join in at any other location during the programme and any time in the future without limitation.

Diploma and Advanced Diploma of 

Solution Oriented Counselling


Overview

When Milton Erickson began working, inventing and experimenting with therapy in the 1930’s there was little to choose from in terms of different therapy styles. In the1960's, there were about 60 different therapy models, and today there are more than 250!!!! It is humbling to note that despite the variety; virtually all of the research data finds roughly equivalent results for all models. And perhaps we only need to look at the world to see that that is so. In fact it is the similarities rather than differences between the models that account for the effectiveness of psychotherapy.

The Solution Oriented Approach honours these similarities, acknowledges that many of the 250 have their roots in Erickson’s work and in a refreshing answer to today’s consumerism, attempts to simplify and unravel the confusion.

Michael Lambert’s 1992 research points to the existence of four factors common to all forms of therapy that contribute to change; 1: extra therapeutic [40%]; 2: Client – therapist relationship [30%]; 3: placebo, hope, and/or expectancy [15%]; and 4: structure, model, and/or technique used[15%]. As humbling as this is we can see that by creating a trusting, legitimizing relationship, observing and listening to the client and reconnecting them to their life outside therapy, utilising expectancy and trusting in our own individual therapeutic creativity we can maximise our effectiveness. But how do we observe and listen in order to most effectively provide a context for the missing learning?

This is the core of Erickson’s work specifically and the Solution Oriented approach generally.

This diploma offers more than just information and techniques. It was developed by Rob McNeilly and grounded in his 25 years of teaching and learning including personally from Milton Erickson, Humberto Maturana, Julio Ollala and Rafael Eccheveria to name a few. It is a rare opportunity to have the ideas of such people distilled into one approach with Rob’s uniquely creative, playful and illuminating style. Following 10 years working in General Practice with the inherent frustrations of the medical model that appear common to doctors, counsellors, psychologists and allied health workers alike, it was a relief to discover a different way. As a student of Rob’s I was impressed by the refreshing learning experience he offered that usefully allowed me to change how I observed and how I could use what I saw to facilitate learning and change in a client. As a teacher of this approach I have developed a passionate interest in the effectiveness of this way of learning, in students and clients alike.

What you will learn
    •    A solid foundation of Solution Oriented language forms.
    •    Micro-observational skills and what to do with these observations.
    •    Techniques for engaging even the most difficult clients.
    •    Utilisation of the emotions and body in effecting change.
    •    Familiarity and trust in your own creative process.
    •    How to confidently adapt these ideas into your own style.

In the advanced diploma this learning will be extended by exploration of
  • listening
  • creating expectancy
  • generating trust
  • shifting unhelpful beliefs
  • self care


Course Details

The diploma involves 16 workshop days over 8 months and the advanced diploma 10 workshop days over 10 month with lecture, supported by written notes, discussion, but the emphasis is on actual demonstrations and the opportunity for you to practice recurrently in pairs or triads. This diploma values experiential learning over acquisition of information such that you will feel confident to incorporate these ideas into your work immediately.

Assignments, relevant to the actual clinical utilisation of the learning offer valuable opportunities for reflection. Unlimited free supervision and personal therapy is available throughout the course and for 1 year following completion of the diploma. All diplomas can be repeated free of charge at any time in the future.

The opportunity for supervised clinical experience will be available in some locations for diploma graduates through a low cost community clinic and provide valuable experience for future employment opportunities.

Diploma of Solution Oriented Family Therapy

Overview

This diploma will be of interest to anyone wanting to work with children and their families, or to family therapists looking for a forum to extend their work in a supported way. This programme provides an opportunity for hands on, experiential learning around family therapy, and even those working with families will benefit from these experiential workshops which provide an opportunity to practice. This programme creates a context for a family to emerge from a group of individuals while exploring the moves that make this possible. These moves are foundational to this work. Managing the mood, joining, and creating connections to unite a family group will be recurrently emphasized, and this in turn will create the possibility to practice with the family that emerges.

CET diplomas have been based in Melbourne and running nationally for 25 years, providing a solid foundation grown from this experience and the feedback of hundreds of students about what is useful in a learning environment. These diplomas provide a refreshing return to emphasising a client’s actual experience, and away from the heady intellectualisations that have been distilled as techniques. This work has evolved from its origins in Rob McNeilly’s time spent with Milton Erickson in the 70’s, through his working and teaching to the present day. In our teaching we emphasise demonstration and practice over lecture and information. Where Erickson was a consummate teacher, we are interested in the experience of learning. Rob has explored this face to face with teachers such as Rafael Eccheverria, Humberto Maturana, Julio Olalla and Fernando Flores to name a few, and has brought this learning to teaching ,creating a community of educators locally, nationally and internationally

Lecture and content in these programmes takes on a very different feel for the student as we ‘speak it’, rather than ‘speak about it’. A meticulous focus on language which is generative shows a consistency of approach in our working, teaching and supervision of students. The generative importance of emotions and the body is similarly coherently expressed.

We invite students to observe a demonstration session, a client, a lecture, a book or paper and even their own writing from within their own experience, and to explore their own individual response and learning. Students learn a way of individualising the therapy to the client, while being in the experience of having the teaching individualised to them in a way that allows a texture of experience that is rare in modern education practices.

In this approach diagnoses are acknowledged and then the individual is allowed to emerge in his/her entirety, uncovering concerns, strengths, resources and often banal human dilemmas. At face value this way of working reveals engaging, practical and deceptively simple sessions, which provide an experiential blueprint from which to practice, allowing exploration of the many layers and subtleties of the use of language, emotions and the body to generate learning which is sustainable and available for future experience as well as past reflection.

In my own learning, initially in these programmes beginning in 2000 and then as a kind of Sorcerers Apprentice to Rob, learning to teach and teaching to learn, I have developed a deep appreciation of the value of experiential learning, and a fascination in facilitating learning in others. My interest in Family therapy has grown from 11 years in a family based general practice, with the added joy for me in learning to be part of a solution oriented conversation that reconnects a family, articulates concerns and uncovers family strengths and resources that assists their being together in a more satisfying and sustaining way.

An Invitation

My purpose here is to invite anyone already working with individuals or couples to play with transferring their talents to working with families, and to provide a forum for exploration and practice for any family therapist to extend themselves in a safe and supported way.
What you will learn
    •    A map to simplify the complexity of a family’s presenting issues.
    •    Competence and confidence in managing the mood within a family and group.
    •    Leading with that mood and the possibilities for the actions that can emerge
    •    A way of observing that will allow increased sensitivities to maintaining this mood throughout a difficult conversation
    •    The use of language as a generative tool for creating solutions
    •    Ways of working with emotions and the body to add texture to the solution experience.
    •    A framework for running a family session   where each participant’s individual style of therapy can be incorporated and built on.
    •    Clinical applications dealing with commonly encountered family problems.

creating lasting solutions in counselling

connecting clients with their experiences


We want solutions for our troubled client, and we want the solutions to last.

The solution approach to counselling is recognised as one of the most effective methods of assisting clients to resolve their problems and create their own individual solutions. It builds on the ground-breaking contributions of Milton Erickson, Steve de Shazer, Insoo Kim Berg, and Bill O'Hanlon.

Even after a client finds a solution that is effective, concerns remain - will they act on it; will it happen, will it last?

Problems often capture our attention and take over clients' experience, blinding them to their solutions, or even the possibility of a solution.

In this workshop we will explore simple and effective ways of connecting clients with the experience of their solutions, moving them past the idea, the hope, the wish of a resolution.

Overview
When Milton Erickson began working, inventing and experimenting with therapy in the 1930’s there was little to choose from in terms of different therapy styles. In the 1960's, there were about 60 different therapy models, and today there are more than 250!!!! It is humbling to note that despite the variety, virtually all of the research data finds roughly equivalent results for all models. And perhaps we only need to look at the world to see that that it is the similarities rather than differences between the models that account for the effectiveness of psychotherapy.

The Solution Oriented Approach honours these similarities, acknowledges that many of the 250 have their roots in Erickson’s work and in a refreshing answer to today’s consumerism, attempts to simplify and unravel the confusion.

Lambert's research in 1992 and Wampole's in 2001 add statistical evidence supporting the core of Erickson’s work specifically and the Solution Oriented approach generally - the importance of
    creating a trusting, legitimizing relationship,
    observing and listening to the client and reconnecting them to their life outside therapy,

    trusting in our own individual therapeutic creativity

Diploma and Advanced Diploma of Solutions in Counselling

simply learning a natural approach to solutions in counselling

Background
This diploma offers more than just information and techniques. It was developed by Rob McNeilly and grounded in 25 years of teaching and learning including personally from Milton Erickson, Humberto Maturana, Julio Olalla and Rafael Echeverria to name a few. It is a rare opportunity to have the ideas of such people distilled into one approach with Rob’s uniquely creative, playful and illuminating style. It was extended by Gabrielle's 10 years experience in General Practice with the inherent frustrations of the medical model that appear common to doctors, counsellors, psychologists and allied health workers alike. She was relieved to discover a different way. As a student of Rob’s she was impressed by the refreshing learning experience he offered that usefully allowed her to change how she observed and  use what she saw to facilitate learning and change in a client. As teachers of this approach Rob & Gabrielle share a passionate interest in the effectiveness of this way of learning for students and clients also.

Overview
When Milton Erickson began working, inventing and experimenting with therapy in the 1930’s there was little to choose from in terms of different therapy styles. In the 1960's, there were about 60 different therapy models, and today there are more than 250!!!! It is humbling to note that despite the variety, virtually all of the research data finds roughly equivalent results for all models. And perhaps we only need to look at the world to see that that it is the similarities rather than differences between the models that account for the effectiveness of psychotherapy.

The Solution Oriented Approach honours these similarities, acknowledges that many of the 250 have their roots in Erickson’s work and in a refreshing answer to today’s consumerism, attempts to simplify and unravel the confusion.

Lambert's research in 1992 and Wampole's in 2001 add statistical evidence supporting the core of Erickson’s work specifically and the Solution Oriented approach generally - the importance of
    creating a trusting, legitimizing relationship,
    observing and listening to the client and reconnecting them to their life outside therapy,
    creating expectancy and
    trusting in our own individual therapeutic creativity
to maximise our effectiveness.

This diploma offers a unique opportunity to learn the principles and applications of one of the most rapidly growing treatment models in contemporary psychotherapy. The scope of the program is comprehensive, and presents the essential elements in an elegantly accessible way and is open to practitioners of all levels of experience who are passionate about their work.

simple
There is an explosion of counselling models and approaches, each new model claiming to be the best.  By exploring each individual's experiences, we can have a simpler and more direct access for the client to their own unique resources.

In this programme we will explore simple, elegant and respectful ways of connecting clients with their own unique resources.

learning
Traditionally learning has been based on theory and the property of a select few experts. By contrast, these diplomas have a minimum of theory and explanations, focusing instead on demonstrations and recurrent practice so that participants will have sufficient skills to begin to use their learning in their clinical practice immediately.

a natural approach
Counselling has been assumed to be a special, different, even weird experience. Erickson spoke of problems being everyday experiences that get stuck, and so can also get unstuck in a helpful way. By utilising individual clients’ individual skills, overlooked skills can be disclosed, re-connected, and found to be readily available, leading to a fail-safe method that will become obvious to the client and so, readily useable.

solutions
Rather than problem solving, the solution approach draws on a client's innate resourcefulness, re-connects them with these resources, and because they come from the client, they can be easily accessible. Ericksonian hypnosis creates a wonderful context for a trusting therapeutic relationship, helping to restore self-trust in individual clients to allow this process to be realised.

counselling
Traditional counselling relies on theories and manualised protocols which are applied to clients in a "fit where it touches" approach, creating resistance and limited compliance. When we begin with the principle that any problem has a solution waiting to emerge, this natural progression can be readily accessed by all clients and incorporated in any clinician’s practice.

What you will learn
• The evolution of counselling throughout recent history.    
• A method of creating genuine rapport.
• Questions to connect clients with their overlooked resources.
• Conversations to engage clients in their counselling process.
• How to create possibilities even in the most "resistant" or "chronic" situations.
• How to include moods and emotions in solutions
• How to design strategies
• Positive reframing
• How to apply this approach to:
• anxiety, panic & depression
• post trauma
• childhood abuse
• pain management
• couples work
• family relations
• performance enhancement
• coaching
• any other clinical situation

Course Structure
The diploma involves 14 workshop days over 8 months and is a prerequisite for the advanced diploma of 10 workshop days over 10 months. Each includes recorded lectures, demonstrations and a written text. Assignments, relevant to the actual clinical utilisation of the learning offer valuable opportunities for reflection and any diploma can be repeated free of charge at any time in the future. The emphasis is on live demonstrations and the opportunity for you to practice recurrently in pairs or triads. These diplomas value experiential learning over acquisition of information so that you will feel confident to incorporate these ideas into your work immediately.

Accreditation
These programmes are accredited by CET, as evidence shows overwhelmingly that external accreditation has no correlation with effectiveness of learning or application.


Summary
These diplomas provide learning opportunities which are individualised, practical, relevant, easily understood, readily translatable into clinical practice, all in a safe environment of respectful support.

Diploma of Solutions in Hypnosis
in Darwin

simply learning a natural approach to solutions in hypnosis

with Dr Rob McNeilly


Background
This diploma offers more than just information and techniques. It was developed by Rob McNeilly and grounded in 25 years of teaching and learning including personally from Milton Erickson, Humberto Maturana, Julio Olalla and Rafael Echeverria to name a few. It is a rare opportunity to have the ideas of such people distilled into one approach with Rob’s uniquely creative, playful and illuminating style. It is complemented by Peter - a psychologist since 1976. In order to facilitate the move from lecturing into beginning a private psychological practice, Peter began learning from Rob McNeilly in 1986. With a background in Educational Psychology, Peter assisted Rob in developing the initial learning assignments for CET programs, and was the Dean of Students at CET between 1989 and 1994. During this time he was also founding editor of etCETera. In 1996 he was awarded a Distinguished Service Award from CET. As teachers of this approach Rob & Peter share a passionate interest in the effectiveness of this way of learning for students and clients also.

Overview
When Milton Erickson began working, inventing and experimenting with therapy in the 1930’s there was little to choose from in terms of different therapy styles. In the 1960's, there were about 60 different therapy models, and today there are more than 250!!!! It is humbling to note that despite the variety, virtually all of the research data finds roughly equivalent results for all models. And perhaps we only need to look at the world to see that that it is the similarities rather than differences between the models that account for the effectiveness of psychotherapy.

The Solution Oriented Approach honours these similarities, acknowledges that many of the 250 have their roots in Erickson’s work and in a refreshing answer to today’s consumerism, attempts to simplify and unravel the confusion.

Lambert's research in 1992 and Wampole's in 2001 add statistical evidence supporting the core of Erickson’s work specifically and the Solution Oriented approach generally - the importance of
    creating a trusting, legitimizing relationship,
    observing and listening to the client and reconnecting them to their life outside therapy,
    creating expectancy and
    trusting in our own individual therapeutic creativity
to maximise our effectiveness.

This diploma offers a unique opportunity to learn the principles and applications of one of the most rapidly growing treatment models in contemporary psychotherapy. The scope of the program is comprehensive, and presents the essential elements in an elegantly accessible way and is open to practitioners of all levels of experience who are passionate about their work.

simple
Hypnosis has been understood as complex, special, even dangerous, requiring unique legal restrictions. It has been associated with magic, the devil, loss of control, and only relevant to susceptible individuals.

In this programme we will explore simple, elegant and respectful ways of connecting clients with their own unique styles of experience.

learning
Traditionally learning has been based on theory and the property of a select few experts. By contrast, these diplomas have a minimum of theory and explanations, focusing instead on demonstrations and recurrent practice so that participants will have sufficient skills to begin to use their learning in their clinical practice immediately.

a natural approach
Hypnosis has been assumed to be a special, different, even weird experience. Erickson spoke of the everyday trance as a natural experience that can be enhanced in a clinical situation. By utilising individual clients’ individual skills, overlooked skills can be disclosed, re-connected, and found to be readily available, leading to a fail-safe method will be made obvious and readily useable.

solutions
Rather than problem solving, the solution approach draws on a client's innate resourcefulness, re-connects them with these resources, and because they come from the client, they can be easily accessible. Ericksonian hypnosis creates a wonderful context for a trusting therapeutic relationship, helping to restore self-trust in individual clients to allow this process to be realised.

hypnosis
Traditional hypnosis relies on manualised scripts which are applied to clients in a "fit where it touches" approach, creating resistance and limited compliance. When we begin with the principle that hypnosis is an experience that involves focus of attention and absorption in that experience, this natural ability can be readily accessed by all clients and incorporated in any clinician’s practice.

What you will learn
 • The evolution of hypnosis throughout history and a contemporary understanding.
• A fail-safe method of assisting any client to experience hypnosis.
• Observational skills to track an individual client's hypnotic experience.
• Ways to create rapport and restore trust.
• Creating possibilities even in the most "resistant" or "chronic" situations.
• The creation of learning as a process towards solutions.
• The use of therapeutic metaphor.
• Become familiar with the classical hypnotic experiences of dissociation, age regression, etc.
• How to apply this approach to:
• anxiety, panic & depression

• childhood abuse
• pain management
• couples work
• family relations
• performance enhancement
• coaching
• any other clinical situation

Course Structure
The diploma involves 14 workshop days over 8 months and is a prerequisite for the advanced diploma of 15 workshop days over 10 months. Each includes recorded lectures, demonstrations and a written text. Assignments, relevant to the actual clinical utilisation of the learning offer valuable opportunities for reflection and any diploma can be repeated free of charge at any time in the future. The emphasis is on live demonstrations and the opportunity for you to practice recurrently in pairs or triads. These diplomas value experiential learning over acquisition of information so that you will feel confident to incorporate these ideas into your work immediately.

Overview
When Milton Erickson began working, inventing and experimenting with therapy in the 1930’s there was little to choose from in terms of different therapy styles. In the 1960's, there were about 60 different therapy models, and today there are more than 250!!!! It is humbling to note that despite the variety, virtually all of the research data finds roughly equivalent results for all models. And perhaps we only need to look at the world to see that that it is the similarities rather than differences between the models that account for the effectiveness of psychotherapy.

The Solution Oriented Approach honours these similarities, acknowledges that many of the 250 have their roots in Erickson’s work and in a refreshing answer to today’s consumerism, attempts to simplify and unravel the confusion.

Lambert's research in 1992 and Wampole's in 2001 add statistical evidence supporting the core of Erickson’s work specifically and the Solution Oriented approach generally - the importance of
    creating a trusting, legitimizing relationship,
    observing and listening to the client and reconnecting them to their life outside therapy,
    creating expectancy and
    trusting in our own individual therapeutic creativity
to maximise our effectiveness.

simple
Hypnosis has been understood as complex, special, even dangerous, requiring unique legal restrictions. It has been associated with magic, the devil, loss of control, and only relevant to susceptible individuals.

In this programme we will explore simple, elegant and respectful ways of connecting clients with their own unique styles of experience.

learning
Traditionally learning has been based on theory and the property of a select few experts. By contrast, these diplomas have a minimum of theory and explanations, focusing instead on demonstrations and recurrent practice so that participants will have sufficient skills to begin to use their learning in their clinical practice immediately.

a natural approach
Hypnosis has been assumed to be a special, different, even weird experience. Erickson spoke of the everyday trance as a natural experience that can be enhanced in a clinical situation. By utilising individual clients’ individual skills, overlooked skills can be disclosed, re-connected, and found to be readily available, leading to a fail-safe method will be made obvious and readily useable.

solutions
Rather than problem solving, the solution approach draws on a client's innate resourcefulness, re-connects them with these resources, and because they come from the client, they can be easily accessible. Ericksonian hypnosis creates a wonderful context for a trusting therapeutic relationship, helping to restore self-trust in individual clients to allow this process to be realised.

hypnosis
Traditional hypnosis relies on manualised scripts which are applied to clients in a "fit where it touches" approach, creating resistance and limited compliance. When we begin with the principle that hypnosis is an experience that involves focus of attention and absorption in that experience, this natural ability can be readily accessed by all clients and incorporated in any clinician’s practice.

Diploma of Solutions in Hypnosis

simply learning a natural approach to solutions in hypnosis

Background
This diploma offers more than just information and techniques. It was developed by Rob McNeilly and grounded in 25 years of teaching and learning including personally from Milton Erickson, Humberto Maturana, Julio Olalla and Rafael Echeverria to name a few. It is a rare opportunity to have the ideas of such people distilled into one approach with Rob’s uniquely creative, playful and illuminating style. It was extended by Gabrielle's 10 years experience in General Practice with the inherent frustrations of the medical model that appear common to doctors, counsellors, psychologists and allied health workers alike. She was relieved to discover a different way. As a student of Rob’s she was impressed by the refreshing learning experience he offered that usefully allowed her to change how she observed and  use what she saw to facilitate learning and change in a client. As teachers of this approach Rob & Gabrielle share a passionate interest in the effectiveness of this way of learning for students and clients also.

Overview
When Milton Erickson began working, inventing and experimenting with therapy in the 1930’s there was little to choose from in terms of different therapy styles. In the 1960's, there were about 60 different therapy models, and today there are more than 250!!!! It is humbling to note that despite the variety, virtually all of the research data finds roughly equivalent results for all models. And perhaps we only need to look at the world to see that that it is the similarities rather than differences between the models that account for the effectiveness of psychotherapy.

The Solution Oriented Approach honours these similarities, acknowledges that many of the 250 have their roots in Erickson’s work and in a refreshing answer to today’s consumerism, attempts to simplify and unravel the confusion.

Lambert's research in 1992 and Wampole's in 2001 add statistical evidence supporting the core of Erickson’s work specifically and the Solution Oriented approach generally - the importance of
    creating a trusting, legitimizing relationship,
    observing and listening to the client and reconnecting them to their life outside therapy,
    creating expectancy and
    trusting in our own individual therapeutic creativity
to maximise our effectiveness.

simple

In this programme we will explore simple, elegant and respectful ways of connecting clients with their own unique styles of experience.

learning

a natural approach
Hypnosis has been assumed to be a special, different, even weird experience. Erickson spoke of the everyday trance as a natural experience that can be enhanced in a clinical situation. By utilising individual clients’ individual skills, overlooked skills can be disclosed, re-connected, and found to be readily available, leading to a fail-safe method will be made obvious and readily useable.

solutions
Rather than problem solving, the solution approach draws on a client's innate resourcefulness, re-connects them with these resources, and because they come from the client, they can be easily accessible. Ericksonian hypnosis creates a wonderful context for a trusting therapeutic relationship, helping to restore self-trust in individual clients to allow this process to be realised.

hypnosis
Traditional hypnosis relies on manualised scripts which are applied to clients in a "fit where it touches" approach, creating resistance and limited compliance. When we begin with the principle that hypnosis is an experience that involves focus of attention and absorption in that experience, this natural ability can be readily accessed by all clients and incorporated in any clinician’s practice.

Diploma of Solution Oriented Family Therapy

Overview

This diploma will be of interest to anyone wanting to work with children and their families, or to family therapists looking for a forum to extend their work in a supported way. This programme provides an opportunity for hands on, experiential learning around family therapy, and even those working with families will benefit from these experiential workshops which provide an opportunity to practice. This programme creates a context for a family to emerge from a group of individuals while exploring the moves that make this possible. These moves are foundational to this work. Managing the mood, joining, and creating connections to unite a family group will be recurrently emphasized, and this in turn will create the possibility to practice with the family that emerges.

CET diplomas have been based in Melbourne and running nationally for 25 years, providing a solid foundation grown from this experience and the feedback of hundreds of students about what is useful in a learning environment. These diplomas provide a refreshing return to emphasising a client’s actual experience, and away from the heady intellectualisations that have been distilled as techniques. This work has evolved from its origins in Rob McNeilly’s time spent with Milton Erickson in the 70’s, through his working and teaching to the present day. In our teaching we emphasise demonstration and practice over lecture and information. Where Erickson was a consummate teacher, we are interested in the experience of learning. Rob has explored this face to face with teachers such as Rafael Eccheverria, Humberto Maturana, Julio Olalla and Fernando Flores to name a few, and has brought this learning to teaching ,creating a community of educators locally, nationally and internationally

Lecture and content in these programmes takes on a very different feel for the student as we ‘speak it’, rather than ‘speak about it’. A meticulous focus on language which is generative shows a consistency of approach in our working, teaching and supervision of students. time travel – the past

Posted on July 28, 2010 by Rob

We have all had the experience of looking at a school photo or some holiday snaps and being transported back to that time. When we approach hypnotic experiences as if they are extensions of everyday experiences, they become more accessible and easier to connect with.

Visiting a memory and becoming absorbed in it sounds to me much preferable to “age regression”!

Also, in this solution approach, we are more interested to assist a client to some useful resource from their past rather than exploring some supposedly causal trauma. To assist someone to re-experience an ability to survive some traumatic event is likely to be much more useful than simply reliving it, with its possibility of re-traumatising them.

How to revisit any past experience will be guided by the question “What’s missing?” for this client.

If there is an overwhelming emotion associated with a trauma, returning to a time before the even can allow for an opportunity to gather up some resources, which may involve additional people, perhaps even their adult self, so they can then weather the situation and come out stronger. It can also be helpful to allow the client to be a disconnected observer, so there is sufficient emotional disconnection. This can avoid the paralysis of the overwhelming emotion, and create an experience of completion – “I survived it!!”.

If there is an emotion of helplessness, help can be imagined; if injustice, then justice; if unfairness, some sort of revenge. Whatever is missing for any individual client can be  imagined, pretended or enacted, and this “unreal” experience is experienced as if it were to be real, and so assists in healing.

connections and disconnections

Posted on July 22, 2010 by Rob

I find it helpful to think of all problems as containing disconnections from resources or connections with unwanted experiences.

This allows us to ask what connections are missing that we can help the client to connect with, and what connections are unhelpful that would be useful to disconnect from.

For me, this is way preferable than exploring “dissociative phenomena”. Simpler, more likely to be useful for a client, and adding to our effectiveness – something we all want, specially our clients!

We can then assist our clients to direct their attention away from problem experiences by disconnection or dissociation, and towards preferred, solution experiences through connections or associations.

experiences in hypnosis

Other so-called complex and advanced hypnotic “phenomena” like age regression and progression, anaesthesia and analgesia, amnesia – so many complex Greek-derived jargon words which can confuse and overwhelm – can be viewed as extension of ordinary experiences – more soon.

early learning

Posted on July 5, 2010 by Rob

Seeing what’s missing can help; experiencing what’s missing can help; stories to enhance the experiencing of what’s missing can help; and … sometimes, even that seems like too big a leap of faith.

In this situation, stories about early learning can bridge the chasm, and make a small beginning possible, getting past the paralysis that overwhelm produces.

When we know what a client likes, we can safely explore explicitly or implicitly, how they learnt that.

“When you first learnt to [do what you like] you [had the problem] and as you learnt, you overcame that difficulty, and [found the missing resource]“.

This can allow such a respectful, and gentle beginning to the process of moving from problem to solution.

stories can help

Posted on July 1, 2010 by Rob

Sometimes a client can see the missing resource, and recognising it is enough. Sometimes it helps to let someone “sit in” the experience of it, to “soak it up”, which is where hypnosis comes into its own.

Other people can see what’s missing, and not see the possibility for them.

Stories can help here.

If we say “I had a client, not unlike yourself, who solved their problem”, our client has the chance to discover that they are not a freak – there are others! Also, if someone else got over their problem, the possibility for this client to get over their problem is based on a shared reality, and moves beyond being a mere hope.

Other stories about life, pets, children, nature etc. can also help to make this possibility more real. These stories are likely to be most relevant if they relate to this client’s individual area of likes. If they like fishing, stories about solving the problem that brought them to us in a fishing context will make sense. If they like cycling, then stories about cycling will be easiest to connect with.

If you’re interested, next time you’re with a client, find a story from your clinical experience that parallels the client’s experience, making sure that the story ends with a good resolution, and then see if you can create another parallel story related to the client’s likes.

connecting with the missing resource

Posted on June 26, 2010 by Rob


The question compels … “How can we assist a client to connect with this resource?”. Some people when they see the possibility of a connection make the connection and have a direct experience of their solution, and that’s all that’s needed. And, some see the possibility of a connection and we can assist them by making the connection for them … “Speaking in front of a crowd is just the same as watching your garden grow”

Sometimes the connection can be unclear for us, so we can off the idea “I wonder what connections you can make, are already making, have already made”.

The resource is always there, in my experience, and connecting is an expected outcome, although of course sometimes it’s not obvious, or even seen as a possibility.

Our mood of expectancy can be so pivotal in assisting the client to make their connection.

experiencing the missing resource

Posted on June 24, 2010 by Rob

When we know what we’re looking for, the next question concerns where we might find it.

If we invite a client to become focused and absorbed in an experience they like – walking in nature, reading, gardening – and then comment on the physiological changes we can observe – blinking, breathing, stillness, etc. – we can explore, with the client, the experiences they are now absorbed in, and look for the missing resource or experience.

My experience is that we will always find that missing resource or experience in their likes, and because the experience comes from the client, it is readily available, readily accessible, easily useable.

what's missing?

“If you don’t know where you’re going, you may end up in a different place” is an old Irish saying, so if we have the question “What’s missing?”, we are more likely to find it.

accessing resources

Posted on June 18, 2010 by Rob

We also saw that exploring “likes” is a delightful way of predictably accessing resources! So … we can ask a client to recall or imaging doing the activity that they like, and then … invite them to focus, become absorbed and we can comment on those bodily changes that we can observe to enhance them.

This not only provides a way into the hypnotic experience, but also generates an experience of experiencing their likes, their enjoyment, their resourcefulness …
