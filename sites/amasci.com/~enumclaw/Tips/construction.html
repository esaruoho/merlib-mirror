Construction Sample of the Stone House




      
Here Bob uses a crowbar
      to align one of the first rocks. As the walls grew, he carried
      all the rocks up ladders and scaffolds. He dug up all the rocks
      in the house from the grounds as he planted the rhododendrons.


 


      
To haul the rocks to the
      construction site, Bob dragged them on a stone boat, or sled,
      made of heavy timbers. I can still remember him struggling with
      a hand crank to start the cantankerous dozer.


      
He placed an early order
      for the truck and received one of the first off the assembly
      line after World War II.


      





      

      
Bob built the first section
      of the house over a basement. The small room at right contained
      a coal room and furnace. I used the other rooms for a variety
      of building projects, including a kayak, a pram, and a lawnmower
      engine car.


      
Bob's use of masonry was
      strongly influenced by his bicycle trip around the gardens and
      estates of Europe in 1934.
