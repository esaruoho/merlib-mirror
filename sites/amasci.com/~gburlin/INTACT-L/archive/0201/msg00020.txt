My letter to the editor, printed in today's SF Chronicle LIVING section, in 
response to last Sunday's article, is copied below.

Editor- Your article "Jewish bris ceremony shows you're never too young for 
tradition" (Dec. 30, 2001) implies that all Jews practice circumcision. On 
the contrary. There is a growing minority of Jews throughout the Reform, 
Conservative and Renewal traditions of Judaism in the United States, and in 
many other countries, including Israel, that reject this practice and we have 
replaced it with a non-cutting naming ceremony similar to that done with 
newborn girls. This form of bris (or brit) is called "bris shalom", or "bris 
b'li (without) mila" in contrast with "bris mila". There are a number of 
Rabbis and other lay leaders throughout the country, including the Bay Area, 
who will officiate at such ceremonies, or will aid young couples in creating 
their own ceremonies.
