Engine 621 Visits a Fire Lookout Tower 

 Part of effective fire suppression is knowing your surroundings. 
On rainy, overcast mornings 

 when we're caught up on project work, we drive back roads to better
prepare us 

 in case of a fire. Knowing how to get somewhere fast, the
terrain, and available 

 water sources are all extremely important. In the process,
we get to visit places most people 

 don't get a chance to see. One day, we heard about an old,
abandoned DNR fire lookout tower someone 

 had spotted off in the distance. The tower didn't appear
on any maps, and you couldn't see it from any main roads. 

 Since "adventure" is Engine 621's middle name, we set out to find
it. After driving 

 an hour on some obscure roads and beating around in the brush,
here's what we discovered. 

 The lookout is about 50 feet off the ground. You get to the
top by negotiating 5 flights of rickety stairs. 

 The view from the inside. Today, we were in the clouds. 
Elevation 

 around 3,700 feet or so. On a sunny day the view would be
spectacular. 

 Zack checking out the scenery to the east. "No fires to report,
Puget." 

 It's kind of a long way down (and up). Probably not the best
place for 

 someone with a fear of heights. 

 The outhouse down below had an interesting interior paint job. 
Probably 

 done by a very bored fire watcher. 

 Another shot of the tower. It's in excellent condition. 
Surprisingly enough, 

 only a small amount of vandalism has taken place. Since it's
on private land, and pretty 

 difficult to find, it hasn't been ruined like so many of the other
lookouts in the Northwest. 

 photos and text by engine leader Joel
