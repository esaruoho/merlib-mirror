The cabin

Picking a cabin site

We went to the Okanogan eight times in 1998, to get to know the place thoroughly, explore the surroundings, and visit with our neighbors. Actually, it turns out that we bought quite a bit of land, and we were still finding gullys that we'd never visited after six trips there. Part of our perambulations involved looking for a place to put a cabin; the plan was to find a location that was sort of hidden in the pines with peek-a-boo views, rather than grand expansive views from the top of a hill. It also had to be accessible from somewhere you can get a truck to, since builders, unlike climbers, don't really go in for hauling heavy weights long distances up hills. We found a really nice spot early on, with just one minor problem: the access jeep track crosses the wetlands most of the year, and only dries out in the Fall.

Finally, we found the perfect place on the middle 40. The access easement actually comes in from the South, rather than the East on the original land, and goes right through a neighbors goat yard: just an added attraction really though.

On to the final orientation: we spent a weekend at Pinehaven doing the final things like camping on the site to get an idea of where the sun rises and sets, and meeting the builders Bob and Jane, who are seeing our project through. They've been very good at telling us some practicalities of building, trying to keep us honest and prevent headaches later on. We finally pegged out our cabin plan on the site, nestled in a small grove of pines, so as to minimise the visual impact of the place, and at the same time have views of the Eastern front of the Cascades.

The foundation

Memorial day 1999 was the first of our work parties. Our friends David, Masami, Alina and Page found their way out there (rather to their surprise, given the really strange directions we'd given them - up the unmarked jeep track for 3 miles, take a left on a worse jeep track, go through a goat yard, past two dead cars...). We got busy with a number of tasks, from making a start on pulling invasive weeds, cutting low branches (a fire hazard) from the trees near the cabin site, even brushing out a place to turn vehicles around down below. We also had our first visitors to the future cabin: our neighbor Connie on her horse knows how to make an entrance! Of course, it wasn't all work; we did quite a bit of hiking, exploring and listening to the wind in the trees.

Alas, the long weekend went by too fast, and still the foundations had not been excavated. So, Arvia and I came back a couple of weeks later to finish the job. It was hard work, around a foot to be dug out of about half the area of the cabin. No dozers either! But, we finished it, and now Bob and Jane get to do their piece of the project.

A cabin appears!

We took a drive out over the July 4th weekend, and were mildly astonished to find that there really was a cabin there! Just where we'd excavated, and very cute. Bob's not quite done yet, the roof still has to be installed, and the upstairs door to the balcony is missing, but it's pretty functional right now. I had misgivings about how tall the thing was going to be, but we found that it fits in nicely from behind the meadow, and is pretty well hidden from the hill above.

The first morning, we woke up to something we'd been missing in western Washington for ages: sun! That was why balconies were invented...

Finishing the interior

It seemed as though we were going to be hauling bales of insulation for ever! But, finally it's drawing to completion, and we're creating cozy spaces: especially the one near the woodstove. Due to the impending winter, these were the first priorities - the normal winter maxima are around -5C and at night it goes down to around -15C.

On Thanksgiving, the first snows of the season showed up, right on schedule, and we fired the stove up for the first time. Everything worked well in this first test, except that after a solid day of burning wood, the spark guard on the chimney got clogged up with fibres. We're going to have to figure this one out - possibly the thing to do is just take off the guard for winter, when we'll be burning the most anyway, and it isn't really needed.

Drywalling

Our little escort staggers under a couple of slices of standard 4x8, so it's going to be a slow process getting all the materials up there. But, it's progressing in fits and starts. We've just about finished the area around the stove - next to come is the kitchen area.

Plumbing

Water is our major project for this coming summer. If we can get a handle on it, then lots of things will be much nicer.

This is going to be a never ending project, so far as we can tell. But, if you feel inspired to help out, well just jump in!
