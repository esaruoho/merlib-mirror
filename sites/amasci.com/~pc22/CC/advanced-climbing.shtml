Advanced Climbing Experiences

Instruction and experiences that go beyond those offered in the Intermediate Climbing program. While not a climbing course in the formal sense, ACE is a loosely woven program of in-town seminars, practice area experiences, and climbing trips to areas outside of the Pacific Northwest. Topics range from aid climbing to risk management, winter ice climbing to advanced rescue techniques. You may take all ice related ACE experiences, or all rock, or any combination that suits you and the kind of climbing you prefer to do.

The basic structure of the ACE program consists of one or two in-town lecture-seminars followed by an outdoor experience. As an example, a lecture on aid climbing with slides of selected climbs and a show of necessary equipment and how each item is used, will be coupled with one or two trips to practice climbing areas where you will learn to pound pins, trust fifi hooks, tie off short, swing leads, and a myriad of other techniques. The final adventure might then be a trip to Yosemite, Hueco Tanks or Joshua Tree, where you could do a multi-pitch aid route in grand style!

Each topic will stand alone, almost as if it's a mini-climbing course in itself. A variety of topics will be offered throughout the year on a random basis, and you may take as many as you like.

A core group of topics is required for attainment of an ACE certificate. The core topics will include successful completion of the advanced rescue seminar (both glacier/ice and high angle rock portions); attendance at a risk management seminar; and attendance at either the high altitude or conditioning seminar. Fees will be dependent on the activity. Required reading will be announced for each ACE offering.

Who is eligible to participate in ACE adventures? All Intermediate graduates and Intermediate students who have successfully completed all of their required field trips, have done a minimum of two rock and one ice climb, and have completed their third year as an Intermediate student. Leaders of seminars and trips will have the discretion to deny any applicant who they feel does not have an appropriate climbing background. Why so restrictive? Well, the ACE program isn't for everyone. It's for those people who want to push the envelope and learn skills and techniques not commonly taught in our climbing courses. Since the aim of the ACE program is to teach advanced climbing skills, a certain level of competency and commitment must be exhibited by each enrollee to ensure safety and to make this fun for everyone.

The schedule of ACE events is published in the appropriate editions of The Mountaineer magazine. Signup is through the clubhouse or leader as stated. Fees should be made payable to The Mountaineers.
