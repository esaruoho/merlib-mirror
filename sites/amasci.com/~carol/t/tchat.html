#tinnitus Online Chats

Roger Williams came up with the idea of meeting on IRC for realtime chats, and he started the #tinnitus channel in July 1996. Thanks Roger!

You can join the #tinnitus chats with an IRC program, or with a java-enabled web browser.

Join #tinnitus using IRC

Connect to the NewNet IRC network, then /join channel #tinnitus.
Or if you don't know how to join a chat using an IRC program, see the documents in IRC Help.

Join #tinnitus from your web browser

To join #tinnitus using your web browser, it must enable Java. Please read these instructions first.

After clicking the link below:

  1. Click the button labeled "Click here to enter"
  2. Optionally, replace the text "Java-user" (after the word NickName) with your chat nickname. Nicknames must be 9 characters or less and cannot contain blanks.
  3. you MUST replace the text "Oasis-2000" after the word Channel, with the word tinnitus
  4. click Connect Now

Join #tinnitus chat.

#tinnitus chat mailing list

The #tinnitus chat mailing list is a small, low-activity discussion group for people who participate in tinnitus chats on IRC. It exists mainly to provide information about chat schedule changes or other IRC issues, and to supplement the IRC chats in terms of support, information & conversation. It's likely to be less T-oriented than alt.support.tinnitus simply because most general T discussion is probably better directed to the newsgroup. This list lets us deal with IRC without boring newsgroup readers, and also enjoy the group that's formed without having to "stay on topic" 100% of the time.

To subscribe, send email to tchat-list-request@eskimo.com with the word subscribe in the Subject: of the message. You will receive confirmation email which also tells you how to send mail to the list, or how to unsubscribe if desired.

Note: a "rotary" is a server name which will connect you to one of a group of servers in a round-robin fashion. They're used to distribute the network load more evenly and to make it easier for people to find servers.

You can ignore the "ports" column if it means nothing to you, except AOL users MUST use port 6670 or higher! When ports other than the default port (6667) are available, they will often connect faster.
