 Big Dogs or Little Dogs 

 For those that frequent pulls, most have noticed the smaller
dogs seem to pull more in proportion to their weight than the big
dogs. The big dogs pull more weigh; but if you divide the weigh
pulled by the weight of the dog, the smaller dogs have higher
ratios. 
 The following charts plot percentiles of this ratio against
dog weight. 
 For those that don't know what a percentile is, I will
explain. If we take several sample measurements of something and
order them in ascending order, then the 50 th 
percentile would be the value in the center (median), and the 80 th 
percentile would be the value 80% of the way toward the end. For
example: eleven values

 Value number 5 (center 90) is the median or 50 th

 The following graphs plot several percentiles of the ratio of
the weight pulled vs. the dog's weight on the vertical axis, and
the horizontal axis is the dog's weight (dogs were bucketed into
5 pound divisions). The first chart is on wheels and the second
on snow. The top purple curve is the maximum ratio occurring.
Then progressing down, the yellow is 90 th percentile,
turquoise is 80 th , red 70 th , green 60 th .
The blue curve mostly along the bottom is the average. 

 The labeled tick marks for dog weight (horizontal) start at
zero, and step by 20 and end at 220 pounds. The vertical axis on
wheels starts with a labeled ratio of 10 and each labeled tick
steps at 10 until 60. For dogs 150lb and up, there is not enough
data to get a good percentile, and the last weight only has two
dogs. 
 Note how the ratios decrease with dog weight. We are just
starting to study this with statistics and really need more data.
We don't know if the lighter dogs are proportionally stronger, or
if the increased resistance due to the heavier cart is causing
the decrease.
