Camp Ocean Pines is holding a bow making workshop.  Learn to make a bow, bowstring, and arrow in the old ways by primitive technology expert, Joe Dabill.  Date:  Oct 14-16, 2005.  Cost: $250/participant, includes food, lodging, all materials, instruction in skills necessary to make a simple bow and arrow from native materials.  Check in Friday evening 5 pm.  Seminar instruction Friday evening, all day Saturday, Sunday morning.  Participants learn and do the following:

* choose raw materials

* engineer and craft the "stick" into a primitive bow

* make a bow string

* learn basic flint knapping to create a simple projectile point

* complete the shaft with your point, fletching, and sinew.

Joe Dabill has facilitated workshops around the country and has been an instructor for the Santa Cruz Mountains Natural History Association, the Catalina Island Marine Institute, the Santa Barbara Museum of Natural History, the California Academy of Sciences, the Miwok Archaeological Preserve of Marin, the Rabbitstick and Wintercount Primitive Skills Gatherings, and has been hired by native American tribes to teach their children the old skills.   

Cost of the weekend workshop is $250/person and includes room, board, materials and instruction.  Be prepared for hard work with hand tools as you turn sticks and stones into effective primitive hunting implements. We provide the safety goggles and bandaids!  Participants will also be able to purchase museum quality knives, bows, arrows, arrowheads, drills, and fire kits from the instructor.  If you're not knapping flint, fletching an arrow, or shaving just a little more material off your bow, you can spend your free time taking a nap, visiting the elephant seal rookery, touring Hearst Castle, walking through the forest or along the beach, OR PRACTICING ARCHERY!   

I've gone through the class and made a really fast bow from Yew. I encourage you and your friends to sign up.  Limited to the first 16 participants.  More info and registration on the website at http://www.campoceanpines.org/ or feel free to call us at the numbers below.

The Desert Studies Program at CSUF offers anthropology classes through the CSUSB Extended Learning Program.   I have taught an anthro class in that program for the last 12 years.   The details are as follows: The Art and Science of Flintknapping. Course No.:  ANTH 1000 (administered through CSUSB Extended Education Program) Units:  2, Fees:  $294 (credit)/$189(non-credit)/$132 Dates:  Feb. 25 (Friday evening), 26 (all day and evening), and 27 (morning and early afternoon) Where:  ZZYZX--Desert Studies Center, near Baker, California (Between Barstow and Vegas, off of I15). The California State University system operates a desert field station in heart of the Mojave Desert near Baker, California.  Throughout the year, the Center hosts a variety of courses in several disciplines.  The flintknapping class is a weekend learning experience taught at the Center for those who want to understand and make stone tools.  Students stay in a dormitory setting and cafeteria-style meals are provided (these are included in the fees).  Most of the class time is spent doing flintknapping. Lectures occur on Friday and Saturday evenings.  For archaeologists, this course provides information on debitage types associated with different lithic technologies. It is surprising how much some students progress in just a weekend.  Bed clothes/sleeping bag are/is needed.  Also, pieces of leather or other durable material should be brought to serve as lap and palm pads.  The raw material and basic tools needed for making stone tools are provided. To register, potential participants should contact CSUSB Extended Learning at 909-880-5975.  More info can be obtained at http://cel.csusb.edu.
