 Delphi (&#916;&#949;&#955;&#966;&#959;&#943;) 

 Delphi is an incredible place which still has a very spiritual and mystical
aura about it (when it's not being drowned out by tour buses). Situated
high up on the side of Mount Parnassus, it was the site of the famous Oracle, a
priestess who would interpret the will of Apollon. 

 Temple
 Of Apollon Entrance 
 The major archeological site at Delphi is the Sanctuary of Apollon,
 a large sacred area which was used for temples, treasuries, monuments,
 and the like. The centerpiece of all this is the Temple of Apollon. 
 This is where the famous Oracle at Delphi was located.
 According to legend, the Oracle was located here after a shepherd
 noticed his goat acting strangely whenever going near a particular
 chasm. He went to investigate and upon breathing the fumes
 emanating from the chasm, he was overcome by them and started speaking
 inarticulately. After news of this spread, a tripod was placed
 over the chasm opening, where a woman would sit and take on the role of
 the person who gave oracle. Later a temple was built on this spot. 
 Political and military leaders, as well as ordinary citizens, would
 come to Delphi to visit the Oracle for help in making decisions. 
 Several Temples Of Apollon were built on this spot over time; only a
 few columns from the last one are standing today on the foundation. 
 The words "Know Thyself" and "Nothing In Excess"
 were supposedly inscribed on the temple at one time. 

 Lyn on Stairway To Nowhere 
 It seems this stairway may have never been finished; it leads right
 to a wall. There may have been a wooden stairway built over it (if
 you look closely at the wall on the left you can see some holes where
 wooden beams might have been mounted). 

 Base of Statue Of Apollon Sitalkas 
 This was the base of a large statue which was more than 50 feet
 high. If you look closely you can see a lot of writing engraved in
 it. Some of this had to do with the statue itself. However,
 at Delphi there is writing all over everything. These were to
 record various events, treaties, winners of competitions, etc. 

 Stadium From West End 
 One of the major events that took place at Delphi every eight years
 (later changed to every four years) was the Pythian Games. These
 included both musical contests (since Apollon, the patron god of Delphi,
 was also the god of music) and athletic contests. The Pythian
 Games were second only to the Olympic Games in importance.
 The stadium was built to house some of the athletic contests. 
 It is the best preserved ancient stadium in Greece, although the current
 form with the stone benches wasn't built until the second century AD.
 Originally, the spectators sat on the ground.

 Theatre From Above 
 The theatre was where the aforementioned musical contests of the
 Pythian Games were held. It has an incredible view of the valley
 below. 

 Benches Of Theatre 

 Stone With Roman Inscription 
 As I mentioned earlier, there is writing inscribed on stones nearly
 everywhere you look at Delphi. This particular one seems to date
 from Roman times - it's written in the Roman alphabet rather than in
 Greek. You can quite clearly see the words "Populus Delphius"
 - the people of Delphi. Also, near the top, I can make out "Imperatorem
 Galleis," which I believe refers to the Gallic
 Empire . There's a lot more, including what is presumably a
 date of some kind (a long string of what looks like Roman numerals) but
 I can't quite make it all out, especially all the fine print at the
 bottom. :-)
 One of the things that I learned while visiting Delphi is: If you
 want something to last, make it out of stone. 

 Evening Valley View 

 Omphalos 
 Zeus wanted to find out where the center of the world was, so he
 released two eagles; one from the east, the other from the west. 
 They met over Delphi, so Delphi became known as the center of the
 world. The Omphalos, or Navel Stone, was placed here to indicate
 this.
 The original Omphalos was in the Temple of Apollon; this one
 (currently in the Delphi Museum) is a later copy, possibly from early
 Roman times. 

 Sphinx Of The Naxians 
 This Sphinx, also now in the museum, greeted visitors to the
 sanctuary from atop a 40-foot high column. 

 Swimming Pool at Gymnasium 
 A little ways down the road from the Sanctuary of Apollon are the
 ruins of the Gymnasium. Athletes trained here, and it was also a
 center for public speaking. This was actually a swimming pool
 which was filled by from the sacred Kastalian Spring which is nearby. 

 Sanctuary of Athena Pronaia - Tholos From Above 
 This is the classic "postcard shot" of the Tholos at the
 Sanctuary of Athena Pronaia. Tholos means "beehive" and
 is used to refer to this type of circular building. These are
 actually very rare in ancient Greek architecture (most Greek buildings
 were rectangular) and this is the best known and best-preserved Tholos. 
 Little is known about the original purpose of this building. It
 may have been a temple but if so, it is not known who it was dedicated
 to. It certainly must have been an important building given the
 high quality of workmanship and the unusual structure. It is quite
 impressive to look at.
 Three of the original 20 columns were reconstructed in modern
 times. This helps to give an idea of what the building might
 have looked like . 

 Sanctuary of Athena Pronaia - Tholos From East 

 Sanctuary of Athena Pronaia - Thesauros of the Massaliotes 
 There are a number of remains of thesauroi (treasuries) around
 Delphi. These were small buildings which looked somewhat like
 temples but were used to commemorate victories in battle and presumably
 to store treasure. 

 Sanctuary of Athena Pronaia - Lyn In Temple Of Athena 
 Unfortunately, due to Delphi's mountainside location it is subject
 to bombardment by falling rocks. Lyn is sitting in the shadow of
 an enormous boulder in the middle of what was once the Temple of Athena.
