Santorini (Thra, Θήρα)

Santorini is one of the most famous islands in the Cyclades.  It was originally called Thira (and in fact many Greeks still call it that).  The island is volcanic, and in fact a huge eruption in the 16th century BC caused massive destruction and significantly changed the landscape.

Well known for its natural beauty and many picturesque churches, Santorini is very tourist-oriented.  The sheer cliffs on the inside of the crater are stunning, and the caldera in the middle is still active.

Wine Fields
Santorini is covered with vineyards.  But due to the harsh dry climate, the grapes are not grown on trellises; instead, the vine is coiled up on the ground.
Mules on Stairs to Old Port
For decades, visitors to Santorini would arrive at the port at the bottom of the cliff, where there is a long winding stairway up to Fira, the biggest town on Santorini which is perched atop the cliffs.  You could ride a mule which is a lot easier than walking.

There is also a cable-car now which you can ride.  But the mules are still there.

Unfortunately, the ferries and most ships now dock at the new port, which is further south and is pretty boring in comparison.
