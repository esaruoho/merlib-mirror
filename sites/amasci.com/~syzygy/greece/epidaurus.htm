Epidaurus (Επίδαυρος)

Epidaurus was a center for healing.  The large sanctuary at this site was dedicated to the god of healing, Asclepius.  Asclepius was usually depicted holding a staff with a serpent wrapped around it.

Unfortunately, while Epidaurus has an extensive archeological site, not many of the pictures I took there were very impressive.

Theatre at Epidaurus
The theatre here is world-famous since it is the best preserved of all the ancient Greek theatres.  It had been completely buried, and was excavated in the late 19th century and subsequently renovated.  (You can see in the foreground the difference between the original and renovated portions of the benches.)

The acoustics of this theatre are famous, and for good reason.  While we were sitting on the very back row, a woman from a tour group gave a demonstration by dropping coins onto the altar base in the very center of the orchestra (where the man is standing in the photo).  I had no problem hearing the coins hit the ground.  I also was able to hear quite clearly when she struck a match.

Classical Greek theatre is performed here every summer as part of the Epidaurus Festival.
