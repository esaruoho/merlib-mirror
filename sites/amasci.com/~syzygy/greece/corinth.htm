Corinth (Κόρινθος)

Corinth was a major trade and manufacturing center throughout the classical Greek period.  It benefited from being near a narrow isthmus separating the Corinthian Gulf and the Saronic Gulf.  Ships were taken out of the water and pulled across land to get to the other side.  In modern times there is a canal built through this isthmus allowing ships to pass.  In fact, the idea of building a canal there goes back thousands of years.  The Roman Emperor Nero ceremoniously started building a canal around 62 BC, but this was unsuccessful and the canal wasn't actually finished until 1893.

Corinth reached its peak later under Roman rule, when it became famous for extravagance and decadence - sort of the Las Vegas of its day.

Lechaion Road
This road, the main road into the city, connected ancient Corinth to the nearby port of Lechaion.
Peirene Fountain
This is the fountain that still supplies water for Ancient Corinth to this day.  The water actually came from behind the arches to the left, but at one point the large basin in the center of the courtyard was filled with water as well. 
Lyn Looking at Marble Wall
The interesting thing about this wall was that it was covered in beautiful grey marble, but only an inch thick or so.  Underneath the marble, there is a regular stone wall.
Temple of Apollo
The most incredible thing about the Temple of Apollo is that unlike most large Greek temples where the columns are made from drums stacked on top of each other, its massive Doric columns are carved from single blocks of stone.  How they managed to move these and lift them into position, I have no idea.

Acrocorinth - Entrance
Up on a hill overlooking Corinth, there is a fortress called Acrocorinth.  This fortress has been used since Archaic times and contains ruins dating from as early as the 7th century BC all the way up to the 19th century.  At one point in the 13th century, while under Byzantine occupation, it withstood a five-year siege by the Franks before finally being captured.

This picture shows the three gates that a visitor must pass through to get inside the fortress.  There is also a moat to cross - visible at the lower right.
