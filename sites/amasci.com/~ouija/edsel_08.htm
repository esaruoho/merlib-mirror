August 3rd, Sunday 2003: Worked on my boat today, haven't run it in a year or so and two of the spark plugs turned to mush so it's off to get a new set and plug wires too...the neighbors cat decided to shed about thirty pounds of cat hair in the boat...so I have a better cover on it now.  I need to send down the last two steering components to Portland and my favorite steering specialist shop ProSteering [http://www.prosteering.com], they did a stellar job on the steering box so I need them to do just the last couple of items.  Heck, I need to make sure the car is as good or better than new, I want some cruising miles out of her. If you have steering needs, go to them, ask for Mike, they are great folk!

August 21st, Thursday 2003:  Called Ray at the transmission shop, they are about done however their mechanic that is working on the transmission is going on vacation for a week so as soon as he gets back, he'll finish up the last couple of items and it should be ready around September 3rd or so, yea!

August 24th, Sunday 2003: Crawled under the car today, poor thing has been on stilts for months now.  I heavily undercoated the bottom with the rubber insulator mixture and have a couple of pictures of the finished product including the patches and fixes underneath, good as new...wait...better than new, hehehe.  IN anticipation of the transmission arrival I have straightened up the area around the car as it will have to be lifted into the ceiling again in order to roll the frame under the car...I drug it out without tires on before so I will have to raise the body a lot father this time in order to clear the rear axle hump.

August 29th, Friday 2003: The new parts arrived from ProSteering in Portland today [http://www.prosteering.com]...they look great! Nice caps plugging all the appropriate holes and a good coat of black paint. The power steering RAM didn't arrive with a pair of washers, a pair of rubber grommets and the bolt for the end (Similar in design to that of the end of a shock that fits through a hole in the frame with the grommets allowing for some play and vibration absorption). So, da DA, Parts Challenge! (I Knew this one would be a good one)...okay, Napa didnt have them (That was a surprise)...Schucks didn't which was not a surprise, I even tried True-Value but they didn't have rubber grommets the right size. AutoZone didn't have them either but by this time I figured, okay, I'll just buy one shock and the hardware comes with them...should be easy right? Here's how the dialogue went at AutoZone: 

Me: I need to buy one shock, the cheapest one you have (pointing at one on display for about $15 (20 Canadian). 

Ted: Sounds fine, for what kind of car? 

Me: Doesn't matter, I just need the washer hardware and grommets that it comes with, like that one for $15 (Pointing at the shiny box containing the mystery shock). 

Ted: So what year is the car? 

Me: Doesn't matter, whatever works, how about that one...(pointing yet again at the jewel like treasure hidden in the shock box). 

Ted: (Confusion spread over his face at the utterance of "Doesn't Matter") 

Me: How about a 65 Mustang, doesn't really matter, the one in that box there would be fine (again, pointing at the ever elusive box a mere six foot from me and even closer to Ted). 

Ted: (Punching a few numbers)...Six or Eight Cylinder...(My turn to roll my eyes) 

Me: How about the V8 one, it really doesn't matter, I'm just going to throw the shock away because all I need is the mounting baggie of hardware that it comes with. 

Ted: (Punching a few numbers again)...Okay we have one for $60 bucks or one for $15, one is heavier use like a V8 might have. 

Me: How about the $15 one, since I only need the washers and grommets, the rest of the shock is a waste for me. 

Ted: (Punches a few buttons, reaches around behind him and ... yes ... I am going to say it ... he grabbed the one I was pointing to all along)...We have quite a few of these in stock, looks like (looking proud of himself for discovering the elusive shock). 

Me: Great I'll take that one. 

Ted:  Just one? (Looking puzzled) It is always a good idea to replace them in pairs (looking completely serious at his own words). 

Me: ...uh...just one please, I only need the parts bag inside, you can keep the rest. 

Ted:  Since you are buying the cheapest shock, I don't suppose you want to buy the extended warranty? (Once again, serious were his words)... 

Me:  No (My own look of puzzlement must have comical...no one caught on though). I bought the shock, took out the baggie and handed the box back to him. 

Ted:  (Puzzled once again) Don't you want the shock? (Yes, he was serious)... 

Me:  Nope, just the bag, maybe someone else wants the shock; I really don't feel like just hucking it in a dumpster since it is new. 

Ted:  (Looking puzzled again...Ted called for his parts pal to check on what to do with the shock)...I guess we could just hold it for you in case you need it? 

Me:  Sure, lets do that. (I left with my baggie of goodies for the RAM and left the counter folk confused and +1 on their shock inventory)

September 6th, Saturday 2003: Today was the busy day...I loaded the transmission onto the kids' radio flyer wagon and stowed it the night before in the garage with the shiny new torque converter too. I cleaned the garage floor one more time and swept twice to ensure a clean floor before I had to crawl under the car as it would be my luck I needed to get under there and would end up face deep in dust, spider webs, and bits of glass for an older broken plant pitcher. Cracked out the old cherry picker (odd name, couldn't ever use it to pick cherries...yeah, probably an old joke) and lifted off the dust cover from the fresh engine I rebuilt months ago. With some effort I got the engine off the stand and dangling in the air...luckily a couple of friends came by last night and we spun the frame in the driveway to face the garage.

I dusted off the beautiful engine mounts restored back east and then figured out that I didn't know which set of mounting holes to use, there was one set above the other...so I went back through my picture archive and found that the upper set was the set to use. With those mounted in place I wheeled the engine out and over the frame and slowly down into it's spot, it fit perfectly. Wheeled the transmission over next, added the two quarts of transmission fluid to the torque converter (A VERY important thing to do during a restoration or rebuild) and slid it into the bell housing, looking good so far. Lowered the transmission into place and it fit great, a word of warning though. Like Ray from the transmission shop says, there are two view ports for the drainage holes in the torque converter, make sure those are lined up right before you bolt the converter to the flywheel. I remembered this and everything went together great and lined up nice. Forgot to clean the bolts from the old bell-housing...so a few minutes later it was all bolted together and torqued to specs. 

I had to put the steering column back on the frame/car and push it into our cal de sac and spin it around so I could get it under the body. I hoisted the body up into the rafters of the garage and used the cherry picker to life the rear, man I need a lot of clearance to get the frame back under the car...heck, when I took it apart there were no tires or usable springs on it, amazing the height increase. With some grunting and effort the frame slowly slid back under the car and looks great from underneath, all coated and sealed with a shiny new frame all complete with new shiny bolts and brake lines. Problem...oh yeah, body to frame mounts, I need rubber discs...

September 7th, Sunday 2003: "Parts Challenge!" Okay rubber mounts for a frame to body use, one would think that would be easy...nope. Tried Napa for starters figuring that would be easy, yeah they had them in one inch and two inch at the proper thickness...however they had only two, I need eighteen, and it would take them several days to order them and with my car in the rafters suspended with nothing under it but four feet of air and a fresh frame, nope can't wait that long. Called Schuck's figuring they may have such a thing, nope, they don't even carry them or order them. Autozone failed as well, they also don't carry them or order them. So I trundled off to McClendon's hardware thinking maybe they had rubber or something around I could use...nope, they had a few pipe grommets that were close but not thick enough. I drove a few more miles and checked an auto parts store I seldom venture to because the treated me like ignorant crap once. Of course they didn't have anything either but a customer there pointed me at a store in Enumclaw called Enumclaw Autoworks, a store that specializes in suspension parts for customs and hot rods. Thirty miles later, I found they were closed on Sunday...sigh.

Then it hit me, yeah, so I drove back the 30 miles and stepped into Wal-Mart, yeah yeah...don't give me grief. That is where I bought five of these and used a hack saw to make 20 of these.

An hour later I had them all in place, all at 3/4 of a inch according to spec and lowered the body down. I took the forethought of dropping a very long screw driver down each mount hold inside the car, through the pan and through the rubber grommet and through the frame bolt hole so I could easily visualize the rough alignment by the pointing of the handles everywhere...this was very cool and worked great. Lowered it down and it fit 99% right on, I used a crow bar and sweat to move the body just a hair this way and that for final alignment and lowered the body the last time. The frame crouched a little but not much, man this car sits high, but I have to remember that there is nothing inside like seats, dash, etc,. Done! Yeah, took a few pics, made sure the shocks worked good (oh yea) and wha la. And all at 1am in the morning with me having to get to work in the am...yawn and yikes! g'night.
