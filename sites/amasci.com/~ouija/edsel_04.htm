The Suspension

January 4th, Saturday 2003: Today I tore off the entire suspension system, right down to the frame rails, wow the old car looks bare.  It appears that whoever bought the car did no maintenance such as chassis lubrication or brake changing.  The passenger side upper control arm must have clunked when drove, it's axel was loose in the socket allowing for a lot of play (about three millimeters worth) especially during turning.  The ball joints on the passenger side are fine but I will replace them anyways, the drivers side were fair with the upper one being a bit loose but really who cares, they will all be new.  The control arms themselves are dirty but in great shape.  

So far, all the steering joints are covered with a layer of caked dirt and sand, man, where has this car been, there's even sand in the fresh air galleys!  What next...I may start the frame off cleaning soon along with painting the firewall early so I can get the engine compartment back together soon to make more room in my shop.  See ya until next time!

 

My daughter gave me a lot of help cleaning and disassembling.
January 7th, Tuesday 2003: Tackled extracting the spidery remains of the convertible top skeleton...when I bought the car I knew that the arms weren't in the best shape and a few were broken so I knew I was in for it.  The skeleton pulled fairly easily out with some coaxing of WD40, two arms are broken (pot metal is not the strongest) and a couple of large chrome rivets are broken however it is serviceable and a spare set of arms were included with the car so I will have to inventory them and see if the parts I need are there.  Also took out the seats and pre-soaked the frame to body bolts to begin their loosening process, man there are a lot of them.  

Soon I expect to be able to lift the body from the frame and roll the frame out and begin it's restoration, new rubber and all new stainless steel lines will be run and inserted, insuring a long and durable life ahead.  The seats!  Wow were they a mess, the front seat is mostly rusted away and shed about a pound of Edsel Dirt, a phrase I have concocted for this restoral job, it describes the stuff that flakes off the car as the process is going, it looks like dirt, smells like dirt and old car, I wonder where all this stuff came from...even under the car, the frame rails are full of sand and powdery dirt, what a mess.

January 10th, Saturday 2003: Removed the rest of the interior today and started pulling out all the wiring so by tonight or tomorrow, the insides should be bare, yeay!  Almost time to remove the body from the frame...all the bolts are free and just old gravity is holding the body in place.  Now for some hoisting!

January 14th, Tuesday 2003: Removed the padded dash, as hard as nails it was and stuffed with rotten yellowish crumbles that at one time must have been foam.  It was nasty!  Yikes, and smelled...ew.  It was marbled with mold and just awful...and now gone.  Under the speaker grill I found a couple of interesting finds...a 1963 Canadian nickle to match the 1957 Canadian penny that was lodged in the ruins of the front seat springs.  The quarter was resting on a pair of ticket stubs from a movie in 1973...hard to tell but the tickets read "Famous Players, Canadian Corporation Limited".  Toronto Canada.  A little investigation led me to discover that the tickets were from a Vancouver Paramount Theater from 73, hence the $1.25 cost for the movie.  There was also a "Sifto" company packet of "Free Running" salt.  Oddly also, a layer (1/2 thick) of fine powdery dirt like river sediment was all throughout the dash and speaker grill area.  What a mess to clean.  Here is a listing of a few 1973 movies:

January 15th, Wednesday 2003: Removed the instrument panel today (thank goodness for the reproduction version of the Edsel shop manual) leaving just a few dangling wires to remove later...noticed that all of the cables are gone, actually missing, from under the dash that open and close the vents and heater...hope the donor has these...luckily I still have my 58 Ranger, the environmental module from it will fit nicely into the convertible...it is restored with a new electric motor (with a five year full guarantee nonetheless).  With a last sigh, I soaked the bolts that held the body to the frame, not a touch job, just over a dozen bolts hold the cabin to the frame...maybe they'll come free in a few days, the underside of the car looks like a forest floor covered with splotches of tar, dirt, rotten vegetation and spattered with sand.

January 17th, Friday 2003:  Came home from work about an hour early and got a start on removing the bolts from the body, only one broke off in the nut but there were four I didn't have to remove, they had already rusted away or the area was missing around the bolt, just the bolt poking it's head into the cabin of the car looking around for a place to grip but nothing but air left.  One thing, the frame looks good and clean although filled with the same sedimentary fine dirt that the speaker grill was filled with...oddly too, the amount of white sand in the frame rails is really a riddle.  The car appears to have spent all it's first life in British Columbia, probably near a beach (because of the sand everywhere as well as the fine dirt).  

Here we are, the car pals, my girl Karyntha is posing here as she is removing the steering gear with some help from dad.

Frame Removal and Cleaning

January 18th, Saturday 2003: With all the bolts out I used my trusty cherry picker to lift the front of the cabin off the frame a couple of feet and supported that under a pair of sturdy stands.  Lifted the rear with old red again and the body moved easily from the frame.  Pulled the frame out from under the car using Goldie (My old truck) and left it in the spot normally reserved for our 97 Stratus (Rosy).  So for now I will retire the cabin to waiting in the garage as I take care of bringing the frame and suspension back to life.

January 19, Sunday 2003: Inspected the welds on the frame today, they show no rust or cracking which is great...one odd thing, the rear drivers shock mount was gone and someone welded a few pieces of brake drum together to create a makeshift mount (that had also broken)...I will have to yank one off the donor or make one.  Began the process of cleaning the frame today, wow, it is huge, I stood back today and drank in the enormous I beam construction that went into this car, impressive.  I guess I will have to run a majority of the brake lines and gas lines before I return the cabin to the frame.  In cleaning I found a LOT of dirt inside the frame rails, very dry and most of it blew out on the first try....thanks goodness for compressed air.  At sometime the car had an encounter with concrete, choking one of the stabilizer bar shackles was concrete, a lot of hammering and pounding finally removed it.  Score again, between two of the rubber frame to cabin seals was a 1957 quarter, must have been put there when it was built.  With daylight running out, I completed cleaning the first four feet of the frame and gave it a coat of some very special black paint designed for frame applications to protect and seal.  

I probably won't get a chance to do more work until next weekend as the frame is in the drive way under cover but outside so the noise of cleaning might bother neighbors...sigh...the small completed section does look great, and my daughter helped me with a lot of the cleaning.  Until later, nite!


January 25th, Saturday 2003: Well, it has been a few days since I have had the chance to work on the car...mainly the pouring rain has prevented me from removing the cover from the frame but today there was about four hours of sunshine so with a lot of scrubbing, a few pints of chemicals, more scrubbing and treatments, I got through to just by the rear axel in the frame resto.  This frame is actually in great shape and coming together fine.  Funny thing, I had a break then came out and saw a few drops of water so I packed my tools inside and just as I covered the frame a torrent of rain began to pummel me and the cover, very lucky it was covered.  Until next time!

January 29th, Wednesday 2003: In preparation for completing the frame I am ordering the parts I need to replace the ball joints and all the rubber bushings and stops.  I started on the task of removing the ball joints from the control arms, man, turns out of all four balls, only one is bolted in, the rest are riveted...here is what I suspect, that bolted one is on the same side that the upper arm axel was very worn so probably, the worn axel caused the ball joint to wear prematurely and thus the replacement of the original riveted one with a bolted one.

January 30th, Thursday 2003: So, with much grinding, drilling, and pounding, the ball joints are free from their control arm jails and off to freedom in the metal recycling can.  The four control arms are caked with years of dirt, oil, and who knows what so this weekend I'll get them looking new and inspect them for any defects.  Nite for now!

Just my luck too, the first one I removed happened to be bolted and was easy, that certainly set me up for a disappointment with the other three...lots of drilling (1/8th inch pilot followed by a 3/8th through the middle of the rivets).

February 02, Monday 2003: The car has been sitting the last few days, my Grandad is in the hospital suffering from a stroke so the family is in a bit of chaos.  Early Saturday I did get the passenger side control arms cleaned, dipped and painted, they're ready to go.  Also found the company that supplies the chemicals to Rhino for pickup truck liners, it is a thick rubber substance that is very very tough and water/chemical resistant.  They direct sell this stuff in a can too, about 60 bucks for a dozen cans, I will post their info if it works out well as an undercoating (industry companies such as Ford and Chevy buy this stuff from them so I suspect it is good...we'll see).
