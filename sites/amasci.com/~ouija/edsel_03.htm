The Tranny

December 22nd, Monday 2002: "Parts Challenge!" Napa so far seems to have been the most consistent.  Found the air cleaner, oil filter and new distributor cap there.  They win and with same day delivery if the parts aren't there, very cool.  I have done a fair amount of engine work and restoration in the past but many have told me to NOT to tackle an automatic tranny.  So I will anyway.  Kanter supplies a great kit to rebuild the entire tranny (http://www.kanter.com) and the torque converter will probably come from the Transmission Parts Company at: http://www.tpcconverters.com/ford1.htm.  I am still planning this thing.

December 24th, Tuesday 2002: Removed the transmission today, caked with tons of "Edsel Dirt" probably the result of old leaky valve covers.  Removed the bell housing and set to cleaning the transmission.  So with it clean now, do I want to rebuild it or have a local shop do it...I used to work with a guy that managed a transmission shop and he has told me some stories so I am very guarded against letting someone rebuild it.  When I was pulling the tranny, the lines looked good so I sat them aside for later but the changer motor (teletouch module) looked like a large blob of tar and sand...oy.  I will put that aside and clean it later.  Someone converted the car over to an auto on the floor years ago, a very long time ago looking at the wear of the makeshift shifter...wonder why they did the conversion.

December 26th, Thursday 2002: Spark plug wires arrived today after a small mix-up and a nitro kit showing up at my house instead...wow they are blue, that's alright, it will be fine, I like a little variety.

December 28th, Saturday 2002: Finished cleaning the transmission, when you do this it is best to seal the end up where the yoke enters, I ended up with a large pool of fluid down my side and into my shoe...ew.  Sat down today with the Teletouch module and began the archeology on the many layers of dirt, oil, dirt, sand, dirt, and oil, and dirt again.  Wow, it's about half the size it was and smells awful.  Pulled off the gear housing and found out why it probably failed, it was full of corrosion, waxed grease, and mold.  

Obviously at some time it acquired a leak and filled with water.  Ah ha, also found out why it was converted to a floor shifter, one of the delicate teeth inside the spinning sensor was broken off at the base, so from the looks of it, park was broken and could not be shifted via the module.  Luckily I have a great working NOS changer so I will just put the broken one out in the spare Edsel which is now more like the repository for half working parts.

December 29th, Sunday 2002: Found the old power steering pump in the trunk, rusted solid from wheel to bowl...luckily I have one of these in my shop too, a NOS I landed last year at the Puyallup Car Meet.  Tomorrow I will call around and see if anyone wants to rebuild my transmission.  According to the cars odometer it has seen 69,000 miles, which is probably accurate according to the info I have so far: In a wrecking yard in 76 for three years...then in storage pretty much from then until I acquired it.  So in 76 it would have had the chance for 20 years of driving around Vancouver BS and the greater northwest...and since it is a convertible, they generally have a lower mileage per year so 69k is probably accurate.  

January 2, Thursday 2003: Found a reliable transmission shop today, in fact the owner just rebuilt a transmission for a large Edsel two weeks ago, that's where I'll take it.  Wonder how much it'll cost...we'll find out later I guess.
