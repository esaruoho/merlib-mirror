The Firewall and Beyond!

Here's where we start to see the car coming back to life...now I have to find all the parts...

 

September 12th, Saturday 2003:  I began by stripping and cleaning the firewall and painting with a good solid black lacquer paint (staying in context of the car's age).  I was thinking of opting for the firewall to stay blue like the rest of the car but heck, I would be spending a lot of time cleaning that firewall between drives and that's not easy.  Today I also tacked the wind shield wiper setup...luckily the parts car, we'll call it Ted in honor of the guy on the previous page...'nough said.  Ted willingly gave up his drivers side pulley hardware over to the old gal, luckily Ted has a set of working wires and pulleys so I was able to re-wire the car fine and they work great now, took a couple of hours to get it just right though.  Finally tore off the Power Brake booster unit, a Bendix, that I have a rebuild-kit for...the guys at Napa said I was plumb crazy to try to rebuild my own but I did it anyway and hey, as long as you follow directions, can cook, or chew gum while blinking, you can do it too.  It turned out great, just need to find some Bendix Vacuum Oil to work in the new leather or I found, certain mineral oils will work too.  Inside the old unit wasn't that bad but most of the rubber pieces were spilt, cracked, and no longer rubber.  Maybe tomorrow I will get to start more firewall projects...night!

After, very nice and shiny.

September 13th, Sunday 2003: Ya know, I need to get the starter rebuilt!  PARTS CHALLENGE!  Okay Napa here I go, first hit and boom, they will send it out tomorrow and have it rebuilt, around $160 bucks too...basically because it is a complex starter compared to the new fangled ones.  Nice but Napa (253-862-1314 here in Bonney Lake) gives a lifetime guarantee and ya can't beat that!.  The power steering hoses will be here next week too for a neat tuck under the car.  Need to get the brake booster back on the firewall and get a 20inch length of brake hose so I can setup the brakes and bleed em.
Here the boost sits, a heap of rust, dust, and age
Here the booster sits all new and very cool!

September 16th, Tuesday 2003:  Sick as a dog all last night, the local state fair did me in...however had some good news today.  Turned in my starter on Sunday to good old Napa and boom, done already this AM!  Yeah, and they did a great job too, even slipped on the new Bendix end for me.  The starter went on okay and I replaced the transmission plate too so now the dust won't get in.  Gotta rest, I still haven't recovered from the Puyallup yet...zzzzz

 

September 20th, Saturday 2003:  Took the nose off the donor car today, man what a lot of work but I was lucky, most of the bolts were easy to take off, the donor Corsair was pretty rust free.  So next I need to tear all these bits and pieces down to metal, scrub them till they sparkle, and primer them.  Done, hood and all, the donor looks a bit light in the front hehehe.

The inner fenderwells were pretty grungy however not really rusty at all and just need a good cleaning and fresh coat of paint.  I also undercoated every surface that could be exposed to water.

You can see here the cleaned and prepped fender well next to the one that is in line to be cleaned and prepped.  Looks great!  You can also see to the right the old steering column, man this thing was a mess, the entire cylinder was a rusted mess and the wiring had bubbled into a play dough collage of plastic.

 I called the company that is doing the power steering hoses for the car, they just got in the last fitting for one of the hoses and they should have them done this evening and will ship them on Monday, very very cool and I can't wait to see them.  Oh yeah, the company is called Classic Tube and they seem very nice and have a large variety of standard hose and high pressure hose styles they can do for you.

September 27th, Saturday 2003: Today was great and we are still having a wild warm summer, very odd for such a normally rainy place but heck, it is giving me a change to get some work done on the car and see it moving faster.  Okay, today I completed the grill restoration and primering including place the great set of letters and great chrome horse collar back on the section of the front I finished.  Yeah.  It was a heck of a lot of work, undercoating, scraping, sanding, primering, smoothing, drinking sun tea, ya know, the usual car resto stuff.  My daughters birthday is today and she's sad that she can't help me today on the car but I promised she could help tomorrow.  I just need to get the right and left fenders cleaned and ready to put back on the car...but I don't think I have 40 hours in just tomorrow :)  G'night!

September 30th, Monday 2003: The wife went off to another one of her meetings so the kids and I worked on the car together...we finished buttoning up the front end, it is all bolted together (about 48 bolts and 20 nuts later)...and looks great. The new coil springs and shocks are a bit much though and I am concerned that the car will not level itself out but we'll see, I had thought that it might settle down a bit with the front end on but nope, turgid as ever. So next I really need to start on the list of things left to do (aside from body work in the rear)...night! 

You can see here the use of a bungee to hold the front in place while I wrestle a fender into position to bolt up.  You can see the nice and well working brake master power cylinder in place and the cardboard shroud protector.

 With the fender on and the cardboard removed, the front is beginning to take shape...again, there are a LOT of bolts in this, oy!  If you look to the drivers lower side you can see the pulley of the freshly rebuilt power steering pump.  I still haven't put in the heater core yet or the defrost and heater fan.


October 5th, Sunday 2003: My short weekend limited my projects for the car but I was able to stick to my goal of doing something for the car at least once a week or more...I am starting the electrics, yes, tackling them next I think, is the most logical since much of the car depends on it...I went over my inventory of parts and created a database of the six boxes of items I have. In box five I had the power window motors...here we go, too some finding but figured out the positive, ground with reverse and forward...three of the four motors worked great and were in good shape, the last one, a rear motor near where the top would empty water, was a solid piece of rust and of course didn't work. I found at McClendon hardware, a similar match for the motor but I need to confirm that. 

The power steering hoses arrived and they are awesome, shiny and very very rugged, stainless steel and braided hose, looking good. It was easy to send the originals and get duplicates made, just contact http://www.classictube.com, they are the best and the hoses were executed with precision and style. Okay enough of the pitch... 

Yet another cool motor was the wiper motor...yes, not a vacuum driven unit but the rare motor that was only used for a very short time at the beginning of the assembly runs in Wayne Michigan. Apparently the source of the motors wasn't able to fill the Edsel orders back then and the rest of the cars used the standard vacuum wiper motor (poor performing at best)...so, about two hours later, some soldier and a new chunk of wire to boot, the motor was ready to test. Hooked the 2 amp charger to it and kicked off the switch, wha-la, worked perfectly...so I installed it onto the shiny new firewall (not forgetting the thin rubber insulator between the motor rest and the arm)...found the one wiper that came with the car and ran it through some paces, it worked and worked well...can't wait to find another wiper. Sometime this coming week the wiring harness from Painless wiring will show along with their remote solenoid kit so we'll see how it continues....g'night! 

October 6th, Monday 2003: The wiring harness arrived today from Painless, man it looks cool...heavy too.  I opted for the pickup truck version to get the extra length wire to the rear of the car which is about as long as any quad- cab long bed hehehe.  I guess before I start I need to extract the old wires from the car and get a few that didn't come with the kit:

1 each:  Starter hot wire, goes from the solenoid to the starter, yep, need a new one, length for the Citation: 24 inches.
3 each:  Grounding straps, goes from the block on each side to the frame, you may want to also run a third from the block to the body or frame to the body, length for the Citation: 18 inches.
1 each:  Battery hot wire, goes from the battery to the starter solenoid, length for the Citation: 16 inches.
1 each:  Battery ground wire, goes from the battery to the front of the block, typically the head and away from heat, length for the Citation: 20 inches.

October 7th, Tuesday 2003: Today I put together a sub-woofer box for the trunk of the car.  Yeah, it is kind of thinking ahead, but I am looking for projects that don't take much cash right now so this is a good one since I have the wood, glue, and time.  I took some of the finer bits of chrome off the donor car and will apply those to the sub-woofer box tastefully to give it that just "Edsel" look...lettering and a side marker badge, not much but unique enough for me.  I also used a good motor to raise one of the rear windows today...looks like there is some surface pitting...hopefully I can get this removed by a good glass shop...sounds like a Parts Challenge!  Not today though...tired and going to call it a night.  Night!

Nasty dried, stiff, brittle and hazardous original wiring...let alone a very trashed look for the poor neglected dash.
Being in the junk yard for so long was not kind to this old car...that is changing...slowly.

October 9th, Thursday 2003:  Tore out all the wiring in the car today, nothing could be saved as all the wiring had gone so brittle that it was a fire hazard in waiting...very bare now...I installed the harness fuse panel in position near where the old one was, looks great.  I set out running the wires in groups to the front or engine area, dash/ interior, and rear/trunk.  The fuse panel actually screwed right to the firewall where some of the older hard block fuses were screwed, looks solid and like it belongs there.  It's been...a long night, g'night!

 


October 11th, Saturday 2003: Began the daring feat of cutting on the car...yes, I have heard the saying, "It takes a brave person to restore a car and a braver one to cut one up!"  So I did some cutting.  Last year I found a set of very nice 1946 Buick tail lights and an idea hit me, marker lights for my old Chevy...but that car went away long ago and these chrome beauties sat on the shelf waiting.  Back in 46 they were small, as most tail lights were...but as a marker light for a door, they were great.  So I cut a hole in each door on the car, about where one might think to put a speaker, towards the rear and lower part of the door.  Wired the lights into the courtesy lights, and wha la, and they look stock and very cool!

Here you can see the passenger door prepped and marked for the location of the new "marker light".

 

 

 

 

 

 

A jig saw and metal cutting tip later, there is a nice hole in the door, I took some sheet metal and fabbed up a dual light holder so the red and the white lens light up or can be wired to light separately.

In place now for a test fit and wow, it sits nicely and with accurate measuring, it will fit nicely onto the door panel as well and look stock.

Here is a shot of the drivers door, done and wired, very cool I think.


October 12th, Sunday 2003: Hooked up most of the front wiring today, starter solenoid, main fusible link and single wire high output alternator with internal regulator.  This'll make for a very neat engine compartment and safe too.  I also added the piece of gold, Cadillac horns that I kept from the old caddy that I sold last year to a collector.  It is always my intention to maintain the "link" of my cars so I install at least one part from the previous car or cars on the latest project, hence the horns and marker lights for the doors.  Tested my first circuit today, the door lights.  In the past I was testing from the light side, thus seeing the lights but not seeing if the switches worked.  They did, and on the first try.  I have a ten amp charged hooked to where the battery would sit, so testing the electrics is a breeze now.

October 13th, Monday 2003:  Bank holiday so I stayed home and worked a bit more...tore apart the steering column and found out that it was completely destroyed inside to the point that all the wires had melded into a rainbow of puffy melted plastic.  I can see why the car had been converted to a "floor" shifter.  The donor car came to the rescue again, it's guts and cogs were in great shape.  I was able to solder a few loose connections and install the column into the car...I tested the switch and it works fine for blinkers so I will at least not have to replace that...I found several locations on the net for wiring schematics to re-hook the shift motor up, I am very concerned that I will not do this right but I have a lot of supporting documentation and a good ohm meter...I am missing a coil and need to finish out the spark plug wires too...man my list seems to be getting larger.  I hope tomorrow to pickup the new battery cables and the starter cable too.  Things are going along well...I think.

October 18th, Saturday 2003: Got up early today and completed all of the wiring up front...turned out nice and clean as well. The wiring harness has all the relays built into it so the firewall and fenders are clean of archaic electronics. Headlights, high beams as well as an extra set of dim glow red parking lights just behind the grill are working great thanks to the "Painless" wiring harness I picked up. I bought the Pickup truck version because of the extra taillight wire length and that was a great life saver. Today we also went out and did a little pumpkin hunting.

(left) Here we are picking out the seasons squash and (right) getting some fresh crab with Uncle Bob at his new beach house.


October 19th, Sunday 2003: Completed running the wires into the cabin of the car from the front end so I could test all the lights, yes everything works great...strangely enough, the headlights from the car, which went into the junk yard in 1973 with the car, still work fine but I will probably replace those soon rather than wait for an inopportune burn out. In order to get the dash wired up I need to basically clean it otherwise I will have to take it apart again to paint it. That's enough for tonight...oy.

October 20th, Monday 2003: Well I did it, scrapped the entire dash down to bare metal, primered and lacquered it up nicely in a ghost blue and white combo, turned out good. I figured that I had better do it right so I don't have to take it apart later...so I was able to start hooking up wiring today but can't test too much out yet. I need to clean, paint, and check the dash, original switches, and clean all the original chrome. Luckily I have a donor car that was a hard top so there is a chance that some of the chrome won't be pitted.

Here you can see some of the new wires for the ignition coming out of the dash and a nice shot of the hole for a 6x9 speaker....and a sad location for a new radio.  "Custom Auto Sound" will solve that issue.



October 22nd, Wednesday 2003: Cleaned up the dash chrome today and removed all the masking tape from the painting job, looks very nice. I added the dash chrome, the glove box and light for it and things are shaping up well. I ran the wires for the headlights to the switch (turns out the original switches all test out very well so I will use them and have spares for the ready). First try went well...the lights work, the dimmer works too and the small red dot on the dash lights letting me know the high beams are on. So far, this painless harness has been 100% solid on! I installed and hooked up the environmental computer (the enviro disk run on a platter that opens and closes the vents and heater unit as dialed on the dash)...it worked great as it should have, spent about 300 bucks getting the special motor rebuilt down in California last year for my old Pacer (and kept it when she left). The dash is done, I just need to run the wires for the antenna switch to wherever the antenna will go, oh yeah, and I need to install a radio sometime, maybe in December. Next I need to finish out the tail end of the car and setup the turn signals...g'night!

Here is the chrome dash insert that houses the instruments, note the farthest cup to the left has the remains of a broken compass in it, there is no speed sensor adjustor on this dash either, it would be in place of the small "E" disc in the center lower of the panel.  69K miles show on this odometer.  I toyed with the idea of resetting it, to show it's new life but nope, this is a continuation life for the car.

You can see on the left, one of the eyelids for the front of the car...these were heck to take apart and clean, never had to use vice grips on a screw before.

 Here you can see the "Global Blue" color I chose for the interior and dash.  It is nearly ready for the coat of white for the lower dash.  I used a special paint that is used on appliances to ensure chipping from bumps will be fairly harmless and shrugged off
.

(Below):  You can see the newly cleaned, refinished and painted dash instrument cowling.  The chrome inserts that house the indicators for "generator" "engine cold" etc,. are in place, nicely cleaned and I repaired the lettering on the back, they look great when lit.

October 25th, Saturday 2003: Today went well, I am very glad I ordered the generic pickup truck 18 circuit Painless kit, the length of wire was needed for the truck. Got all the wiring done, converted the rather poor single blinker ends into duals so the entire blinker and park lenses blink for turning, much brighter and easier to see. The trunk light needs a mercury switch but I will add that later so I will have a dim trunk until then. One feed from the dash light switch will light the parking lights in both the "Park" and "Drive" settings, found that out when the rear lights didn't work on "Drive" so I fixed that. Now I just need to add in the ignition switch and radio. The painless harness comes with lines for the power door motors and locks, however since the Edsel doesn't have the locks, I can use the extra two circuits for locks to work the rear windows so I have all four windows working great now. The glass is awful but they move up and down well enough. I was also able to use the Air Conditioning feed to the engine compartment as the power feed for the electric windshield wipers. The only wire I had to run myself was the wire for the blower motor low and high settings and that was easy. 

October 27th, Monday 2003: Installed a single pioneer four way speaker into the dash, I will run a small set of tweeters on the second radio channel and a pair of speakers in the rear seat area. The truck will hold a speaker case for the subs and the CD changer...all those will come in a month or two though...for now, g'night.

Here you can see here the glove box is in with it's light in place, the dash cowling is on and the steering column is in place with it's new coat of "Global Blue".  You can also see the new dimmer switch plate on the floor as well as the lack of courtesy light covers for the lights up front, solid gold and hard to find I have heard.  I have one, I will see about fabbing up a mold and make a few.



October 29th, Wednesday 2003:  Hooked up all the blinkers and brake leads, took some work but figured out what worked best for the old gal, to the right is the schematic for this in case you need it.  The blinkers work great, all the lights work now and work well.  All the dash lights work including the keyhole light and glove box.  I also ran the wires for the ignition switch so I could officially test the accessories.  Everything is coming together...next I will have to work on the door motor wiring and the seats.  Later!

The picture to the right shows the Painless wiring colors and numbers in relation to the Edsel colors.  Just wire the painless wires into the holes shown on the steering column blinker cube and your good to go!

It really is nice to see some of this coming together after so long. I also tested out a six position, joy-stick like switch that was mounted on the drivers side of the car near the Courtesy light...I don't know what it was used for yet, but it tests out fine and after a good cleaning and polish it's back in place too. Maybe it was for the top...not sure. Where's my camera, yeah I have been lax about getting pictures on the site...be patient :) G'night!

Here the instrument cluster is now in place and looking smart.

November 11th, Tuesday 2003: Installed the steering wheel, buttons, horn ring and all the chrome bits, man it looks great in the Global Blue paint scheme. I made a list today of the things I need to complete before I can take the car out of the garage...man, I came up with about 83 items...found a compass for sale on http://www.edsel.com announced long ago but still available, neat, that means the dash will be 100% complete. Took a little work but I took a Dolphin Instrument, a clock, and tore apart the original clock that was a ball of rust and swapped them using the original clock casing thus preserving the fit, looks totally original (I even used the original hands from the old Edsel clock, they fit the new electronic clock with just a little bit of tweaking, a quick brush up with period colors and bam, looks nice. I really need to get those pictures developed...g'night!

November 16th, Sunday 2003: Lots of little projects I need to complete today or soon so I will see what I can get done with as little exposure to the very cold garage...tucked most of the wiring under the dash using the nice ties that came with the Painless Wiring Harness, which by the way, was the best move I made on the car in dealing with wiring. The generic harness for a truck worked great, plenty of length to reach the rear lights and accessories. Wiring up the light for low oil pressure, cold engine and hot engine lights too. Also hooked the horn officially to the column and it works great. Acquired a water pump pulley to finally get somewhere on starting the old gal...I still need to pick up a crank pulley that will allow me to run the power steering correctly. What a day though...getting easier to figure out what to do next, the list is getting smaller and smaller. Oh yeah, a compass is coming from Oregon, that will complete the dash (aside from a new radio which will be sometime in January). To recap: All the lights work, in all modes, brakes on high beams with blinkers (a bit much for the 10amp charger but it works), wipers, blower, emergency flasher (need a toggle though and a place to put it), cigar lighter, antenna switch for up and down, all the senders for idiot lights...i still have the mystery of the joystick like switch on the side dash, don't know what that operates (but it tests out fine). Goals: Next major things before crank over are: Radiator restored and a new set of lower and upper hoses, driveline restored, bracket for alternator lower side (sits in generator location though), pulley for the crank, set of electric fans for the radiator, and battery. G'night!
