Charnjibber (Glorantha Creature used in the Castle adventure here)

Stats Average Attributes
STR as prey varies Move as prey
CON 3d20 31-32 Hit Point Avg varies
SIZ 2D6+12 varies Treasure Factor varies
INT 9 9
POW 2d6+12 19
DEX as prey varies
CHA as prey varies
Weapons SR Attack Damage Parry Points
Tentacle 3 50% + varies equal to damage bonus or grapple 35% 25

Attacks: as last eaten prey including tentacles
Skills: as prey but cannot use the knowledge skills or communication skills
Magic: Typically will have nine points of spells, cannot use the spells of the prey
Armor: as per prey.  However, a Charnjibber cannot be incapacitated or knocked unconscious and cannot even be killed by ordinary means.  If it is reduced to "0" hit points, its "corpse" lies inert for a few days while it devolves into the form of a previous, smaller prey.    For instance, a killed Charnjibber lion might "backslide" into a human or dog.  If the creature is dismembered, the separate parts devolve individually (a man's right hand might turn into a shrew or a large spider).  burning the corpse (unless it has the chaos feature of "highly flammable") or dissolving it in acid only delays the reanimation by several weeks.
Combat Tactics: The Charnjibber attacks with all its tentacles on a single strike rank, followed three strike ranks later by its normal attacks or spells.  Normally, the Charnjibber uses its tentacles to keep a prey creature from running away or to hold it still so its natural weapons can kill it.  It can flail with the tentacles to cause damage, doing damage equal to the creature damage bonus.  The tentacles do 1d3 damage if the Charnjibber has no damage bonus to speak of and will only do one point of damage if the Charnjibber has a negative damage bonus.
Chaos Features: Has all the features of the last eaten victim plus roll 1d100.  If the result is less than the Charjibber's POW x 3 then it has a chaos feature, roll again, and continue to roll again and add additional features as long as the results are under the POW x 3 result.

Description: The Charnjibber is a bizarre and highly chaotic creature whose natural shape is unknown.  It is only found in the dry season.  A Charnjibber always takes the shape of whatever creature it last swallowed, plus the addition of 1d6 tentacles.  At the start of the dry season, Charnjibbers are rather small, taking the form of rats or lizards.  As the season progresses the creatures gradually increase in size, as they manage to pull down and eat bigger and bigger prey.  Near the end of the season charnjibbers are found in the form of tentacled sabertooth tigers, titanotheres or even dinosaurs.  Charnjibbers can only devour beings with physical bodies.  A Charnjibber always takes the STR, SIZ, DEX, and CHA (APP) of its current form, but retains its own CON, INT, and POW.  It gains all the special powers and natural skills of its chosen form therefore if a Charnjibber eats a vampire, it gains the ability to grain magic points, hypnotize at a glance, and change shape.  It would also gain a vampires special vulnerabilities.  If a Charnjibber eats a being lacking CON, INT, or POW, refer to the "Lacking and Gaining Characteristics" rules section of the RuneQuest book.  Since a Charnjibber has a fixed INT score, it cannot use the spells of the prey it envelopes and assumes.  A Charnjibber likewise cannot use the knowledge skills, or communications skills of its prey.  Special hit location rules apply to the Charnjibber.  When the prey is consumed and assumed, roll 1d20 to determine the location where the tentacle appears, there are 1d6 tentacles to determine this way.  If a given number is rolled more than once, reroll.  If the prey creature has a separate missile hit location table, use common sense to extrapolate the hit locations.  A hit location may be entirely turned into tentacles in this manner.  Example: the gamemaster wishes to generate a charjobber that has eaten a lion.  He rolls 1d6 for the number of tentacles and rolls a "5", so 1d20 ius rolled five times and compared to the creatures hit location table.  The rolls are 4, 8, 9, 11, and 20.  The rolls of 8 and 9 conflict so the gamemaster shooses to reroll the 9 and gets an 11.  This also conflicts with the previous result so he rolls yet again for a 13.  The final hit location table looks like this:

 Location Melee Missile
Right Leg 01-02 01-02
Left Leg 03 03
Tentacle #1 04-05 04-05
Hindquarters 06-07 06-09
Tentacle #2 08-09 10-11
Forequarters 10 12-14
Tentacle #3 11-12 15
Tentacle #4 13-14 16-17
Left leg 15-16 18
Head 17-19 19
Tentacle #5 20 20

Note that the right foreleg has been entirely replaced by tentacles.  The missile hit location table is also provided.  Here is how it was figured: tentacle one should still overlap the left hind leg and hindquarters location, so it was unchanged. Tentacle two covered part of the forequarters, so it was moved to location 10-11.  Tentacle three covered part of the right foreleg, so it became location 15, and tentacle four covered the other part of the right foreleg, plus part of the left foreleg , so it became location 16-17.  Tentacle five covered part of the head, so it became location 20.  This system for determining a Charnjibbers hit locations may seem cumbersome.  The gamemaster is free to simple choose hit locations as he sees fit or simply select a single location where the tentacles will be added.

Skills: Spot Hidden 45%, Listen 45%, Armor Making 50%, Tracking 50%
Magic: Bladesharp(2), Heal(2), Disruption, Fanatacism(2), and Mobility.  Typically they will have 1d3 more spells if their INT allows.  Cyclops will often have one or two points of one shot Rune Magic.
Armor: Whatever they want to wear, they have the ability to tan leather to an incredible hardness yet with supple flexibility to allow good motions without a lot of squeaking.  Typically they will be wearing three or four point leather with studding.
Combat Tactics: A Cyclops can use his Great Scimitar in one hand and shield to bash with in the other.  They will often fanaticize opponents to thoroughly enjoy the battle for in their veins runs tremendous war lust.  They are always willing to negotiate, even in a fight, to a peaceful end as long as the battle is no longer fun or entertaining.

Description: Amongst the race of Ogres, an offshoot known as the Cyclops appeared just before Godtime.  The race is thought to have been a distant tribe of law worshipping Ogres that were cursed by their chaos brothers for their short sightedness in switching sides to that of law.  The curse brought them then loss of one eye. All newborn Cyclops appears with just one eye and have since the curse was placed upon them dozens of centuries ago.  They look similar to Ogres but have a bulkier and taller frame, sharp teeth like their cousins, and are unable to grow hair on top of their heads but they can grow hair down the back of their head and down their back like a lions mane.  Ogres tend to follow a deity of Law that accepts them even though they still have the taint of chaos, the same taint that follows their Ogre cousins.  Ogres make their own weapons and take great pride in their smithing ability.  It can take months for a Cyclops to perfect his Scimitar and anyone but another Cyclops can wield the weapon in one hand without striking the ground or a nearby person (friend and foe).

Combat Tactics: Dung Elementals tend to throw a few steaming spears then rush in biting, punching, and breathing their nasty breath.

Description: Dung Elementals are strange concoctions from the mind of a very twisted sorcerer.  They are akin to an elemental of earth origin but also take strength from the myriad of bacteria that makeup their intellect, the community can host a spirit and with some extra magical incentive on the sorcerers part, it becomes an elemental.  A dung elemental is bound by the normal rules of elementals however only a sorcerer has access to the magical spells that allow the final animation of such a creature.  There are four strengths of dung elementals, the first stage is known as the Esbeedee (also called the Silent But Deadly: SBD) and is small, soft skinned, smells the worst, and very dark brown to black in color.  The second and more powerful stage is known as the Barker.  The Barker is larger, being close to that of a dwarf or larger in size, its exterior is a hard carapace and ranges from dark brown to light green.  The third stage is known as the Grand Lincoln, stands as tall as an ogre, is as hard as stone and ranges in color from dark green-brown to light beige.  The last stage is known as the Grand Ghost because of its stark white or off white-greenish color.  The Grand Ghost is powerful indeed and should be avoided for its sheer size and brute force are hard to combat.  The shell of the Grand Ghost is as hard as iron, its breath is the deadliest and it has been known to posses magical powers.  The breath of a Dung Elemental can cause the person in the area smelling it to be overcome with flatulence and vomiting unless the make a CON resist versus the attack strength of the Dung Elemental.  The effects last for 1d20 rounds.  Below is a listing of the type of elemental and any skills or stat bonuses it gains due to rank:

Dung Rank Special skills or bonuses
Esbeedee Breath range is POW x 5 in meters and will also cause the victim succumbing to the effect to drop whatever is in their hands.  Can change form to a semi-liquid and slip through a crack as narrow as a human hair.
Barker Breath range is POW x 4 in meters.  The STR and SIZ score of this battling turd is +1d6 each and the AP is + 2.
Grand Lincoln Breath range is POW x 3 in meters.  The STR and SIZ score of this battling turd is +2d6 each and the AP is + 4, and INT is +1d6.
Grand Ghost Breath range is POW x 2 in meters.  The STR and SIZ score of this battling turd is +3d6 each and the AP is + 6, INT is +2d6.  There is a POW x 2 chance that the elemental will also have the ability to cast 1d8 points worth of spells determined randomly.

Bash 6 45% 1d6

Skills: Hide 65%, Listening 45%, Move Quietly 35%, Tracking 80% (Tracker only)
Magic: This creature is immune to all spells that effect the mind however, ignite will cause damage similar to Disruption but doing 1d6 damage.
Armor: The loose nature of the creature gives it an AP from four to six however, and depending on the debris that the creature is made of, that score could be higher or lower. Typical example: Sand AP2, Leaves AP1, Oak Branches AP3, Metal Coins AP4, Loose stone or gravel AP4, Metal Daggers or Small Weapons AP5, Large Metal Weapons AP6, Large Stone or Rock AP7.  
Combat Tactics: This being seeks to shred its victims to bits and draw the blood into its spirit thus it will Whirlwind when it thinks it can do without being attacked.  When Whirlwind is used, the AP of the creature drops to one point as it loosens itself to cover the target and drink blood.  It takes one combat round to change into a Whirlwind, this gives time for the parts to release the humanoid form and begin spinning in a dangerous vortex of blazing fast wind.  While in the Whirlwind form, the creature can still use its Bash attack but due to the lack of AP while in such a form, it will only do so if pressed.  Pummeling damage is produced from each buffet of the Whirlwind attack, this damage is caused by blood being extracted from the creature from all of its pores (yes, very painful).

Description: The Debris Spirit appears as a large pile of similarly matched junk such as a heap of rotting rags, a pile of broken swords, or even a pile of corpses.  It will have a humanoid form with two arms and two legs and what might be considered a head and torso.  Two small pinpoints of light will indicate the creature has eyes of some sort, these allow it to see in the dark as well as a person can see in daylight.  A sorcerer typically must create this type of guardian and enchant the mess to bow to his will.  This dangerous spirit will lay in wait for a trespasser and spring to action in 1d3 combat rounds.  During its formation it will have an AP of one, once formed it will be higher and begin defending its area.  It will not leave an area by more than fifty feet unless created to do so such as a Debris Tracker.  A Debris Tracker is a creature much like a Debris Spirit however it is bound together with magic that allows it to follow one person, tracking them down utterly to exact vengeance or even just to deliver a message.  Once this deed is accomplished, the Debris Tracker will collapse in a heap, it's spirit released and magic gone.

Spirit-Mass Creature (Spirit-Mass Golem)

Stats Average Attributes
STR 4d6+21 35-36 Move 5
CON 1d100 50 Hit Point Avg 75
SIZ 2d100 100 Treasure Factor varies
INT 10 10
POW Special varies
DEX 1d6+2 4-5
Weapons SR Attack Damage Parry
Punch 4 50% + varies 1d8+4d6 15% + varies
Kick 4 50% + varies 1d8+4d6
Frost Breath (Deaths Breath) 0 85% CON vs. CON or target frozen solid (Up to 1/2 the size of the Beast)

Attacks: Prefers punch and kick, will breath about once every six rounds as applicable
Skills: Spot Hidden: 15%
Magic: Spells and magic do not effect this being but Discorporation can, see below.
Armor: Typically the substance of the mass spirit will be of at least five points and could be as high as ten points worth of protection
Combat Tactics: The Mass-Golem will punch and kick every other round and will once every six rounds or so, breath in an attempt to freeze the foe then continue on to the next.  The breath of the dead is very powerful and can only be used three times per day, if a fourth time is attempted, the creature will fall apart after breathing and cannot reform for a full week.

Description: A Spirit-Mass golem is created out of the worst situations.  Typically when a mass slaughter occurs the spirits move onward to the spirit plane, but sometimes this is not so and especially in the case of a pitched and highly desperate battle.  In such a case, the spirits of the defeated will each enter an object in the room and usually of the same type if possible.  Forming itself into a humanoid shape when any part of it is disturbed, the Golem will battle the intruder in an attempt to kill it or will battle it until it leaves the general area of the original massacre.  This creature is related closely to the Debris Spirit however this is a supernaturally occurring beast that is made up of dozens or more spirits thus giving the creature a high enough POW rating that it is unnecessary to show here.  Each spirit that contributes to the creature will lend two points of SIZ and one point of CON to the final construction, each spirit will typically inhabit a weapon, piece of armor or similar object that is plentiful in the area of the slaughter.  When a creature is generated, roll 1d100 to determine the number of spirits that make up the construct (there have been larger subjects seen).  The material that a creature is made of will effect the AP that it has, soft items such as leathers, wood, or papers will have three to five points, hard woods and soft metals will have five to eight points, hard metals and alloys will have five to ten or higher.  As damage is taken, pieces will fly off thus reducing the SIZ and CON of the creature and eventually reducing it back to rubble.  For those that feel they need to know the POW rating of the creature, feel free to estimate each spirit as a POW of 13 (Go figure) so an average creature with a 100 SIZ, will have 650 POW!!!  It is not possible to "bind" such a creature by any known current means, but in the past sorcerers were able to perform this feat and create Super-POW magical items and constructed machinations.  A discorporation spell is the only effective spell against this creature and will cause a random location to disperse and the spirits released and if the location is the chest, then the creature falls completely apart.

An example creature might be made up of spheres of metal about the size of an American Bowling Ball which is what is used in an adventure here on this site.  There were two creatures found in a room area containing hundreds of the metal spheres...these creatures were made up of fifty balls.  When damage is taken, balls are knocked free with a 5% chance that one will strike a character fighting it or within thirty feet (there are seventeen balls per location on each of these beasts).  At least neither of these beasts are made of the dangerous and volatile brass shot (found in an adventure here)....but in case you really need to know, any hit that scored a "Special Success" or "Crush" on the creature would cause at least one ball to explode with a further 10% chance that 1d6 more would explode however this would be the limit of the explosions of any one hit.  If the creature hit a person and scored a "Special Success" or "Crush" then the creature successfully controlled the explosion of a ball to effect just the one creature doing 1-6d6 to it (determined randomly).

A wraith seeks to heal and cure itself by extracting the life force from its victims. When a wraith touches a victim it will drain points from that character, points from a random stat and gives these points to itself, driving up the large negative score of their Charisma. Once the CHA score of the body is restored to what it was in life, the wraith is considered reborn and lives again (loosing any benefits of being a wraith). Wraiths will detect as undead until they are fully reborn, very clever wraiths will blend right into normal society yet keep a physical distance when possible for they still carry the stench of the grave with them until reborn.
