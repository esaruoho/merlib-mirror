 Toasty goodness. 

 When I went down to Mom's a couple weekends ago, I both got to open Christmas
 presents and bring back some for Mike, too.

 One of the things Mom got for us both was a toaster. "There's one that you
 might wonder why I got it for you," she said. But I knew as soon as Mike
 and I opened it... Mom got the toaster for herself, here at our place.

 My mother is a toast addict. I understand why, of course. If I weren't diabetic,
 I'd probably be a toast addict, too. I love the stuff. In my family, growing
 up, there was little more comforting than a nice, perfectly prepared piece
 of buttery golden toast. And hot buttery English muffins... oh dearie, I'm
 about to faint.

 (Side note: Mom eats her toast burnt. Ick. She burns it on purpose! Weird.)

 One of our traditional comfort foods was the tuna muffin, and I remember
 many a tuna muffin and chicken noodle soup being the highlight of a rainy
 day when I was little. (Tuna muffins are English muffins topped with
 tuna and cheese, by the way, for those of you not in the know.)

 I even broke up with someone over tuna muffins, once. Well, there was more
 to it than that, of course. But when, during the course of an arguement where
 he was comparing me to his mother (bad idea, that) he thundered, "And where
 anywhere in the world is a tuna muffin part of dinner?" ... well, that was
 the last straw, and he moved out not long after. Never mock a woman's comfort
 food.

 Whever Mom has come up to stay with us, she's had two complaints:

 no coffee maker, and

 no toaster.

 Neither Mike nor I drink coffee on a regular basis, so there was no need
 for a maker; however, I found the slimmest, most economical maker that took
 up the least space on the countertop AND came in white to match the rest
 of the appliances, and bought it. Now there's a coffee maker for Mom.

 Since Mike has never shown interest in a regular diet of toast, I hadn't
 planned on getting a toaster. I remember Mom asking, about a year back, "If
 I got you a toaster oven, would you use it?" And I answered with a resounding,
 "No!" We don't have room for a toaster oven, for heaven's sake.

 So when we opened up the Christmas present and found a slim, white toaster,
 I just laughed. Last week I squeezed it onto the counter between the coffee
 maker and the pasta machine.

 And tonight, although I really didn't feel like eating the crabcakes and
 pasta I'd gotten us for dinner, I did feel... well, like having a bite of
 toast. The toaster biscuit with butter and cloudberry jam hit the comfort
 spot which had felt a little neglected.

 Thanks, Mom!
