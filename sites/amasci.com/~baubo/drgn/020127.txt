 Writing stuff. 

 Well, there's about an inch of snow out there now. Wet snow, good for making
 snowmen and throwing at loved ones. :) We don't have the kind of yard where
 we can go out and play, but it does look very attractive on our decks and
 little bridges.

 Today I plan to work on the book. Write, write, write, edit, edit.

 I've been trying to get past the feeling that my success with NaNoWriMo was
 a fluke. I have a lot of work done on the novel. I just need to get past
 the stuff I've done so far, delete the uneccessary and nasty bits, and keep
 going.

 Tonight I finally made a step that helps get me off in the right direction:
 a title, at last! It's probably a draft title, of course, but all the same
 it's something I can use instead of referring to it as "my NaNoWriMo thing"
 or the "Oh My God What Am I Going To Write About Novel" (the former draft-draft
 title).

 Beyond the Call 

 For those of you who've read it, I pretty sure you'll get it.

 I had it online until just a few days ago, when I ran out of room on my web
 account and had to start deleting things. So it'll be a while before it's
 available again, sorry.

 Anyhow, I'm pleased.

 I have a history with semicolons. 

 I don't like them much, you see. As an editor, when I see them in someone's
 writing, I tend to look for an alternative which wouldn't use a semicolon.
 They break up the flow, call attention to the act of writing, when the reader
 shouldn't be aware of anything other than the literary world they've been
 transported to. I see a semicolon and - bam! - I'm slammed back into my bones
 and out of the writer's world. It's especially bad in dialogue. Nobody speaks
 with a semicolon. It's a writing thing.

 Folks in the monthly writing group have heard me say it many, many times.
 I'm sure they're tired of hearing it by now. "When you're reading," Julie
 says, "you shouldn't be aware that you're reading. Things like semicolons
 distract the reader and remind them that they're looking at printed words
 on a page." And my friends smile indulgently and nod... which is all right,
 really. Everybody has their own peeves, and mine being semicolons is easily
 indulged.

 (The lesson Julie badly needs to learn: how not to write in passive voice.
 I know.)

 So last night I continued my read of Stephen King's
 On
 Writing and I came across:

 ...Language does not always have to wear a tie and lace-up shoes. The
 object of fiction isn't grammatical correctness but to make the reader welcome
 and then tell a story... to make him/her forget, whenever possible, that
 he/she is reading a story at all. 

 I'm really beginning to like this King guy more and more.
