Steel

Here are some of the steels available to knifemakers,

1095 Simple tool steels with 7/10 of 1 percent of carbon or .95 Carbon simular to O-1 but at the best price available. Makes good springs, knives, tools etc.

154-CM A high-carbon, high-alloy, space-age, stainless steel. Carbon 1.05%, Manganese 0.5%, Chromium 14.0%, Molybdenum 0.4 - 0.55%.

416 A mild stainless, the knife makers choice for bolsters and guards; not suitable for blades.

420 A stainless spring steel much used in inexpensive production knives from Taiwan. Carbon content from 0.15 to 0.6% the balance is 1.0% Manganese and 12-14% Chromium.

440C The most popular high-carbon stainless used by custom knifemakers for many years. 0.95 - 1.20% Carbon, 0.40% Manganese, 17.0% Chromium, 0.50% Vandium, 0.50% Molybdenum.

A-2 An excellent air hardening tool steel used by handmade knife makers and now used by speciality makers like BlackJack as well. Contains about 1% Carbon, 1% Molybdenum, and 5% Chromium.

D-2 An outstanding knife steel, a high-carbon, high chrome tool steel which is often used for the steel cutting dies in every tool and die shop, exspensive. With 1.5% Carbon, 1% Molybdenum, 12% Chromium and 1% Vanadium,

Damascus Steel A shop forged mixture of steel that is sometimes a softer steel mixed with a harder steel, done for strength and/or beauty.

O-1 O-1 is a simple and basic tool steel that can be hardened to well over 60 Rc. With .9% Carbon, 1% Manganese, 5% Chromium and .5% Tungsten. It is a great general purpose tool steel and is very forgiving to the inexperienced knifemaker. This oil-hardening tool steel simular to 1095.

I use 1095 High Carbon Steel, I suppose that at a later date I will use another type of steel but I will explain why I chose 1095 high carbon as the steel of my blades. When the decision for me to make knives was made I decided on a set of ideals to follow. The first to provide a good design and quality product. Second was for my prices to be reasonable. Third, was to produce my product in my own shop and not be reliant on sending my blades out to profiled or hardened. After visiting allot of gun and knife shows and gathering all my information, I was ready to proceed. Listening to some old timers who prefer high carbon steel to stainless and after comparing price and the hardening processes, my decision was made.

 

Grinds

There are various grinds being used by knife makers today. They are...

Convex is best for cutting things that are heavy and hard, where a large amount of force is used, such as bone or wood, because the cutting edge is backed by heavy steel which is unlikely to break. Convex grinds are harder to sharpen and usually have to be re-shaped after heavy use. Randall, Black Jack knives are some of the more famous knife makers who use the convex grind.

Hollow is suited to a keen cutting edge and when a deep cut is desired, The term"hollow" is from the sides of the grind that are hollowed out when ground along the edge. It cuts more easily but can be weaker than other grinds. I use Hollow Grinding because it gives a superior cutting edge and is easier to re sharpen. I also use a dual hardening technique that strengthens the blade and will be discussed in a later paragraph.

Straight grind or simple wedge is halfway between hollow and convex grinds and is very popular with allot of knife makers. I use a chisel grind which is, hollow ground on one side and straight on the other.The chisel grind produces a stronger blade with the fine cutting effect of a hollow ground blade. I only use this on thinner steel (1/8"), similar to straight but with a hollow grind, it has a very acute cutting angle..

Tanto Tips

Tanto blades compared to standard designs.

Tanto blades have always been known as being able to pierce armor. This comes from the grinding of the tip, I refer to the tip grind as a chisel grind. Ground on a chisel bevel it gives it a full thickness all the way to the tip for piercing or prying (it's never is a good idea to pry with or throw a knife but, if you have an emergency situation it's best to have one designed to take the pressure). The standard tip has a very good cutting action but is weak from where the taper to the point starts. The tanto tip is stronger, the taper starts closer to the tip and gives it a fuller thickness to the point. My tanto blades after hardening and tempering have been tested on car doors and steel drums, the tanto's always come through without breaking- they may need to be resharpened but they are built strong!

A quick note on guards if you stab anything with reistance- my Tactical Tanto has a 1/4 inch thick oval welded guard and is designed so your hand doesn't slide up on the razor sharp blade. But with my guardless knives like my Utility Tanto you may take a chance of running your hand up onto the blade in a stabbing action - Please Be Careful!

 

Dual Hardening Process

 

I receive 1095 high carbon from the foundry and it arrives in 72" lengths. After cutting out the profile and grinding the blade the blade is stamped with my logo and hardened in a gas forge...

When the heated blade reaches non-magnetic the steel structure has changed and it is ready to be quenched in oil. I will next explain my duel hardening process that gives Boyer Blades it's razor cutting edge with a strong spring back.

 

When I quench the knife in the hardening oil I leave the back of the knife out of the quench. Then putting the knife in an oven at 400 degrees for 2 hours to temper the hardened edge and leaving the back a spring hard. The edge is about 58-59 Rockwell and this the back is a 54-55 Rockwell hardness. Since I don't have a Rockwell tester at my shop ( I have tested my process with a Rockwell tester at another location) I use a file to test the edge for hardness prior to tempering, a file will cut into softer steel but at full edge harness it will not cut into the steel of properly hardened blade.

When I displayed my knives at the Montana Knife makers show in 1996, I had time to talk to Wayne Goddard. He helped me to develop my hardening technique, Wayne also uses a torch method of tempering a fully hardened blade, playing the torch over the back of the blade while immersing the edge in water which softens the back leaving the edge at a cutting hardness. I have found this technique to a bit harder and I still prefer the first edge quench process.

 

Knife Handles

As per my goal to produce a quality knife I had next to decide on knife handles. Since viewing a video by Bob Loveless as he explained that he prefers Micarta for durability and strength I, chose Micarta as my main handle selection. Micarta will not shrink and will hold up in foul weather conditions. Bob Loveless uses wood and Stag he still prefers Micarta for wearability.

I use black Micarta and para-cord wrapped handles for my survival knives. Para-cord is comfortable and can easily be replaced by the user in the field. Nylon para-cord, when wet gives a very good grip and is also usable in an emergency for shelters. I also have a new hidden tang knife that uses a resin impregnated laminated wood, it is very durable and has a black ebony finish to it.

I also have just found a source for 1/4" slabs of Cocobolo & Bocote handle material. Cocobolo and other hardwoods make a knife's "look" a little less menacing and more presentable in an urban environment.

Sheaths

*Note: I have as of November 200 started using Conealex. for my sheaths and Tek-lock multi clips.

There are allot of sheath materials used today, leather, injected molded plastic, kydex. I wanted to use leather but, with the high price of leather hides growing each day and the need for an exspensive leather sewing machine I decided against it. Plastic injection molded sheaths are only good for a production knife company. This left Kydex. Kydex has the ability to be molded by hand to custom each sheath and blade using the basic of tools. I also use an internal material that doesn't scratch the blade like so many other kydex sheaths and is quiet on the draw for military applications. Some of my sheaths have a dual left or right handed, front or back horizontal carry sheath system that has a belt loop & boot clip. I also have a over the shoulder harness system similar to a Blackie Collins design as a added extra feature.

Handles

I offer two types off handles for my new blades, removable handles and foundry cast aluminum handles. Removable handles allow the blade to be re-furbished and powder coated as the knife needs to bake in a 400 degree oven for 20 mins. Also new for 2000 I now offer foundry cast aluminum handles. This produces a solid metal knife that is very stong. I have further information on my knife making page.
