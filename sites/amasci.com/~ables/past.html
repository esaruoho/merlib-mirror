I was born in Austin, Texas in 1960. I have lived in Fort Worth and Beeville, Texas, in addition to Austin. My family is from all over the state, but most of them are concentrated in and around Austin and the piney woods of East Texas. That's me in about 1973, before I became folically challenged.

I graduated from The University of Texas at Austin in 1982 with a Bachelor of Arts in Computer Science.

Austin is a beautiful place. Even though I had lived my entire life in or near there, 1995 seemed like the year to pull up stakes and try out someplace new. I had just finished my first book and I was looking around to see what to do with the rest of my life and a friend at IBM in Basingstoke (south of London) thought I'd be a good candidate for a Web developer with their group. His boss was in Austin on business soon thereafter and we talked and decided to go for it. I sold my house, packed my belongings, and was admiring my empty living room after the movers left when I got a phone call. The British Government had turned down my work permit! We knew it was a risk, but it seemed to be a small one. Turns out, since I was being paid graduate-school wages (because that's what was in the budget and I was willing to take it) they decided I must not be anything special and therefore they weren't going to let me take a job away from a local. Sigh. The really frustrating thing is that there was a hiring freeze right after that and the local they were saving the job for never got it anyway because they weren't able to fill it. Argh!

So with no place to live and having already paid the movers half of what it was going to cost (they were moving things into storage), I figured I might as well think about going somewhere else. I could move across the country as easily as I could move across the street. Having always wanted to move to Colorado, I came up here to look for a job. Another friend worked at HP in Fort Collins and suggested I come over for an interview. Networking is truly an amazing thing, isn't it?

So I've been living in Northern Colorado since 1995 (I had only wanted to for ten years). I feel really at home here, it was surprising. But I've always loved cold(er) weather (than Texas, which isn't hard to find!). I always wondered what ironic twist put me in that climate and wondered if some kid in North Dakota was complaining about how cold it was every season. I don't know if Colorado is the final destination of my life, but it's home for now, and it suits me just fine.
