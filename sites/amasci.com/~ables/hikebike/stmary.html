St. Mary's Glacier

St. Mary's Glacier is off of I-70 as you head west out of Denver. It is supposedly a remnant of an actual glacier (it looks more like a snowpatch that just never melts, but the area that it cut out certainly looks like it was originally from a glacier).

A three-quarter mile hike up from the road brings you to a lake (approaching a lake from below is really strange, you step up to level ground and your face meets the water level just a few feet away!). The lake is the melting from the glacier itself which is above you on the next peak. A waterfall is at the bottom where the runoff comes out from under the glacier ice and runs into the lake. The lake is at 10,560 feet.

You can climb around the bottom of the ice to a point where you have to get into the snow that's on top. About another half-mile hike up the snow and you find the ice caves at the top edge of the glacier (where the ice on the bottom has melted but not on the top so a small cave has formed between the ground and the ice, presumably because of wind and evaporation, I guess). You can see water melt off the ice and begin its travel down the mountain between the rocks that are under the ice.

As you hike further up you find a meadow full of wildflowers and scrub growth. Nothing very large has time to grow up here, so it's very flat. By what I would guess was 12,000 feet or so you decide you've seen what there is to see and you turn around to go back down. Sure do wish you had a pair of skis when you hit the glacier again (there are a bunch of snowboarders out practicing their jumps). Although lugging them up the first three-quarters of a mile sure would have been hard!
