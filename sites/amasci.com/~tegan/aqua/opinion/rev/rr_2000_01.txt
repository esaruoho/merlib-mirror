Reviews for the week of Jan 6th: Alphabet Supes #1, Birds of Prey #15, Iron Man #25, and Young Justice #18
Extras: MicroColour Microfiche, Galaxy Quest, and Harry Potter and the Prisoner of Azkaban
I'll have another load of reviews tomorrow or so, but there were SO many books this week that I had to split 'em.

Alphabet Supes #1
This is a multi-groaner book.  The puns were so bad, and flying so fast, that I couldn't help but love it.  It was fun, very literary in a gentle way, and just plain interesting.  The cast is too large to really get to know the characters, but Krueger managed to introduce us to many of them anyway.  I, for one, hope there are more issues.
RAPID VERDICT: Buy it.

Birds of Prey #15
This is another strong issue of a generally good book.  Another unexpected guest appearance made this particular issue totally worth it from my point of view.  Add in the sub-plots and the other appearances... this book rocks.  I did have a little difficulty in a couple of places following the story: the art wasn't as clear as it could have been.  But I enjoyed the story enough to forgive the mistakes.
RAPID VERDICT: Check it out.

Iron Man #25
This is it, the last one.  No more Iron Man for me after this issue.  And that's just fine, because all the dangling plot threads were gathered up and resolved.  While there are a lot of fight scenes in this issue, there is also a good chunk of characterization, and the end result is a book that left me a little misty-eyed and very, very happy that I picked up this run of Iron Man.
RAPID VERDICT: A fitting finale.

Young Justice #18
While the team is blamed unfairly for events in the last few issues, the plot still manages to thicken.  The strength of the story is in the way multiple plots are handled, set aside, picked up again... in other words, this is a typical Peter David book.  But this is the first issue of this particular book in which I really felt like the end came too soon.  I could have done with another twenty pages.  But with the crossover coming up involving this team, I'm sure every issue until then is going to end on a cliffhanger.
RAPID VERDICT: You like Peter David's work, buy it.

MicroColour Microfiche
For those of you who don't know what I'm talking about just from the title, MicroColour International Limited (www.microcolour.com) is a company that, among other things, produces Microfiche reprints of many Golden Age comic books.  Microfiche is a wonderful medium: it is portable, it works on machines that 90% of the US population has easy access to, and it's fairly inexpensive when compared to the originals.  I admit that I was a little leery of buying microfiche that I'd have to take to a library to view properly, but then I read a brilliant (and, in retrospect, obvious) suggestion during a debate on the Marvel newsgroup to go buy a used microfiche reader!  A little bit of poking around revealed a great local source for such things (Boeing surplus) and a hint around Christmas-time resulted in my very own Microfiche reader.  To make a long story even longer, I immediately called MicroColour and ordered two sets of microfiche (at about $36 per set).  These sets reprint More Fun Comics #72, 74-77 and Adventure Comics #123-127 (one issue per fiche).  Most of those issues contain Aquaman stories.  I've now read bits of all the issues, and have formed an opinion of the format itself.
Microfiche are naturally harder on the eyes than regular comic books.  There is something about the light that can make it difficult to read for any length of time.  However, Golden Age stories tend to be extremely short, and it's very easy to get through multiple stories without getting too bleary.  Navigating around the stories is a little difficult: I couldn't just turn to the page I wanted, I had to search a little until I got the hang of it.  Reading a story straight through is easy once you've found the first page, though.  The books that they took the images from for the microfiche were not always in the best of shape.  I was a little disappointed by some of the covers: they seemed to be chopped a little on either side.  However, the fun of reading a book I'm unlikely to ever meet in person more than made up for it.  Despite having bought these mostly for the Aquaman appearances, I found myself reading (and enjoying) every story in every book.  Ok, the Superboy story in which a popularity contest is won, in part, by a fist-fight didn't impress me much but even that was fun to read.  As for the price, I thought and still think that at just over $7 an issue this is a bargain.  You can't find photocopies of these stories for that cheap, much less the books themselves, and these fiche take up very little storage space.  If you are a fan of the Golden Age, or have any curiousity about the Golden Age, these are a great way to check it out.
RAPID VERDICT: I want more.  A lot more.  I want all of them.

Galaxy Quest (Movie)
I cannot say this is a good movie.  To be completely honest: the plot was weak, the story was silly, and the characterizations were mostly unkind to fans.  However, I was laughing all the way through and enjoyed it immensely.  It was that kind of a movie.  This is the kind of movie that will have its own cult following in a couple of years, and it's the kind of movie that will be quoted from extensively.  It's the kind of movie that people will like despite themselves.
RAPID VERDICT: Ah, it was fun.  See it, fanboy.

Harry Potter and the Prisoner of Azkaban
After the second book, I felt a very strong urge to immediately read the third.  If the second book was better than the first, would the third be even better yet?  Yup.  This one is significantly better than the first two, though if you'd told me that before I read this, I would have thought it impossible.  And so I'm now eager for the next book, due out in July...
RAPID VERDICT: Perfect for comic book fans, read 'em!




Reviews for Jan 8th: Akiko #37, Avengers #25, Batman: The Gotham Adventures #22, Superman: For The Animals, Green Lantern #122, Martian Manhunter #16, Rising Stars #4, and Xena #4.

Akiko #37
Not the most interesting of the stories in this arc, though the art and interaction between characters are great, as usual.  No, the reason to get this book is for the back-up story: "The Tale of Beeber Rabbit".
RAPID VERDICT: Akiko is always pretty good.

Avengers #25
A bunch of former Avengers join with the team to clean up the remains of that annoying crossover I mentioned last month.  Still, it was readable.
RAPID VERDICT: Not too bad.

Batman: The Gotham Adventures #22
A nice Barbara Gordon story, in which we get an even stronger sense that Jim Gordon knows exactly what Babs is up to, and approves.  It's also a tale about living with the consequences of your actions.  It's not a happy story, but it's an extremely good one.
RAPID VERDICT: GET THIS.

Superman: For The Animals
If the good Batman story wasn't enough, it came polybagged with this Superman tale.  But it's not really a tale about Superman, it's a tale about a kid who is learning right from wrong, and is inspired by Superman.  And if you've got a heart, you'll enjoy it.
RAPID VERDICT: GET THIS.

Green Lantern #122
He's knows something is very wrong, so he trusts his enemy.  Only Kyle.  Nah, I'm being unfair.  This story actually had a lot of Silver Age feel to it, and wasn't bad at all.  Now that Marz is leaving the book, I'm beginning to really enjoy his writing.  Figures.
RAPID VERDICT: Worth a peek.

Martian Manhunter #16
Whoa.  I didn't know a comic book from DC could have that much sex in it.  Ok, it's alien sex but still!  I've said before that I don't like DC's outer space, and I don't care for this kind of story, but the finish was very good.  I actually enjoyed this issue.
RAPID VERDICT: Check it out.

Rising Stars #4
I like this series for its narrative.  There is nothing really special or super-neato about the story being told, but I enjoy the way the story has been presented.  While it's not the best comic out there, it's been consistently pretty good, and worth picking up.  This particular story could be a key element in the overall plot, or a red herring thrown in to confuse us.  I hope it's the former.
RAPID VERDICT: Another interesting story.

Xena #4
Finally a Xena story I can sink my teeth into!  While still following closely the continuity in the show, this issue finally explains what is happening before setting out to tell the tale.  And the difference, for me, is immense.  This is a much easier issue to understand than the ones before it, and a good story.  This is what I want to see from my Xena comics.
RAPID VERDICT: A good one, finally.




Reviews for the week of Jan 12th: Avengers: United They Stand #5, Earth X #11, Hourman #12, JSA: The Liberty File #2, Superman Adventures #41, and Titans/Legion: Universe Ablaze #1

Avengers: United They Stand #5
ARGH!!! Why are they cancelling this series!!!!  WAH!!!!  *ahem*  Just had to throw a temper tantrum there.  This has been a good series, especially the last three issues.  I'm sorry to see it go.  This was another strong issue, with an interesting Hawkeye story.  I'm gonna miss this series.
RAPID VERDICT: Check it out, if your retailer bothered to order it.  ARGH! (yes, I'm bitter)

Earth X #11
This issue has more in common with #9 than with #10.  It's good.  Things happen.  I think I'll repeat my opinion that this series could have worked almost as well as a four issue mini.  As fascinating as the concept is, it's very tired after being drawn out for so long.
RAPID VERDICT: Better than most of the issues in this series.

Hourman #12
Another book polybagged with Superman: For The Animals.  I think I'll give my extras to a kid.  Anyway, a fairly good issue of Hourman.  It's still a good book, but I'm not fond of the One Million Universe.
RAPID VERDICT: So-so.

JSA: The Liberty File #2
If you like pristine, non-spoiled stories, DO NOT FLIP THROUGH THIS BOOK BEFORE READING IT.  I'm glad I didn't.  The twist will no doubt be decried as predictable by the intellectuals, but I loved it.  And I was totally fooled, to boot.  Not the best Elseworld ever written, but I truly enjoyed the finale.
RAPID VERDICT: Fun, fun, fun!

Superman Adventures #41
Twenty-two one page stories.  With some real gems sprinkled in there.  Packaged with the now infamous Superman: For The Animals.  If you like Superman and want a really good stand-alone book, get this.  It's fun.
RAPID VERDICT: A good package.

Titans/Legion: Universe Ablaze #1
Dan Jurgens, current Aquaman writer, with Phil Jimenez, writer/artist of the incredible Tempest mini-series, and Tom McCraw, former Aquaman colorist and the person that taught me that coloring *does* matter.  Sorry folks, I'm too biased.  I'd like this book if it sucked rocks.  *ahem*  However, after reading it, I only recommend it if you are a BIG fan of one or both of the teams that cross over in this mini, especially at this price.
RAPID VERDICT: *I* liked it.




Reviews for the week of Jan 19th: Aquaman #65, Astro City #21, JLA: Created Equal #1, JSA #8, Legends of the DCU #26, Titans #13, Universe So Big #1, and Usagi Yojimbo #35.

Aquaman #65
I'll say this right off: I don't like, at all, what Jurgens is doing to Atlantis.  And I have a distinct problem with a pot-bellied future Garth.  However, once I get around those bits, this is a pretty solid story that has me interested in its outcome.
RAPID VERDICT: About average.

Astro City #21
What is it like to be a comic book fan in Astro City?  About the same as in our universe, it appears, with a few minor differences.  You can still hit a con and see Mark Waid and Devin Grayson... which actually made the story work less well for me.  As fun as it was to see real comic book personalities showing up, it also kicked me right out of the story, out of the comfortable zone of suspension of disbelief I pull around me when I read a comic.  However, since that was the only flaw in the story, I think I can safely recommend it anyway.
RAPID VERDICT: Fun, fun, fun!

JLA: Created Equal #1
The owner of my comic shop is a Marvel fan, and she loves the X-books.  Rarely does she run across a DC book she likes.  She LOVED this book and is looking forward to the next issue.  And so am I.  There are so many little homages and unexpected perks.  And because it's an Elseworld, certain bits of regular continuity are happily ignored for the sake of the story.  It's a bit pricey, but worth a look.
RAPID VERDICT: Check it out.

JSA #8
Doctor Mid-Nite, much better drawn than in his mini, makes an impact on the JSA immediately.  He made this issue worth reading, and I didn't even like the character all that much.  It's a nice story, part 2 of 3, and although it suffers from middle child syndrome, it manages to overcome.  An interesting issue.
RAPID VERDICT: It was ok.

Legends of the DCU #26
That's suppposed to be Vulko?!?  Since when did Vulko have pointed ears?  *ahem*  I haven't read the story which this is a continuation of (the famous "Joker Fish" tale), but the premise isn't hard to pick up.  There are frequent reminders that this is in Silver Age continuity, but even with the reminders I find the story a little too over-the-top to swallow at times.  Aquaman was never that ignorant.  But despite all that, the story was fun and it's nice to see the original Aquaman again.  I'm looking forward to the second part.
RAPID VERDICT: Pretty amusing.

Titans #13
Soap Opera city.  This issue has a little too much angst and not enough story.  Could be a powerful issue... if you like the way Grayson has handled the characters so far.  I found Garth's "I got a wife!" crap to be very annoying, though, more so in this issue than in previous ones, and that just turned me off to the whole book.
RAPID VERDICT: About average.

Universe So Big #1
Another book I was just trying out for the heck of it, and I think I'll keep getting it.  The art is very interesting, a blend of regular artwork with icons thrown into thought balloons, and down the sides of pages in one case.  The characters are intriguing, there's something positive about a robot that loves hot cocoa.  While it's not *quite* my style of book, there's enough there to bring me back.
RAPID VERDICT: Interesting.

Usagi Yojimbo #35
When so many books this week disappointed, I knew there was one on the list that couldn't, and wouldn't, fail to impress me.  I waited to read that book last, savoring it like I would dessert.  Good thing, too.  Another fine issue of Usagi Yojimbo has arrived and managed to brighten the whole week's pile.  If you aren't getting this book, you should really check it out.  This is possibly the best, and most consistent, comic book on the market today.
RAPID VERDICT: Buy this book.




Reviews for the week of Jan 26th: Age of Bronze #6, Avengers #26, Batman: Haunted Gotham #2, Batman Beyond #5, Colonia #5, JLA #39, Supergirl #42, and Xena #5
Extras: Hieroglyph #1-3

Age of Bronze #6
Not For Children!  This continuing tale of the Trojan war has some elements that I hadn't seen before, but are no doubt right there in the many, many books of Greek legends.  This is a book worth the read.  Pick it up, check it out.
RAPID VERDICT: Buy This Book.

Avengers #26
Ouch.  The biggest screw-up in Avengers history might have just taken place, and nobody realized it until a little too late.  I'm now very interested in this plot, and what Busiek is doing to his team.  Better than the last issue.
RAPID VERDICT: Worth a peek.

Batman: Haunted Gotham #2
This could easily have been the last issue in a two-issue mini-series, but there are two more issues to go.  This Elseworld isn't about Batman.  It's about Gotham City.  And the fun is just getting started.  While I'm tried of Batman Elseworlds, this one has got a hook in me: I really want to know how Gotham City got to be this way.
RAPID VERDICT: I'm getting interested.

Batman Beyond #5
Some strong story elements that didn't quite come together.  It's not a bad story, but it didn't mesh and came out as a bit of a jumble in the end.
RAPID VERDICT: Average.

Colonia #5
The conclusion of the first plotline comes with the news that the book isn't going to be able to go bi-monthly after all.  That's ok with me, as long as it still comes out.  This is still one of the better books out there, and now there's a challenge to the readers printed in the back.  Have you figured it out?
RAPID VERDICT: Buy This Book.

JLA #39
A bunch of neat ideas in search of a story.  There were some bits I liked *a lot* about this issue, but overall it was an incomprehensible mish-mash of plotlines that failed to work as a single issue.  I'm going to be real glad when the new creative team comes on board.
RAPID VERDICT: Bleah.

Supergirl #42
One reason I like PAD's writing is that he's one of the few writers around who can balance humor and seriousness without losing the impact of both.  When he's on, he's the best at it there is in the comic book industry.  While this issue was heavy on the humor, there was also a very serious side, including one heckuva cliff-hanger.
RAPID VERDICT: Worth a peek.

Xena #5
This book has become better since the start of the series, but it still has a ways to go before it equals or surpasses the Topps version.  The continuing storyline is a liability that the Topps version avoided by making their book a series of mini-series.  The advantages with that format included the ability to jump from serious to silly without any leap of logic.  This series has been pretty much all serious so far, and I think I prefer the silly Xena.
RAPID VERDICT: About average.

Hieroglyph #1-3
While there isn't a whole lot of story per issue, there is some good art, and a decent mystery.  These books seem to be more concerned with setting up an atmosphere than telling some exciting adventure, but they manage both.  Despite passing on this mini when I first saw it, I'm kinda glad I went back and bought it anyway.  Look at it in the store, if you like what you see on a flip through, you might want to read a little closer.  This is a four issue mini, the fourth issue isn't out yet.
RAPID VERDICT: Strange and interesting stuff.
