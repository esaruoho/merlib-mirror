 INTRODUCTION 

 1.1 Who is Aquaman? 

 Aquaman is a DC Comics Superhero character who has graced the pages of DC comic books for well over 50 years.

 Top 

 1.2 So he's a Superhero? What are his powers? 

 Aquaman is the literal King of the city of Poseidonis (Atlantis), and the figurative King of the Seas. He can breathe underwater, swim at high speeds, and control sea creatures through mental telepathy. He also has a gift of languages, and can understand and be understood by any sea creature, and most humans.
 He also has great strength (certainly superhuman, possibly in Wonder Woman's class - too many references to count), very dense physical structure (as a result of life in the crushing pressures of the ocean depths - likewise), superhuman speed (not in Flash class, but faster than a normal human - see Aquaman '94 #3) and excellent night vision - certainly superior to that of Batman, for example (see JLA Secret Files #2).
 After the loss of his left hand, he had S.T.A.R. Labs provide him with a powerful harpoon tool. The harpoon can be retracted into its casing, or be temporarily replaced with a prosthetic hand. He can make it spin at high speeds to act as a drill, or he can fire it and control it, and the cord it is on, cybernetically. During the Aquaman parody, he also mysteriously got a "regular hand" attachment that he uses more often than not, now.
 He also learned that he had a mental block, and discovered that his control over sea life was much greater than he suspected. He can also sense virtually any life in Earth's Oceans through "The Clear". He also has a form of sonar, and can "see" even when blinded.

 Top 

 1.3 Why should I be interested in Aquaman? 

 For the same reasons you would be interested in any popular culture icons. He is interesting both as a character in a story, and as the story of how the character came to be. This FAQ will try to cover both aspects of him.

 Top 

 AQUAMAN'S HISTORY 

 2.1 What is his origin? Where can I read it? 

 Before I jump into this very loaded question, I need to set up Aquaman's history as a comic book character for you.
 Aquaman was created in 1941, during the Golden Age of comic books, and has survived through the Silver Age until the current, Modern Age. During that time period, comic books have changed considerably, as have the public's perceptions of comic books. Most characters that have been around as long as Aquaman have more than one origin, and more than one starting point. Aquaman himself has at least four origins, which I'll list for you now:

 Top 

 2.1.1 Golden Age/Earth Two... 

 The very first appearance of Aquaman was in More Fun Comics #73, in 1941. This monumental issue also marked the first appearance of two more great DC heroes: Green Arrow and Speedy. This issue would cost you over 6 thousand dollars in its best condition (if you could find a copy). Not to worry, though, the story was reprinted in Secret Origins #7, which should only cost a couple of bucks from your local back issue bins. 
 This story included Aquaman's origin. It was drawn by Paul Norris, and possibly written by Mort Weisinger. After the mysterious "Aquaman" saves a group of civilians on a lifeboat (they were being fired at by those pesky Nazis), he tells them his origin. His father, scientist Tom Curry, studied the lost Kingdom of Atlantis, and taught Aquaman how to live and breathe underwater and to communicate with sea life. He goes on to mention that his father has died, and that he's continuing his studies. That's right folks, the Golden Age Aquaman had no super powers!
 A later retelling of his origin in More Fun #80 left out Atlantis.

 Top 

 2.1.2 Silver Age/Earth One... 

 Pinning down where Aquaman's Silver Age career began is a bit tough, but most folks agree it started with his new origin in Adventure Comics #260 (1959). This version of Aquaman's origin introduced Arthur Curry, a completely new Aquaman. While not as expensive as the "real" first appearance, Adventure #260 could still run you more than 500 bucks! 
 In the story, Aquaman is revealed to be the son of Atlanna, an exiled Poseidonian (for leaving to check out the outside world) and Tom Curry, a lighthouse keeper. Adventure #260 was drawn by long-time aqua-artist Ramona Fradon. She was the premiere penciler for Aquaman's adventures throughout the Silver Age.
 Another possible beginning to Aquaman's Silver Age adventures is Showcase #30 (1961). Although later than Adventure Comics #260, it recapped his new origin and was the successful test run for Aquaman's solo series. This story is reprinted in Super DC-Giant #S-26.
 Comic Book scholars also like using Adventure Comics #218 as Aquaman's first Silver Age adventure. That issue came out November 1955, the month that J'onn J'onzz first appears.

 Top 

 2.1.3 Modern Age... 

 In the Legend of Aquaman (1989), his origin was changed yet again. This time we learn his mother Atlanna was the Queen of Atlantis, and we never learn exactly who the father was, though it is implied that his father is the late King. He was abandoned on Mercy Reef as a baby because of his blond hair, the mark of a curse in Atlantis. He got the name Arthur Curry from a lighthouse keeper of that name who found him as a child and raised him like a son. When he returned to Atlantis he was captured by the dictatorial regime of the time, and recognized his mother in captivity. After escaping, he became a hero, still wearing the prison uniform from his home. When he finally returned, he was welcomed by a free city who had been following his surface exploits, and was free because of the example he set when escaping. He identified a picture of the late Queen as his mother, and was given the throne.

 Top 

 2.1.4 Current... 

 The final version of his modern origin is explained in Peter David's Atlantis Chronicles (1990). In it we learn that Aquaman is the son of the wizard Atlan and Queen Atlanna. His true Atlantean name is revealed to be Orin.
 Please note: This origin does not directly contradict The Legend of Aquaman, as it was written less than a year after that special came out. Instead, Peter David wove his origin into the previous origin and fused them. To read all of Aquaman's current origin, you need to pick up both The Legend of Aquaman and The Atlantis Chronicles. You should also get Aquaman: Time and Tide, which introduces his dolphin family, and shows his first heroic actions.
 Of course, they just explain his origins, they don't explain the physical changes that make people do a double-take of the current version and say, "That's Aquaman?!?" It started in his 1991 series, in which he grew out his hair. Then in Time and Tide while reading (and adding to) the Chronicles, he starts to grow a beard. At the start of his current series he had the beard and long hair. In issue #2 of the current series, he lost his hand, and replaced it with a hook in issue #0. After a fight with Lobo, his costume is torn so badly he gets his new costume and look (issue #5). Lastly, after his harpoon is damaged, he has it replaced at S.T.A.R. labs with a cybernetically controlled version (issue #9).

 Top 

 2.2 Why does he have so many origins? 

 As Kevin Dooley (Editor of Aquaman 1991-1999) has explained, the reason for the multiple origins is he has to keep current. Everything that happened in 1941's origin wouldn't make sense to today's readers. Everyone hasn't had a chance to read the original version, so they have to give those readers an origin that they can relate to.

 Top 

 2.3 What was the Golden Age Aquaman like? 

 His only real power was breathing underwater. He had no telepathy, and used other methods to communicate with sea life. Although he fights the Nazis in his first issue, the War was never his focus. He spent a lot of time fighting his arch-enemy Black Jack. Occassionally he would join in the war effort, but his stories were, for the most part, stand-alone adventures based in the seas. In addition, most of his adventures were short, between 6 and 10 pages. The villains he fought were generally non-powered, generic bad guys. Even Black Jack was a simple, non-powered, modern pirate.

 Top 

 2.4 What was the Silver Age Aquaman like? 

 After getting his new origin, the Silver Age Aquaman quickly collected a side-kick and supporting cast, which he'd been lacking for much of his career. Aqualad changed the focus of the book and helped younger readers get into the stories with glee, imagining themselves in Aqualad's place, or at his side. Aquaman became popular enough to support his own book, and soon new characters were introducing themselves, like the mischievous Quisp, an other-worldly imp; Mera, a Queen from another dimension; Vulko, an advisor and friend; and Tula, a rebellious Atlantean teen who liked Aqualad.
 Eventually Mera stayed with Aquaman, and they married. Aquaman continued to fight crime, and had a son.
 But in the late Silver Age/early Modern Age, things started going wrong for Aquaman, as his enemies conspired to destroy him, and did so. He was forced to fight Aqualad to save his son, and lost Aqualad's trust during the fight. Then, running off to wreak vengeance on the bad guys, he left Mera alone to deal with his dying child. When he finally returned, his son was dead and Mera couldn't bear to be with him.
 Aquaman was full of angst and regret. And that's how he entered the Modern Age.

 Top 

 2.5 What happened to Aquaman between the Silver Age and the Current Series? 

 After his solo series was canceled in the famous "DC Implosion" of 1978, Aquaman didn't have many solo adventures. He made up with Mera and disbanded the Justice League, then showed up in cameos and the Justice League until his first miniseries came out in 1986.
 This miniseries, written by the late Neal Pozner, debuted a new, blue costume and concentrated on the magic of the Atlantean city of Thierna Na Oge. A little was said about a follow-up, but nothing related ever really surfaced until issue #21 of the 1994 series. Although this series is out of continuity, it has influenced the way both Aquaman and Aqualad have developed since.
 His next solo appearance came in the 1988 Aquaman special. The book wasn't good and didn't become popular. That's all you need to know.
 Finally something got going in 1989. Curt Swan and Keith Giffen created both a miniseries and a special that year. The former dealt with Mera, Aquaman's then-wife, going nuts and leaving him. His life was left in torment. The latter, The Legend of Aquaman, retold his origin in a beautifully drawn story.
 In 1990, Peter David wrote The Atlantis Chronicles, a story that only features Aquaman in the last issue, but nonetheless would have a lasting impact on the Aquaman mythos.
 In December of 1991, 50 years after his first appearance, Aquaman got his second ever solo series. This series was doing well under the authorship of Shaun McLaughlin, but Peter David was poised to take over and DC didn't say no.
 In the Summer of 1994, Aquaman: Time and Tide, a mini-series written by Peter David, came out. This mini-series complemented The Atlantis Chronicles, and set-up the current Aquaman series, which started in late 1994.
 Peter David left the book with issue #46 due to creative differences. Dan Jurgens is the current writer, and will write the series until it ends with issue #75.

 Top 

 2.6 What is the Current Aquaman like? 

 During that whole Zero Hour thing, Aquaman got his hand chomped off by piranhas, had a hook put in place, and changed his entire costume--all in a couple of issues! Writer Peter David still insists that he was planning these things for awhile though, and the master plan did slowly revealed itself.
 Since the end of the Silver Age, Aquaman has been one of the more angst-ridden characters in the DCU. Various writers attempted to put his angst to rest, only to have it resurrected again by the next writer who took him on. The 1986 series was all about Aquaman dealing with his emotions, but the 1988 special put him right back into angstville. The Legend of Aquaman was positive, but the 1989 mini that it came out with had Mera leave him for good! Even the 1992 series had more angst and anger for Aquaman, including an issue in which he tries to kill Black Manta in revenge for the murder of his son.
 Peter David decided to go with the flow, and Aquaman: Time and Tide has a very depressed Aquaman reading the Chronicles of Atlantis to try to figure out why he is so messed up. At the start of his current series he's about as low as a hero can get. Suffering from massive depression and apathy, he is drawn out by his oldest friend, Aqualad, and eventually goes on a quest to figure himself out. By the end of that storyline, Aquaman managed to tame his inner demons and find peace, a major and welcome change.
 Unfortunately, after Peter David's run, DC ran an Aquaman parody for a few issues that attempted to demolish all that had been established, and succeeded in driving away most of the long-time Aquaman fans. See the next section for more information.
 The new Aquaman has yet to establish himself.

 Top 

 2.7 What is the Aquaman Parody? 

 Unfortunately, what can only be taken as an Aquaman parody was published under the "Aquaman" title for 14 issues (Aquaman #50-62 and Aquaman Secret Files #1). This run only bore a slight resemblance to Aquaman. The characters had the same names, but that was about it. This parody, which could be referred to as "The Vicious Sea-King", had no redeeming qualities whatsoever, except that it finally ended and a new writer was allowed to attempt to salvage the pieces of the Aquaman mythos.
 Thanks to this parody, Aquaman lost most of its traditional audience, including almost all the female readers. The sales dropped dramatically, and at the end of the parody DC decided that Aquaman was not worth salvaging, and hired Dan Jurgens to finish the series.
 The biggest storyline fallout from this parody was that Tempest and Dolphin were both given brain transplants that led to them becoming "white trash" and having a shotgun wedding. Dolphin's pregnancy was drawn out into Aquaman #63, where the next writer had to deal with it.
 Another noticable change was that Aquaman's hook was immediately replaced with a mysterious and never explained cybernetic hand which he now favors over the hook.
 The recommendation of the author of this FAQ is that you avoid these issues entirely, because even as a parody they are very poor. If you insist on punishing yourself with them, it is best to think of them as the writings of a lazy sensationalist journalist in the DC Universe who didn't bother to do his research and only thinks that this is what Aquaman's Atlantis is like. They do not stand well against any other Aquaman stories ever published.

 Top 

 2.8 How, exactly, did he lose his hand? 

 In the second issue of the current series, we learn that Aquaman does not control sea life. He asks for their obedience and is granted it. Some sea life, like piranha, don't necessarily listen to him. The villain, Charybdis, uses this against Aquaman by shoving his hand into piranha-infested water. The piranha devour his hand. He replaces it with a harpoon that has special meaning for him, then later with a cybernetically controlled harpoon tool.
 Later in the series we learn that Aquaman *can* control sea life, but the price for forcing his control onto them is that he loses the respect of said sea life, and turns them against him. They will rebel if he lets up his control. Aquaman chooses to respect the sea life, and earns their respect by not using the full extent of his powers.
 Another price he pays for exerting his control is the "Kordax Effect". When he becomes a tyrant like his ancestor he begins to look like his ancestor: green and scaly.

 Top 

 2.9 What are some good jumping-on points for the current series? 

 If you can afford and find the series from the beginning, I'd recommend it. However, since that's likely not practical, I'll list some issues you ought to get, and some places where it would be good to start reading the series.
 Issues to get:

 Issues 1, 2, and 0 are a trio that set the tone for the current series. If you can't get all three, find issue #2. This is the infamous piranha issue.
 Issue #5 has the first appearance of Aquaman's new costume, but also is the beginning of a mini-arc. This one isn't needed unless you're intent on seeing how the new costume came about.
 Issue #9 is a stand alone issue that introduces Aquaman's new harpoon to the world. This is a good issue to get for that reason alone, although the issue itself is not terribly impressive.

 Jumping on points:

 Issues 1-25 are one mega-arc, so issue #26 is actually a good starting point, as long as you know that Aquaman has changed. This is also the Final Night crossover issue.
 Other starting points: #29, #31, #35, #38, #47, and #63. The earlier the better, of course, but all of them work for a first-time reader.
 Avoid issues #50-62 and Aquaman Secret Files #1. These are all low quality Aquaman parody, and do not represent Aquaman at all.

 SUPPORTING CAST 

 3.1 Aquaman has a big supporting cast, who are they? 

 Every good character has others in his/her life that help make him/her interesting. Aquaman is a prime example. Here are some of the people who play an important part in his life.

 Top 

 3.1.1 Aqualad/Tempest... 

 Aquaman's best friend, ward, and conscience. In the original continuity, Aquaman found the boy, Garth, in a life pod. He was exiled from Atlantis because of his purple eyes and his fear of fish. Purple eyes indicated that a person would eventually not be able to live under the ocean. Aqualad was the exception that proved the rule.
 In the post-crisis continuity, he was exiled by the peaceful Idyllists after his father, the King, went insane and tried to build an army. His purple eyes were a sign of the mystic potential he contained.
 After many adventures with Aquaman and the Teen Titans, Garth was transported to another dimension where he was trained by Atlan, Aquaman's father, to fulfil his magical potential. In the excellent Tempest: Prophets and Kings miniseries, Garth did just that and became Tempest, DCU's newest magical hero. Tempest's powers are fully examined in the letter column of the Tempest mini-series, and you can check it out to find out what he's now capable of.
 You can currently follow his adventures along with Aquaman's in the current Aquaman book and in The Titans.
 During the Aquaman parody, Tempest was somehow perverted from a sensitive, intelligent, and shy man into typical teenage idiot with no control over himself or his sex drive, and was forced into a shotgun wedding with a similarly perverted Dolphin. Although this characterization and the ramifications of it are completely off, the editors decided to see it through and the child that is supposedly Tempest's was the focus of the first arc under the new writer. Hopefully future writers will be allowed to ignore the Aquaman parody.

 Top 

 3.1.2 Mera... 

 Aquaman's wife is Mera. Mera is a queen from another dimension who gave up her throne in order to be with Aquaman. They had a son together and when he was killed by Black Manta, she left him for the first time.
 After a lot of angst and blame, the two got back together. But Aquaman was a hero, often absent from his throne. When an invasion of Atlantis succeeded while he was away, Mera began to go insane. After defeating the invaders, he tried to reason with Mera, but she was too far gone to even understand him, and left, presumably going back to her home dimension.
 That wasn't the case, though... Thanatos, an old enemy of Aquaman, had captured Mera and caused the insanity. In his dimension, a world of dark creatures that considered themselves gods, she had another child. She escaped from Thanatos temporarily into Aquaman's world, but was still under Thanatos' control and returned, followed by Aquaman and Dolphin. After Thanatos' control was broken, she admitted that she didn't know whether the child was Aquaman's or Thanatos', as she had him nine months into her captivity.
 After escaping from the dark world, the child (AJ) started to age rapidly, and Mera returned with him to the dark world to save his life. AJ appeared again to bring Aquaman to his war-torn world, where Aquaman assisted in defeating invaders. AJ stayed behind in the other dimension as that world's new Aquaman while Mera and the original Aquaman returned to DC Earth.
 Mera declared their marraige null and void after reading some old Atlantean law books.

 Top 

 3.1.3 Arthur Jr and AJ... 

 Artie Junior was born in Aquaman #23 of the first series. He never got past his diapers though, as he was fatally injured by Black Manta in Adventure Comics #452, and died in issue #60 of the original series. Swaying from traditional comic form, Artie Jr. is still dead. He was 3 years old when he died. His most recent appearance was as a ghost in Aquaman Annual #4.
 AJ is a different character , born in Thanatos' dimension. We do not know who the father of AJ is. For some time, Mera believed that AJ was the original Artie Jr. as she was living out fantasies woven by Thanatos. AJ is now the Aquaman of another world, continuing Arthur's legacy.
 PLEASE NOTE: Artie Jr and AJ are TWO DIFFERENT CHARACTERS. I've been informed by people who apparently haven't read the Peter David series that I'm wrong about them, but just read the Aquaman Ghosts Annual for further proof. Arthur Junior is still dead.

 Top 

 3.1.4 Vulko... 

 A scholar, an advisor, a one-time king... What can be said to describe Vulko?
 In the Pre-Crisis continuity, Vulko was Atlantis' leading scientist, inventing solutions to problems caused by Aquaman's arch-enemies.
 In the current continuity, he is considerably more important.
 Vulko was advisor to the King when Aquaman was born. Though he had no problem with the blond child, the King did and abandoned him on Mercy Reef, despite Vulko's protests. Years later, Vulko taught his fellow prisoner, Aquaman, the language and customs of Atlantis. Inspired by Aquaman's escape, Vulko helped to overthrow the government and re-establish the monarchy. Serving on the counsel, he also welcomed the hero Aquaman back into the city, and helped him discover that his mother had been Queen.
 During Aquaman's reign, Vulko was vital in running the city and maintaining order, often acting as King.
 When the citizens decided to leave Poseidonis during the Earthquakes, he went along and advised the young leader Koryak. Under Kordax's power, he helped commit the crimes against the city of Tritonis, and was punished by Aquaman along with the rest of the people. After returning home, he found that he deeply resented the changes in Aquaman, and plotted treason along with Koryak.
 In the current continuity, Vulko is Aquaman's oldest living friend.

 Top 

 3.1.5 Tula... 

 Tula was a fun-loving Atlantean girl who took a liking to Aqualad. After taking him out on an ill-advised adventure, the two became inseparable and had many adventures together, including a stint in the Teen Titans. Tula died during the Crisis, and Garth has only recently been able to get over her death.

 Top 

 3.1.6 Dolphin... 

 Dolphin hooked up with Aquaman after they were both captured by Charybdis in issue #1 of the current series. Dolphin shot Charybdis after he forced Aquaman's hand into the piranha-infested water.
 She was Aquaman's companion on his quest to find out a bit more about his true heritage. She stayed with him through thick and thin, even fighting off Mera in one issue, until he began to get truly nasty. She left him when he started to bear a resemblance to Kordax, an enemy of Aquaman, both in looks and nature.
 Dolphin's origin is still mostly mystery. We know she was abducted as a young child and experimented on (Secret Origins #50), and that Kordax was involved. She is strong, fast, and knows how to fight. She can also talk with sea creatures. She was being controlled by Kordax for a time, and this was part of what made her fear Aquaman so much.
 During the Aquaman parody, Dolphin was turned into a typical American "White Trash" slut, who got pregnant out of wedlock then demanded a shotgun wedding out of Tempest. This characterization is so off that it should be laughed off and ignored. Unfortunately, the editors at DC lost all their marbles and decided to honor this version, and the new writer was forced to follow-through with the pregnancy and ramifications from the "wedding".

 Top 

 3.1.7 Koryak... 

 Koryak is Aquaman's son from a liaison in his youth. His mother Kako became the fire elemental Corona, and he journeyed to Poseidonis and promptly started to lead the people there. He accidentally led them into slavery under Kordax, and fought Tempest while under Kordax's control. He was banished from Poseidonis after attempting to take the throne from Aquaman.

 Top 

 3.1.8 Others... 

 Atlan: Ancient Wizard from an earlier era of Atlantean history. Aquaman and Ocean Master's father.
 Atlanna: The Queen of Atlantis. Aquaman's mother. Deceased.
 Kako: Aquaman's lover for one night. Mother of his son Koryak. Now the fire elemental Corona.
 Pakkul: Aquaman's whale friend while growing up. Died during Artie Jr.'s birth.
 Tusky: A walrus who had some adventures with Aquaman. Now performs at a ocean park.
 Storm and Imp: Seahorses who acted as Aquaman and Aqualad's mounts during some adventures.
 Topo: An octopus who helped Aquaman in many adventures, then became a nursemaid to young Artie Jr. for a time.
 Porm: Dolphin who raised Arthur after finding him on Mercy Reef.
 Arthur (Tom) Curry: Aquaman's adoptive father, a lighthouse keeper.
 Arion, Tsunami, Neptune Perkins: Other sea-based heroes whose lives have intersected with Aquaman's.
 Deep Blue: Also known as Debbie, Tsunami's daughter. A "writer" changed her code name to Indigo for no apparent reason.
 Cal Durham: Former lackey of Manta gone good, hasn't shown up in some time.
 Flash II (Barry Allen): First hero from outside world to meet him.
 J'onn J'onnz (Martian Manhunter): Aquaman's best pal from the JLA.
 Wonder Woman: Aquaman "saved" her in the '95 annual. Romance between them is brewing in the current JLA book.
 Black Canary, Green Lantern II: Fellow original JLA members.

 Top 

 3.2 Does Aquaman have a "Rogues' Gallery"? 

 Oh yes. His has in the past been one of the weakest galleries in the DCU, but he's got a few good ones in there to keep him busy.

 Top 

 3.2.1 Black Manta... 

 Murderer of Aquaman's son, Arthur Jr. Supposedly killed off in '91 series.
 Manta was revealed to be a black man who was tired of racism and discrimination on the surface world, and intended to conquer the oceans as a new homeland. Instead of actually doing it, though, he got hung up on trying to destroy Aquaman and Atlantis.
 Later, he apparently took up Neron's Offer (Underworld Unleashed) and became a half-manta, half-human creature who could live in the deepest depths. This new Manta has lost his original purpose (perhaps the cost of Neron's gift?) and seeks only power. He apparently died in an ocean trench while confronting Aquaman, but returned in the Aquaboy one-shot, and indicated that he regretted taking Neron up on his offer.

 Top 

 3.2.2 Ocean Master... 

 Aquaman's half-brother Orm. Kako's cousin.
 Orm first became aware of Aquaman through Kako, and hurt Kako badly. Later, he became Ocean Master to try to take everything away from Aquaman. His jealousy is simple insanity: If Aquaman is happy, he must do something to destroy that happiness. The two have repeatedly clashed, though Aquaman usually beat Orm, who had no powers.
 Orm also took up Neron's Offer, and was given powers with two drawbacks. The first was that his face became horribly pocked and scarred, and the second was that he must hold onto his staff at all times, even when sleeping, or suffer agonizing pain.
 Was working with Lex Luthor and his Injustice Gang battling the Justice League, then worked with the island nation of Cerdia to destroy Atlantis.

 Top 

 3.2.3 The Scavenger... 

 A strange criminal who basically recovered things from lost ships for profit.
 Reformed in the last issue of the '91 series.

 Top 

 3.2.4 The Shark... 

 Actually more of a Green Lantern villain, the Shark managed to conquer Atlantis once, and stole the city from Aquaman during the current series.

 Top 

 3.2.5 Kordax... 

 Kordax is a second-generation Atlantean (his father lived in Poseidonis before the city was submerged). That makes him very old. He's also very powerful, both in magic and physically. He bears a distinct resemblance to Aquaman: both are blond, both command the creatures of the sea, both have lost a hand.
 Introduced in Atlantis Chronicles. Apparently killed himself while battling Aquaman.

 Top 

 3.2.6 Others... 

 Fire Trolls: The Fire Trolls battled Aquaman, Aqualad, and Quisp in the first issue of Aquaman's first solo series.
 Thanatos: Uses fantasies to control people's thoughts. Killed in Underworld Unleashed, when he impersonated Aquaman.
 Admiral Strom: An agent of an alien race attempting to take over the Earth. Killed by his masters for failure.
 The Fisherman: A strange dude without any eyes. Worked as a member of the Terrible Trio.
 O.G.R.E.: An evil organization that Aquaman faced a couple of times in the Silver Age. Had an annoying tendency to ruin his vacations.
 Charybdis: Insane terrorist who lost his partner, Scylla when a bomb she was working on blew up. Tried to gain Aquaman's powers, but was shot by Dolphin and fell into a river full of piranha. During the Aquaman Parody, the writer tried to bring him back as Piranha-Man, but Piranha-Man only makes sense as a different fifth-rate villain.

 Top 

 3.3 What teams has Aquaman belonged to? 

 Aquaman was an original member, and has been on-and-off since then (on right now), of DC's premiere team Justice League of America. He even led them for awhile and started the second. In the Justice League he also established relationships with Superman, Batman, Wonder Woman and the like.

 Top

 This one's a difficult question if you go by post-Zero Hour continuity. Here's the ones that are known:

 First met Superman: Aquaman ('94) Annual '95, during a god-driven storm.
 First met Flash (Barry Allen): Aquaman: Time & Tide #1, during a heist by The Trickster.

Logically, since this is the first public appearance of both BC and MM, Aquaman cannot possibly have met them at any other time previously.)

 AQUAMAN'S HOME 

 4.1 Where does Aquaman live? 

 Aquaman was abandoned as a child on Mercy Reef, and was raised by Dolphins. He hasn't really got a "home town" in that sense, which make the oceans in general his home. He also spent some time in a lighthouse learning about humans from Arthur Curry.
 Of course, once he became a hero he was welcomed back into his ancestral home, the city of Poseidonis in the area known as Atlantis. He is king of Poseidonis, and started to raise a family there. After a lot of adventures, Poseidonis is the place he returns to, which makes it his home.

 Top 

 4.2 And the Aquacave? 

 We mustn't forget the Aquacave, the home of Aquaman's many treasures and momentos, and the place he goes to escape the pressures of being a superhero and king. For a time, he and Mera lived in the cave when they exiled themselves from Atlantis.

 Top 

 4.3 What else is in Atlantis? 

 The primary cities of Atlantis are the rivals Poseidonis and Tritonis. The people of Poseidonis bear a strong resemblance to surfacers, with legs and no fins. The people of Tritonis are half-human, half-fish, commonly referred to as Mer-People by surfacers.
 The city of Shayeris (Crastinus) is the home of the Idyllists, Garth's people. These people are pacifists who dislike the violent ways of the heroes.
 Thierna Na Oge, crushed by a Millenium Giant, is the city of mystics and home to much of the magic undersea.

 Top 

 THE COMIC BOOKS 

 5.1 What books has Aquaman been in? 

 Top 

 5.1.1 Golden Age... 

 His Golden Age run consisted of MORE FUN COMICS (73-107, Nov 1941-Jan 1946) and ADVENTURE COMICS (103-259, Apr 1946-Nov 1959). He also appeared in an early issue of World's Finest.

 Top 

 5.1.2 Silver Age... 

 Aquaman's Golden Age run in ADVENTURE COMICS continued into the Silver Age (260-280, May 1959-Jan 1961), and then he jumped to SHOWCASE for a four issue test run (30-33, Jan 1961-Jul 1961). The results were good, and after a short run in DETECTIVE COMICS (293-300, Jul 1961-Feb 1962) as a back-up, he got his first solo series.
 The AQUAMAN series ran for 56 issues, from Feb 1962 to Apr 1971 bimonthly. After a two year gap he jumped back into ADVENTURE COMICS for a three issue back-up (435-437, Oct 1974-Feb 1975), then later for a longer run (441-452, Oct 1975-Aug 1977) as the main feature. A month after the climax of the storyline in Adventure 452, Aquaman was back in his own book, which started numbering where the first series had left off (57-63, Sep 1977-Sep 1978). This book was cancelled in the infamous DC Implosion.

 Top 

 5.1.3 Modern Age... 

 Between 1978 and 1986, Aquaman had another long run in ADVENTURE COMICS, and some back-up stories in ACTION COMICS after that. His Justice League appearances during this time are nearly as important for the character as his solo stories, as he took charge of the League after an alien invasion, disbanded the original League, and started a new one. He then left the League to spend more time with his wife.
 He didn't really get back into the limelight until his mini-series of 1986. The mini, which introduced a new costume for Aquaman, was very successful (in terms of reader reaction), but didn't lead to anything (1-4, Feb 1986-May 1986).
 After a forgettable special in 1988, he came back in style in 1989 with THE LEGEND OF AQUAMAN (a new origin story) and a second mini-series (1-5, Jun 1989-Oct 1989). The following year Peter David wrote the excellent ATLANTIS CHRONICLES (1-7, Mar 1990-Sep 1990), and Aquaman began to take off again.
 In December of 1991 he got another solo series, starting again from issue #1. It did well, but Peter David was poised to take over the book, and rather than simply switch writers, DC decided to relaunch the series. The 1991 series lasted 13 issues (Dec 1991-Dec 1992).
 DC relaunched Aquaman with his third mini-series, AQUAMAN: TIME AND TIDE (1-4, Dec 1993-Mar 1994), which went into even more detail on Aquaman's origins. The current series started in August 1994.

 Top 

 5.3 Which Aquaman books are in continuity? 

 Aquaman is very, very lucky in this respect. Only one of his many series has got the official out-of-continuity stamp on it. The four issue Neal Pozner series of 1986 is the only Aquaman series that is totally out of continuity. Despite this, other Aquaman writers have incorporated that series into Aquaman's history.
 Other stories that are out-of-continuity for Aquaman are his other origins, and any story that has been directly contradicted by the current book. His Golden Age stories can be handed off to a different Golden Age hero (like Neptune Perkins or even Atlan) and therefore not simply thrown out.

 Top 

 5.4 What is 'The Atlantis Chronicles'? 

 In 1990, Peter David set out to rewrite the history of the DC Universe's Atlantis. The result was the excellent Atlantis Chronicles mini-series. It starts just before the sinking of the city of Poseidonis and ends with the birth of Aquaman. Introduced in the series are a cast of characters that gives background to Aquaman that he never had before, including: Orin, the monarch of Poseidonis when the city was domed and sunk; Kordax, a second generation water-breather and Orin's grandson; and Atlan, the second son of a warrior King and a wizard who survived to be Aquaman's father. 
 In addition to adding these elements, the series also retconned Aquaman to be Atlan's son, and gave him an Atlantean name: Orin. It also retconned Aquaman's brother, Ocean Master, and added him back into the mythos.

 CREATIVE TEAMS 

 6.1 Who created Aquaman? 

 That's a tough one. The truth is no one knows for sure. DC, along with most other publishers at the time, failed to keep records. The only credits given in the books were to artists (Paul Norris in Aquaman's case).
 Many people believe that Mort Weisinger created him. He is credited with the creation of many of DC's most popular characters of that time, including the still-popular Green Arrow. In addition, Aquaman's survival through the Golden Age into the Silver Age argues that someone liked him enough to help keep him in print, like, oh, say, Mort Weisinger. Of note: on the Aquaman Filmation series of 1967, Mort was the sole story consultant. Also of note: Mort Weisinger used Aquaman as a guest in several Superman stories.
 There is apparently some evidence that he may not have been involved in Aquaman's creation, though, so we get back to the truth: No one knows for sure. The evidence is strongly in favor of Mort Weisinger being the creator, but no one knows.
 What we do know is that the first artist on Aquaman was Paul Norris, and he designed the look that lasted almost unchanged until 1986.

 Top 

 6.2 Is it true that Aquaman was just a rip-off of Marvel's Sub-Mariner? 

 The Sub-Mariner, Namor, first appeared in 1939. Two years later Aquaman showed up. You might think, then, that Aquaman is a rip-off, but comparing the two might just change your mind.
 Namor was a water-breather, half-human, who came from a place called "Aquaria" and hated air-breathers. He was both a hero and a villain in his early stories, sometimes fighting the Nazis, often beating up on New York. He was super-strong and could fly.
 Aquaman was a water-breather, totally human son of an American scientist who had discovered Atlantis. He was always a hero, and never conquered New York. His only "super" power was breathing underwater. Unlike Namor, though, Aquaman stuck around through the long years, always in print.
 They have actually gotten more similar through the years. Namor was eventually linked to Atlantis, and "Aquaria" retconned out, and Aquaman became super-strong. But when they started, they were very different.
 The presence of Namor may have inspired the creation of Aquaman, but Aquaman is not a rip-off of Namor.

 Top 

 6.3 Who drew him in the Golden Age? 

 Paul Norris was the first Aquaman artist, and drew the first ten Aquaman stories.
 He turned over the duty to artist Louis Cazeneuve who drew him for the reminder of the More Fun run, and into the Golden Age Adventure Comics.
 At some point, John Daly took over the art chores, but I currently don't have any more details.

 Top 

 6.4 Who drew him in the Silver Age? 

 Ramona Fradon was the primary artist on Aquaman during much of his Silver Age Adventure Comics run.
 The original (1962) series was first drawn by Nick Cardy. Working with writer Bob Haney, he managed to draw almost the entire original series until Dick Giordano came in from Charlton Comics. 
 Jim Aparo and Steve Skeates then took over until the book was canceled. When picked up again, the book was drawn by a number of folks, including Jim Aparo.

 Top 

 6.5 Who dragged him through those miniseries? 

 1986: Neal Pozner-Story, Craig Hamilton-Art, Steve Montano-Inker.
 1989: Keith Giffen-Plot/Layouts, Curt Swan-Pencils, Robert Loren Fleming-Script, Al Vey-Inks.
 Atlantis Chronicles: Peter David-Writer, Esteban Maroto-Art.
 Time & Tide: Peter David-Writer, Kirk Jarvinen-Pencils, Brad Vancata-Inks.

 Top 

 6.6 Wasn't there a series before Peter David took over? 

 Yes. Aquaman's second series was written by Shaun McLaughlin. This was an excellent run that had Aquaman becoming Poseidonis' representative to the UN, and featured a particularly good issue spotlighting Aquaman and the Martian Manhunter's friendship. This series also revamped Thanatos into the villain that Peter David later used, and included a brief cameo of Mera in Thanatos' thrall.
 This series also had a nice arc to it that was never resolved, including a plot thread involving one mysterious Minister F'ancha of Poseidonis. For more information about this series, check out the Second Series Resolution Notes .

 Top 

 6.7 Okay, who messed him up? 

 The Current Series started with: Peter David-Writer, Marty Egeland & Jim Calafiore-Pencils, Howard M. Shum-Inks, Tom McCraw-Colors, Kevin Dooley-Editor, Eddie Berganza-Asst. Editor.
 Another writer and artist team turned in 14 issues of Aquaman parody that aren't worth counting or collecting. These are issues #50-62 of the 1994 run, and Aquaman Secret Files #1. Avoid these, because even as parody or elseworld they don't work and are of extremely low quality.
 Starting with Issue #63: Dan Jurgens-Writer, Steve Epting-art, Mike Kaluta-covers, Tony Bedard-Editor.

 See also: Atlantis Chronicles and Time & Tide (both above).

 AQUAMAN'S IMPACT ON POP CULTURE 

 7.1 What kind of Aquaman 'stuff' is out there? 

 If you're out looking for clothes, you might consider some vintage Aqua-underoos, made in the 1970's. You might also consider a lovely orange t-shirt/costume from the same time period. Heck, you could even find a pair of Aquaman gym socks if you had the time (and clams)!
 If you're looking for some toys to add to your collection, there were many action figures, which are listed in the next section. Or for you real collectors, try finding a rare Japanese vinyl bootleg, complete with really big head.
 There's also pencil cases, swim rings, liquid embroidery, chewing gum and tattoos!
 Next valentines day, tell that special someone you really care with an Aquaman valentine (w/art by Dick Giordano!).
 Fine dining is never complete without your Aquaman and Aqualad collector cups from 7-11. But why stop there? Pepsi also put out Aquaman glasses, with two different designs!

 Top 

 7.2 Did Aquaman have a lot of action figures? 

 Oh yeah.
 Starting with Mego, Aquaman had no less than four different types of Mego figures, from the Pocket Super Hero up to the World's Greatest, which most everyone is familiar with.
 There was also a Captain Action Aquaman Disguise, and an Aqualad disguise for the Action Boy figure. To go along with the Captain Action, Ideal also put out a now rare Mera doll.
 Kenner put out an Aquaman figure in its popular Super Powers line, which came with a mini-comic also starring The Flash and Penguin.
 Toy Biz put out an Aquaman figure in their fairly unpopular DC Heroes line. He came with a trident and seahorse. There was a mistake variant of this figure who had completely green arms, instead of orange and green.
 Kenner put out two variations of Aquaman in their recent Total Justice line, and a repaint of those figures was made available in the Warner Bros Stores (in a JLA line). The WB Studio Store has released an Aquaman Bean Bag toy.
 Kenner recently released two new Aquaman figures. A 12" modern Aquaman (with hook and beard) was a KB Toys exclusive, and can still be found. A 9" Classic Aquaman was released to general circulation.
 The lack of good Aquaman figures has also inspired many creative and talented people to create their own action figures. Check out http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/aqua/media/custom.html for a few examples.

 Top 

 7.3 Didn't I see Aquaman in a cartoon once? 

 Yup! He recently appeared on the animated Superman Adventures show, in an episode called "A Fish Story".
 He also co-starred in the Superman/Aquaman Adventure Hour produced by Filmation. This is the series which had him and Aqualad riding seahorses named Storm and Imp. This show actually re-ran in syndication until around 1983, when it was eventually pulled from release. It was hugely popular on CBS for 2 seasons on Saturday morning prime time, then switched to early Sunday mornings from 1970 through 1974, a remarkable run for any cartoon show. It was recently re-run on USA Network's Cartoon Express, and the show has now moved to the Cartoon Network where it moves through the rotation.
 Marvin Miller did the voice of Aquaman, and Ted Knight was the Narrator and many of the villains.
