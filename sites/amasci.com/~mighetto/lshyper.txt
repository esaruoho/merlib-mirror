 Hypertext 

 Hypertext is a large net of cross-referenced text which allows reading in many sequences. At its
lowest level, hypertext can be regarded as a database management system that allows the
connection of chunks of information (nodes) in an intuitive way without the traditional database
record and file structure. Information is collected in nodes. The nodes are connected with
associative links that reference related information in other nodes. Nodes and links are the key
concepts in hypertext. 

Hypertext started as early as 1945 when Vannevar Bush , the science advisor of President
Roosevelt envisioned a mechanism for on-line text retrieval that would mimic the brain's way of
processing information. This machine was called Memex. It was never created, but the dream of
associative information retrieval was not forgotten. It became hypertext.
