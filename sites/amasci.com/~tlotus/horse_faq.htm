Sahara Horse Safari FAQ

Your tour guide, Fergany Alkomaty, has been leading
horse safaris through the remote reaches of the Sahara Desert for more than 20 years.

Fergany makes his home in Nazlet El Samman, a 7,000 year old village which built at the feet of the Sphinx.

Q:   What's included in the cost of my tour package? 
A:  The cost of your ten-day package includes:  

  •      Round-tip airfare between New York and Cairo
  •      Group-based ground transportation
  •      Taxes
  •      Five-star hotel accommodations(double-occupancy) 
  •      Group meals and gratuities (drinks not included)
  •      Horse, tent and mat rentals
  •      Support crew services
  •      Guide, Chef and Egyptologist
  •      Site entrance fees
  •      Group entertainment
  •      Private entrance into the Great Pyramid
  •      A guided tour of the Egyptian Museum
  •      Walking tours of Old Cairo
  •      A traditional Egyptian folklore show
  •      Shopping excursions and the Khan El Khalili
  •      Bazaar 
  •      A Nile River dinner cruise

Q:  What's not included?  
A:  The following:    

  • Visa and passport fees 
  • Transportation to and from our New York departure point
  • Gratuities and transportation for services provided on a personal basis
  • Items of a personal nature such as telephone calls, faxes, laundry, room service, meals and/or snacks not eaten with the group, viewer-paid TV programs, etc.
  • Two evening meals
  • Drinks with meals
  • Bottled drinking water 

Q: What is the riding portion of the horse safari package like? 
A:  Riders often describe their trek through the desert as an "exhilarating," "meditative" or "grounding" experience.  Because support vehicles carry the group's equipment and gear, riders travel lightly on wide-open terrain.  Be prepared to spend 4-6 hours a day in the saddle (English).  This time will be broken up by stops at historical sites and for meals. 

Q:  How much riding experience do I need? 
A:  Some riding experience and knowledge of basic skills are helpful.  The most important requirement, however, is that you feel comfortable on and around horses and that you are capable of riding all day. 

Because riders are grouped according to their ability level,  this tour is appropriate for both the 'seasoned' and the 'sometimes' equestrian. 

If you have concerns, or if you do not know how to ride but would like to learn how, please contact us regarding our learn to ride package add-on. 

Q:  What are the Egyptian people like? 
A:  In general, you will find them to be kind, generous and exceptionally good-natured. As a result, they are often described as Egypt's greatest treasure.

Q:  What's the weather like? 
A:  Weather in the desert from October through the latter part of November is mild with temperatures ranging from the mid to upper 70's during the day to the mid to lower 60's at night. Expect the temperatures to hover near the mid 60's during the day throughout most of the winter with evening temperatures averaging in the mid 50's. Typically, the air is fairly dry and the the majority of days are bright and sunny. Occasional strong winds can blow, especially during the winter and spring months. In the event of severe winds, support vehicles will be available to transport riders to safety. 

Q:  What will we eat? 
A:  You will eat traditional Egyptian meals which are simple and natural.  Preparation will be, for the most part, 'Middle Eastern' with breads, cooked beans, rice, eggs, hummus, falafel, cheeses and beef or chicken kabobs (occasionally farm-raised camel) being the staples of  your diet-- especially during the camping portion of the trip.  In restaurants, your options will be wider.  You should remember, however, not to eat any uncooked vegetables or fruits which nave not been or cannot be peeled.   

Q:  Will we be safe in the desert at night? 
A:  While in the desert, your personal security is assured by members of the Egyptian Tourist Police who will be accompanying and attending to you at all times, both day and night.   

For additional information on personal safety issues, please contact the U.S. Department of State Overseas Citizen Services at : #202.467.5525.      

Q:  Is there an age limit? 
A:  We have accommodated riders from the ages of 10-79. Reservations are accepted on a case by case basis according to riding abililty and experience.
