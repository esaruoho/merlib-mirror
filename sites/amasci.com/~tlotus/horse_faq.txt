 Q: What is the riding portion of 
 the horse safari package like? 
 A: Riders often describe their trek through the 
 desert as an "exhilarating," "meditative" or "grounding" experience. 
 Because support vehicles carry the group's equipment and gear, 
 riders travel lightly on wide-open terrain. Be prepared 
 to spend 4-6 hours a day in the saddle (English). This 
 time will be broken up by stops at historical sites and for 
 meals. 
 Q: How much riding experience 
 do I need? 
 A: Some riding experience and knowledge of basic 
 skills are helpful. The most important requirement, 
 however, is that you feel comfortable on and around horses 
 and that you are capable of riding all day. 
 Because riders are grouped according 
 to their ability level, this tour is appropriate for 
 both the 'seasoned' and the 'sometimes' equestrian. 

 If you have concerns, or if you 
 do not know how to ride but would like to learn how, please 
 contact us regarding our learn to ride package add-on. 

 Q: What are the Egyptian people 
 like? 
 A: In general, you will find them to be kind, 
 generous and exceptionally good-natured. As a result, 
 they are often described as Egypt's greatest treasure. 
 Q: What's the weather like? 

 A: Weather in the desert from October through the latter 
 part of November is mild with temperatures ranging from the 
 mid to upper 70's during the day to the mid to lower 60's 
 at night. Expect the temperatures to hover near the mid 60's 
 during the day throughout most of the winter with evening 
 temperatures averaging in the mid 50's. Typically, the 
 air is fairly dry and the the majority of days are bright 
 and sunny. Occasional strong winds can blow, especially 
 during the winter and spring months. In the event of severe 
 winds, support vehicles will be available to transport riders 
 to safety. 
 Q: What will we eat? 

 A: You will eat traditional Egyptian meals 
 which are simple and natural. Preparation will be, for 
 the most part, 'Middle Eastern' with breads, cooked beans, 
 rice, eggs, hummus, falafel, cheeses and beef or chicken kabobs 
 (occasionally farm-raised camel) being the staples of 
 your diet-- especially during the camping portion of the trip. 
 In restaurants, your options will be wider. You should 
 remember, however, not to eat any uncooked vegetables or fruits 
 which nave not been or cannot be peeled. 

 Q: Will we be safe in the 
 desert at night? 
 A: While in the desert, your personal security 
 is assured by members of the Egyptian Tourist Police who will 
 be accompanying and attending to you at all times, both day 
 and night. 
 For additional information on personal 
 safety issues, please contact the U.S. Department of State 
 Overseas Citizen Services at : #202.467.5525. 

 Q: Is there an 
 age limit? 
 A: We have accommodated riders from the ages 
 of 10-79. Reservations are accepted on a case by case 
 basis according to riding abililty and experience.
