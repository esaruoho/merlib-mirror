Preface

Founded in 1990, The Evergreen State Society works to strengthen healthy communities by fostering nonprofits and informal organizations.

The Evergreen Awards call on the members and friends of the Society to nominate individuals and organizations who have made outstanding contributions to the communities of Washington state. On Washington's Birthday each year, the nominees are honored at a reception and the ten Washingtonian Award recipients (selected by a panel of past nominees) are recognized for what they have done. Washingtonian Award receipients are invited to name a Washington charitable organization to receive a small donation from The Evergreen State Society in their honor.

Information for Nonprofits is a comprehensive website organized as a resource for people who work in, support, or care about nonprofit organizations. It offers practical tips for starting and managing nonprofit organizations, discussion of legal, political and theoretical issues affecting nonprofits and their work, and directories of other resources. The address on the World-Wide Web is  http://www.nonprofit-info.org/ . Information for Nonprofits is supported in part by a grant from Chevron.

Fronts Steps is America's neighborhood newsletter, a quarterly four-page compilation of ideas and information for people who want to be good neighbors and build good neighborhoods for themselves and their communities. It is distributed to over 40,000 households by more than 50 partner organizations in 40 states. Front Steps is supported in part the by SAFECO Corporation. The text of back issues of Front Steps can be read at  http://www.tess.org/

Civil Society in Everyday Life is a monthly breakfast discussion offered in cooperation with the Center for Ethical Leadership and the Institute of Public Service at Seattle University. Participants engage in continuing exploration of ways to build strong communities institutions through everyday acts of engagement and imagination. The breakfasts are open to the public and take place on the second Tuesday of each month.

The Society maintains a positive working relationship with the agencies of Washington state government whose activities affect the climate for nonprofit organizations. Frequent discussions are held to streamline and strengthen their policies and procedures. A listing of these agencies is available from the Society on request and may be accessed at  http://www.tess.org/misc/gov2.htm

1998 Evergreen Award Nominations

Achievement Rewards for College Scientists Seattle Chapter

In recognition of continuing support for excellence in science education at the University of Washington.
Achievement Rewards for College Scientists was begun following the launch of Sputnik to provide fellowship support to deserving students in the natural sciences, engineering and medicine.

The Seattle Chapter raises funds to help the University of Washington recruit outstanding students in the sciences and engineering.  Each Fellow receives $5000 per year for three years, which, when combined with a teaching or research assistantship, strengthens the University's recruiting offers in comparison with those of peer institutions. Currently 84 students are receiving this support. Since 1978, more than $2,370,000 has been awarded to 298 students. A large number of these students have remained in the Seattle area after completing their education, thereby increasing the pool of highly educated workers in this community.

Achievement Rewards for College Scientists, Seattle Chapter, makes a continuing contribution to the excellence of the University of Washington and to the vibrant economy of the Seattle area.
 
W John W. Creighton, Jr. Philanthropist and Leader

In recognition of a lifetime of service to community projects and institutions.

Jack Creighton, the retiring Chief Executive Officer of The Weyerhaeuser Company, has done an enormous amount with and for the nonprofit and educational communities throughout his life.  Nationally, he is a past-president of the Boy Scouts of America.  Locally, he has chaired a campaign to raise money for the Mt. Zion Academy. Through his work with the University of Washington Press, he has raised money to establish its most prestigious endowed series of publications.  Jack and his wife, Jan, have endowed scholarships for Native Americans at Washington State University.

Jack Creighton works quietly and modestly. There are undoubtedly many other deserving accomplishments known only to a few whose lives have been brightened and whose communities have been strengthened by his generosity and energies.

W Dennis and Sandy Evans Leaders, Volunteers for Outdoor Washington

In recognition of their efforts to build community and the Iron Goat Trail.

Dennis and Sandy Evans have together set a record of 271 "twelve hour" volunteer days during the past seven years working on the Iron Goat Trail as members of Volunteers for Outdoor Washington. This section of the Great Northern Railroad grade at Stevens Pass was constructed in the 1890s and abandoned in 1929.  In partnership with the U.S. Forest Service and other organizations, Volunteers for Outdoor Washington is converting the abandoned right-of-way into a barrier-free hiking trail to be enjoyed indefinitely.

Dennis and Sandy Evans came to this project as individuals, fell in love with the trail and with each other.  After an unusual email and work-party courtship, they were married in a ceremony on the Iron Goat Trail and each year celebrate their anniversary with a trail building work party. They have served as hosts at the nine-day "Volunteer Vacation" work party on the trail. Dennis Evans has used his carpentry skills for the construction of the many large trail bridges and stairways while Sandy Evans coordinated the retreat where the Volunteer Trailwork Coalition was formed to bring together all trail users and land management agencies. Dennis and Sandy Evans were elected to the Board of Directors for Volunteers for Outdoor Washington in 1994 and have served for four years.

By their commitment to sustaining the trail resources of the state of Washington, Dennis and Sandy Evans contribute to the appreciation and understanding of its natural wonders and to the opportunities future generations will have to enjoy life in the Evergreen State.
 
William Forte Volunteer, The Chelan County Museum

In recognition of enterprise in preserving the heritage of railroading.

After retiring from a career in railroading, William Forte returned to the home of his youth in Cashmere only to face life-threatening illness and a near-miss encounter between his personal automobile and an errant crane. During his recuperation, he focused on the maintenance of the Chelan County Museum's old-time railcars. The result is clearly visible in his improved health, as much as in the restored wooden structures and authentic coat of paint on the Museum's Great Northern caboose and steel-sided Burlington Northern passenger coach. Further evidence of his restored energy and continuing service can be seen in the work he has done on the Railway Express platform cart and other rail equipment in the Museum's collection.

William Forte's community service is not something he has come to late in life, though. Before leaving Cashmere at the start of his career, he was the organizer of the town's first ever Pioneer Days parade.

By drawing on his skills to hasten his recovery and for the betterment of the community, William Forte has enhanced the value of the railroad collection of the Chelan County Museum for all to see.

W Helen Freeman Founder, International Snow Leopard Trust

In recognition of worldwide partnerships for wildlife conservation and understanding.

Helen Freeman was the first Curator of Education at Woodland Park Zoo.  Nearly 20 years ago she developed a strong interest in the endangered snow leopard whose habitat is remote high-altitude areas of 12 countries in Asia and what was then the Soviet Union.  In 1981, she founded the International Snow Leopard Trust, a Seattle-based organization which has built partnerships in the snow leopard countries with scientists, governments, local nonprofit organizations, and indigenous peoples.

At the heart of her strategy was finding innovative, low-budget solutions to create "win-win" outcomes benefitting the snow leopard, its fragile environment, and the people who share that environment.  As a result of her energy, persistence and imagination, the Trust has grown, generated new science, sponsored more than 90 grassroots projects, educated people both in Seattle and in Asia on conservation issues, enhanced the image of Seattle as a leading center of conservation activities, gained support from around the world, and built an important and still-growing network of significant new relationships between the Pacific Northwest, the wildlife conservation community, and the snow leopard countries.

The Trust today is a major effort engaging people around the globe in conservation and development, but it would not exist had not one person Helen Freeman seen a problem and set out to solve it.
 
W Makda Gebremedhin Student and Community Leader

In recognition of commitment to community in her new land.

Makda Gebremedhin grew up in northeast Africa weathering terror.  After coming to America at age 13, she experienced anxiety in her American neighborhood with crime, violence, gangs and drugs an environment she abhorred.  Fortunately, Makda stood tall in the face of it all.  She committed herself to doing something about her neighborhood's problems. She pitched in to the High Point UNITY Project renovating the local YMCA and founded The Future Is Ours Club for kids.

As her work was recognized, her fame grew and she won numerous awards.  But she did not rest.  Makda Gebremedhin continued her leadership with 16 additional organizations all of which express her goals and ideals for the common good of humankind.  Among the groups benefitting  from her energies are Cascadia Quest, the Seattle Youth Involvement Network, Mothers Against Violence in America, Every Kid Counts and the 1993 Hazard Free Home Program.

In June 1995, only four years after arriving in this country, and the youngest of hundreds of graduating seniors in her high school, Makda was selected its "outstanding graduate."  Today, an honored pre-med  student at the University of Washington, she continues her community service.

Charlie Jenner, a 1997 Evergreen Award Winner, writes about Makda Gebremedhin, "Makda changes lives.  She changed mine.  Makda is my mentorand only 19 years old!"
 
W Dr. Bill Grace Founder and Director, The Center for Ethical Leadership

In recognition of leadership in the development of leadership.

The Center for Ethical Leadership, under the leadership of founder Dr. Bill Grace, has served the Seattle community for seven years by providing free or low-cost training in ethical leadership, civic responsibility, and collaborative problem solving.
In this time, the Center brought diverse people together to identify and resolve the frustrating and complex social problems of our day. In its work, the Center encourages people to understand their core values and to call on these values in serving the common good via community service projects.

The Center maintains a commitment to youth and people of underserved neighborhoods to help them find a voice in the work of improving Washington communities.  It courageously leads the community toward social justice and sustainability.  Since its inception, the Center has reached more than 1,000 adults and 300 youth, has inspired more than 50 Citizen Action Teams contributing more than 6,000 hours of service to neighborhoods in need.  Through its programs, it has touched the hearts and lives of businessmen and women, employees in government agencies, high school sophomores and juniors who had not yet had a positive role model, religious leaders, and neighborhood activists.

The greater Seattle region is fortunate to have an organization such as the Center for Ethical Leadership focused on calling its citizens to the common good.  The region is also fortunate to have a service-minded and visionary leader such as Bill Grace carrying on his passion for transformational leadership so convincingly and unswervingly.

Reed and Denise Jarvis Klondike Gold Rush Centennial Celebration

In recognition of commitment to community vitality and historical understanding.

On July 17, 1897, the SS Portland arrived in Seattle carrying 68 miners from the Yukon with wooden boxes filled with gold, nearly two tons in all.  The Klondike Gold Rush was on. Seattle would never be the same.

During 1995 Reed and Denise Jarvis started organizing a centennial celebration for July 1997, to celebrate 100 years since the gold arrived in Seattle from the Yukon. As a retired National Park Service historian, Reed Jarvis had a commitment to public history as well as a personal interest in the changes that happened in Seattle as a result of the gold rush.

During the following year the Jarvises organized a board of directors of 24 people to help them create a centennial celebration.  These two leaders worked from their home to involve people in Alaska, the Yukon, and the state of Washington, with the goal of including in the celebration descendants of gold rush miners, businesses still in existence that had been active in 1897, and historians who could add to our understanding of this dramatic event of lasting influence on the city and region. In addition, Reed Jarvis gave dozens of presentations and slide shows to communities, schools, and service clubs in order to bring to the community an understanding of this significant event of Seattle history.

To contribute to the centennial observances, there were also museum exhibits portraying the Klondike Gold Rush at the Washington State Historical Society Museum, the Whatcom Museum of History and Art, the University of Washington Library, and the Museum of History and Industry in Seattle.

The hard work, devotion, and persistence of this couple resulted in the striking Seattle waterfront celebration on the weekend of July 19-20, 1997 and widespread attention to these long-ago events of such lasting importance to Seattle and the Pacific Northwest.

W Mary Kerr Founding Member, Northwest Children's Fund

In recognition of many years of commitment to safe and healthy lives for children.

Mary Kerr is a founding member of the Northwest Children's Fund that has been in existence for 14 years.  In that time, through Mary's tireless leadership, the Children's Fund has raised close to $2 million which has been distributed to agencies serving abused and neglected children primarily in central Puget Sound region.  She is the glue that holds the organization together and the reason why so many children at risk have been helped.

With many other volunteers, Mary Kerr has built the Northwest Children's Fund into an enduring resource for our region's at-risk children.

W Martha Lehman and Mavis Hogan Co-Chairs, S.A.F.E. Place Committee

In recognition of effective advocacy for high standards in child care.

The S.A.F.E. Place committee is made up of 18 dedicated individuals who share the goal of educating the public about the benefits of licensed childcare facilities. The committee works to enlighten the community about the importance and value of following the regulations, performing background checks, serving nutritious meals, and all the other elements that go into operating a safe and secure childcare facility. As a nonprofit, the group is very creative and imaginative in the use of resources and the best way to draw on its members' energies.  The time spent and the dedication of the committee members demonstrates their generosity of spirit in their community life.

As co-chairs of the S.A.F.E. Place Committee, Martha Lehman and Mavis Hogan have demonstrated superior leadership skills in addressing a topic that is both urgent and controversial.
 
Stephanie Lile Curator, Washington State Historical Museum

In recognition of creative leadership in heritage education.

In 1997 the Washington State Historical Society published A Field Guide to the Washington State History Museum, written and designed by Stephanie Lile.  Stephanie Lile is curator of museum education at the Museum and currently project coordinator for an electronic version of the Field Guide in the form of a Children's History Lab.

An insightful, colorful and helpful guide for teachers to the permanent exhibit at the Museum, the Field Guide has captivated teachers and educational administrators, aiding them in bringing the full range of Washington's history to audiences of every age.
 
Mcfarland Richards and Graf  Advertising in the Public Interest

In recognition of commitment to community service.

The Seattle advertising agency Mcfarland Richards and Graf often turns its creative talents and technical skills on a pro bono basis to the task of generating public interest and support for the work of nonprofit organizations. In recent years, the NW AIDS Foundation, the Juvenile Diabetes Association, Zion Preparatory Academy, the YMCA, Assumption St. Bridget School, and Providence Medical Center's Mt. W Dr. Carol Minugh Educator and Organizer

In recognition of contributions to education for underserved communities.

Dr. Carol Minugh founded The Evergreen State College's Reservation-Based/Community- Determined BA program in 1988. Working in partnership with the Makah, Quinault, Skokomish and Port Gamble S'Klallam Tribes, it allows The Evergreen State College to provide college education to tribal members on reservation sites.  The course of study is determined by each tribal community, in cooperation with program students and faculty.  In the past three years alone, the program has enrolled 86 students and graduated 28, most of whom continued by filling important jobs on their reservations.  Last year, the program passed a key milestone with the addition of a part-time community-based faculty member at each of the reservation sites.

In 1997 Dr. Minugh returned to campus to organize a partnership with Maple Lane School, a Washington state institution for juvenile offenders.  Under her leadership and guidance, Evergreen students tutor and mentor Maple Lane inmates.  This education experience benefits the students through meaningful community service, and benefits Maple Lane residents by providing inspiring opportunities to learn. The result is a program which can bridge the way to college  for incarcerated youth and provide them with college credits.

By extending the benefits of education to underserved communities,  Dr. Minugh has shown throughout her career an inspiring belief in the rights,  and the abilities, of all Washingtonians.

Jacki Pearce-Droge Creator of the Community Involvement and Action Center

In recognition of contributions to the community through college preparation for citizenship.

Jacki Pearce-Droge began in 1988 to develop and coordinate the Community Involvement and Action Center at the University of Puget Sound.  The Center has proved so successful that it has become a regular part of the University's programs; she now works full-time as its manager. Some college educators call what this program does "service-learning." At the University of Puget Sound, it deserves the name "preparation for citizenship," a timeless and fundamental goal of a liberal arts education.

The Center's  program today is extensive.  Because of Jacki Pearce-Droge's efforts, fully 70 percent of University of Puget Sound students are now actively serving the community as volunteers working with more than 160 community agencies.  This is a remarkable accomplishment, and it is all the result of an inspiring genius for cultivating and matching the talents of students, and their desire to serve, with the real needs of a not-so-"academic" world.

In the "Kids Can Do!" program, University of Puget Sound student volunteers work as tutors and mentors of "at-risk" children between the ages of seven and 14 in Tacoma's Hilltop neighborhood. The energy they bring to the task inspires community pride, creativity, generosity of spirit, and constructive leadership. In the Student Literacy Corps, the University works in partnership with Tacoma Community House to provide student tutors in the areas of adult literacy and English as a second language.

It is impossible to put a dollar value on the lives that these various programs save from a cycle of poverty, drug abuse, crime, and despair.  Such programs, above all, provide hope to troubled children, a chance to succeed and make something of themselves, to become high school and even college graduates, to contribute in their turn to producing future generations of citizens.

Providence General Children's Association Community Benefactors

In recognition of contributions to the parks and the people of Everett.

The Providence General Children's Association is a totally volunteer organization, founded in 1947 to support child welfare.  Since that time, it has steadily and quietly made contributions that have directly affected the lives of thousands of children.  In 1997, the Association donated $25,000 to the Everett Parks Foundation to build a new playground at Forest Park, the venue for the summer day camp for children with disabilities initiated by the Association in 1997. The Association did not stop with a financial contribution, they pitched in with volunteer help to assemble the equipment.  Their donation matched the grant given by Kimberly-Clark for park improvements; as such it set a new standard in Everett for partnership between a corporation and a service organization.  Because of their gift, the Friends of Everett Parks Foundation was able to purchase new playground equipment at Forest Park in time for a July 14 opening, to the enduring delight of thousands of children.

This spirit of comprehensive community service ensures a brighter future for children with disabilities and for everyone who enjoys Everett's parks.

W Seattle Tilth Carl Elliott, Gardening Coordinator

In recognition of leadership in urban ecology.

1998 is the 20th anniversary of Seattle Tilth. "Tilth" is an English word used to describe the structure and quality of soil. In medieval monastic traditions, the word was used to describe the cultivation of wisdom and spirit. Seattle Tilth is a member of the Tilth Association of Washington, a statewide network of gardeners and farmers promoting sustainable agriculture.

A nonprofit membership association, Seattle Tilth is devoted to educating people about organic gardening and urban ecology. This group provides essential and accurate information to help Seattleites improve the environment and build communities through gardening.

Seattle Tilth's urban agriculture center in Wallingford includes a year-round demonstration garden, composting displays, and a children's garden. Tilth sponsors regular workshops for children and adults, the Edible Plant Sale, and the Tilth Organic Harvest Fair. Tilth started the Master Composter Program, training home gardeners in all aspects of composting including the use of worm bins for food-waste recycling.

Seattle Tilth is an invaluable resource for all who live in the greater Seattle area . . . and beyond.

W Gene Sharratt North Central Washington Education Leader

In recognition of leadership in the community and in education.

As Director of the North Central Washington Educational Service District, Dr. Gene Sharratt travels widely through Washington state facilitating effective educational discussions and inspiring audiences with his vision of schools for their communities. Washington State University's College of Education named him "Distinguished Practitioner" in 1997.

In his hometown of Wenatchee, he has led civic initiatives, fund-raising campaigns, and worthy projects benefitting every part of the community. With his wife Carol, he co-chaired the Festival of Trees to raise money for the new civic center and he led the committee that strung lights on the old Columbia River bridge linking Wenatchee and East Wenatchee. He has chaired the Chamber of Commerce, organized several "Make a Difference Days," and leads a Distinguished Lecturer Series to bring outstanding thinkers and speakers to local audiences.

As an individual and a leader, Gene Sharratt has given his skills generously toward building healthy communities throughout our state.

Toppenish Historical Society, Publications Committee Edith Foster and Lowell Evans, Co-chairs

In recognition of leadership for recording the community's heritage.

For the past six years, the Toppenish Historical Society has been working on the publication of a book, Toppenish from Sagebrush to 1997.  In December of 1997, the efforts came to fruition. Co-chairs Edith Foster and Lowell Evans guided the project to this successful conclusion.

It is more than a history of influential people, those who rose "above and beyond the call of duty." This book chronicles the day-to-day lives of people in every walk of life, those who took the reins to etch the city out of the sagebrush, as well as those who quietly eked out a living in the sometimes harsh environment.

The project was no small task.  First, funds had to be raised through a variety of projects drawing on committee members' skills and the traditions of the community. Once the necessary money was in hand, the job of soliciting stories from families started in earnest.  Many phone calls and visits were made.  After receiving the stories, members were up late into the night, researching historical details, and editing and reediting so the narrative flowed well.

The result is a beautiful hardbound book that has fostered pride in the city. It is 322 pages long and includes 174 pictures. One woman, after doing research for her family, said she was instilled with pride in the accomplishments of her ancestors, of which she was previously unaware.  She has continued research, traveling several times out of town to check official records and to visit sites that are significant in her family's history.

The book includes stores from people of all ethnic backgrounds reflecting the diverse cultural mix of Toppenish.  Hispanic, Native American, African American, and other ethnic and national traditions are represented in the book.

Linda and Scot Whitney Organizers of Olympia's Harlequin Players

In recognition of commitment to preserving the public spaces for community life.

Linda and Scot Whitney, who operate a successful graphic design business in Olympia, have been leaders in the South Sound arts community for many years: Scot as a producer of animated films and winner of stage direction honors at the 1990 Seattle Directors' Festival; Linda directing plays for various drama groups.  A few years ago they reorganized Harlequin Players, an extremely successful venture in presenting alternative forms of drama.

In the past year, the Whitneys and Harlequin have raised funds to purchase Olympia's downtown Tri-State Cinemas building, which had been closed by its former owner.  The Whitneys are working now toward renovation of the building in the summer; they have applied for and received grants and organized successful benefits including "Love Letters and Sweet Notes" on Valentine's Day.

The Harlequin building will give theatre groups another venue for mounting productions in Olympia and contribute further to a vital environment for the performing arts in Washington's capital city.
