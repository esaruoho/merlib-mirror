WASHINGTON STATE NOW Legislative Update

February 27, 1998

 

http://www.eskimo.com/~feminist/wastnow.html

 

Priority Message Hotline is 1-800-562-600

Call all members and ask them to OPPOSE SB 6290 and HB 2395.

Message: Dont endanger womens health and safety!"

 

CHOICE:

  • SB6290 Parental notification for abortion. Position: Oppose. Status: Passed to House Rules 2/26. Action: Call all House to oppose.

 

  • HB 2395 Abortion procedures ban bill. Position: Oppose. Status: Pased Senate Law and Justice 2/25 with referendum clause. Action: Call Senators to oppose. Message: We believ e that women, when faced with having to make a decision regarding their own health, those decisions are made by the women and her own doctor. This bill would limit certain medical procedures used in abortions.

 

AFFIRMATIVE ACTION:

 

  • HB 3010 No! 200 Alternative. Position: Support. Action: Call all House members.

 

  • HB 3011 Phases out gender and race and replaces them with economic based Affirmative Action. Position: Oppose. Action: Call House members.

 

 

SECOND PRIORITY ISSUES

 

  1. WELFARE:
  • 6331 - would have eliminated child only cases for teens on welfare not in approved living arrangements. Position: oppose. Status: died in Senate Health Committee 2/6/98.
  • 2589 - original bill would have eliminated good cause exemption for disclosing parents name to qualify for welfare. The substitute version remedies that but leaves out language protecting issues around adoption. Position: Oppose w /out more protections. Status: Senate Hearing 2/20.
  • 2900 - pro rata reduction in welfare grants for each day missed under Workfirst plan requirements. Position: monitor. Study only. Status: Senate Hearing 2/24.
  • 2901 - Would limit the job search component of Workfirst to 12 weeks total. Assessment would have to come sooner. Position: support assessment and some limitations on job search but need more flexible language to insure recipients aren't forced into community service jobs with no chance at training. Position: Monitor. Status: Senate hearing 2/24.

 

 

LESBIAN/GAY RIGHTS:

  • 1130 - banning same sex marriages. passes House and Senate 2-6-98. Governor vetoed same day and the House and Senate voted to override the Governors veto. It is now law.
  • 5400 - same topic, dead due to action taken above.
  • 2586 - would prohibit domestic partner benefits. Position: opposed. Status: bill failed to be brought up for a vote in House Law and Justice Committee before 2-6-98 cut off. Dead at this time.

 

CHILD SUPPORT:

  • 2406 - would have created a presumption in favor of the parent who supposedly would have been most likely to grant access to the child to the other parent creating a presumption in their favor in custody issues. A parent raising even logical co ncerns such as driving time, concerns about alcohol abuse etc. would have been at risk of being the "unfriendly parent". Position: oppose. Status: DEAD
  • 2407 - Shared parenting. The latest version of mandatory joint custody. Among other things it would have done away with the best interests of the child standard. Position: oppose. Status: failed to move out of House Law and Justice before 2-6-98 cut-off.
  • 5086 - would prohibit courts from granting post-secondary education child support. Position: oppose. Status: DEAD.
  • 6135 - covenant marriage. Couples who entered into such a marriage could only divorce after meeting a list of criteria. (Return to fault divorce). Position: oppose. Status - DEAD.
  • 6193 - fault divorce for all. Position: oppose. Status: Dead.

 

WOMEN' s HEALTH -

  • 5278 - in its original form this bill would have forced chemically dependent pregnant women to have Norplant etc. The new version focuses on making alcohol and drug treatment available, expanding access to birth control and clarifying dependenc y procedures. Position: support. Status: Passed to Appropriations 2/26. Money in Senate budget.
  • 3009 - expanding access to family planning for substance abusing women. Position: support. Status: Failed to be acted on before 2/10 cutoff/DEAD.
  • 3010 - expanding access to family planning for women in prisons/jails and those in treatment. Position: support. Status: Failed to pass out of House Appropriations by 2/10 cutoff/DEAD.
  • 2592 - would direct the Governor to seek a waiver from the Federal Title XIX program to expand Medicaid eligibility for men and women up to 200% of the federal poverty level for birth control. Position: support. Status: Bill dead in Appropriations but we are supporting proviso language in budget..
  • 6616 - would provide money to do screenings for breast and cervical cancer for low income women. Position: support. Status: Dead in Senate Ways and Means. 1 million for this in Senate budget. Hall House Appropriation s to support.

 

VIOLENCE AGAINST WOMEN:

  • 2351 - adds sexual assault victims to the victim address confidentiality program run by the Secretary of State. Position: support. Status: Senate Rules. Call Senate to support.
  • 2350 - would create a statewide registry for sex offenders. Position: support. Status: Hearing in Senate Human Services and Corrections 2/18.
  • 5305 - makes it a crime to use drugs to facilitate rape and criminalizes the possession of certain drugs. Position: support. Status: Passed to House Rules, call House to support.
  • 2532 - federal full faith and credit act. establishes that protection orders from other states and countries will be enforceable in Washington State for victims of domestic violence and sexual assault. Position; support. Sta tus; Dead.
  • 2685 - would establish privileged communication for counselors of domestic violence victims similar to sexual assault victim advocate/counselors. Position: support. Status: Needs to be out of Senate committee by 2/27 .
  • 2910 - clarifies that innocent victims of domestic abuse (battering and child abuse) will not be discriminated against in insurance. Position: support Status: Senate hearing 2/25.

 

 

SENATE BUDGET:

 

Support:

  • 1 million for low income womens health screenings for cervical and breast cancer
  • $250,000 for pilot projects for SB 5278 (see above).
  • $350,000 to fund child care licensing problem notices.

 

Oppose:

  • No money to fill ECEAP slot cuts
  • No money to access Federal Child Health money (we could lose 47 million a year).
  • $92,000 cut to sexual assault programs
