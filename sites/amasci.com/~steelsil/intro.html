Tim Dunn -- Autobiography of the Artist

As a boy of 12, I had the wonderful experience of taking a three month tour of Europe with my grandparents. We went to all the major museums, cathedrals, and architectural monuments. Subsequently, we made a similar tour of Mexico. These trips were the beginning of my interest in art and anthropology.

My first college major was anthropology, but I changed it to psychology when I decided I would starve if I didn't do so. Frustrated by the academic approach to psychology, I dropped out of college. Starting with the nest egg of a small inheritance, I took up stock trading, and made money trading on my own account. I invested the proceeds in bonds, and decided travel. The method I hit upon was to buy a sailboat and cross the seas in it, even though I was a complete landlubber.

After sailing to Hawaii and back to Seattle in my first rather conventional sailboat, a 35 foot sloop, I decided to build my own 34 foot junk-rigged schooner with a pilothouse to get the helmsman out of the rain. I built this boat, the "Batwing," and spent years cruising the remote islands sprinkled across the Pacific ocean.

I developed an interest in yacht design, and took up drawing sailboats' lines. Soon, I was seduced by the beauty of the curves and shapes for their own sake, and I began to draw objects of beauty as well as doing technical drawing of objects of utility.

I returned from my voyages, and completed bachelor's and master's degrees in psychology. I married, and my wife and I opened our own own counseling practice, Green Lake Counseling Center. We also subsequently co-founded a drug and alcohol agency. For years, my main connection with art was as a collector. My wife and I would go to the galleries every month, we would see all the art museum exhibits, and we would focus on museums and galleries when we traveled.

My wife, my partner and I sold the drug and alcohol agency, and I decided to turn my hand to the arts and crafts. I had always had an interest in art and art history, and my travels broadened the knowledge of "primitive" art which I had aquired through the study of anthropology and my chief hobby, art history. Being part Cherokee, I was also interested in exploring the artistic side of my native american heritage.

Thus, my interest in art, nature, anthropology, Native American culture, drawing, and boat building ultimately combined to lead me to found my own metal art & craft workshop. At our workshop, "Steel Silhouettes", my assistants and I make metal art and crafts to my designs featuring Northwest wildlife and our Northwest coast Indian art in a new medium, steel.
