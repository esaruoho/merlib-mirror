The Art Show

The Art Show is one of the longest running convention traditions since the first conventions were held long, long ago, in hotel far, far away. In keeping with that tradition we offer a place for the professional and amateur artist alike to showcase their works of art. And as a part of that tradition, there are some rules and guide lines that go along with it. We are trying to be fair to both professional and amateur alike.

Displaying Your Art Work

Any paid member of the convention may enter artwork into the art show. This means we supply you a place to put your artwork on display for the rest of the con attendees to come and view it. If you so desire, you may put the art work up for sale. If you don't wish to sell the art, that's fine, come show off your skills anyway.

The art show is open to all kinds of art, both 2D and 3D works are allowed. but we request that the content reflect anime, manga, or Japanese images and/or styles. The art show reserves the right to deny entry of any piece it feels that does not fit the concept of an Japanese anime convention.

There is no limit to the number of pieces that can be entered into the art show, however, because we only have a finite amount of space, we are asking that all artists restrict their works to fit within about a 4'x4' area. The art show staff reserves the right to limit the number of pieces per artist if hanging space becomes extremely limited. If more space does become available, the extra space will also be on a first come, first serve basis.

If you wish to sell your art through our art auction, you must assign a 'minimum bid' for the piece and then wait to see if anybody bids on the piece. If you do not wish to sell a piece, please notify the art show staff when you enter the piece to the art show, we will mark the piece appropriately as 'Not For Sale'. The art show does NOT charge a flat hanging fee to enter artwork, instead we charge a 10% commission on all sales. So you just wish to come show off your work and not sell anything, it's free!

The Art Show will begin accepting art work no later than Noon on Friday (or earlier if possible). The art show will continue to accept artwork as long as there is still available space or until noon Saturday, which ever comes first.

When you enter your art work into the art show, you much label each piece with the media used to create the piece. This especially holds true for photocopies, prints, and/or computer printed art. Prints from limit editions or numbered print sets will be allowed if the print being hung comes with a certificate of authenticity signed by the artist and/or has the proper number registry on the print. Only one instance of any given piece shall be allowed in the art show, no multiples (as with prints, computer print outs, etc) of the same piece will be allowed.

The art show can not accept art work sent to it by U.S. MAIL or any other parcel carrier. Your art work is safest in transit while it is in your own hands, in the mail ANYTHING can happen, and probably will. Therefore we require all artwork to be delivered by hand to the art show.

If you wish to submit something to the art show but will not be able to attend the convention yourself, or feel that you may arrive too late to enter anything, you may assign an agent over your works. An agent is someone whom you have authorized to work on your behalf during your absence. The person who you choose to be your agent must be a paid member of the convention, and must deliver a signed letter of authorization from you to the Art Show proving that you have given your consent for their help. An agent will then be able to enter, remove, and collect any monies from the art show for you.

Q: Can a single person act as an agent to more than one artist at the same time?

A: Yes. As long as the person has the appropriate authorization letters, they may act as agent for as many artists as they wish.

All two-dimensional artwork *MUST* be framed, backed or matted by the time the art work is presented for entry to the art show. Other than minimal hanging supplies (hooks, clips, etc), the art show will not be able to supply frames, backings or mattes. By framing, backing, or matting your work, it will have a stronger appeal to the viewer, and will be safer while hanging in the art show.

All three-dimensional works such as models and resin figures should be solidly mounted on a pedestal, base or stand to keep them from falling over and being damaged. The Art Show staff will provide the best security possible for all the artists in the show, but they cannot be held responsible for damages due to improper supports under the pieces. Also, by placing your work on a stand or base, it has a lot more appeal to the viewer than if it just sets on the top of a table.

The art show will accept adult themed pieces. 'Hentai' or material that could considered too "adult" for the general public will be hung in a special area closed off from the common viewing area. The Art Show staff has the final word on if a piece should be in the "Hentai Section" or not. The Art Show staff also reserves the right to refuse any art work into the art show if it is considered far beyond the boundaries of good taste. All artists whose works are to be placed in the 'Hentai' must be 18 or over.

Pick-up of artwork and artist checkout may begin one hour (approx. 2pm) after the art auction begins on Sunday afternoon. If you need to leave the convention earlier, arrangement scan be made for someone to act as your agent to pick up your artwork and/or payment.

The art show claims no right to the reproductive rights of any art work displayed in the art show. All rights remain with the artist. To this effect, no cameras or video recorders are allowed into the art show.

The Art Auction

If a piece of artwork catches your eye, and is for sale, you can try and purchase it through our art auction. Each piece of art will have a piece of paper attached to it called a 'bid sheet' which tells the name of the artist, name of the piece, the media used to create the piece, the minimum bid for the piece, and a couple of blank lines underneath. Those pieces that are not for sale will have a large 'NFS' (not for sale) marked through the bid lines and will NOT have a minimum bid listed.

Until the art show closes at noon Sunday, all con attendees have the chance to come in and peruse the artwork on display and if they choose, place bids on certain pieces. To place a bid, all you need to do is mark on the bid sheet (in an AVAILABLE bid space) your name, your badge number, and your bid. All bids must be a minimum of one dollar above the previous high bid and only in whole dollar amounts. If anybody does enter a bid of other than whole dollars, the art show may correct their mistake by rounding UP to the next whole dollar. Any piece of art work that receives three bids during normal viewing hours is automatically held over for our live auction on Sunday at 1pm.

To avoid the problem of last minute bid jumpers, any bids that are received after 11:30 on Sunday automatically send that piece to the live auction at 1pm on Sunday (regardless of the actual number of bids on the piece).

At 1pm on Sunday, the live auction starts (check the program guide for the correct room). The live auction is a classic auction where you have to be present to continue to bid. An auctioneer will continue the auction for each piece by starting the biding at the last marked bid.

Q: How do I know if the piece I was bidding on went to auction?

A: Best thing to do is try to check the status of the piece between 11:30 and noon on Sunday when the piece is still hanging in the art show. It's usually pretty obvious at that point if the piece is going to auction or not. If you couldn't make it back to the art show before the art show closed, either go to the auction or check after the auction finishes. Usually the art show staff doesn't know all the pieces that are going to the auction off the top of their heads, and by Sunday morning are rather busy getting ready for the auction to check for you.

Paying For the Artwork

Once you are established as the high bidder on a piece of art work, you can pay for your piece(s) starting at 1pm on Sunday. You may pay by cash, personal check, or traveler's checks. We do NOT accept credit cards. No one may pay for artwork before 1pm on Sunday.

Artist Checkout : Claiming Your Unsold Artwork and Collecting Payment

If you are an artist (or artist's agent) and want to pick up your unsold pieces of art, you will be able to do so starting at 2pm on Sunday. *IF* you have to leave early on Sunday, please talk to the art show staff as early as you can in the weekend and make arrangements. If you can wait until 2pm, it would be greatly appreciated, as the art show staff really are quite busy from about 11pm through the end of the auction, and it would be a great help to them.

The art show tries to pay cash to all artists as they leave, but this may not be possible. If we can not pay you cash we will mail you a check, post-marked no later than one week after the close of the convention. So please make sure that your address information you supply to us is correct!

Remember the art show does take a 10% commission of all sales in the art show.

Any artwork that is left behind after the art show closes will become property of the art show. The art show will try to return it to the artist to the best of it's ability, but it makes no guarantees about being able to do so.

Q: I brought in my artwork, but I'm leaving early. Can I have somebody else pick up my unsold artwork and any payment I may receive?

A: Yes. All you need to do is write up a letter naming and authorizing some particular person who will be acting as your agent for pick up of unsold artwork and payment (if any). Then deliver this letter to the art room staff as soon as you can and we will happily deal with your agent on Sunday at pick up!
