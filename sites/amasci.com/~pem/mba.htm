MBA miscellany

| top of page | | rankings | MBA program info links | MBA miscellany | gmat | paul's place home |


  • the MBA was invented in the US about 100 years ago
    | MBA History (Economist)|

  • in 1996-97 there were 97,619 MBA degrees awarded in the US, and many more worldwide
    | MBAs awarded (US) 1967-1997 |

  • the business of educating MBA students is a multi-million dollar industry

  • much of what is written about the MBA is based on the top ten MBA programs (| Harvard | | Stanford |, etc.) in the US, and may not be applicable to MBA programs at other institutions

  • popular books have been written by MBA students detailing their experiences at Harvard and Stanford
    "Snapshots From Hell" by Peter Robinson (Stanford)
    "Year One" by Robert Reid (Harvard)

  • a few similar books, although about engineering and law school, are;
    "One L" by Scott Turow
    His experience in the first year of Harvard Law School

    "The Idea Factory" by Pepper Martin
    Experience of a graduate mechanical engineering student at MIT.


    "Letters from Law School" by Lawrence Dieker Jr
    Second year of Tulane Law School

  • in the US, MBA education is a consumer good, MBAs of all flavors ( quality, cost, flexiblity, etc.) are offered -- one of the biggest MBA providers, the ubiquitous, University of Phoenix has been dubbed "McCollege" because it churns out grads like McDonalds produces hamburgers | business2.0 | Discussion Board re: U of P |

  • quality considerations for prospective MBA students in Canada have been simpler than for their US counterparts, because there are fewer universities, and historically Canadian universities have been fairly uniform in terms of quality, for example in, "The Gourman Report (1987)", ratings of medical schools (programs rated on a 0 to 5 scale (5 being best)) the 16 Canadian medical schools garnered ratings between 4.90 to 4.18, whereas, the 125 US medical schools displayed a much wider range of ratings (almost triple), garnering ratings between 4.94 to 3.02, however, going forward there is bound to be much more variation in educational quality in Canada, with additional universities offering MBAs each year, as well as the increasing globalization of the MBA market, especially through the application of digital technology to deliver education

  • there has been much debate in recent years about the value of an MBA, both in terms of what you learn and what you'll earn, mainly as a result of an article , "The End of Business Schools? Less Success Than Meets the Eye", by Stanford Prof Jeff Pfeffer and grad student Christina Fong
    | Mercury News | | business2 | Academy of Mgmt |

  • in the US, university accreditation is through six major regional organizations, msacs, neasc, nasc, ncacs, sacs, wasc, which provide general accreditation, there is also specialized accreditation available for business schools, meeting higher standards, through the association to advance collegiate schools of business (AACSB), there are approx 2000 schools of business in the US, but only about 450 schools worldwide have earned aacsb accreditation, a list of aacsb accredited schools worldwide (including Canada), is here
