 quality considerations for prospective MBA students in Canada have 
been simpler than for their US counterparts, because there are fewer
universities, and historically Canadian universities have been fairly
uniform in terms of quality, for example in, "The Gourman 
Report 
(1987)", ratings of
medical schools (programs rated on a 0 to 5 scale (5 being best)) the 
16 
Canadian medical schools garnered ratings between
4.90 to 4.18, whereas, the 125 US medical schools displayed a much wider
range of ratings (almost triple), garnering ratings between 
4.94 to 3.02, however, going forward there is bound to be much more 
variation 
in 
educational quality in Canada, with additional universities
offering MBAs each year, as well as the increasing globalization of the
MBA market, especially through the application of 
digital technology to deliver education

 there has been much debate in recent years about the value of an MBA, 
both
in terms of what you learn and what you'll earn, mainly as a result of an 
article
, "The End of Business Schools? Less Success Than Meets the Eye",
by Stanford

Prof Jeff Pfeffer and grad student Christina Fong |

|

 in the US, university accreditation is through
six major regional organizations, msacs, neasc, nasc, ncacs, sacs,
wasc, which provide
general accreditation, there is also specialized accreditation 
available for
business schools, meeting higher standards, through the
association to advance collegiate schools of business (AACSB),
there are approx 2000 schools of business in the US, but only
about 450 schools worldwide have earned aacsb accreditation,
