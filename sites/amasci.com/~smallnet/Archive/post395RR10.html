From Bruce Stough < sbs1@visi.com >
Andy Woitowicz helped me by modifying throttles for a couple of diesel engines, and I would like to report the results.

I have several PAW .19s and enjoy them very much. However, I never liked the idle performance. They seem to run too rich at idle, and respond slowly when the throttle is advanced. Andy opened out the air bleed on one of the throttles from about 1.0mm to about 1.6mm and added an adjustment screw. When correctly adjusted, the engine now responds quickly to throttle changes and shows no tendency to load up at idle.

The second experiment was to add an air bleed hole to a VA throttle (supplied by David Larkin). These were developed for small copies of the Mills engines made by VA, but screw directly into the MP Jet Classic .040 engine. As well as drilling the air bleed hole, Andy added an adjustment screw. At first I had some problems with this carburetor, but this turned out to be caused by the throttle barrel closing first on the air inlet side rather than on the cylinder side. A needle file corrected this, and now the MP Jet Classic (purchased from Carlson Engine Imports) throttles fine. On a wood 7x4 prop it ranges from around 4k to 7.8k rpm, and response is quick. The engine is new and I am sure it will improve with time and further adjustments.

The MP Jet Classic starts very easily and is light. Its power is somewhere between a .020 and a .049 glow, but it has no problem with fairly large props. A great little engine!

Bruce Stough

From "Larry Renger" < larry_renger@earthlink.net >
If you have no access to diesel fuel, get some "engine starting fluid" and mix it 2/3 - 1/3 with either Castor Oil or 70 wt Pennzoil. Not wonderful, but workable for playing around until you can get the real thing. There are sources for getting the stuff shipped to you. My guess is that Eric Clutton and Aerodyne make the best fuel. I have used Red max (reputed mfr. for Eric) and Davis fuels with acceptable results too.
If you can get real, straight ether, then you can mix a pretty acceptable fuel with the 1/3 - 1/3 - 1/3 mix. You may not get the most power, but you won't hurt the engine.

From David Larkin < dslarkin@blvl.igs.net >
John,
The FROG 80 (049) diesel is a very nice little diesel. I have one and really like it. The basic diesel fuel is 1/3 ether, 1/3, castor oil, 1/3 kerosene (not methanol). You can add 2-3% ignition improver if you can get it (amyl nitrate, amyl nitrite, or iso propyl nitrate).

The FROG 80 had an 'O' ring on its contrapiston and it is conceivable that this might have to be replaced before the engine will run.

Diesel fuel can be obtained in the US from Red Max, Eric Clutton, Aerodyne or Davis Diesel.

For the correspondent who was interested in an exhaust ring (manifold) for his PAW 55, these are available from PAW. However I never recommend them as they do absolutely nothing to reduce noise, but they can make the engine harder to start and tune, particularly if the operator is not experienced. They are simply provided to meet a demand, regardless of whetherv it is a sensible idea. There is the argument of 'directing the goo', but in fact when you operate a diesel without a muffler or collector ring, you get a clear exhaust without goo. Only when you add the muffler or collector ring do you get goo. So the device only solves a problem that it itself caused. Despite this, there are many people who absolutely have to fit an exhaust ring!
Best regards,
Dave Larkin

From "Andy Woitowicz" < ajw@ntl.sympatico.ca >
I am compelled to disagree strongly with Eric Clutton's assertion that sub-piston induction hinders good throttling. The original split case VA .049 has plenty of it and it idles and throttles better than any engine I've ever seen in my 39 years of modeling. Very close by is the VA MK2 which also has sub-piston induction. With modifications to the throttle, both the Norvel and WASP .061 throttle extremely well. Both have sub-piston induction. I've installed adjustable airbleed throttles to a number of diesels and throttling improved dramatically. I've done MP Jet Classic diesels, Indian Mills replicas, and most recently a PAW .049/061 for a dedicated diesel fan in Singapore. A good engine and a properly designed, adjustable airbleed throttle, is all that's needed, sub-piston induction or not.

Andy Woitowicz

John Antonen asked about a diesel fuel recipe using Methanol.. I am sure he means Kerosene or the clean burning heater fuel or lamp oil.

Little diesels run and start best when the ether content is around 40-50% Kerosene content should be 25% to 30% Castor oil should be 20-25%

The standard 1/3, 1/3, 1/3 blend will work too and is easy to mix by proportion. I use a 1000 ml beaker to make a batch. Stir it well and pour it onto good tins/cans with a foil lined lid.
