Dave Campbell <  > adds:

Ian McQueen remarked about a "magic" muffler, intended to provide a resonant boost over a range of speeds. He wrote that the MVVS 2.5 (.15) diesel is available with one of these.

"Magic Mufflers" are muffled tuned pipes. They were designed back when the AMA was trying to slow pylon racers down by making full-wave pipes illegal. Most guys back then were running 1/4-wave pipes on their smaller racers (QM .15), so it was a natural to do the same for .40 racers, and stick a muffler around them to meet the sound rules.

There may have been a few sold under the guise of improving power across the rev range, but it's fair to say that they are made only for the purpose of improving top end power. Performance Specialties sells them for O.S. pattern motors (they are basically Nelson mufflers, as the two companies are hand in hand), and Jett Engineering makes them for their motors. That about says it all!

Don Typond <  > sent:

In response to Sam Brauer's comments about static electricity problems with plastic covering films in Posting #423...

Try wiping the film, and the backing paper, with a sheet of laundry fabric softener ("BOUNCE" or equivalent). Even used sheets seem to have enough of the anti-static stuff left in them; save them after drying the laundry.

I've had excellent static-removing results with these sheets, especially on "heavier" films like UltraCote. After removing the backing paper, I wipe both sides of the film, and have never had a problem with bits of debris jumping onto the film and showing up later as lumps under the covering.

When I say "wipe," I mean contact the film LIGHTLY with the fabric softener sheet. You don't want to transfer any fabric softener chemical onto the adhesive. That's why used sheets are better.

This technique may be more difficult to do with thin films like Solarfilm Lite... I've not tried it. Don Typond

Jim Branaum <  > warns:

For those of you building the Pica 1/12 scale Corsair, you will need to avoid rudder flutter.

The rudder control rod comes out the side of the fuselage just in front of the hinge line. If you have sanded the bevel to the rudder on the wrong side, the rudder will not lie flat when you make the initial wrap of Monokote. The thing you MUST do is insure the bevel is on the correct side, as you WILL experience flutter if the wrap-around is not extremely tight.

If the wrap-around has to deal with the control rod carrier, the rudder will NOT lie down flat, and there is not much you can do to avoid flutter.

I got lucky with mine, and heard the rudder begin to flutter before I really hammered it on a WOT down line. Since I am too late to be careful, I now have to cut the Corsair's rudder off and use regular hinges.

Jim Branaum

Does anyone know a source of information for mixing colors from Sig or Brodak dopes? I'd like to know how to get the burnt orange used on the Curtiss Robin and some other planes of the "between wars" period. It was a pretty common color at one time, so I'm hoping there's a formula out there.

Jim Rundle

...Jim, I'd mix the dope "by eye". The only caveat is not to exceed THREE colors in the blend. As your Art department there at Cornell can verify, the more different pigments you employ in trying to match a desired color, the closer you'll approach MUD.

I'd work exclusively with Brodak dope. That (made by Randolph, manufacturers of airplane dope since 1919) uses opaque pigments; Sig dopes employ the translucent pigments that the auto industry prefers, to give a "look of depth" to car finishes.

For a "burnt orange" color, I'd try BP 1013 (Cessna Orange) with just enough BP 1065 (Brown) added to obtain the "burnt" effect you want.

BTW, if you don't have one, John Brodak can supply a color chart of his dope line. Don't bother him until next week, though: he's mighty busy now with the annual Father's Day Control-Line Extravaganza at Brodak's HQ (Carmichaels, Pennsylvania -- not far from Washington, PA, but kind of hard to locate on the narrow 2-lane roads among the hills and dales between Wheeling, West Virginia and Pittsburgh...)
