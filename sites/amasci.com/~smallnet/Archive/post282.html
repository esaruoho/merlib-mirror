Jerry Burk <  > relates:

During some of the war years Frank V. B. Ehling was stationed in Hobbs, New Mexico, my home town. Most of us were in awe of him but he seemed a very nice guy. Shy and retiring I would say. He had a biplane with him with an Elf for power but I never did see it fly.

He would tear his airplanes up hand gliding them! He apparently was a strong believer in the hand glide. The biplane was damaged thusly on the one day he had it out.

He struck a deal with the local "best" modeler to help him with his model designs. Frank would conceive the design, build the basic framework, and have the USAF photo lab take the construction pictures. If pictured, the engine would be glued in. Then the model would come in to my friend and be wired for ignition (usually), covered, and the engine properly installed, etc. Brought up to flight status; then back to the Base for color doping, detailing and more lab photos. Then back to my friend for its test flights, etc.

Two that I specifically remember were a pair called "Small Stuff" and "Full Boost". Both were U-controls. And incidentally, to the best of my knowledge, Frank never did learn to fly U control! At least not in Hobbs. He cracked up every time he tried to fly!

Full Boost was a very long tail moment airplane that was a good flyer and lasted for years. Small Stuff never did fly. It had a Bantam in it that Frank thought he could run on one pen cell and a large Aero coil. It wouldn't run. Finally they attached two batteries and had them hanging out the bottom of the fuselage. Still never made a flight. But when Model Airplane News published the plans it was said to fly with three different engines and two different length lines. I learned about "puffery" from that!

During the intervening years I would always talk to Frank at the Nationals I attended, and would tell him about Hobbs, our friend Joe Brown, etc. It would rather boggle his mind that I had obviously been there -- but he couldn't remember me! But hey, I was a 14-year- old kid. Who ever remembers those?

Take care. Jerry Burk

....Jerry, replicas of Frank Ehling's "Elf Biplane" have shown up at two SMALL meets that I attended, and flew quite well. Of course, we know a lot more about model flying technique now than even the "experts" did in the Good Old Days...

And about "puffery" in the model publications: several designs that I know of had never been flown before publication. Examples: some of Walter Musciano's control-line scale models, including the Curtiss P-1 and P-36; a beautiful towline glider published in AeroModeller long ago, called the Celestial Horseman; and "Winged Lightning", which appeared in MAN and its "Air Age Gas Models" collection.

The magazines have always been hungry for construction projects, and there's seldom been a plentiful supply of well-tested model airplane designs, complete with accurate drawings, good photos, and usable explanatory text. [JW]

....Jack, I gave up on "wheel collars" long ago. They are made from "free machining brass", and there's just NO WAY you can keep a hardened steel allen screw from stripping the brass threads out when you try to tighten the screw enough so that vibration doesn't make it fall off in flight.

Instead, I use brass hex nuts: # 4-40 for 3/32" wire and # 6-32 for 1/8". Drill the nuts out to the exact wire diameter; then tin the axle with 60-40 solder. I install one nut at the inner end of each axle to act as a lateral retainer, soldering it in position.

(The nut is a really snug fit on the wire the way I do it, and some- times needs to be "screwed" onto the tinned steel wire. That way no clamping is needed to make the nut stay put during soldering.)

Last, I install the wheels and solder another drilled-out brass nut to the exterior end of each axle for the retainer. This sort of assembly never fails from vibration; yet is easy to take apart when necessary -- and without heat, too. [JW]

David Owen <  > advises:

re Rich Border's OS.15FP/APC prop problem: The OS.15FP has a 5mm shaft. That is slightly over 3/16" diameter. Buy a length of 1/4" diameter K&S tubing from your local hobby store. Find a friend with a lathe and ask him to run a 5mm drill through, then part off several 3/8" lengths. These will fit the APC props and solve your centering problem.

You cannot thin the prop hub with safety, and there should be no necessity to do so -- unless the shaft has been shortened or you are using a very thick spinner backplate.

I'll make you a propeller bush if you can't sort it out yourself.

Lastly, do heed JW's advice about the dangers of power drilling plastic props! The best way to enlarge a prop center hole is by using a Fox Reamer.

Dave Owen

Gordon McArthur <  > addresses the same topic:

I have solved exactly that problem by making a sleeve from a short piece of brass (K&S) tubing. Find the correct size & cut off a short length by rolling the tube back & forth under a snap-off knife blade. A bit fiddly to do, but you should soon manage it. If necessary clean up the ends with a small file.

<  > asks:

I must come out of lurking to ask, "What is the magic orientation of the spraybar on the throttle of the PAW .049 diesel?" Mine seems a stubborn brute, with a miserable idle.

Paul Forrette <  > also wants to know:

What is the proper position of the spray bar on the PAW 06? How can we set it till we know? Yes, I know you've told us before, but can you tell us again? I forgot. Happy 2000. Paul

....Here's the deal. PAW R/C carb spraybars have two cross holes. These are NOT straight across -- and that's on purpose. Eric Clutton and I have found by much experimentation that the optimum positioning of these fuel orifices is "symmetrical". That is, the cross holes should each be a trifle below center of the intake passage when the throttle is fully open.

In Eric's most recent letter he said "Position the spraybar so that you cannot see either orifice when looking straight down the barrel opening. That seems to be close enough."

One further tip: make CERTAIN that the air bleed hole in the PAW carb body is clear. That does get clogged sometimes, with a detrimental effect on the throttle action.

....As for the "stubborn brute" PAW .049 with its "miserable idle", it seems more probable to me that the trouble lies in insufficient ether in the fuel. Use John Deere brand "Starting Fluid" as an ether replenisher: Eric tells me that it contains almost 80% ether and works better than the less-expensive & more common brands of "starting fluid", with their lower ether content. [JW]
