I've had a bunch of health problems this year, and (thankfully) am over all but the psychological ones I was born with! As a result, I have flown little.

I spent the time designing and building a little cabin biplane (34" top wing span), powered by an .061 P.A.W., FMA receiver and servos, film container fuel tank, covered in Litespan.

Since this plane is very short-coupled and is only 3-channel, it took some time to get "sorted out"; but what a kick after I did!

All in all, even with all the "down" time, I've had more fun with fewer flights, than I've had in 50 years!

Martin Hepperle <  > contributed:

Just a short answer to the MBPEP (multi-blade-prop-equivalence problem):

The power absorption of a propeller is linked to its diameter to the power of 5.

Assuming you have props of the same blade shape you can write: For a 2 bladed prop having diameter D2: P2 = 2*Cp*D2^5 For a 3 bladed prop having diameter D3: P3 = 3*Cp*D3^5 (Cp is a constant depending on the blade shape)

What you want is props of equal power consumption; thus P2 = P3.

Solving for the diameter D3 gives: D3 = D2*(2/3)^1/5 = D2*0.922 Thus a diameter of 90% should be quite close.

For a four bladed prop, you would end up at 87% of the two-bladed prop's diameter.

PS: Power is also related to the rpm to the power of 3 and thrust scales with diameter^4 and rpm^2.

Martin Hepperle

On another topic, I make TE stock for the NJAPF P-30 kits with a jig/fixture/widget (what the heck is the difference between a jig and a fixture, anyhow?) that sits on a drill press table.

In the DP chuck is a 2" diameter sanding drum. For the widget, imagine a 'device' that will support the rectangular stock (horizontally, in a track) that sits at an angle to the vertical, corresponding to the desired shape. This allows feeding the strip into the side of the drum sander -- against the rotating direction -- otherwise the strip would draw in and jam.

In use, the strip is pushed through about 1.5" so that I can grab Its end on the output side and pull it on through evenly. That first 1.5" is often gouged a bit, but the rest of the TE looks pretty good. Mine are just 1/8" X 1/4", but one could make bigger TEs this way; although if they're to be much bigger, they could be shaped in stages.

Because I make 20 trailing edges at a time, I have a shop vacuum hose inlet set near the sanding drum to draw away the dust.

AL
A. A. Lidberg model plan service

...Al, 50+ years ago there was a difference between a jig and a fixture. A fixture held material for shaping operations, while a jig held parts in position for assembly purposes. Nowadays I think the words are used interchangeably.

As for shaping model parts with a drum sander, I've noticed that most of the drum sanders on sale for use in home workshops don't run particularly "true". Their rubber drums are off-center. That makes the sanding action irregular, and limits most of the abrasive action to one local area on the sanding sleeve.

For that reason, I use a different method for pre-shaping my trailing edges. My table saw provides the quickest and smoothest output. A "planer blade" does the cutting, and the balsa strip stock is guided by a block of hardwood clamped to the saw fence. This block has a "channel" sawed into its lower right edge that fits the trailing edge "blank strips".

The saw blade is of course tilted to the proper angle; and the fence position is adjusted to provide just the proper thickness of the finished TE.

I label each of my TE "guide blocks" with the stock size and the necessary saw tilt angle. That way, re-setting for a new batch becomes quick and precise. [JW]

Adding to the comments by Jim Smith in SMALLnet #434: I am going to shrink the Great Planes 'Tutor' down to about 2/3 original size, and try it 'wet'. I'm spoilt for choice as to powerplant: VA Bambi replica (much more powerful than the original), ED Baby 0.25 replica, or a VA Elfin 0.25.

With a 50 mAh or 100 mAh battery pack, and the usual nano servos and micro receiver, it should be possible to preserve the 'slo fly' characteristics, allowing for some deterioration due to scale effect.

If forced to, I can put an .010 up front -- in fact, a comparison under the different power options could be fun!

A possible tip for those using these small beam-mount engines: Commercial mounts are hard to find (read 'virtually non-existent'). A couple of years ago, whilst visiting the UK, I picked up some 1/2A black nylon mounts. These were apparently SLEC ones (SLEC = Sun Lane Engineering Co) and on returning home found they were far too small to fit even a Cox TD, let alone any of the other ones. They are however, a perfect fit for the Bambi and ED replicas mentioned, and also the Schlosser 0.25.

I believe -- (but I'm not certain) that Hobby Lobby sells these same brand mounts. They are not marked with either size or manufacturer name, but the one in question measures 15mm/9-16" between the beams, and 27.5mm/1-1/16" diameter. There is a 9/16" diameter inner hole thru the centre of the backplate (which allows you to use a tank from 9/16" K&S tubing to increase the engine run on the small diesels).

Chris Murphy, Christchurch, New Zealand

...Chris, for quite a while I've had the problem of finding molded plastic motor mounts to fit odd-size engines. I solved that by taking a smaller-width mount, carefully sawing it in half on its vertical centerline; then re-assembling it with a thin plywood or hardwood spacer epoxied between the halves.

This works extremely well, and there's really no stress on the glued joint once the mount is firmly attached to the model's firewall.

I'd describe the process in detail, but I don't want to insult anyone's intelligence. (We SMALLsters are noted world-wide for our incredible personal ingenuity.) [JW]
