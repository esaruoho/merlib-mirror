Here are my ideas on rust inside diesel and glow engines.
1. Synthetic oil. I've heard that the manufacturing process sometimes leaves behind residual traces of corrosive acids.
2. Too much raw fuel left inside the crankcase.
3. Additives in the fuel, some of which are "nitrate" based.
4. Wrapping a hot engine in a cloth to keep the dirt out of it might allow condensation of moisture in the air.

My practice is to run the engine dry and not use the idle or throttle lever on the transmitter to shut down an engine. Remove the fuel line if you must and run the engine dry that way. I use after-run oil of various types but like a few drops of automatic transmission oil. Air tool oil is good since it dilutes residual fuel and never seems to gum up an engine after long storage. I never load an engine with after-run oil since it does cause difficult re-starting. Four or five drops is all that is needed. Flip the prop a few times and head home with a protected engine.
Regards
Bob Christ

From david owen < owendc@1earth.net >
RE airbleed throttles. I did say that an adjustable airbleed is essential for proper mixture adjustment, but this is not to say that a non-adjustable unit cannot be made to operate quite satisfactorily. In the case of the smaller PAWs, the needle valve holes, which are at about 100 deg to each other, must face evenly down the carby at the fully open position. Some of the earlier alloy-bodied PAW throttles wore rather rapidly, allowing air leaks around the barrel. This problem has been rectified by PAW with the adoption of steel bodies (there are other reasons). Particular care must be taken with the linkage, which should lightweight, yet not loose or sloppy. It is important not to allow the barrel to float or vibrate.
There are literally many thousands of very satisfied PAW users out there. PAW have always made honest, user-friendly engines at a price that has maintained them in business for 50 or so years. Must be doing something right!
PAW and its agents around the world are ready to assist any PAW owner. So, if you have a problem that you can't sort out yourself and particularly if you are new to diesels, contact a PAW agent in the first instance.
Declaration of interest; The writer has sold and serviced PAW engines since 1975.
