Ron Arnold:
A Farewell Salute to Ronald Reagan, Optimist

It was my privilege in 1981 to be selected as the official biographer of Ronald Reagan's controversial Secretary of the Interior, James Watt. Watt opened all the Interior Department's files to me and instructed every Interior executive to grant me unlimited interviews, unprecedented access for any writer. President Ronald Reagan had no objection and let the chips fall where they may.

They fell into the bestselling At the Eye of the Storm: James Watt and the Environmentalists, which went through 5 printings in one year.

The experience gave me a profound respect and admiration for the man who hired Watt despite the certain knowledge it would raise a firestorm of criticism from the anti-development left. Ronald Reagan picked people who could do the job and let them do it. If it created public debate, well, public debate is what American politics is all about. He was called the Great Communicator for good reason. He knew he could get his points across and he expected others to do the same.  Let the people decide. That was the Reagan style.

It was his style because he was an optimist. He believed in people and he believed in his mission. He wanted to give America a new beginning and he did. The press called it "the Reagan Revolution" and it was.

Ronald Reagan was the embodiment of the Yes We Can! attitude. For eight world-changing years he guided our nation through a tangle of issues and left us a legacy of achievement. At home he found an unorthodox path out of recession ("Reaganomics") and gave new life to the Republican Party. In foreign policy he set the stage for dismantling Soviet communism: "Mr. Gorbachev, tear this wall down!" He felt his greatest legacy was ending the Cold War safely.

And now he has gone on ahead. We grieve for his family. We grieve for America. We grieve for ourselves. He died at 93 on Saturday, June 5, 2004 after a ten-year battle with Alzheimer's.

But our loss is gain in other times. His legacy lives on. He left us stronger. He left us greater. And shortly before the twilight of Alzheimer's first took him from us, he wrote the words that will leave us wiser.

"When the Lord calls me home, whenever that may be, I will leave with the greatest love for this country of ours and eternal optimism for its future. I know that for America there will always be a bright dawn ahead."

He wasn't just an optimist. He was an eternal optimist.
