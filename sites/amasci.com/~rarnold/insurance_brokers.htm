Why we care: America's faith in the free enterprise system has been seriously shaken by corporate scandals in the headlines, from Enron and Tyco to Shell's overstating its petroleum reserves. The insurance industry is essential to the well-being of our nation, but it has come under scrutiny for potential scandals over conflicts of interest and self-serving practices. The insurance industry is highly regulated with the goal of providing consumer choice, but two practices have aroused concern, one called Placement Service Agreements and the other called Leveraging. While it is rare for the Center to support government regulation, it is also typical of the Center to examine each issue on its own merits. Insurance companies have sensed public unease over these two practices and some have called for closer public scrutiny. CDFE supports the insurance industry in its effort to avoid scandals and provide the best service possible to the public.

 

ISSUE

The Washington Legal Foundation recently wrote to attorneys-general and insurance departments in New York and California requesting that those offices investigate and consider regulating questionable practices. 

         Recently scrutinized in the Financial Times, Placement Service Agreements, or PSAs, inherently compromise a broker's duty to act in the client's best interests by encouraging brokers to refer business only to companies that pay them contingency fees. As a result, customers run the risk of paying more for coverage they do not need, and potentially receive less in terms of overall policy benefits. PSAs also referred to as revenue sharing and contingency agreements in the mutual funds industry have already drawn criticism and have been investigated by the SEC.

         A related practice, known as leveraging or tying has also drawn fire from consumer groups.  Heres how it works: Insurance companies get referrals from brokers who can also sell reinsurance (insurance for insurance companies).  In leveraging, brokers will threaten to withhold referrals, unless an insurance company agrees to fill its reinsurance needs through that broker.  This can lead to inflated prices for reinsurance (which get passed along to insurance customers) and a market that unfairly shuts out competition (because captive companies cannot search for the best prices). 

 

In addition to WLF, the following groups/individuals have already weighed in on PSAs and/or leveraging:

         J.P. Morgan Securities (JPMorgan)

         National Consumers League (http://www.natlconsumersleague.org/reinsurancepr.htm)

         Jeffrey Wilder, Dept of Economics at MIT (now with the antitrust division of the Department of Justice) (http://irm.wharton.upenn.edu/S02-Wilder.pdf)

         NY State Insurance Dept. (http://www.ins.state.ny.us/cl98_22.htm)
