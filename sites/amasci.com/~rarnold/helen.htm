Helen Chenoweth-Hage   1938-2006

Lawmaker for the ages
Her legacy will enrich generations yet unborn

Helen Chenoweth-Hage died in a rollover
car crash October 2, 2006, while holding
her toddler grandson, who survived with
his mother at the wheel, as they drove
near home in Tonopah, Nevada.

Lawmaker, fighter for the right, teacher,
you left us so enriched, Helen.

 

Biographical Directory of the United States Congress:

CHENOWETH-HAGE, Helen P., a Representative from Idaho; born in Topeka, Kans., January 27, 1938; graduated Grants Pass High School, Grants Pass, Oreg.; attended Whitworth College, Spokane, Wash.; self-employed medical and legal management consultant, 1964-1975; manager, Northside Medical Center, Orofino, Idaho; state executive director of the Idaho Republican party, 1975-1977; chief of staff, then campaign manager, to Representative Steven D. Symms; co-owner, Consulting Associates, Inc; guest lecturer, University of Idaho School of Law; elected as a Republican to the One Hundred Fourth and to the two succeeding Congresses (January 3, 1995-January 3, 2001); was not a candidate for reelection to the One Hundred Seventh Congress; died on October 2, 2006, in Tonopah, Nev.

She was one of those rare people recognized by her first-name only, like Madonna. In Idaho, if you said "Helen," nobody said "Helen Who?" They knew.

And they knew where she stood on the issues because she was unlike other politicians.

Helen said what she meant and meant what she said.

Her friends admired her and even her most hateful detractors acknowledged her outspoken honesty.

Helen married legendary property-rights icon Wayne Hage in 1999. Wayne died of cancer in June, 2006.

Wayne and Helen
 
They are both gone now.

Goodbye, great souls.
May your spirit live in us, always.
