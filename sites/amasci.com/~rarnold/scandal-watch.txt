SCANDAL-WATCH 

 Finding corporate problems before 
 they become Enron and WorldCom disasters. 

 To see our current search for problems in the 
 making, CLICK 
 HERE . 

 Anti-corporate watchdog groups scrutinize 
 corporations out of hostility. Their watchdogging intends only harm to 
 corporations and the free enterprise system. It's time pro-free 
 enterprise watchdogs added some balance to the public debate, trying 
 to keep the system honest rather than trying to destroy it. 

 A few "Cash Before Crash" executives who 
 enriched themselves by selling out just before their firms collapsed 
 have stained the reputation of all corporations even though executives 
 comprise only a tiny fraction of the employees and shareholders who make up the 
 corporation. The whole company suffers because of the crimes of a few. 

 And corporations with corrupt executives 
 comprise only a tiny fraction of corporate America. Multitudes of 
 honest and hardworking corporations get unfairly lumped with the 
 proverbial "few bad apples." 

 Worse, flawed practices such as "creative 
 bookkeeping," kickbacks, insider trading, and collusion can creep into 
 business unchecked until small problems become gigantic disasters. 

 In order to restore public confidence in 
 corporate integrity, pro-free enterprise watchdogs must provide their 
 own research and educational resources to help keep business honest.

 The Center has launched its project, 
 Scandal-Watch, to "scan for scandal" by examining economic sectors for 
 signs of trouble. When we find problems, we will publish our findings 
 in the spirit of helping free enterprise, not harming it. 

 For the Center's first examination, go to
 Scandal-Scan .
