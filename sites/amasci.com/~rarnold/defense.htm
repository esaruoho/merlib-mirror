Senior Fellow Robert Bidinotto on:

The environmentalists' war on our national security
posted 11/12/03 (edited Wednesday, Nov 12, 2003 16:04)

Our military men and women are fighting and dying in Iraq and Afghanistan, and defending our interests in other trouble spots around the world. But while politicians of both parties posture about "supporting our troops" and guaranteeing them the best in training and assistance, theyve also passed a mountain of environmental laws that greens now use to undermine our soldiers safety and preparedness. Rather than focus on training, our military personnel must now worry about complying with the endless requirements of statutes such as the Endangered Species Act, the Marine Mammal Protection Act, the Clean Air Act, and many others.

As a result, in Arizona the Air Force must move or cancel its practice bombing runs if antelope are spotted within 5 kilometers of a target area. In Idaho, low-level combat flying missions are prohibited because the noise might disrupt the mating of elk. Navy ships assigned to support bombers flying over Afghanistan were forced to cancel a missile-firing exercise off the California coast, for concern about nearby seals.

Consider the situation at Fort Hood, Texas, one of the Armys main training installations. There, 66,000 acres, or 33 percent of its total training area, are now reserved as habitat for two allegedly endangered species, the black-capped vireo and the golden-cheeked warbler. Out of consideration for the birds, soldiers arent allowed to veer off trails, and are even limited in their use of camouflage netting. Combined with other environmental restrictions, 84 percent of Fort Hood is currently subject to some kind of training limitations.

Consider the situation at Fort Lewis, Washington, where, according to an Army spokesmans congressional testimony, "70 percent of the training land is designated as critical habitat for the threatened Northern Spotted Owl, and yet the Northern Spotted Owl is not even present anywhere on the installation."

Consider the situation at Camp Pendleton, California, which is home to the 1st Marine Expeditionary Force, the Corps oldest and most highly decorated division. Last February, Marine Lt. General Richard Kelly reported that, thanks to the Endangered Species Act, training at the camp "is not realistic."

Though the camp has a 17-mile beachfront, protections for 18 allegedly endangered species prohibit Marines from conducting mock amphibious assaults on all but 1,500 meters of shoreline. To keep one supposedly endangered bird, the gnatcatcher, out of harms way, Marines simulating amphibious assaults on Pendletons Red Beach are required to take buses between scenes of battle. Theyre also limited in practicing off-road maneuvering in their vehicles, and are generally restricted to driving on designated caravan routes. Digging protective foxholes--once a staple of training--also is severely limited.

Now, a pending lawsuit by the Natural Resources Defense Council--the same group that initiated the 1989 Alar-on-apples hysteria--would make restrictions at Pendleton even worse. The NRDC suit would bar Marines from training near "critical habitats" for six species, including the gnatcatcher, the tidewater goby, and the arroyo toad. When added to areas already subject to restriction, the proposed new designations of critical habitat would put 56 percent of the base, plus 65 percent of the Miramar Marine Corps Air Station in nearby San Diego, off-limits to training activities.

This is obscene. The very survival of the young men and women whom we count upon to defend our nation depends on their receiving the most realistic military training, under the widest possible range of environmental conditions. Yet vast expanses of their own military bases are now taboo, and the realism of their training is undercut, so that some toad might not get squashed by a soldiers misstep, or that some lusty elk might not endure the trauma of coitus interruptus.  

But the undermining of training is not the only, or most dangerous, aspect of the greens' attack on our national security.

Last year, for example, the Natural Resources Defense Council filed another lawsuit that enjoined the Navy from deploying a new low-frequency sonar system, claiming it might violate the Marine Mammal Protection Act. How? Because environmentalists speculate, repeat, speculate that the high-intensity sonar may be causing some whales to beach themselves. And under the marine mammal laws absurd definition of animal "harassment," any military action that becomes an "annoyance" to the animals, and has the "potential to disturb" them, is prohibited.

But while whales will be protected from harassment, we Americans may not be as well protected from harassment by our enemies. Thats because the NRDCs legal harassment of the military is preventing the Navy from using its new sonar to track advanced, ultra-quiet submarines that have been deployed over the past six years by China, Iran, and North Korea.

Ordered by a federal judge to reach a settlement with the NRDC, the Navy finally agreed this past month to restrict use of the new sonar only to the eastern seaboard of Asia, to avoid using the system near the coast, and not to use it during seasons when whales are migrating.

The Pentagon has asked Congress to loosen the laws definition of animal "harassment" to allow the sonar to be used more widely, but the NRDC has pledged to fight, declaring that any such change would "substantially reduce the level of protection to marine mammals." Which, in their worldview, is far more urgent than the protection of American people.

At a time when terrorist-sponsoring enemies, such as Iran and North Korea, are developing nuclear weapons, it speaks volumes about the moral character of environmentalists such as those at NRDC that they worry less about the survival of their fellow Americans than about the survival of migrating whales.

It speaks additional volumes about the eco-corruption of our laws and courts that the once-proud U. S. military is now forced to beg and bargain with such characters for the right to defend American lives--and their own--with the best technologies and training available.
