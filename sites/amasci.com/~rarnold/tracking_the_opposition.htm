Tracking the Opposition on Todays Frontier

A panel presentation made by Center Executive Vice President Ron Arnold to
the 2002 Joint Conference of CropLife America and the California Plant Health Association,
October 8, 2002, in Indian Wells, California

 

Fifty years ago it would have been crazy to ask whos against agriculture.

Today, its crazy NOT to ask. Not if you want to stay in business.

Today, animal rights activists, biotech crop protesters and anti-pesticide radicals are hitting agriculture hard.

An IRS document tells us there are more than 4,000 environmental groups in America today. And the number is growing.

Thats a modern frontier that could made you crazy, paranoid at least.

Well, here's the good news: youre not paranoid.

Now the bad news: they really are out to get you.

And today, they increasingly have the clout to do it. They have money. They have members. They have media.

Theyre a credible threat to the survival of large scale agriculture worldwide.

But who? Whos out to get you?

My organization, the Center for the Defense of Free Enterprise, spends most of its time finding those answers.

We track the opposition.

Then we counterpunch.

How do we do that?

We begin by tracking events. We see whos bringing lawsuits against industry, whos lobbying Congress, whos organizing protest demonstrations, whos advocating violence, and whos firebombing research labs.

That fills up a whole database. We post the details on one of our three websites (www.cdfe.org; www.undueinfluence.com; and www.ranamuck.org).

The database is full of all-too-familiar names like PETA -- People for the Ethical Treatment of Animals and other big names like the Natural Resources Defense Council.

Its also full of less-familiar but important names like the Environmental Working Group and the Rainforest Action Network.

Once we have the database assembled, we scrutinize it, probing suspicious groups for tax law violations, lobbying abuses, financial irregularities, and just plain criminal activity.

What we find most often, as we did with PETA and the Environmental Working Group, is evidence of IRS tax law violations. The IRS is very particular how nonprofit organizations operate.

But it is stunning how arrogant and irresponsible these groups can get. They break the rules. They think they can get away with it.

PETA, for example, donated $1,500 to the Earth Liberation Front, which is an FBI-declared domestic terrorist group involved in dozens of arson attacks nationwide.  Amazingly, PETA didnt even try to hide it. The donation is plainly listed in their own Form 990 annual tax return for the year 2000.

When confronted about it by six different reporters, PETA offered six different explanations. It was for habitat improvement, it was for animal protection, it was for this, it was for that, but never for terrorism.  

As noted on the OReilly Factor, If you give money to Al Qaida and say its for goat enhancement, youre still paying for terrorism.

What do we do about such arrogance? We fight back.

The counterpunch is to file a complaint with the IRS to strip the offender of its tax exempt status.

Our organization has filed three such IRS complaints in the past two years -- one against the Rainforest Action Network, one against PETA, and one against the Environmental Working Group.

I think youll find the last two most interesting.

Our organization has been tracking PETA more than 10 year, since 1991. We even profiled it in our 1993 book, Trashing the Economy. PETAs philosophy is extreme. Its web site says: animals are not ours to eat, wear, experiment on, or use for entertainment.

PETA tries to enforce that philosophy on everyone else, frequently resorting to civil disobedience.

In mid-2001, we felt PETA had gone far enough, and began working with legal counsel to file a complaint against them with IRS Commissioner Charles O. Rossotti.

Filing an IRS complaint is not to be taken lightly. It took us nine months to build the case against PETA. Its like a prosecutor making a case against a felony suspect. You cant make any mistakes.

In early March of this year, the work was done, and we filed our complaint with the IRS.

In it, we made the case that PETA had:

         Stolen trade secrets;

         Advocated arson;

         Assaulted business executives, and

         Supported the Animal Liberation Front (ALF), an FBI-declared domestic terrorist group, by acting as a media conduit and being involved with a $2.5 million arson of a Michigan State University animal laboratory in 1992. The arsonist was captured, convicted and sentenced to nearly 5 years in federal prison.

That last one has been a puzzle since the day it happened. There are too many dots that no one has connected.

For example, in our complaint, we informed the IRS that court documents showed a link between Rodney Coronado, the arsonist, and Ingrid Newkirk, one of the two co-founders of PETA.

According to the Governments Sentencing Memorandum, Coronado sent two packages to Newkirk at a friends home, one just before the arson, another just after the arson.

The second package contained criminal evidence stolen from the torched MSU laboratory. The prosecutor wrote, Newkirk had arranged to have the package delivered to her days before the MSU arson occurred.

When police executed a search warrant on the home of Newkirks friend, they found plans for a raid on Tulane Universitys Primate Research Center and false identification for Rodney Coronado and the other co-founder of PETA, Alex Pacheco.

You dont have to be a rocket scientist to guess that PETA and the arsonist were probably on familiar terms. Yet we can find no record that law enforcement has ever followed up on that linkage.

Our complaint urged the IRS to take action on that and all our findings.

The first thing people usually ask us is how the IRS investigation is going.

The short answer is, nobody knows except the IRS.

The long answer is, privacy laws forbid the IRS to even say whether they are conducting an investigation.

So, technically, we dont know.

However, watching PETAs response tells us something is definitely going on.

As in most of these cases, PETA denies everything, but has shifted much of its operation from civil disobedience to lobbying.

The case of the Environmental Working Group was completely different. EWG, as it is known, is a longtime opponent of large-scale industrial agriculture, an anti-pesticide shop, and an advocate for organic farming. It does not engage in civil disobedience, but works within the system by lobbying Congress.

And, man, do they lobby Congress!

Thats what snagged our attention two years ago when we saw huge amounts of foundation money come pouring in to influence the 2002 Farm Bill.

EWG, like PETA, is tax exempt under Section 501(c)(3) of the United States Tax Code. Such organizations can only lobby up to a certain percentage of their annual budget -- 20 percent for direct lobbying, 5 percent for urging others to influence legislators.

We found newspaper accounts of EWG lobbying that must have cost far more than those percentages.

So we began to dig into it. Heres the summary of what we found, as quoted directly from our complaint to IRS Commissioner Rossotti:

 

We believe that EWGs tax exemption should be revoked immediately because the organization:

         Functioned as an illegal political action organization, including pressuring Vice President Gore during his run for President;

         Lobbied the California legislature for a year without registering as lobbyists

         Submitted false or misleading reports to the IRS about its lobbying expenditures;

         Hid its lobbying political expenditures while operating as a project of the Tides Foundation;

         Operates as a lobbying organization, including receiving a $1.62 million grant to lobby the 2002 Farm Bill.

 

Now that last item is utterly appalling. That $1.62 million grant came from the Chicago-based Joyce Foundation, which itself was created in 1948 with industry money from lumber mills and timberlands.

The Joyce Foundation said on its website that the $1.62 million to EWG was "to support a concentrated program of agriculture policy reform." Sounds innocent enough.

But when you look on the Joyce Foundations Form 990 annual report to the IRS it says that grant was, "For work on 2002 Farm Bill."

Why would the Joyce Foundation tell the public one thing and the IRS another?

And if "working" on the 2002 Farm Bill isnt lobbying, what is it?

Whats more, foundations are not supposed to give any money at all for lobbying.

And what did EWG lobby the Farm Bill for?

First, to eliminate subsidies for major crops such as cotton and wheat.

Second, to drastically reduce America's large-scale industrial food producers by plastering large farms with untouchable conservation areas.

Third, to subsidize only "organic food" production.

Why would Joyce fund such a program? Well, its Form 990 shows not only the grants it gives away, but also their own investment portfolio.

And those investments look incriminating to me.

The Joyce Foundation had over $1.8 million invested in Canadian Wheat Board securities when they gave that money to EWG. Is Joyce using EWG to hurt American wheat and benefit their investment in Canadian wheat?

The Joyce Foundation also had more than $410,000 invested in General Nutrition Company, a provider of vitamins, herbals, and vegetarian guides. Is Joyce using EWG to hurt large scale agriculture to benefit herbal growers?

Our IRS complaint against the Environmental Working Group made these facts perfectly clear to the IRS.

Again, the IRS wont say whether theyre investigating the Environmental Working Group, but weve noticed that EWG is blustering more and lobbying less.

IRS complaints are a successful way to encourage the arrogant to follow the rules. But we mustnt think of them as a magic solution to organizations that try to put you out of business.

The problem with hostile forces is too big for that. Ive documented the scope of the problem in my book, Undue Influence. The subtitle says it all: Wealthy Foundations, Grant-Driven Environmental Groups, and Zealous Bureaucrats That Control Your Future.

In closing, let me briefly deal with the most important question about this modern frontier for agriculture:

What do you do about undue influence?

What you dont do is roll over and get in bed with your enemies.

What you do is fight back.

If you think your opponents will negotiate with you in good faith without the fight, let me give you the benefit of my thirty years experience dealing with them.

I call these Arnolds Iron Laws of Environmental Activism:

First, A Deal with Environmental Group A is Not A Deal With Environmental Group B. Let me remind you that there are at least 4,000 environmental groups in America, and they're all lined up one behind the other saying, "Let's make a deal."

Second, You Can Never be Clean Enough or Green Enough. No matter how good you get, there will always be another extremist yelling its not good enough.

Last, You Can Never Outrun an Environmentalist To The Left. It you think environmentalism is about the environment and not about political power, try to explain why their newest groups have names like the Anti-Capitalist Convergence and Green Anarchy. And no, I didnt make that up. They're real groups.

If you keep these axioms in mind, and act very wisely, you can find a way to win.

This is a new frontier you must win.

For upon you, the food and the fate of us all depends.

Thank you.
