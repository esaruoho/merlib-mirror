The Letter~C

Calendar of Lone Dog
One of the more famous examples of First Nation picture writing. In this instance, Lone Dog, a Dakotan

Calligraphy
Art form of penmanship generally done with nibs that create thick/thin strokes. (Whiting 1982)

Cancellesca Corsiva
See Chancery Cursive.

Capitalis Monumentalis
Roman copybook used on monuments, and the like.

Capitalis Rustica
Condensed Roman copybook used on monuments.

Cardinal Numbers
Numbers used to represent quantity

Caroline
Copybook created by Alcuin of York at the direction of Charlemagne, Emperor of the Holy Roman Empire.

Carolingian
System of cursive penmanship that was the predecessor to Chancery Cursive.

CD Slant
Upslant of between 127 and 134 degrees in Graphoanalysis.

Centre
  1. The Area where the point is at.
  2. The world, as it is experienced.
  3. The resolution of the core belief of the structure of the individual.

Centrifugal Direction
  1. Moving away from the center.
  2. Moving towrds others

Centripetal Direction
  1. Moving towards the center.
  2. Moving towards one's inner self.

Certar
Writing system of Middle Earth. These were devised asnd mostly used for scratched or incised inscriptions. Commonly Cirth is translated as runes. The Lord of The Rings (Tolkein: 1965) Part Three, Appendix E describes this language.

Certhas Daeron
Cirth writing system of Middle Earth. Originally devised to represent the sounds of Sindarin, it was expanded for other languages. The Lord of The Rings (Tolkein: 1965) Part Three, Appendix E describes this language.

Chancery Cursive
  1. System of Penmanship used for Ecclesiastical Purposes during the 15 Century, popularized by Ludovico Arrighi. Letters are curvilinear, but slope 10 degrees to the right.
  2. Used as a synonym for Italic Script during the 21 Century.

Character
  1. General term for any self contained element of a writing system
  2. Conventional term for a unit of the Chinese writing system in East Asian Scripts.

chi squared
  • Statististical term used for calculating the deviation from the norm.
  • Chi scores less than 0.01 are statistically significant.
  • Chi scores greater than 0.01 imply mere randomness.

Chiromancy
This is the study of hands, to divine the future. It has no relationship to handwriting analysis, despite attempts by authors such as Chiero to do so. His books that do mention handwriting analysis, do so in the context of graphomancy.

Chirograph
Archaic term used to refer to handwriting.

Chukchi
Spoken in northeast Siberia. It is written with a Cyrillic alphabet. Most noted for the fact that k is pronounced differently by men and woman, as is rk.

Chu' Nho
Vietnamese writing system based upon Chinese Han writing. Used until circa 1790 CE. When this writing system held sway, less than 10% of the population could read. « The final u in Chu should have a tilde on it. »

Chu' nôm
Vietnamese writing system developed for Emperor Quang Trung [ 1776 - 1792 CE ] as a replacement for Chu' Nho. It did not recieve official recognition, and was not used by Vietnamese intellectuals. « The final u in Chu should have a tilde on it. »

Cipher
A means of concealing a message. The letters of the message are substituted or transposed for other letters, letter pairs, or polygrams.
Origin: Arabic: Sifr: Nothing

Cirth
also known as Certar. For more details The Lord of The Rings (Tolkein: 1965) Part Three, Appendix E describes this language.

Civil Service Copybook
Family of copyboooks taught and used by the British Civil Service. The best known example is Vere Fostor Copybook.

Classificatory Variable
A variable that represents a characteristic of the research subject.

Clockwise Letters
Letters which are made in a clockwise direction. [ b, h, j, k, m, n, p, r ]

Closed Form
Lines enclose the entire inner part of the formation.

Code
A method of concealment that may use words, numbers or syllables to replace the original words and/or phrases of a message. One specific way in which it differs from ciphers is by the substitution of an entire word, in contrast to ciphers, that substitute or transpose individual letters.
Origin: French: Codex: A writing tablet.

Codex
Folded pages gathered and sewn together, to produce what is now known as a book.

Codex Sinaitus
The earliest complete Greek book which survives.

Cognitave Dissonance
One sees what one wishes to see.

Cold Reading
Using body language to guide what one states.

Concurrent Validity
The correlation between the a criterion measure that is collected at the same time as the mesure we wish to validate.

Consonant
  1. A brief portion of an utterance in which obstruction of to the air stream is created in the vocal tract.
  2. A character designating such a sound

Content Validity
The extent to which a test covers the content area it is intended to cover.

CopperPlate Writing.
Copybook taught in England, similar to Spencerian Writing in the USA.

Correlation
The degree to which two or more measures tend to measure the same attribute.

Correlation Coefficient
The statistical index of correlation, usually designated as r. The value ranges from +1.00 to -1.00, which indicate a perfect positive correlation through to a perfect negative correlation.

Covariance
The extent to which two variables tend to vary together in value across subjects.

Cree
First Nation language of Canada that has a fourth person pronoun.

Cretan Linear
Ideographic writing system in Crete from which Linear A and Linear B developed. This script runs from left to right.

Cryptoanalysis
The science of deciphering messages, especially by those not meant to read such messages.

Cryptography
The science of hiding messages, so that others will not be able to read them.

Cryptology
The science that includes making secret messages. Cryptography, and reading those messages anyway. Cryptoanalysis

Cuniform
Writing system that developed in Mesopotamia, circa 3 000 BCE.

Cuniform Wedges
Wedges made for the different cuniform signs. These are:
  1. Horizontal, wedge on left
  2. Perpendicular, wedge at top
  3. Oblique, wedge either at top or bottom, but to the side.
Horizontal symbols, wedge on right, are not found, as that requires a left hand to make.

Cursive
  1. Written or formed with the strokes of the letters joined together and the angles rounded.
    Source: The Mirriam-Webster Dictionary

Cursive Roman
Described as loose, irregular, scratchy, and difficult to read Source: Tannenbaum: 1930, p 7

Cursive Writing
  1. Writing in which letters are joined in each word as prescribed by most standard Models. (Whiting 1982)
  2. Flowing, showing the influence of the motion of the hand in writing, often with joined characters. (Daniels & Bright 1996)

Cyperus Papyrus
Tall papyrus plant in Egypt that was used for making papyrus.

Cypro-Mycenaean Script
Writing system from 1 400 - 1 200 BCE, found on Cyprus.

Cyrillic Script
Alphabet used for writing Russian and many Eastern European languages. It is most prominant where the Greek Orthodox Church was dominant. It is based on the alphabet that was created by Brother Cyril, a missionary sent by the Byzantine Patriarch to Moravia.
