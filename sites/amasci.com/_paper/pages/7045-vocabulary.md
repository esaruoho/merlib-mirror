# Vocabulary

*Source page: `~robertf/monorail/vocab.html`*

**Vocabulary**\
Highway engineers, railroads, transit planners, and monorail people all use certain words with meanings especially for their profession. We'll try to cut through some of that jargon. I will also include some names and acronyms used in the Seattle Area, especially since this web site is open to visitors all over the world and most will have no idea of what some of the local names refer to.\

<td data-valign="TOP" width="73%"><strong>NOTE:</strong> There is another <a href="../transit/vocab.html">vocabulary for general transit terminology</a><span></span>.<br />
 <br />
<span id="#alweg"><strong>Alweg -</strong></span> The German company that designed and built the Seattle Center Monorail and several other monorails around the World, including the one at Disneyland. The company later went out of business.<br />
 <br />
<span id="#alwegdes"><strong>Alweg design -</strong></span> Designed similarly to the monorails designed by the Alweg company.<br />
 <br />
<span id="#atgrade"><strong>at grade</strong></span> - Refers to operations along the surface of the ground. Most streets, roads, and railroads are <strong>at grade.</strong><br />
 <br />
<span id="#beam"><strong>beam</strong></span> - The rail that the monorail train runs on.<br />
<br />
<span id="#beamway"><strong>beamway</strong></span> - The pathway formed by beams placed end to end along which the monorail train operates. Also called a <strong>guideway.</strong> Equivalent to a track in a railway.<br />
 <br />
<span id="#blueline"><strong>Blue Line -</strong></span> A future line proposed for the Seattle Monorail Project, that would approximately go north from Downtown Seattle along Aurora Ave. N. and south from Downtown to South Park.<br />
 <br />
<span id="#bombard"><strong>Bombardier -</strong></span> A Canadian company that builds monorails.<br />
 <br />
<span id="#column"><strong>column</strong></span> - A vertical supporting post that holds up the monorail guidway. Called a <strong>pylon</strong> by monorail people.<br />
 <br />
<span id="#dest"><strong>destination -</strong></span> A place to which people go. <em>For example:</em> Safeco Field is a major destination for baseball fans.<br />
 <br />
<span id="#dwell"><strong>dwell time -</strong></span> The length of time a train or other transit vehicle waits at a station after arriving and until leaving.<br />
 <br />
<span id="#elevated"><strong>Elevated Transportation Company -</strong></span> A company established by the City of Seattle to design a monorail system and place it on the ballot for Seattle voters in the general election of November 2002. Now that the election is over and the monorail has been approved, the Elevated Transportaiton Company has been disbanded and replaced by the Seattle Popular Monorail Authority.<br />
 <br />
<span id="#ElliottB"><strong>Elliott Bay -</strong></span> A roughly semi-circular bay of Puget Sound. Downtown Seattle is located on Elliott Bay.<br />
 <br />
<span id="#etc"><strong>ETC -</strong></span> Acronym for Elevated Transportation Company&lt;<br />
 <br />
<span id="#goldline"><strong>Gold Line -</strong></span> A future line proposed for the Seattle Monorail Project, that would approximately go east from Downtown Seattle onto Capitol Hill, then north through the University District to Lake City and south to connect with Link light rail in Rainier Valley.<br />
 <br />
<span id="#grade"><strong>grade</strong></span> - <strong>1.</strong> The surface of the ground. <strong>2.</strong> The steepness of a slope. <em>The train has to go up a 2% grade.</em><br />
 <br />
<span id="#gradesep"><strong>grade-separated</strong></span> - Operating at a level above or below surface traffic, such as subways, the Alaskan Way Viaduct, an elevated railway (such as Vancouver, B.C.'s Sky Train), and, of course, a monorail.<br />
 <br />
<span id="#grnline"><strong>Green Line -</strong></span> The designation given to the first of five monorail lines planned for Seattle. The Green Line is the one currently being designed and built, and will go from West Seattle to Crown Hill via Downtown Seattle.<br />
 <br />
<span id="#guideway"><strong>guideway</strong></span> - The preferred term for the rail or track upon which the monorail train operates.<br />
 <br />
<span id="#headway"><strong>headway -</strong></span> The amount of time between the arrival of trains or other transit vehicles. <em>i.e.</em> a headway of five minutes means that a train arrives every five minutes. This could also be defined as the time between departures, or is commonly referred to the time between trains.<br />
 <br />
<span id="#hitachi"><strong>Hitachi -</strong></span> A large company in Japan with a wide range of products, including monorails based on the Alweg design.<br />
 <br />
<span id="#initiatv"><strong>initiative -</strong></span> Under Washington State law, a specific kind of petition which can be initiated by any citizen and which proposes a new law. Copies of the initiative are made available for people to sign, and if enough registered voters sign the initiative, it must be placed on the ballot to be voted upon in an election.<br />
 <br />
<span id="#keyarena"><strong>Key Arena -</strong></span> A large roofed sports arena located at the west side of the Seattle Center. It is the home of the Seattle Supersonics basketball team and the Seattle Storm women’s basketball team. Various other events are also held in Key Arena.<br />
 <br />
<span id="#kingco"><strong>King County -</strong></span> A political subdivision of Washington State. King County includes the large city of Seattle and several smaller cities including Shoreline, Renton, Tukwila, Kent, Auburn, Seatac, Federal Way, Burien, Woodinville, Bothell, Kirkland, Bellevue, Redmond, and Issaquah.<br />
 <br />
<span id="#link"><strong>link -</strong></span> <strong>1.</strong> In a transportation network, a connection between two nodes. <strong>2.</strong> The name that Sound Transit chose to give to their light rail system (see Link Light Rail).<br />
 <br />
<span id="#linklr"><strong>Link Light Rail -</strong></span> The name Sound Transit has given to its proposed light rail system. A short Link Light Rail line is under construction in Tacoma, and a much longer line is proposed for Seattle but is currently delayed by various problems.<br />
 <br />
<span id="#node"><strong>node -</strong></span> A point of origin or destination in a transportation system. In the case of the monorail, each station is a node.<br />
 <br />
<span id="#origin"><strong>origin -</strong></span> A place from which people leave. <em>For example:</em> Downtown Seattle is a major origin of passenger traffic at the end of the work day.<br />
 <br />
<span id="#pierce"><strong>Pierce County -</strong></span> A political subdivision of the State of Washington. Pierce County is the next county south of King County and includes the large city of Tacoma and several smaller cities such as Puyallup, Graham, Orting, Lakewood, and Gig Harbor.<br />
 <br />
<span id="#pugetsd"><strong>Puget Sound -</strong></span> A large body of water, long north and south and fairly narrow east and west, but highly irregular, that connects with the Pacific Ocean and extends into the interior of the State of Washington. The Seattle area is on the east shore of Puget Sound.<br />
 <br />
<span id="#purpline"><strong>Purple Line -</strong></span> A future line proposed for the Seattle Monorail Project, that would approximately go east-west through Ballard, Fremont, Wallingford, The University District, and Sand Point.<br />
 <br />
<span id="#pylon"><strong>pylon</strong></span> - A vertical column that supports the guideway of a monorail.<br />
 <br />
<span id="#redline"><strong>Red Line -</strong></span> A future line proposed for the Seattle Monorail Project, that would approximately go east-west between South Park and Martin Luther King Way S.<br />
 <br />
<span id="#safecof"><strong>Safeco Field -</strong></span> A major-league baseball stadium just south of Downtown Seattle. The home of the Seattle Mariners baseball team.<br />
 <br />
<span id="#seahawks"><strong>Seahawk Stadium -</strong></span> A new large stadium just south of Downtown Seattle and on the north side of Safeco Field. Seahawk Stadium is the home of the Seattle Seahawks football team. In this case “football” refers to American football. The stadium will also be used for soccer matches. Soccer is the game called “football” in other countries.<br />
 <br />
<span id="#seactr"><strong>Seattle Center -</strong></span> A large complex of buildings, amusement rides, fountains, and other attractions located just north of Downtown Seattle. The Seattle Center is approximately 1/3 mile (500 meters) on each side.<br />
 <br />
<span id="#mrproject"><strong>Seattle Monorail Project -</strong></span> The name being used by the SPMA for the project to build a new monorail system in Seattle.<br />
 <br />
<span id="#spmauth"><strong>Seattle Popular Monorail Authority -</strong></span> The agency established by the City of Seattle to manage the design, construction, and operation of the new Seattle monorail system.<br />
 <br />
<span id="#snohomco"><strong>Snohomish County -</strong></span> A political subdivision of the State of Washington; the next county north of King County. Cities in Snohomish County include Everett, Marysville, Mukilteo, Monroe, Snohomish, Arlington, Mill Creek, Lynnwood, Edmonds, Mountlake Terrace, and part of Bothell.<br />
 <br />
<span id="#soundtra"><strong>Sound Transit -</strong></span> A governmental agency designing and operating a regional rapid transit system for Snohomish, King, and Pierce Counties.<br />
 <br />
<span id="#spma"><strong>SPMA -</strong></span> The Seattle Popular Monorail Authority.<br />
 <br />
<span id="#straddle"><strong>straddle bent -</strong></span> A large beam with vertical "legs" on each end, shaped somewhat like an upside down and flattened letter “U”, designed to straddle a street so that a monorail guideway can be supported over the street. Straddle bents are usually used in situations where the usual pylon would be in the way, such as in an intersection or where there is no place in the middle of the street that is not a traffic lane.<br />
 <br />
<span id="#switch"><strong>switch</strong></span> - A device that moves the rails of a railway or the beams of a monorail in such a way that a train can move from one track or guideway to another. Yes, there <u>are</u> switches for monorails!<br />
 <br />
<span id="#traction"><strong>traction motor -</strong></span> A motor that provides the power to move a train or other vehicle.<br />
 <br />
<span id="#washdc"><strong>Washington, D.C. -</strong></span> A large city on the east coast of the United States. It is the capital of the U.S., and is where the President, Congress, and many government offices are located. It is not the same as the Washington where Seattle is located. It is very far from Seattle. D.C. stands for District of Columbia, a district set aside for the capital of the United States.<br />
 <br />
<span id="#washst"><strong>Washington State, or State of Washington -</strong></span> One of the 50 states that make up the United States of America. It is located in the northwest corner of the United States by the Pacific Ocean. It is just south of the Canadian province of British Columbia. Seattle is the largest city in the State of Washington. In this web site, the name “Washington” is used alone (without “State”) when it not likely to be confused with Washington, D.C.<br />
