# Delphi (Δελφοί)

Delphi is an incredible place which still has a very spiritual and mystical aura about it (when it's not being drowned out by tour buses).  Situated high up on the side of Mount Parnassus, it was the site of the famous Oracle, a priestess who would interpret the will of Apollon.

<td><a href="images/Greece/Delphi/Temple$20Of%20Apollon%20Entrance.jpg">Temple Of Apollon Entrance<br />
</a>The major archeological site at Delphi is the Sanctuary of Apollon, a large sacred area which was used for temples, treasuries, monuments, and the like.  The centerpiece of all this is the Temple of Apollon.  This is where the famous Oracle at Delphi was located.
<p>According to legend, the Oracle was located here after a shepherd noticed his goat acting strangely whenever going near a particular chasm.  He went to investigate and upon breathing the fumes emanating from the chasm, he was overcome by them and started speaking inarticulately.  After news of this spread, a tripod was placed over the chasm opening, where a woman would sit and take on the role of the person who gave oracle.  Later a temple was built on this spot.</p>
<p>Political and military leaders, as well as ordinary citizens, would come to Delphi to visit the Oracle for help in making decisions.</p>
<p>Several Temples Of Apollon were built on this spot over time; only a few columns from the last one are standing today on the foundation.  The words "Know Thyself" and "Nothing In Excess" were supposedly inscribed on the temple at one time.</p></td>

</a>One of the major events that took place at Delphi every eight years (later changed to every four years) was the Pythian Games.  These included both musical contests (since Apollon, the patron god of Delphi, was also the god of music) and athletic contests.  The Pythian Games were second only to the Olympic Games in importance.
<p>The stadium was built to house some of the athletic contests.  It is the best preserved ancient stadium in Greece, although the current form with the stone benches wasn't built until the second century AD. Originally, the spectators sat on the ground.</p></td>

</a>This is the classic "postcard shot" of the Tholos at the Sanctuary of Athena Pronaia.  Tholos means "beehive" and is used to refer to this type of circular building.  These are actually very rare in ancient Greek architecture (most Greek buildings were rectangular) and this is the best known and best-preserved Tholos.  Little is known about the original purpose of this building.  It may have been a temple but if so, it is not known who it was dedicated to.  It certainly must have been an important building given the high quality of workmanship and the unusual structure.  It is quite impressive to look at.
<p>Three of the original 20 columns were reconstructed in modern times.  This helps to give an idea of what the building <a href="images/TholosReconstruction.jpg">might have looked like</a>. </p></td>
