**June 22, 1996 - Arapahoe Basin**

- <img src="img/abasin.gif" data-align="right" data-border="0" alt="A-Basin logo" /> Well, I thought Steamboat was going to be my last skiing of the season, but it turned out not to be so! I met a friend from Austin at [A-Basin](http://www.colo.com/ski/abasin.html) for just a *little* more before the season *really* ends! It was dirty and rocky on the lower half of the mountain, but the upper half was pretty reasonable. They had just gotten 2" of new snow on Friday night. Wasn't what you'd call ideal skiing, but what do you want for late June!?

<span id="2"></span>

**April 13-14, 1996 - Steamboat Springs**

- <img src="img/steamboat.gif" data-align="right" data-border="0" alt="Steamboat logo" /> Finally had a chance to return to the first place I ever went skiing, [Steamboat Springs.](http://199.45.246.18/WinterNet/world/usa/co/steamboat.html) Only this time I was able to drive instead of fly! We closed them down, this was the last weekend Steamboat was open this season, and with a late-season snowstorm rolling through just after we arrived, it was two days of great skiing. Too bad they had scheduled the closing, although with some of the melting going on at the base (even after the cold Saturday) I guess I see why. Saturday the clouds decended and you couldn't see 20 feet in front of you at the top. That made for some exciting skiing. But Sunday the sun peeked out on a regular basis and it was gorgeous.

- <img src="img/keystone.gif" data-align="right" data-border="0" alt="Keystone logo" /> Yes, had to make another trek back to Keystone. A little slushy as it is getting a bit warm even at the base of the mountain. The last weekend of night skiing was ~ far too crowded... sigh. Another lousy day in paradise.

**March 16-17, 1996 - Winter Park**

- <img src="img/winterpark.gif" data-align="right" data-border="0" alt="Winter Park logo" /> Took some visiting friends to [Winter Park](http://www.skiwinterpark.com/wpmj) for the weekend. It snowed about 15" over the two days, mostly cloudy the whole time. Learned to ski in powder whether I wanted to or not! Fun but cold.

<span id="6"></span>

**March 9, 1996 - Copper Mountain**

- <img src="img/copper.gif" data-align="right" data-border="0" alt="Copper logo" /> Well, car trouble sidelined me last weekend, but after getting a new one (!) I was back on the road this week. Went back to Copper with some folks who are much better than I am and wound up on a few black diamond slopes ('I don't do bumps!!'). I managed a couple of face-plants and one fairly spectacular fall (judges gave it a 9.5). It was not a pretty picture. But, as someone said to me on the shuttle back to the parking lot, 'Of all the things in the world that suck, this isn't one.'

<span id="7"></span>

**February 24, 1996 - Breckenridge**

- <img src="img/breck.gif" data-align="right" data-border="0" alt="Breck logo" /> I had never been to [Breckenridge](http://www.brecknet.com/) to ski yet, I had only been to the town. The ski area is really nice. Parking isn't as easy as some but the shuttles run frequently. The skiing was some of the best I've ever experienced, but much of that was due to the snowfall from last week to be sure. We stayed with some friends that night and explored downtown a little more (and then got in some ice skating at Keystone the next day).

**February 17, 1996 - Snowy Range, WY**

- <img src="img/snowyr.jpg" data-align="right" data-border="0" alt="Snowy Range logo" /> Ninety minutes up U. S. 287 and a little west of Laramie, WY, is a little "neighborhood" ski area called Snowy Range. It has cheaper lift tickets, less traffic on a holiday weekend, and shorter lift lines. It was nice, although they could use some snow now, the dumping from a few weeks ago is wearing off. But now I can say I've been to Wyoming.

**December 23-24, 1995 - Monarch**

- <img src="img/monarch.gif" data-align="right" data-border="0" alt="Monarch logo" /> [Monarch Ski Area](http://www.rmii.com/monarch/) is on Highway 50 between Gunnison and Salida in the San Juan range in southern Colorado. It's a small, but very personal (and not very crowded) resort. They're low-tech, they don't even use barcode readers on their lift tickets yet. ;-) But it is a great place to spend Christmas, especially when it's sunny and in the teens and 20s every day. No snow-making here, so there are a few rocks, but they had just gotten some new power, so it was pretty nice. The runs from the highest lift take you along the Continental Divide and give you a breath-taking view in all directions, including Pike's Peak which is all the way over by Colorado Springs! Monarch Lodge also serves the best filet I've **ever** had, and I've had some good ones!

<span id="11"></span>

**December 16, 1995 - Winter Park**

- <img src="img/winterpark.gif" data-align="right" data-border="0" alt="Winter Park logo" /> [Winter Park Resort](http://www.skiwinterpark.com/wpmj) is actually made up of 3 individual ski areas: Winter Park, Mary Jane, and Vasquez. They're all connected together and it is hard to realize you've passed from one to the other unless you're paying attention. But we skied them all today! It was cold, but not unbearably. The most unbearable part was having to pay full price for lift tickets (\$42!). None of our coupons or membership cards that have worked at other places applied here today. But we got lazy and didn't want to drive to a more distant place.

<span id="12"></span>

**December 9, 1995 - Copper Mountain**

- <img src="img/copper.gif" data-align="right" data-border="0" alt="Copper logo" /> With a **COLD** front destined to move through the area, we decided to go to [Copper Mountain](http://www.aescon.com/ski/copper/) for more skiing. The cold arctic air that came down from Canada this weekend and chilled everybody east of the Rockies did not venture west. So while in Denver, the low Saturday was 4 degrees, west of the Continental Divide, where most of Colorado's ski resorts are, it was a relatively pleasant 30 degrees. I can tell this section of my web page is going to get real monotonous before winter is over!

  [-- back to index --](#top)

<span id="13"></span>

**November 23, 1995 - Keystone**

- <img src="img/keystone.gif" data-align="right" data-border="0" alt="Keystone logo" /> So far, every Thanksgiving in my life has either been turkey with my family or football with Texas A&M. This year, in a break from tradition, I went skiing at [Keystone Resort.](http://www.colo.com/ski/keyst.html) I like my new tradition. I had heard that very few people go skiing on Thanksgiving day itself, so it seemed like the perfect time to go. The snow was great although only Keystone and North Peak were open, The Outback hadn't gotten enough snow yet. We skied until about 7:00pm (Keystone has night skiing). It was neither windy nor cold as it often is there at night. Ok, so I missed the Cowboys beat the Chiefs and I also missed the Horns beat Baylor (it just isn't right to be playing someone else on Thanksgiving... it's one thing not to be playing A&M, but it's just plain wrong to be playing ~ someone else on Thanksgiving!), but through the wonder of video tape this was made right again on Friday. I think I have a new Thanksgiving tradition now. I can't wait for my new Christmas tradition!

  [-- back to index --](#top)

<span id="14"></span>

**November 11, 1995 - Loveland Basin**

- <img src="img/loveland.jpg" data-align="right" data-border="0" alt="Loveland" /> With only the 3rd big winter storm of the season, received 36 inches of snow in a 72-hour period late this week, making a ski trip a reasonable thing to try. Since this is probably the single biggest reason I moved up here, I wasn't hard to convince. It was great, the snow was as good as I've seen it in January (which is not to say perfect, but quite acceptable!). I was able to try out my new skis and found that I'm not in such bad shape after a fairly inactive as I might have thought. 5 lifts and 23 runs were open and the before-Thanksgiving price for lift tickets is only \$24. What a day!
