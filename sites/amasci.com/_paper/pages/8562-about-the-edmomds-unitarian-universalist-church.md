<p><strong>ABOUT THE CHURCH</strong><br />
<a href="#hist">History</a>  <a href="#fac">Facilities</a>  <a href="#affil">Affiliations</a></p>
<p>The Edmonds Unitarian Universalist Church is a voluntary association of men, women and children who come together in community to share concerns for the basic questions of life and to frame those inquiries in the context of religion in the largest sense of the word. We agree upon a set of ethical principles for living rather than promoting the acceptance of a specific religious creed.</p>
<p><br />
<strong>PURPOSE</strong></p>
<p>In the spirit of truth, irrespective of its sources, and in the spirit of universal kinship, undivided by nation, race, or creed, we unite to strengthen our convictions in the value of, and the need for, liberal religion, and through the strength of unity to give expression to these convictions as the organization decides.</p>
<p><strong>BELIEFS</strong></p>
<p>We agree upon a set of ethical principles for living rather than promoting the acceptance of a specific religious creed. Each member accepts responsibility for working out a personal belief system based on life experience and conscience. The approaches taken by individuals are diverse, and we feel that the respect for this diversity is a strength that unites and enriches us. On this basis of mutual respect, we work together to make religious community a reality.  A major part of our community is the celebration of life's fullness and the promotion of  social justice.</p>

<p><span id="hist"></span><strong>HISTORY</strong></p>
<p>The Edmonds Unitarian Universalist Church was originally an offspring of the University Unitarian Church of Seattle. Inspired by Rev. Aron Gilmartin, minister of the University Church, a small group organized the Sno-King Unitarian Fellowship in 1957. For two years the group met informally on alternate Sunday evenings.</p>
<p>Desire for a full Sunday morning service with a program for children led to the rental of the Edmonds Masonic Temple. Three years later, in 1962, the Fellowship moved to the Edmonds High School where it met for the next four years. During that time the congregation decided it needed its own building and a part-time professional minister.</p>
<p>In September, 1966, the new church building opened its doors with The Reverend Robert Fulghum as part-time minister. Rev. Fulghum came for "one year to help the church get its feet on the ground," and stayed for 18, until his retirement in the spring of 1985.</p>
<p>In 1969 the congregation ordained its founding members, Stuart and Maybelle Chapman, as lay ministers. Until Stuart's death the three ministers worked harmoniously and effectively to make the church a strong, liberal religious community.</p>
<p>Maybelle and Rev. Fulghum continued to work as a team until his retirement, after which he continued to serve as Minister Emeritus of the church.</p>
<p>The Reverend Aron Gilmartin came to serve as Interim Minister for the 1985-1986 church year. The Reverend Davis Joyce served as the first full time minister for the 1986-87 church year. Reverend Jaco B. ten Hove was called in June, 1988 and served until July, 1998, at which time he left to pursue a joint ministry with his wife, Rev. Barbara Wells.</p>
<p>The Reverend Elizabeth Kerman served as Interim Minister for the 1999 church year.</p>
<p>In August, 1999 Rev. Edward Brock was called as the new minister and continues to serve in that position. For an introduction to Rev. Brock see the <a href="Minister1.htm">Minister's Page</a>.</p>

<p><span id="fac"></span><strong>FACILITIES</strong></p>
<p>The church building is modern and designed for both formal and informal use. It was designed as a multi-purpose facility to reflect the congregation's orientation toward families and community involvement. Chapman Hall, the main meeting room, can seat up to 225 people and an adjoining infant room permits parents with infants to hear and see the services while remaining with their children. There is a large, modern kitchen with easy access to the public areas.</p>
<p>Nine classrooms provide space for the children's religious education programs on Sunday and are available as meeting rooms at other times. A folding door between two of the classrooms opens to permit gatherings of 30 or more people. A more formal meeting space, The Friarplace, is equipped with a sink, small refrigerator and conference tables. It can accommodate meetings of up to 20 people. A library and offices of the minister, administrator and Director of Religious Education complete the facilities.</p>
<p>A parking lot accommodating 90 cars is located at the main entrance to the building.</p>
<p><span id="affil"></span><strong>NATIONAL AND LOCAL AFFILIATIONS</strong></p>
<p>We are part of the <a href="http://www.uua.org/main.html">Unitarian Universalist Association</a>, one of over a thousand congregations in the United States and Canada. Regionally we are associated with the <a href="http://www.pnwd.uua.org/">Pacific Northwest District</a> and the Puget Sound Unitarian Universalist Council.</p>
