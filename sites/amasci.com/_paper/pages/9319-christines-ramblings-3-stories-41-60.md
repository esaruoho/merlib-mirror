<td><strong>41. The Pure and the Profane</strong><br />
<strong>     I used to say that Kittens was the best and most</strong><br />
<strong>powerful thing I've ever written. But after this one, I had to</strong><br />
<strong>reconsider. This, I none-too-humbly believe, is a phenomenal</strong><br />
<strong>story. Once again, I blazed effortlessly, and loved every minute</strong><br />
<strong>of it.</strong><br />
<strong>     The only time I had to slow down and actually work at</strong><br />
<strong>this one was the sex scene. This one had to be done just right. I</strong><br />
<strong>spent many hours honing those two pages of tormented lust,</strong><br />
<strong>and I am extremely happy with the results.</strong><br />
<strong>     I'm extremely happy with all of it. The highlights for me</strong><br />
<strong>are the abovementioned sex scene, Elisa's desperate race to try</strong><br />
<strong>and save who she thinks is Broadway, Dominique vs. Elektra in</strong><br />
<strong>the basement, and Jericho in the dungeon -- most particularly</strong><br />
<strong>his exchanges with first Goliath and then Angela and Brooklyn.</strong><br />
<strong>     There really isn't much to say about this one except to</strong><br />
<strong>go on raving about how much I love it, so I won't because I'm</strong><br />
<strong>sure you get the idea ; )</strong><br />
<strong>     Title: in Fantasia, the final segment is Night on Bald</strong><br />
<strong>Mountain followed by Ave Maria, and the announcer calls it</strong><br />
<strong>something like "a reflection of the struggle between the sacred</strong><br />
<strong>and the profane." What a great title! But instead of "sacred," with</strong><br />
<strong>its religious connotations, I changed it to "pure," which fits better</strong><br />
<strong>and is also nicely alliterative. In this case, there's a dual struggle</strong><br />
<strong>-- Elektra is given a choice between Broadway (pure) and</strong><br />
<strong>Jericho (profane); Jericho is given a choice between Elektra</strong><br />
<strong>(pure) and Demona (profane).</strong><br />
<strong>     Memorable lines: So many to choose from, but the best is "As above, so below."</strong>
<p><strong>42. Tales from the Skiff</strong><br />
<strong>     I had a lot of time left to account for, a few adventures</strong><br />
<strong>left untold. I was in such a hurry to write The Pure and the</strong><br />
<strong>Profane that I went on and skipped ahead, telling myself I</strong><br />
<strong>would go back and take care of some of the others next.</strong><br />
<strong>     The first section, "Brendan Vandermere and the Temple</strong><br />
<strong>of Doom," came about because  when I tried to turn Brendan</strong><br />
<strong>into a hero (man, what a chore!), I knew I had to give him some</strong><br />
<strong>interests besides society parties and expensive cars. So, in The</strong><br />
<strong>Horror of Innsbrook, I established that he was an amateur</strong><br />
<strong>Egyptologist, a hobby of which Margot haughtily disapproved.</strong><br />
<strong>     Then I thought it might be fun to introduce him to the</strong><br />
<strong>complete anti-Margot (well, that would be Birdie, so maybe not</strong><br />
<strong><em>complete</em>, but certainly far down the continuum). Way back in</strong><br />
<strong>the early 1990's when I started editing the facility newsletter,</strong><br />
<strong>just for a kick I started running a monthly cliffhanger serial,</strong><br />
<strong>"The Adventures of Dakota Jones." It was an easy matter to</strong><br />
<strong>transfer Dakota, fedora and all, into my fanfic (and made for a</strong><br />
<strong>bit of silliness, for of course Dakota was the original name for</strong><br />
<strong>Demona, back when they were thinking of making the show</strong><br />
<strong>more of a comedy).</strong><br />
<strong>     The goddess Kal-Tet is completely made up; I once</strong><br />
<strong>used that name for a variant of the evil deity Calaan in my RPG</strong><br />
<strong>world.</strong><br />
<strong>     Hans Runolf is Inge's brother; they may even be twins. I</strong><br />
<strong>put him in there because when writing something like this, ya</strong><br />
<strong>just gotta have Nazis, it's like a rule. The Orb of Isis was</strong><br />
<strong>borrowed from an episode of The Simpsons ; )</strong><br />
<strong>     For the second part, "Crossbones," I finally decided to</strong><br />
<strong>create a brand new clan (not counting the Squids). But I wanted</strong><br />
<strong>something different. I've always had a weakness for pirates,</strong><br />
<strong>and got thinking about figureheads on ships. Wooden gargoyles.</strong><br />
<strong>Then I realized I could play with parallels, which is something I</strong><br />
<strong>always enjoy doing.</strong><br />
<strong>     I could also get into MacBeth's past. If Xanatos would</strong><br />
<strong>make a good highwayman, who not MacBeth as a pirate? Or</strong><br />
<strong>maybe it's just that I have a total lech for guys in frock coats</strong><br />
<strong>and fold-top boots, that could be it.</strong><br />
<strong>     The idea to open and close each segment with Puck</strong><br />
<strong>doing his Cryptkeeper shtick came from my pal Denis, who</strong><br />
<strong>said it as a joke when he heard the title, but it was such a good</strong><br />
<strong>idea that I just couldn't pass it up ; ) Thanks, bro! It also meant I</strong><br />
<strong>had to come up with some puns, and I think that took longer</strong><br />
<strong>than writing the stories!</strong><br />
<strong>     Title: Broadway was joking about telling their</strong><br />
<strong>adventures to the clan and once he said it, I knew it had to be</strong><br />
<strong>the title.</strong><br />
<strong>     Memorable lines: "Bum-ba-dum-dum, bum-ba-dah, bum-ba-dum-dum, bum-ba-dah-dah-dah!"</strong></p>
<p><strong>43. Three of a Kind</strong><br />
<strong>     It was time to stop fiddling around and get this darn</strong><br />
<strong>story written. I was not looking forward to it, but I'd been</strong><br />
<strong>foreshadowing it just about forever, and knew I had to get it</strong><br />
<strong>done for better or worse. Since I wasn't really sure what was</strong><br />
<strong>supposed to happen, I just started writing and let it go where it</strong><br />
<strong>would.</strong><br />
<strong>     Luckily, it turned out better than I expected. I went into it</strong><br />
<strong>worrying and feeling like it was going to be a chore (and parts</strong><br />
<strong>of it were, figuring out all that timeline stuff for the Archmage's</strong><br />
<strong>jaunts for example), but I came out feeling pretty good about it.</strong><br />
<strong>     Good, even though it was tragic and senseless, ending</strong><br />
<strong>with three dead gargoyles, three catatonic society princesses,</strong><br />
<strong>and the Sisters confined to live the next thirty-some years as</strong><br />
<strong>silent helpless passengers as Tiffy, Muffy, and Babs tour some</strong><br />
<strong>of the finer realms of psychiatric treatments.</strong><br />
<strong>     Not to mention the Archmage discorporated (again) ...</strong><br />
<strong>Jack Chalker's "Rules" say to never believe an evil wizard's</strong><br />
<strong>truly dead until he's apparently died a minimum of thrice, so</strong><br />
<strong>lemme think ... 1.) His plunge into the abyss in Long Way to</strong><br />
<strong>Morning, interrupted by his future self; 2.) His flamboyant</strong><br />
<strong>magical end in Avalon Part Three ... that left one more for me to</strong><br />
<strong>play with ; ) Guess now he's really gone ...</strong><br />
<strong>     I feel really bad for Gabriel, and for those who have</strong><br />
<strong>been wondering about him, I promise, he will be reappearing in</strong><br />
<strong>another story soon. It's been more than a year his time, a year</strong><br />
<strong>spent with Ebon learning to find his way through grief, so he'll</strong><br />
<strong>be ready to move on soon. For Gabriel, I would like to play</strong><br />
<strong>with the "heroic journey" theme, a la Joseph Campbell.</strong><br />
<strong>     Title: ever since I first knew I was going to have to write</strong><br />
<strong>this one, that was the title I thought of it by, of course referring</strong><br />
<strong>to the three sets of triplets.</strong><br />
<strong>     Memorable lines: Gabriel telling off Oberon, particularly "And if you can't be a man, pull your britches up and be a lord!".</strong></p>
<p><strong>44. Hotwire</strong><br />
<strong>     Following T.J.'s debut, I was swamped with letters in</strong><br />
<strong>support of him, curious to see how he got along with the clan.</strong><br />
<strong>Here's this poor guy, his whole life (which wasn't that great to</strong><br />
<strong>begin with) uprooted and restructured, thrown into a castle full</strong><br />
<strong>of weirdos. Unlike the other friends of the clan, he wasn't</strong><br />
<strong>drawn into this by his own desires; he was flung in, and given</strong><br />
<strong>the circumstances of his parentage, he had no choice but to</strong><br />
<strong>stay.</strong><br />
<strong>     I didn't want him to automatically befriend the gargs and</strong><br />
<strong>be all cool about it, nor did I want him to go to the other</strong><br />
<strong>extreme and hate them (seems like most of the people in the</strong><br />
<strong>Gargoyles universe are at one end or the other). He had to get</strong><br />
<strong>to know them, and will probably always be dubious not only</strong><br />
<strong>about them but about his mom, himself, and his place in the</strong><br />
<strong>world.</strong><br />
<strong>     It also seemed exactly right that T.J. and Birdie would</strong><br />
<strong>make a great pair. I don't know why, but it just did. Yet I would</strong><br />
<strong>later find out that their relationship turned out pretty quirky.</strong><br />
<strong>They end up sharing an apartment (Breeding Season) but I can't</strong><br />
<strong>help but think that they're not romantically involved. If there is</strong><br />
<strong>sex going on, it is of the extremely casual variety. And I don't</strong><br />
<strong>even know if there is. T.J. for some reason wound up striking</strong><br />
<strong>me as being ... not exactly <em>asexual</em> but just not terribly</strong><br />
<strong>interested. Then again, his experience with the Weird Sisters</strong><br />
<strong>may have had something to do with it.</strong><br />
<strong>     Anyway ... here's T.J., something of a punk and a rebel</strong><br />
<strong>but at the same time a decent guy (he is one of the strangest</strong><br />
<strong>characters I've ever created; there is a real ambiguity to him</strong><br />
<strong>about every aspect of his life). He has simple tastes and needs.</strong><br />
<strong>All the guy wants is to hang out and have a brewski.</strong><br />
<strong>     The only way I could think of to find common ground</strong><br />
<strong>between him and the gargs was his and Lex's knack with</strong><br />
<strong>machinery. But even that isn't quite the same; Lex is a</strong><br />
<strong>technogeek and T.J. is a greasemonkey. Once he let himself get</strong><br />
<strong>involved, <em>then</em> he could find out that he and Brooklyn had</strong><br />
<strong>some of the same casual tough-guy traits.</strong><br />
<strong>     The story came about because I had this idea for T.J.</strong><br />
<strong>and Birdie to boost a Quarryman van. No real reason; it was just</strong><br />
<strong>one of those things that struck me funny. So I crafted a story</strong><br />
<strong>around it, and involved Lex and Brooklyn because I don't want</strong><br />
<strong>to get too far away from the original characters.</strong><br />
<strong>     Title: T.J.'s first appearance was in FoxFire; Hotwire</strong><br />
<strong>sounds similar and rhymes so I liked that bit of continuity; plus</strong><br />
<strong>it refers to his powers. I was thinking for a time of calling it</strong><br />
<strong>"Hotwire and Jailbird," Jailbird being Birdie's nickname after her</strong><br />
<strong>trip to the slammer for knocking Margot into a glass-topped</strong><br />
<strong>table, and having it be a Bonnie and Clyde, Smokey and the</strong><br />
<strong>Bandit, buddy cop kind of car chase wild lawbreaking romp.</strong><br />
<strong>But that was a tad too corny.</strong><br />
<strong>     Memorable lines: "When I mentioned dying for a beer, this isn't what I had in mind."</strong></p>
<p><strong>45. Dear Diary</strong><br />
<strong>     I had to turn Aiden into a gargoyle but I didn't want to</strong><br />
<strong>make a great big detailed story out of it. Mostly because I was</strong><br />
<strong>psyching myself up to do Breeding Season, and I wanted to get</strong><br />
<strong>to it right away because I knew it was going to be another great</strong><br />
<strong>and epic ride.</strong><br />
<strong>     But I felt Aiden deserved a little bit of the spotlight as</strong><br />
<strong>she underwent her big lifestyle change, and the diary format</strong><br />
<strong>seemed a good way to keep it short. I didn't have to do long</strong><br />
<strong>dialogue exchanges, no descriptive scenes. Just her thoughts,</strong><br />
<strong>and a chance to explore some of my theories about gargoyle</strong><br />
<strong>biology. Such as the unhinging pelvis. Ouchie.</strong><br />
<strong>     And then there was the fact that Aiden, being so <em>nice</em></strong><br />
<strong>and such a <em>wuss</em> was going to be a gargoyle that makes</strong><br />
<strong>even the most saccharine interpretation of Angela look tough.</strong><br />
<strong>She can't roar, she can't fight worth a damn, she's too meek. I</strong><br />
<strong>don't know what will happen the first time Aiden gets in a real</strong><br />
<strong>combat (her only previous experience being in Double Date,</strong><br />
<strong>when the extent of her participation involved smacking a</strong><br />
<strong>Quarryman over the head with her tote bag).</strong><br />
<strong>     Title: self-explanatory.</strong><br />
<strong>     Memorable lines: So, ha-ha, I just flew back from Central Park and boy are my wings tired!</strong></p>
<p><strong>46. Breeding Season</strong><br />
<strong>     It is hard writing a story that spans several months of</strong><br />
<strong>time. Other stuff goes on in the world, so it can be a hassle</strong><br />
<strong>staying focused and not getting distracted while still addressing</strong><br />
<strong>those events and not glossing things over.</strong><br />
<strong>     Ever since Passions, people have been after me to let</strong><br />
<strong>Goliath and Elisa have a kid. I wanted to take this very slowly,</strong><br />
<strong>very carefully. I had to make sure I knew exactly where I stood</strong><br />
<strong>in my opinions on gargoyle reproduction and the influence of</strong><br />
<strong>magic on crossbreeding. Plus, I didn't want to rush it. I didn't</strong><br />
<strong>want to zip them right from a kiss to bed to marriage to family.</strong><br />
<strong>     By the time I got to this point, many of my fellow</strong><br />
<strong>authors had populated their various universes with whole</strong><br />
<strong>throngs of little ones. Did anyone still give a darn how I chose</strong><br />
<strong>to handle it? Well, whether they did or not, <em>I</em> gave a darn.</strong><br />
<strong>Even if it is going to be a long time yet until we actually see the</strong><br />
<strong>finished product in the form of all those hatchlings.</strong><br />
<strong>     As I began writing, I realized this was going to be one of</strong><br />
<strong>those tales that really has it all. It was funny, sexy, caring,</strong><br />
<strong>romantic, sweet ... but it was also dark: Jericho's attempted</strong><br />
<strong>seduction of Angela, and particularly Demona's miscarriage --</strong><br />
<strong>that horrifying and intense tragedy did what I thought was</strong><br />
<strong>impossible, making people actually grieve and feel sorry for</strong><br />
<strong>Demona and Jericho, even after all they've done.</strong><br />
<strong>     Another one of those spontaneous plot hooks popped</strong><br />
<strong>up in this one, in the form of Goliath's alchemist mother. I have</strong><br />
<strong>already developed several possible plots which will involve</strong><br />
<strong>her.</strong><br />
<strong>     Hudson and Delilah. I took some real lumps over</strong><br />
<strong>Hudson and Delilah. Some people did not at all like the idea of</strong><br />
<strong>Hudson as a sexual character. Does seem kinda weird, I admit.</strong><br />
<strong>His relationship with Maria Chavez has never gone beyond the</strong><br />
<strong>occasional kiss; they are both just looking for companionship,</strong><br />
<strong>not passion.</strong><br />
<strong>     But why the hell <em>not</em> Hudson? From Delilah's point of</strong><br />
<strong>view, it made sense. So he's old. So what? Look at the movies</strong><br />
<strong>pairing older actors with young babes. I just today read that</strong><br />
<strong>Catherine Zeta-Jones, who starred opposite Antonio Banderas in</strong><br />
<strong>the latest Zorro movie, is now working on a project with Sean</strong><br />
<strong>Connery and states that he is the hottest man she's ever met</strong><br />
<strong>(tell us something we didn't know, Catherine &lt;g&gt;).</strong><br />
<strong>     So he's old? He's not <em>dead</em>, for crying out loud! Why</strong><br />
<strong>not let him get some action? And then, just to prove how virile</strong><br />
<strong>he is, why not have Delilah lay a record-breaking number of</strong><br />
<strong>eggs? Of course, that part was also inspired by countless</strong><br />
<strong>cartoons, in which the male critter meets a female critter and</strong><br />
<strong>then they march across the screen with a line of little ones that</strong><br />
<strong>look just like him ... ; ) Hudson may be discreet, and as Delilah</strong><br />
<strong>has similar coloring, it might take a while for the penny to drop,</strong><br />
<strong>but eventually someone is going to notice.</strong><br />
<strong>     No, I don't know what the deal is with Dr. Johnson.</strong><br />
<strong>     Yes, I think the Y2K hype is preposterous and silly ... but</strong><br />
<strong>all the same, we're buying a gun and laying in a supply of</strong><br />
<strong>emergency foodstuffs ...</strong><br />
<strong>     Harry the Hammer (what a name!) uses the phrase "dark</strong><br />
<strong>Madonna" to describe Elisa. I borrowed that from Dean Koontz,</strong><br />
<strong>who uses it in The Servants of Twilight. We're going to have</strong><br />
<strong>trouble with him in the future, I imagine.</strong><br />
<strong>     In Future Imperfect, Aiden and Lex learned that Elisa</strong><br />
<strong>and her unborn child were killed by a renegade Avalon</strong><br />
<strong>gargoyle. Drawing again from Dean Koontz, this time from</strong><br />
<strong>Lightning and the concept of destiny struggling to reassert</strong><br />
<strong>patterns that were meant to be, I wanted to build on that</strong><br />
<strong>possibility and therefore it was inevitable that Jericho should</strong><br />
<strong>have the very real opportunity and motive to do it.</strong><br />
<strong>     I never intended for him to <em>actually</em> kill her, though,</strong><br />
<strong>and I initially believed it would be because he discovered</strong><br />
<strong>some nobility buried deep within himself.</strong><br />
<strong>     Then I got to know Jericho, and realized it ain't gonna</strong><br />
<strong>happen. I had to come up with some other reason for him to</strong><br />
<strong>spare Elisa's life, some way that would be almost more warped</strong><br />
<strong>than killing her in the first place. So now, from the depths of his</strong><br />
<strong>obsessive, jealous, possessive slavish devotion to Demona, he</strong><br />
<strong>has become Elisa's secret protector.</strong><br />
<strong>     Once again, I got to borrow Chas, who always conducts</strong><br />
<strong>himself with cool aplomb. And Birdie, who can't resist being a</strong><br />
<strong>smartass even under fire. There has got to be a blowout</strong><br />
<strong>between them, Canmore, Margot, and possibly Brendan at some</strong><br />
<strong>point.</strong><br />
<strong>     Oh, and I screwed up -- I didn't re-watch Mirror</strong><br />
<strong>beforehand, so I didn't remember that Puck uses the tower atop</strong><br />
<strong>the World Trade Center to juice up his spell. No way T.J. and</strong><br />
<strong>Birdie live in an apartment right beside that so they could get</strong><br />
<strong>easily to the roof. Blunder. Sorry.</strong><br />
<strong>     I thought long and hard on the matter of Amber. Finally,</strong><br />
<strong>I concluded that the mother's race dictated more of the baby's</strong><br />
<strong>development than the father's race did. As Elektra's mom was a</strong><br />
<strong>gargoyle, Elektra is a gargoyle who can pass for human. Amber</strong><br />
<strong>is the other way around, a human who can pass for a gargoyle.</strong><br />
<strong>     Her feet will require special shoes, her wings will have</strong><br />
<strong>to be concealed, but she has no tail and her brow ridges are so</strong><br />
<strong>slight as to be almost unnoticeable. I don't think Elisa will have</strong><br />
<strong>any problem showing her off down at the station (though I must</strong><br />
<strong>confess to thinking about the movie Dumbo; Amber sneezes</strong><br />
<strong>and her wings unfurl out of her blankets, wouldn't <em>that</em> be</strong><br />
<strong>fun to explain!). She'll have a much easier time denying the day</strong><br />
<strong>than Elektra, too, but in the meantime Elisa can enjoy the same</strong><br />
<strong>luxury as Eihblin Driscoll -- a child that sleeps straight through,</strong><br />
<strong>no noontime feedings.</strong><br />
<strong>     It should be interesting to see how things go with Rick</strong><br />
<strong>Alvarez, because everyone at the station thinks he and Elisa</strong><br />
<strong>have been carrying on a secret affair. Will Rick risk Goliath's</strong><br />
<strong>wrath by acting like Amber is his daughter? I haven't yet</strong><br />
<strong>decided.</strong><br />
<strong>     Title: I think I was the first one to coin this phrase,</strong><br />
<strong>"breeding season," back when I did A Gargoyles Christmas</strong><br />
<strong>Special. I <em>think</em> I was ... but there are a lot of things that</strong><br />
<strong>become common use. For instance, I know I was the first to do</strong><br />
<strong>the wing joint thing, and the knuckles against the brow ridges</strong><br />
<strong>as a show of affection (based on the fist-to-the-temple from</strong><br />
<strong>Alien Nation, btw, as is the sexy humming that takes place in</strong><br />
<strong>the rookery). Christi gets all the credit for the mysterious "dirty</strong><br />
<strong>tail trick," though!</strong><br />
<strong>     Memorable lines: The thing about tinted glass, David Xanatos thought amusedly, was how easy it was to forget it was only opaque on one side.</strong></p>
<p><strong>47. Shameless Plug</strong><br />
<strong>     I had to. Couldn't resist.</strong><br />
<strong>     So self-indulgent! Masturbatory, even! Crossing over with</strong><br />
<strong>my own self!</strong><br />
<strong>     But I had to.</strong><br />
<strong>     My book, <em><a href="http://www.eskimo.com/~vecna/magelore_index.html">Curse of the Shadow Beasts</a></em>, was due out</strong><br />
<strong>soon and of course I wanted everyone who admired my</strong><br />
<strong>writing to buy it so that I can make money, launch my career,</strong><br />
<strong>become famous, get invited to cons, sell movie rights, buy a</strong><br />
<strong>gigantic house that looks like a castle, and spoil myself and my</strong><br />
<strong>loved ones forevermore. (to order the book, click <a href="http://www.sabledrake.com">here</a> &lt;g&gt;).</strong><br />
<strong>     Since I wanted this to be a fun story that didn't give</strong><br />
<strong>away too much of the novel, I had to set it as a slight prequel</strong><br />
<strong>and couldn't involve Arien because he's too darn moody early</strong><br />
<strong>on. So I chose Cat's quarrel with the Ferrets and the matter of</strong><br />
<strong>Lord Taron's emeralds, which happen before the book begins.</strong><br />
<strong>     The moment I started, having chosen Hudson and</strong><br />
<strong>Brooklyn because I still don't give them enough screen time, I</strong><br />
<strong>realized all the similarities between Hudson and One-Eye, and</strong><br />
<strong>from there everything went great.</strong><br />
<strong>     Side note: in the revision of the second and third books,</strong><br />
<strong>I noticed that Lord Taron kept wanting to turn into Xanatos ; )</strong><br />

<strong>     I feel obligated to apologize to Thomas "Green Baron" Forsyth, who is such a </strong><br />
<strong>good sport about setting me straight when I put my foot in my mouth when it comes to </strong><br />
<strong>dealing with religion. The one time I go and present a strongly religious character, he's </strong><br />
<strong>a nutcase ... sorry, Thomas! </strong><br />
<strong>     Harry was partly drawn from many of the clients I've worked with. But, crazy as he </strong><br />
<strong>is, there must be something going for him ... and he'll find out in a future story that he </strong><br />
<strong>was more right than he thought ... though not about Amber.</strong><br />
<strong>     I had a hard time coming up with a title for this one. It was mostly written (the first </strong><br />
<strong>20 pages) in two nights, and there I was, right near the end, with no title. </strong><br />
<strong>     Finally, on the night I finished it up, I settled on this one and figured it would do.</strong><br />
<strong>Good enough. I wanted to have it done and posted before the Gathering and our move </strong><br />
<strong>and general life craziness set in. </strong><br />
<strong>     I enjoyed this one quite a bit, especiallyt the interactions between Harry and </strong><br />
<strong>Dominique, Elisa and Tony, the poor clan and Amber, and Xanatos and Kurt Masters. I </strong><br />
<strong>even got to use that Dumbo imagery, which had been in my head ever since Amber was </strong><br />
<strong>born.</strong><br />
<strong>     From the Department of Weird Coincidences -- someone (I forget who and couldn't </strong><br />
<strong>find the e-mail, sorry!) pointed out that Elektra means "amber" in Greek, and wondered </strong><br />
<strong>if I'd done it on purpose with the amber pendant and all that ... nope, it was just another </strong><br />
<strong>of those strange things! </strong><br />
<strong>     Yes, Albert the janitor is an homage to Alien Nation ; )</strong><br />
<strong>     Memorable lines:</strong><br />
<strong>     Youre not dealing with Elisa Maza the cop, she said, voice both low and fierce. </strong><br />
<strong>Youre dealing with Elisa Maza the <em>mother</em>, and if I dont get my daughter back in </strong><br />
<strong>the next five seconds Im going to blow your teeth out the back of your goddam head! </strong><br />
<strong>Do you understand <em>that</em>, Tony?</strong></p>
<p><strong>57  The Guardians: Alchemist</strong><br />
<strong>     What fun to play with the future! And when I could play with the future and the past </strong><br />
<strong>at the same time, who could ask for anything more?</strong><br />
<strong>     The trouble with writing about the future, though, as I've already said, is the risk of </strong><br />
<strong>trapping myself. The Guardians series is the actual future, not a possible one like the </strong><br />
<strong>one Lex and Aiden visited. So whatever is the past to those people has to be true. </strong><br />
<strong>     Now we get to see Amber Maza as a grown-up, pretty much just as feisty and stubborn </strong><br />
<strong>as she was as a kid, but with the extra added problem of not really feeling like she knows </strong><br />
<strong>where she fits in. Unlike Elektra, Amber is fully aware of her half-and-half heritage. She's </strong><br />
<strong>different from the other human kids in her generation because she ages a little bit slower </strong><br />
<strong>than them, different from the hatchlings because she ages faster (and had a ten year head </strong><br />
<strong>start while they were still in the egg).</strong><br />
<strong>     But even with all of her troubles, Amber grew up as quite the young woman. Tough and </strong><br />
<strong>quick-thinking, a tad on the impulsive side, but fiercely loyal to her family. When her </strong><br />
<strong>father's life is on the line, she will do whatever she has to at whatever the cost.</strong><br />
<strong>     It was also intriguing to see the adult version of Alex (and just what <em>did</em> happen to his </strong><br />
<strong>parents? How has Xanatos attained his goal of immortality, and what was the price? </strong><br />
<strong>Heh-heh-heh, only the Shadow knows!) and Patricia. Looks like Brendan and Dakota </strong><br />
<strong>are still around too.</strong><br />
<strong>     Fun to know that Demona's still up to no good ... and it looks like Jericho's out of the </strong><br />
<strong>picture by then, and oh, what is in store for her to break every last thread of love between </strong><br />
<strong>Demona and Angela? We shall see ... actually, we shall see not too many stories down </strong><br />
<strong>the line!</strong><br />
<strong>     But the most fascinating character of all was Old-Mother. Lori Summers e-mailed me </strong><br />
<strong>a fic-snippet which featured a future Amber, and spoke of how she had inherited a </strong><br />
<strong>measure of inner serenity from her paternal grandmother. I <em>loved</em> the idea, and it got even </strong><br />
<strong>more fun when I decided to let her develop that trait by the example of actually meeting </strong><br />
<strong>the gargoyle in question.</strong><br />
<strong>     Old-Mother. If she had a name, it would have been Dierdre, the Lady of Sorrows. A </strong><br />
<strong>long and often-sad life, pitied and considered barren by her clan. When her only egg, laid </strong><br />
<strong>late in life, was all that she had left, she broke tradition and paid special attention to her </strong><br />
<strong>son. She was a character who rose admirably above her personal tragedies to come out </strong><br />
<strong>of it a bastion of quiet strength. </strong><br />
<strong>     Her acceptance of Amber erased any lingering doubts that Amber might have had as </strong><br />
<strong>to whether her crossbreed status would have been abhorrent to the old clan. In the short </strong><br />
<strong>time they were together, Amber learned much. </strong><br />
<strong>     Amber also picked up a raving crush on the Hudson of the past, which was something </strong><br />
<strong>I just could not resist doing. Hudson as a handsome young stud ... way to go! The past was </strong><br />
<strong>a lot of fun. Little Goliath throwing mud at little Iago for picking on a certain brother and </strong><br />
<strong>sister, lusty Prince Corwin (one of the Avalon Clan would eventually be named after this </strong><br />
<strong>uncle of Malcolm). Amber playing with the hatchlings, including Elektra's mother and </strong><br />
<strong>young Demona. </strong><br />
<strong>     I'm glad Old-Mother wound up visiting the future. I hope to eventually detail some </strong><br />
<strong>more of her experiences in upcoming stories, and then maybe have some fun getting her </strong><br />
<strong>safely back home.  </strong><br />
<strong>     Memorable lines:</strong><br />
<strong>     Alex hammering the table and yelling "Bullshit!" -- Greg at the Gathering 99 ; )</strong><br />
<strong>     "It's ... a fist!"</strong></p>
<p><strong>58  Indigo</strong><br />
<strong>     Ever since her debut appearance, swinging her tail at Goliath's bachelor party, I have </strong><br />
<strong>been regularly implored with letters wanting to see more of Godiva (uh ... that didn't </strong><br />
<strong>sound quite right; we saw everything in Love Machine already ... but I digress). </strong><br />
<strong>     Having the ghost of Sevarius inhabit her wasn't good enough; folks wanted to see </strong><br />
<strong>Godiva in action, the special kind of action for which I've become notorious. </strong><br />
<strong>     I initially set out to write a naughty Godiva story, plain and simple. But it just wasn't </strong><br />
<strong>working. She was a robot, an android, a machine. I didn't want to re-hash my Trek-fic </strong><br />
<strong><a href="http://www.eskimo.com/~vecna/ffunct.htm">"Fully Functional"</a> and give her an aware personality of her own. Someone needed to step </strong><br />
<strong>in and help out.</strong><br />
<strong>     Not Sevarius. That would be just too creepy. And good old Anton, although he was </strong><br />
<strong>once married and fathered a son, just didn't seem a very sex-oriented person. </strong><br />
<strong>     Then I batted around the idea of letting Birdie take her over by remote control, </strong><br />
<strong>teaming up with T.J. as a sort of half-assed pair of superheroes. Rejected that one </strong><br />
<strong>right away, let me tell you! </strong><br />
<strong>     So there I was, mulling and mulling, and then I decided to have someone else take </strong><br />
<strong>over the Godiva body. Thus was born Sabra Indrani. Sabra was always her first name; I </strong><br />
<strong>never had to think about it (similar things happened with Jericho and Elektra and Tiffy </strong><br />
<strong>Vandermere; the name's just there sometimes and there's no way around it). Indrani was </strong><br />
<strong>plucked from my Character Naming Sourcebook (a Writer's Digest Book Club selection; </strong><br />
<strong>if anybody wants to join this really great club, let me know so I can send you my "refer a</strong><br />
<strong>friend" postcard and get some freebies! &lt;g&gt;).</strong><br />
<strong>     But the more I wrote, the more Sabra turned into a complex and troubling character. </strong><br />
<strong>Not someone resigned to her disability, not someone bravely struggling to do the best she </strong><br />
<strong>can. Not a noble character. She was bitter to the core, hated the world and everyone in in </strong><br />
<strong>and particularly herself. Given the chance at a new body, a sexy fabulous voluptuous body, </strong><br />
<strong>she never looked back. So what if it meant becoming a gargoyle, an outcast, a monster? </strong><br />
<strong>From Sabra's point of view, she already was an outcast and a monster.</strong><br />
<strong>     Sometimes stories don't go the way I intend, and this was a severe example. Was</strong><br />
<strong>supposed to be a light-hearted sexual romp. Turned dark. Way dark. Like a drug, the </strong><br />
<strong>Godiva bodyand the power of sex became addictive and overpowering to Sabra. </strong><br />
<strong>     Her seduction of Jason (there's a lot going on with him; more on that in later stories),</strong><br />
<strong>her encounter with the clones ... and speaking of which, gotcha, I was sure nobody would</strong><br />
<strong>fall for that since I'd pulled basically the same stunt in The Pure and the Profane when the </strong><br />
<strong>Quarrymen smashed Hollywood ... her disturbing relationship with Demona and Jericho ... </strong><br />
<strong>all of it adds up to a really strange series of events.</strong><br />
<strong>     I still don't know what it is about guys and the idea of two girls ... but damn, judging </strong><br />
<strong>by the letters, that scene with Demona and Godiva in the bathtub struck a collective </strong><br />
<strong>nerve like nothing since the idea of MacBeth and Aiden-in-schoolgirl-uniform.</strong><br />
<strong>     Another weird develpment in this one involed T.J. His experience with the Weird </strong><br />
<strong>Sisters must've warped him more than I thought ... he's never seemed all that interested </strong><br />
<strong>in girls since, his relatiopnship with Birdie is occasionally flirty (on her part) but platonic </strong><br />
<strong>... he strikes me as being oddly asexual (odd as hell in my world, lemme tell you!). But in </strong><br />
<strong>this one, he was actually repelled, grossed out, beyond uninterested and into the realm of </strong><br />
<strong>anti-sexual. Not sure what's happening with that boy; I will have to keep an eye on him.</strong><br />
<strong>     On an additional note, the early parts of this story were  tough for me because I know </strong><br />
<strong>very little about computers and technology. Thank you, Tim, for making sure I didn't put </strong><br />
<strong>my foot in it too badly! </strong><br />
<strong>     Also, the <a href="http://members.xoom.com/Sabledrake/obiwan.html">"Obi-Wan is Hot!" site</a> mentioned by Lex does exist; Birdie and I have a lot </strong><br />
<strong>in common and it was a treat to be able to poke fun at myself when T.J. was appalled at </strong><br />
<strong>the story ; )</strong><br />
<strong>     For inspiration on certain scenes, thanks go out to Star 101.5 and their "Saturday </strong><br />
<strong>Night Fever" disco tune program.</strong><br />
<strong>     Memorable lines:</strong><br />
<strong>     "Don't call me lovebunny!"</strong><br />
<strong>     "Try to do a friend a favor for his bachelor party, and --</strong><br />
    <strong>You louse! Punch to the stomach. You cad! Clout to the head. You rotten </strong><br />
<strong>no-good son of a --</strong></p>
<p><strong>59  Beth of Both Worlds</strong><br />
    <strong>This has got to hold some sort of a record for the longest-put-off thing I've ever written. </strong><br />
    <strong>Unless we count ElfLore Book I, which was about 1/3 done when Becca was born and </strong><br />
<strong>then I only looked at it maybe four times in as many years. But for the record, ElfLore </strong><br />
<strong>Book I is now actually finished, thank you, thank you ::bows::</strong><br />
    <strong>Way back a lot of stories ago, I wrote Whirlwind, in which Coyote invited Beth to a bring-your-own-mortal party on Avalon. Then, for funsies, I threw it open to the rest of my </strong><br />
<strong>fellow fanfic authors, inviting them to submit their own versions of such a party. Pick a fae, </strong><br />
<strong>pick a mortal, and have a good time. The results would be posted in Avalon Mists.</strong><br />
 <strong>   Grand idea! It worked wonderfully! Quite a few people joined in, sending stories ranging</strong><br />
<strong>from the touching to the hilarious. And while everyone was having such a good time with it, </strong><br />
<strong>I was able to think about other stories and neglect my own ideas of how said party might </strong><br />
<strong>have gone. Beth and Coyote were far from my mind, and getting further.</strong><br />
<strong>    Then the deadline for Avalon Mists was upon me and I realized no one had sent a Party </strong><br />
<strong>on Avalon story for that issue. Whoops! I had to scramble, scramble fast. I knew the title</strong><br />
<strong>(yes, it's a groaner, whaddaya gonna do?) and had a very vague basic premise in mind -- </strong><br />
<strong>Beth Maza is called upon to save not only Avalon but Earth as well. </strong><br />
    <strong>Considering how little preparation went into it, I am very pleased with how this one came </strong><br />
<strong>out. It's not great, but it'll do. </strong><br />
    <strong>Beth and Coyote happened  back before I knew where the Demona and Jericho storyline </strong><br />
<strong>was going, sort of my first foray into a quasi-incestual relationship. He's <em>not</em> her relative, but </strong><br />
<strong>he <em>looks</em> like her father ... very Freudian, a little weird. </strong><br />
    <strong>I still think Oberon's a twerp ... that might come through just a wee bit in some of my stories ... </strong><br />
    <strong>So I'm writing along, trying to think of how Beth can save the cosmos, and it just wasn't</strong><br />
<strong>working out. She wasn't seeming much of the self-sacrificing type, and Coyote was bound </strong><br />
<strong>and determined to interfere. That paved the way for the division of her into two seperate but </strong><br />
<strong>shared selves, Beth and Mai. She hasn't yet told anyone about that, not even her sister. It's </strong><br />
<strong>all too hard to explain. </strong><br />
    <strong>Because this one actually took place back before Elektra left Avalon, I was able to let her </strong><br />
<strong>be a part of it. And I got to play more with Corwin, who is probably my favorite of the clan </strong><br />
<strong>left on Avalon. </strong><br />
    <strong>Memorable lines:</strong><br />
    <strong>"Everything I Need to Know About Life, I Learned on Avalon,"</strong><br />
    <strong>"Coyote, explain yourself! Does this little bedwarmer of yours bear your seed?</strong><br />

<p><strong>60  Revenge of the Amazon Women</strong><br />
<strong>     I could not believe it when I went back a year to see how many stories I'd written </strong><br />
<strong>between the first appearance of the Amazons and this birthday sequel. Twelve! Only </strong><br />
<strong>twelve! What the hell had I been doing with my time? Prior to that, I'd been averaging </strong><br />
<strong>two stories a month! </strong><br />
    <strong>Then I realized that in the intervening time, I had also written an entire novel (Black </strong><br />
<strong>Roses), most of another (ElfLore Book I), a few Silver Flame stories, and my Star Wars </strong><br />
<strong>fic. I reasoned that under those circumstances, I could forgive myself ...</strong><br />
    <strong>At any rate, here it was September again, and I'd gotten quite a few wondering requests </strong><br />
<strong>over the last year, wanting to know just what had really happened between the lady pilot </strong><br />
<strong>and the drugged, hallucinating Jericho. </strong><br />
    <strong>I thought it was time to set the record straight. And the title, naturally, presented itself </strong><br />
<strong>without question. But I needed a few more Amazons, and as most of them had been </strong><br />
<strong>figments of Jericho's imagination (helped along by pin-ups), I needed some "real" women. </strong><br />
<strong>Did I have any Amazons in my cast? Why, I sure did ... well, one Amazon and a Valkyrie ... </strong><br />
    <strong>So I went back to Club Gung-Ho, picked up Tora Hawke and Inge Runolf, and then </strong><br />
<strong>the mayhem got underway in earnest. The woman of many aliases, code-name Golden,</strong><br />
<strong>wanted a re-match. That meant capturing Jericho. <em>That</em> meant catching him alone.</strong><br />
    <strong>For those who are curious to know what was going on in Birdie's apartment, stay tuned </strong><br />
<strong>for future stories (Aiden will explain a bit about her part in The Boy With the Healing </strong><br />
