...Well, gang, the SMALLnet input has been rather slow lately. That's fine with me: I have plenty of stuff to keep me busy. However, the lack of material means that Postings are widely-spaced...

I need to bring up a point about submissions, however. Sending these in the form of "Attachments" can make things tough at my end. The best submissions from my standpoint are plain e-mailed documents. Those require the least editing and re-formatting. Also, they transfer quickly. "Attachments" take FAR longer to download, because they use many thousands of "bytes" of transmitted infor- mation (as opposed to a few hundred for the average SMALLnet item in a Posting). And guess who has to strip away all those thousands of extraneous data "bytes" in order to come up with the relevant text therein...

...Another point that needs to be made is that the SMALLnet web site "Sources" index is no longer up to date. The man in South Africa who was handling the Sources Index dropped out some time ago. That means that out-of-date addresses are still on the list -- and that some items -- maybe MANY -- are no longer available.

A case in point is Howell H. Gwin Jr.'s offer over a year ago of plans for Goldberg's "Junior Falcon". He had ONE set of plans -- which were snapped up immediately. Howell's name should NEVER have been included in the "Sources Index".

This Fall Bill Lee and I will collaborate on a new way of handling the "Sources Index". Until then, both Bill and I are WAY too busy to make any corrections.

...In the postal mail last week arrived the latest in my old friend Bill Hannan's slender compendiums of intriguing model material. This is "Models & Methods International". It contains lots of photos and text information on the topic of small-sized flying scale models -- including full-size plans (readily Xerox-scalable to small R/C dimensions) of such projects as Leon Tefft's home- built "Contester" lightplane, a 1913 OTTO "Renneindecker" (a German 2-seater "racing monoplane" that won the 1913 "Round Berlin" event at a speed of 100 km per hour), the 1912 Avro "G" cabin biplane (in which the world's first aerial typing was performed!!) and a modern raceplane, the Monnet "Sonerai I".

Also included in Bill's new booklet is a feature of the pre-WW1 Antoinette monoplanes. (A modern replica of one of these was the "British Entry" in Magnificent Men & Their Flying Machines.) There's a full-size plan for a tiny indoor rubber-powered "sorta-scale" Antoinette-styled model. I built and flew one of these some 20 years ago, modified just a little via a more "vintage-looking" landing gear. It was scads of fun -- and now reposes (I think) on top of the big electronic basketball scoreboard in the fieldhouse gym at Westminster College.

Bill's new book also includes a reduced-size plan of his scale rubber- powered 1909 Antoinette -- which also has details of the "Magnificent Men" replica variations.

If you're tired of the same old, same old model airplane designs that fill all too many model field skies today, "Models and Methods" will give you fresh (mostly pre-WW1) project ideas. \[JW\]

Chuck Clemans \< 

 \> reports:

I've just finished building the Crickit from the kit by Mud Duck Aviation. The quality of the kit was excellent, with sharp die- cutting of foam board parts and laser-cut balsa and plywood parts. The instruction book is very complete and well written.

Innovative engineering and a very complete kit make this a fast build. I used mostly 5-minute epoxy, with some CA and carpenter's glue.

I'm looking forward to flying Crickit as soon as the weather clears. We're having a bit of rain for the 4th of July here in Seattle. Not unusual!

My Crickit is powered by one of Eric Clutton's PAW .09 diesels, with a 2-ounce tank. The radio is one of the new JR 610M micro receivers with 241 micro servos and 270 mAh battery. There's plenty of room for a larger receiver and battery, but micro servos should be used because of their location in the tail.

Weight ready to fly is 1 pound 15 ounces. Paint was Krylon OSHA Yellow with Ultra Kote clear for fuel proofing. No additional weight was required to balance.

This is my first Diesel, so I plan on running a couple more tanks on the bench before heading to the field. Thanks to the good information on the SMALLnet and talks with Eric Clutton and Ed Carlson, I'm learning about diesels.

I'll get back to you after first flight.

Happy Landings, Chuck Clemans

Brian Allen \< 

 \> contributed:

Was just reading Posting 326 when I noticed someone asking about a 12-volt power supply for a small foam cutting bow. A friend of mine does foam cutting, and he recently did a demonstration at our local club meeting. He showed a homemade bow (several pieces of dowel and a section of 0.015 inch music wire for the cutting wire).

For the power supply he used a discarded computer power supply. There is a 12-volt tap off this supply, and he hooked that to the bow, using lamp cord I believe. He spent the evening showing people how to cut white and pink foam using this setup. He mentioned that power supplies can sometimes be obtained for free from PC repair places. The power supply is limited in the amount of amps it can output, so you are somewhat limited in bow size and cutting speed. But for a few dollars you can make up a very nice foam-cutting rig.

Milton Dickey \< 

 \> wrote:

I found a throttle sleeve for my spare Cox Tee Dee .020 and test ran it over the holidays. I AM AMAZED! This thing throttles down VERY low, and responded very well from the moment I cranked and needled it!! I hope to have a semi-scale .020 WWI bipe built and flying for the next Maumelle event.

I've been doing a lot of work with throttled TeeDee .049's over the last year or so and had a lot of trouble taming them. I have great running, reliable engines now but had to: (1) Use the "Twin slit" (exhaust port) cylinders that have no free-porting, mill out the center bridge, and select fit/lap an exhaust throttle sleeve.

\(2\) Bush the venturi bore down to .093" & reshape the venturi, with aluminum tubing & JB Weld. (3) Use a Babe Bee glow head with 4 or 5 washers. (4) Use Byron's 1/2A fuel with extra 1/2 ounce of castor & 1/2 ounce of Klotz.

Like it says on my take-to-the-field engine box: "He who dies with the most GOOD Cox engines wins!"

Thanks again! Milton Dickey
