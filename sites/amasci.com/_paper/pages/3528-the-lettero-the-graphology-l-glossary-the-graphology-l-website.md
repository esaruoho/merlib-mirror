# The Letter~O

<span id="ogham">Ogham</span>  
Script used in Ireland and Northern Britain between 500 and 700 CE. Letters consist of a line crossed with short lines.

<span id="open_form">Open Form</span>  
All formations in writing in which the line does not enclose the entire inner part of the formation.

<span id="operational_definition">Operational Definition</span>  
A defintion which explains a concept solely in terms of the operations used to produce and measure it.

<span id="opish">Opish</span>  
English that has been altered by the addition of *OP* after each consonant. For example. Wednesday becomes wopedopnopesopdopay.

<span id="ordinal_numbers">Ordinal Numbers</span>  
Numbers used to represent position or order, in a series

<span id="ordinal_scale">Ordinal Scale</span>  
Measurement scale that requires observations to be ordered or ranked according to the degree to which they reflect the attribute being measured.

<span id="ordinal_scale_correlation">Ordinal Scale Correlation</span>  
The degree to which two the variables correlate with each other, if their scores have been assessed using ordinal scale mesurements.

<span id="ordinate">Ordinate</span>  
The vertical axis of a graph.

<span id="orpiment">Orpiment</span>  
Arsenic trisulphide. A gold like coloring material, which sparkles like mica. Commonly used in Illumination of manuscripts. This substance is also known as arsenic trisulphide.

<span id="orthography">Orthography</span>  
Conventional spelling of texts and their principles

Ostraca  
Unglazed piece of pottery, used for writing on.

> The name derives from the Greek practice of holding secret ballots, to decide who were regarded as being too powerful, or too dangerous to the democracy. Those names were written on an ostracon, and placed in urn. If anyone received too many votes, s/he was condemned to banishment from Athens for ten years.
