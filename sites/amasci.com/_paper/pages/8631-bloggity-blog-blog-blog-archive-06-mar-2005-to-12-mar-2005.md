<p>As a reminder: Almost every sketch in my sketchbook was non-commissioned and done on the floor of a con. These are not the best works of the artists. These are only representative of what the artist can do under pressure in a loud, crowded, and often stressed-out environment. Most of them were done as quickly as possible, to prevent huge lines from forming. Don't judge any of these artists negatively by the artwork you see, instead be as impressed as I am by what they accomplished in far-from-ideal conditions.<br />

<p>Friday, March 11, 2005 <span id="111057583184105534"></span></p>
<strong>More Rapid Reviews</strong>
<p><span class="rss:item"><strong><a href="http://www.amazon.com/exec/obidos/ASIN/1401206565/theaquamanwebsit">Bizarro World</a></strong>: I had higher hopes for this one. It was ok, but a lot of the stories just fell completely flat. It was like the creators were trying a little too hard to wring as much as they could out of the superhero concept. I hate to say it, but the best story may well have been the one with the kids of the Justice League. The one story focused on Aquaman was pretty pathetic, even if the art was nice. Overall, a neutral from me. <a href="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/rapidreviews.html"><img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/3_0_starfish.jpg" data-border="0" width="75" height="15" alt="3 starfish" /></a><br />
<br />
<strong><a href="http://www.amazon.com/exec/obidos/ASIN/1401203116/theaquamanwebsit">It's A Bird</a></strong> by Steven T Seagle and Teddy Kristiansen. This reads like a very long introduction to a particularly excellent story-arc of Superman. Which it may well be in some ways. But the story is barely about Superman, except how he impacts the life of a writer who gets the plum job of writing him. This is much more a story about coming to grips with life... and death. It's an excellent book. Recommended. <a href="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/rapidreviews.html"><img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/4_0_starfish.jpg" data-border="0" width="75" height="15" alt="4 starfish" /></a><br />
<br />
<strong><a href="http://www.amazon.com/exec/obidos/ASIN/1401203086/theaquamanwebsit">Teen Titans: A Kid's Game</a></strong>: It took me awhile to remember the lousy "event" that led up to the start of this series, but there were more than enough hints along the way to remind me once I got into it. Most of my complaints with this series have to do with things that happened before it came out. It was hard for me to divorce those opinions and take this book on its own merits. That said, I think I liked it. I plan on checking out the next collection from the library. This group is still not, and never will be, the Titans in my opinion, but they aren't a terrible bunch of kids after all. <a href="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/rapidreviews.html"><img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/3_5_starfish.jpg" data-border="0" width="75" height="15" alt="3 1/2 starfish" /></a><br />
<br />
<strong><a href="http://www.amazon.com/exec/obidos/ASIN/1563899809/theaquamanwebsit">Y: The Last Man - Unmanned</a></strong>: I had read bits and piecs of this before, but sitting down and reading it all at once made it read <em>so</em> much better. I still have a few problems with the general premise, and I expect that some of the mystery will get solved fairly soon... in the next three collections maybe? This is a decent start, and I can see why the series quickly became popular. <a href="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/rapidreviews.html"><img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/4_0_starfish.jpg" data-border="0" width="75" height="15" alt="4 starfish" /></a><br />
<br />
<strong><a href="http://www.amazon.com/exec/obidos/ASIN/075660592X/theaquamanwebsit">The DC Comics Encyclopedia: The Definitive Guide To The Characters of The DC Universe</a></strong>: I had high hopes for this. I admit I was mildly disappointed when I read the subtitle and found that this so-called encyclopedia was limited to characters, but oh well, I can survive. First thing I did when I got this home from the library is check out the Aquaman-Family, since that's the group I know the best. Aquaman gets a decent two-page spread, but I notice that it's seriously lacking pictures of Peter David's version of the character. It's definitely focused on the present. Ok, I can live with that.<br />
<br />
Next up would be Aqualad... so I flip back a page and ... nope. No Aqualad. Not even a redirect to "Tempest". There is, however, Aquagirl. Not entirely accurate, but close enough. Ok, time to look up Tempest. One-page for him, and not bad overall. Ok, next would be Mera. She gets a small entry, like Aquagirl. Again not bad. Next up would be Vulko. Of course he's not there.<br />
<br />
Ok, how about some Aqua-villains? Black Manta is in here, and has a good half-page. More than Ocean Master, at least, who also makes an appearance. Wait, Atlan gets an entry, but Vulko doesn't? Ug. WHAT? Charybdis gets an entry? At least they didn't put him under "Piranha Man". Corona... she appeared in like two issues. Dolphin's here. At least she deserves an entry. Oh dear... The Fisherman got an entry... The Atlantean sorceress Gamemnae got an entry... The Lady of the Lake got an entry. I like her occupation: "Water spirit". Ok. Flipping on...<br />
<br />
Lori Lemaris and Little Mermaid both got entries, but they aren't really Aquaman family members, so I'll just move on... water elemental Naiad got an entry... Neptune Perkins... wait, <em>Prez</em>??!?? You gotta be kidding me... Ahem... Qwsp got an entry, but not Quisp (no, I don't think they are the same character)... The Red Tornado entry doesn't have a picture of Ma Hunkel, though it describes her. She should have got her own entry... back to the water, the Sea Devils are listed... The Shark... The Thirst gets an entry? Again, focusing on the modern, which will make this book out-of-date faster. Actually, it's already out-of-date... Tsunami, but not her daughter Deep Blue... ok... that's the book.<br />
<br />
Overall, not bad, but it lacks some of the minor characters while including others. The coverage is uneven. And, yeah, it's already out-of-date. This would be much more useful as a Wiki or on-line encyclopedia maintained by DC. Still, it's fun reading, and I would love to spend some time just reading about the more bizarre characters. Too bad they had to be selective and didn't include a lot of them. DC's history is full of fun stuff to cover. I'd like a copy, but I'm not desperate for one. I think I'll leave it on <a href="http://www.amazon.com/o/registry/1LQ0LSAUK0S6I">my wishlist</a>, after all. <a href="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/rapidreviews.html"><img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/3_5_starfish.jpg" data-border="0" width="75" height="15" alt="3 1/2 starfish" /></a></span></p>

<strong>Various Non-Comic Book Rapid Reviews</strong>
<p><span class="rss:item"><strong><a href="http://www.amazon.com/exec/obidos/ASIN/0439372976/theaquamanwebsit">Chasing Vermeer</a></strong> by Blue Balliett, illustrations by Brett Helquist. A decent little book with some twisty mystery and a bit of genuine art history thrown in for the fun of it. While it seems at moments that it's trying to be the Da Vinci Code for kids, it doesn't lean that far into the realm of impossibility. On the other hand, <a href="http://forteantimes.com/mag_info/about.shtml">Charles Fort</a> is a major aspect of the plot, and not entirely in a Fortean way. I liked it, and kids will enjoy it, but it's probably a bit light for many adults, who will find the way the plot wraps up a little disappointing. <a href="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/rapidreviews.html"><img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/3_5_starfish.jpg" data-border="0" width="75" height="15" alt="3 1/2 starfish" /></a><br />
<br />
<strong><a href="http://www.amazon.com/exec/obidos/ASIN/0345461517/theaquamanwebsit">The Coming of Conan the Cimmerian</a></strong> by Robert E. Howard. An extremely well-done compilation of the early Conan stories, with a nice chunk of scholarly excerpts and information, including maps, in as an addendum. This book is the perfect introduction to Howard's creation, and the place for a beginner to start. Or for a person whose only exposure to Conan so far has been the Kusiek/Nord comic book series. If there is any problem with this book, it's that Nord's artwork on the comic has spoiled me so much that the illustrations in this book, good as they are, are disappointing in comparison. Highly recommended. <a href="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/rapidreviews.html"><img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/4_5_starfish.jpg" data-border="0" width="75" height="15" alt="4 1/2 starfish" /></a><br />
<br />
<strong><a href="http://www.amazon.com/exec/obidos/ASIN/0345461525/theaquamanwebsit">The Bloody Crown of Conan</a></strong> by Robert E. Howard. Only three stories, compared with the dozen or so in the previous volume, but wow, what stories! In addition, there are synopsis of never written stories, and fragments from unfinished tales, along with explanations of why the stories were written as best as scholars can figure. I can hardly wait for the third volume of this series, which won't be out until next year some time. Definitely a must for any Conan fan, or anyone even mildly interested in the history of the REAL Conan. <a href="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/rapidreviews.html"><img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/4_5_starfish.jpg" data-border="0" width="75" height="15" alt="4 1/2 starfish" /></a><br />
<br />
<strong><a href="http://www.tvtome.com/Farscape/">Farscape</a> [2-09]: Out of Their Minds</strong>: An alien weapon causes the crew members to switch bodies. Hubby-Eric has a pet peeve with this particular sci-fi TV cliche. Namely, the voices always switch along with the bodies. From a dramatic point of view, this allows the audience to recognize who is in which body, but from a physical point of view it makes no sense. So hubby would be happy to watch this one, in which the voices are only enhanced for a short time after the first switch, after that it's <em>all</em> the effort of the actors, and their actions as characters. Yes, they do pull a little trick to make it easier on themselves and the audience. As for the bad guys, aren't they refugees from <em>Dark Crystal</em>? If that doesn't float your boat, try <em>Villains of the DC Universe Glow-In-The-Dark Sticker Book</em> (392).<br />

<a href="http://www.bbc.co.uk/cult/doctorwho/"><img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/p200503_DWCybermen.jpg" data-border="0" data-align="left" width="100" height="150" /></a>I like the new Doctor Who T-shirts in the Apparel section (425). I think I would like the Dalek shirt the most, but the Cybermen shirt with Tom Baker isn't bad.<br />
<br />
<a href="http://chocolatefactorymovie.warnerbros.com/"><img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/p200503_CharlieAndChocolate.jpg" data-border="0" data-align="right" width="100" height="150" /></a>In the poster section, I noticed the <em>Charlie &amp; The Chocolate Factory</em> poster (481). I have to admit that I hate the <em>Willy Wonka</em> movie from way back when, because when I first saw it as a child I thought all the bratty kids were killed, and had nightmares about them for a long time after.<br />
<br />
So that's enough... time to jump back to the beginning of the book to hit the "big four"<br />
<br />
<a href="http://marvelcomics.com/">Marvel Comics</a> (178) is the usual stuff. I think I'll be getting <em>Incredible Hulk #81</em>, <em>Shanna, The She-Devil #4</em>, <em>Strange #6</em>, <em>Amazing Spider-Man #520</em>, and <em>Powers #11</em>.<br />
<br />
<a href="http://www.imagecomics.com/"><img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/p200503_Pact.jpg" data-border="0" data-align="left" width="100" height="150" /></a><a href="http://www.imagecomics.com/">Image Comics</a> (133-176) is a bit more difficult, as usual. I'm interested in <em>Hero Camp #1</em> (142) which seems to be borrowing some mojo from PS238 in that the main character is a non-powered son of super-powered parents. But what really gets me is the name of the camp: Camp Enokchuk. Heh. It's a four issue mini, and it has a chance of making the list. <em>PVP #0</em> (146), being only 50 cents, will make the list. <em>Lullaby: Wisdom Seeker #4</em> (155) features a twisted version of Oz's Tin Man. This series has the potential to really stink. Next up is <em>Noble Causes #11</em> (157) which is my favorite new super-opera series. And hey, <em>The Pact #2</em> (158) is also coming out, which features the Noble's vacation home. And not a thing in the Top Cow section interests me.<br />
<br />
<a href="http://darkhorse.com/"><img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/p200503_SamuraiExecutioner.jpg" data-border="0" data-align="right" width="100" height="150" /></a>Next we hit <a href="http://darkhorse.com/">Dark Horse</a> (21-56). Still loving the <em>Conan</em> books, and I'll be getting both the regular book <em>Conan #16</em> (26) and the mini <em>Conan and the Jewels of Gwahlur #2</em> (27). I'll admit that the solicit to <em>Concrete: The Human Dilemma #6</em> (30) makes me want to go find all the Concrete stories and read the whole lot even more. But I won't be getting it this time, instead I'll get <em>Usagi Yojimbo #84</em> (30) which will no doubt be excellent and interesting. I'm wondering how many volumes of <em>Samurai Executioner</em> (38) there will be. I'm enjoying them, but I do wonder. Not as many as <em>Lone Wolf and Cub</em>, right?<br />
<br />
And finally, <a href="http://www.dccomics.com/">DC Comics</a>.<br />
<br />
Batman (64-70): I just skipped the movie stuff, as I'll be skipping most of the Batman section itself. The cover to <em><a href="http://www.dccomics.com/comics/?cm=2942">Batman: Legends of the Dark Knight #191</a></em> is interesting, but I've always liked Mr. Freeze. But that's about all that catches my eye.<br />
<br />
<a href="http://www.dccomics.com/features/aquaman/index.html"><img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/aqua/pics/cvr2003/cvra430.jpg" data-align="right" data-border="0" width="200" height="300" /></a>Superman (71-78): I'm mildly intrigued by <em><a href="http://www.dccomics.com/comics/?cm=2969">Superman: Infinite City</a></em>, but not enough to pay $25 for it.<br />
<br />
DC Universe (79-97): <em><a href="http://www.dccomics.com/comics/?cm=2970">Aquaman #30</a></em> looks good, with the use of strands of DNA as chains, I wonder just what sort of plot twist to expect. I'm definitely warming up to Arcudi's writing. <em><a href="http://www.dccomics.com/comics/?cm=2971">Bird of Prey #82</a></em> will also make the list. We ended up ordering the first issue, so we'll probably get <em><a href="http://www.dccomics.com/comics/?cm=2974">Day of Vengeance #2</a></em>.<br />
<br />
I'm sad to see it ending, but I will get <em><a href="http://www.dccomics.com/comics/?cm=2976">Fallen Angel #20</a></em>. There's also a decent chance I'll be picking up <em><a href="http://www.dccomics.com/comics/?cm=2977">The Flash #222</a></em>, though no promises. I suspect hubby-Eric would have grounds for divorce if I didn't get <em><a href="http://www.dccomics.com/comics/?cm=2980">Green Lantern #1</a></em>, so I'll get it.<br />
<br />
<a href="http://www.dccomics.com/comics/?cm=2987"><img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/p200503_JSA.jpg" data-border="0" data-align="left" width="100" height="150" /></a>Speaking of Green, I'll stick with <em><a href="http://www.dccomics.com/comics/?cm=2985">Green Arrow #50</a></em>. I can hardly believe it's hit fifty issues. I'm annoyed that Aquaman didn't make the cover of <em><a href="http://www.dccomics.com/comics/?cm=2984">JLA #114</a></em>, but not surprised. I'll get it, yeah. And <em><a href="http://www.dccomics.com/comics/?cm=2986">JLA Classified #7</a></em>. Nice cover to <em><a href="http://www.dccomics.com/comics/?cm=2987">JSA #73</a></em>. And <em><a href="http://www.dccomics.com/comics/?cm=2989">Legion of Super-Heroes #6</a></em> is on the pull list now.<br />
<br />
Other books I'll be getting from this section include <em><a href="http://www.dccomics.com/comics/?cm=2991">Plastic Man #16</a></em>, <em><a href="http://www.dccomics.com/comics/?cm=2993">Teen Titans #24</a></em>, <em><a href="http://www.dccomics.com/comics/?cm=2994">Outsiders #24</a></em>, <em><a href="http://www.dccomics.com/comics/?cm=2996">The Rann/Thanagar War #1</a></em>, <em><a href="http://www.dccomics.com/comics/?cm=2997">Seven Soldiers: Shining Knight #2</a></em>, <em><a href="http://www.dccomics.com/comics/?cm=2998">Seven Soldiers: Guardian #2</a></em>, and maybe, just maybe <em><a href="http://www.dccomics.com/comics/?cm=3000">Villains United #1</a></em><br />
<br />
<a href="http://www.dccomics.com/dcdirect/"><img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/aqua/pics/toys/2005JLAGiftSet.jpg" data-border="0" data-align="right" width="200" height="300" /></a>Johnny DC (98-100): More no-brainers. I'll be getting <em><a href="http://www.dccomics.com/comics/?cm=3005">The Batman Strikes! #9</a></em> and <em><a href="http://www.dccomics.com/comics/?cm=3010">Justice League Unlimited #9</a></em>. I like the cover to <em><a href="http://www.dccomics.com/comics/?cm=3013">Looney Tunes #126</a></em>, but I won't get it. Probably.<br />
<br />
DC Library (101-108): I'm including CMX, 2000 AD, and Humanoids in this category. I continue to find nothing of interest in it.<br />
<br />
Wildstorm (109-119): Nothing for me.<br />
<br />
Vertigo (120-127): Again, nothing.<br />
<br />
DC Direct (128-132): GAH! A drought of Aquaman for months and months, then they have to put out TWO Aquaman figures (and a Black Manta figure) in one month. Do they hate me or something? While I fully plan on getting these, paying for them might be an issue.<br />
<br />
Luckily, the JLA set isn't out until earlier September, and the Aquaman and Black Manta figures aren't due out until late September. So I might manage to save up enough pennies by then.<br />
<br />
Notes I've seen indicate the JLA Set will retail at about $40. If we get it, I'll probably split it up and sell the Wonder Woman, Batman, and Superman figures while keeping Aquaman and Green Lantern. The Alex Ross figures will retail at about $16-20 each, and it looks like I can order them individually, though I'll have to check with my retailer about them to be sure.</p>
<a href="http://www.dccomics.com/dcdirect/"><img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/aqua/pics/toys/2005AlexRossJLA.jpg" data-border="0" width="300" height="300" /></a>
<p>So that's it for this month's "Things in Previews That Look Interesting But I Can't Afford To Get (with a list of things I am getting just for contrast)". Comments? Questions? Did I miss any really cool books that you know about and would like to point out? Do you disagree with something I wrote, and want to correct me? There's the comments, right below. Post something. And, yes, I accept review copies. I'm willing to give almost anything a try.</p>
