&#10;<h2 id="chapter-1">Chapter 1</h2>
<h3 id="the-advertising-policy">The Advertising Policy</h3>
<p>On November 6, 1996 the <a href="members.html">Seattle School Board</a> passed (5-2) the Advertising and Corporate Sponsorship Policy, <a href="D35_00">D35.00</a>, which would actively solicit corporate advertising and sponsorship in the public schools. The Seattle School District faces a $35 million budget shortfall over the next three years, and the school board hopes to generate 10 percent of the funds they need with this policy. The decision to allow more commercial presence in schools was made in a void of public input. As a result of the disregard for public process, two public forums were held in December 1996 and January 1997, which were attended by parents, students, teachers and citizens outraged at the new policy. The outcome of these forums led to the formation of the Citizens' Campaign for Commercial-Free Schools (CCCS). Our primary <a href="../MainOffice/index.html">goal</a> is to overthrow the Ad policy, as well as to eradicate other forms of corporate influence in the public schools.</p>
In response to the strong public outcry against their decision, on February 19th the school board decided to back down and put a two week moratorium on any action in regards to the policy. There will be a meeting on March 5th to work on a new policy with imput from the PTSAs (Parent Teacher State Associations). CCCS has made <a href="../Civics/recommendations.html">recommendations</a> for a process to write a new policy in regards to advertising. In addition to giving input for a new Ad policy, the CSS will continue to fight against current commercialism in the Seattle Public Schools.<br />
