<td width="100%"><h1 id="blackbird-question">Blackbird Question</h1>
<p>Expert Answer # 65</p>
<p><strong>Published</strong> January 31, 2002<br />
<strong>By <a href="mailto:Dhodge8888@cs.com">David Hodge</a></strong></p>
<h2 id="section"></h2>
<h2 id="question">Question:</h2>
<p>I have just seen your transcription of <a href="../../intermediate/20011101.html">Paul McCartney's Blackbird</a> which is very good however I have been having a debate with another guitarist over the tuning Paul used when he first did it, I read somewhere that it was played in open G tuning but the other guitarist says I am wrong and it has always been played in standard tuning as shown in most books, so I would be very pleased if you can shed some light on this matter. I look forward to hear from you very soon.</p>
<h2 id="answer">Answer:</h2>
<p>Thanks for writing. There's a lot of debate about this and I just don't know. I've tried playing it with open G and the fingering is just really too hard. But after you wrote I thought about it some more and I have an idea. Since the guitar is tuned pretty close to open G anyway and since the chords, okay the fingerings, are done primarily on the second and fifth strings, why not tune the first string down to D, that is, EADGBD tuning? This way you can play it throughout the whole song much as you do the open D (fourth) string. I've tried this and it does work. Again, you can hear that it's not the same but it does give more ringing strings and a nice<br />
overall tone.</p>
<p>I hope this helps. Thanks again for the email and I look forward to hearing what you think of this. If I don't chat with you between now and the first, I'd like to wish you, your family and friends a happy holiday season and a wonderful new year.<br />
</p>
<p><strong>David Hodge</strong> 2002-01-31</p>
