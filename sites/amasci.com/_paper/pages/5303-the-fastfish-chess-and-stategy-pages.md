> ### **Introducing Shahmot:**
>
> \
>
> > 
> >
> > <u>Shahmot</u> is a chess-variant\
> > by <u>Anthony Britton</u> and <u>Britton Enterprises</u>.\
> >
> > Shahmot uses the same moves and pieces as does chess. Where Shahmot differs from standard chess play is in the layout and configuration of the board, and the pieces on it. (See: the example of the [standard Shahmot configuration](#Shahmot_AlphaBoard) below.) As you would guess, Shahmot plays very much like chess. But, there are very important differences as well. Some are immediately appearent. Others are not so obvious. I have collected many of my (early) [impressions about gameplay](#Shahmot_GamePlay), and included them here.\
> >
> > What's in a name: [*SHAHMOT*](#Shahmot_name).\
> > There is a story behind it. *(follow the link to find out...)*\
> >
> > You can get <u>Shahmot</u> from several sources:\
> >
> > > Direct from [*Britton Enterprises*](#Shahmot_getit);\
> > >
> > > From [Three Trolls](http://www.3trolls.com/) Gaming's web-site;\
> > >
> > > From "<u>GAMES Magazine</u>" <span class="small">*(for more info: call 800-426-3768, or write "Games, P.O.Box 469077, Escondido, CA 92046-9077")*</span>\
> > >
> > > Or, visit your local gaming shop -- they may have it!
> >
> > \
> >
> > *(PLEASE NOTE: This is my initial effort in deploying this page. I have much to add. Please stop in from time to time to check in for (hopefully) regular updates. Or, [write me](#page_base) with your comments. I'd love to hear from you!\*
> >
> > \
> >
> > *-MLW* :)

> ### **Playing Shahmot:**
>
> \
>
> > 
> >
> > <u>Shahmot</u> is usually referred to as "a" game. But it is much more than that. First of all, two other fundimental variations are included: *"Alliance"* and *"Feudalism"*. (NOTE: "Feudalism" should not be confused with the strategic boardgame "Feudal", published by Avalon Hill.) So, it is really a <u>set of games</u> -- three(3) in all! But there is yet more...\
> >
> > The "Shahmot" set of games can be played on boards of differing sizes. Currently, Britton Enterprises offers three: the "Alpha", the "Delta", and the "Gamma". Each board is of consequatively increasing size from the Alpha - the smallest, and shown above - to the Gamma - the largest. Each game utilizes one or more configurations of the chess pieces per board, giving four(4) "levels" of play per game.
> >
> > The effect of this (atleast, it seems to me) is that I now think in more and different ways about chess than I did before. And as well, about the possibilities of strategy gaming in general. The result is an impression of "*Shahmot*" as not being so much "a game", or even a "set of games", but is instead a <u>Game System</u>! ...Which happens to be very much like the modern chess of which we are all familiar.\
> >
> > \
