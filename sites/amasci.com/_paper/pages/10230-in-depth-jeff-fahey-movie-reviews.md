## True Blood - Review by Tracy MacNab

\
\
I thought since I loved this movie enough to rent it 5 times and buy it as soon as I can, I would tell you about it, and let you decide for yourself how good it was.\
Stars:\
\
Jeff Fahey as Raymond TrueBlood\
Chad Lowe as Donny TrueBlood\
Sherilyn Fenn as Jennifer Scott\
Billy Draco as Billy (Spider) Mathers\
James Tolkan as the cop on revenge (name has slipped my mind right now)\
\
REVENGE IS THE ONLY TICKET OUT!\
\
Framed for the murder of a cop, Ramond Trueblood (Jeff Fahey) flees New York, abandoning his younger brother Donny (Chad Lowe) to go it alone on the streets.\
\
Ten long years later, Ray returns to find Donny overcome with rage and bitterness and caught up in turbulent and violent struggle with gang warlords. When Ray discovers his brother is a member of the rival gang that framed him for the cop's murder, he is determined to rescue Donny from a impending gang war. Ray sets out to rekindle his relationship with his brother and set the record straight by fingering the real cop killer.\
\
Well, that is what it will say on the back cover when you pick it up for the first time to read. But it does not do the movie justice.\
\
Here is two young boys (Jeff, 18, Donny, 7, or around there) in a struggle to survive while living in a run down apartment, with a father who drinks and caught up in a gang war. Raymong promises Donny that he will take him to see their mother in Ohio as soon as he can get the money for the train. He then puts Donny to bed and heads out to revenge his friend who got knived on their turf a few days earlier.\
\
Ray calls out Billy (Spider) Mathers, "Spiderman....you cut my friend and I am gonna bust you up." And procedes in just that. Ray gets in a few good hits, and knives Spiders face before the cops show up and they all scatter. Ray runs to the top of a building with a cop on his heels, and who does he meet up there? Mathers.\
\
Mathers has a gun and after getting Ray to his knees asks him who his the best now, and Ray stands up, Mathers shoots the cop standing behind Ray and takes off. Ray is caught with the gun by the cops partner (played by James Tolkan) Ray gets away and the cop promises his partner that he will get him.\
\
Ray runs home, gets his brother, and heads to the train station. Once there he is spotted by two cops and flees, leaving his kid brother all alone. Ray is chased in a car, and finally gets away.\
\
Ray returns 10 years later to get his brother, when he finds out that Donny is in the same gang a Spider Mathers. He realizes just how deep in his brother was, and that he needed to get him away.\
\
There is suspense, action, romance (Donny and Jennifer) and a true low budget Jeff Fahey movie. He does a very good job of acting and there are a few actors that you will recognize. If you like Jeff Fahey in blue jeans, short hair, (long at first), and on revenge then you will like the movie as much as me. (it takes a few watching to really enjoy it).\
\
There is so much to write that there just is not enought room. So go and rent this movie and see for yourself. It is interesting to see that Raymonds dad in the show is also Jeffs dad in real life!\
\
Trace\

## Backfire - Review by Trace MacNab

\
\
When I picked up this movie for the first time, it was a blend of excitement (because I found a Jeff movie) and pleasure at seeing Jeff Fahey act again.\
\
Jeff is definately a talented actor, and convincingly shows it in this murder mystery/suspence thriller.\
\
"Backfire"\
\
Before the night is over...someone will be rich, someone will be dead, and somone will be avenged.\
\
Starring...\
\
Karen Allen as Mara\
Jeff Fahey as Donny\
Dean Martin Jr. as Jake\
Keith Carradine as Reed\
Dinah Manoff as Jill (off of Empty Nest)\
\
Produced by Danton Rissner\
Directed by Gilbert Cates\
\
90 minutes....Rated R\
\
Karen Allen (Mara), is the wife who wanted more from life than poverty. Jeff Fahey is Donny, the husband that the war almost destroyed...Keith Carradine is Read, the stranger who answers everyone's darkest prayers. Dean Paul Martin is Jake, Mara's lover she wants more of...\
\
With this excellent cast only heightens the chilling pleasures of this crafted suspense thriller. Mara and Donny are the ideal couple, young, rich and attractive. Only thing wrong with this picture perfect marrage are Donny's (Jeff Fahey) horrible nightmares of Vietnam that has haunted his dreams for over 10 years.\
\
The fury of artillery, the screams of the wounded men and the ominous putt-putt of choppers rage with in him and suddenly, there is blood everywhere...\
\
The scene opens on Donny (Jeff Fahey) at war trying to get help as guns blaze and bullets shoot over his head and land nearby. He runs for shelter and watches as his Comrades get hit and die. Donny crawls through a stream to a friend who got shot and then the scene turns gruesome. Donny wakes up in sweat as he shakes and lights a cigarette with the help of Mara (Karen Allen) his wife.\
\
Jeff Fahey plays Donny, a Very rich man that was sent to the Vietnam war, as he was there he witnessed horror, hell, madness, torture, compassion which no one could understand unless you have been there too. He came back from the war over 10 years ago and every night since that day he has been living in a nightmare.\
\
Karen Allen is Mara, a poor girl who grew up and married a rich, handsome man. But decided she wanted more from her life than just being Donny's wife. She could not stand the nightmares that invated their lives, so she got herself a lover, Jake (played by Dean Paul Martin Jr.) and together they went about getting rid of Donny and having it all themselves.\
\
Keith Carradine is Reed, the drifter in town that Mara picks up in a bar one night and proceedes to seduce him. Running from the law, Mara convinces him to stay and help her get rid of Donny once and for all.\
\
Jill (played by Dinah Manoff) is Donny's sister who has come back to their home town to be with her brother.\
\
As time goes by strange things start happening to Mara and she is convinced that Donny is out to drive her insane. The mystery of it all, cause Donny is in a coma.\
\
This is a movie worth giving it at least twice to watch to catch all the happenings going on. I tried not to give anything away so you could enjoy it yourself. ****It is truly a movie about betrayal, deceit, and murder which are the fuel of the plot twists that ignite the tension to a shattering climax. I give this movie at least 3 start out of 5 for acting, interest, and ability to pull it off.\
\
Is That Only A Backfire Or Is Someome Firing Back?\
\
By Tracy MacNab\

## "Silverado" - Review by Trace MacNab

\
Well I finally had a chance to sit down and write a review on another one of my favourite actors Jeff Fahey in the movie smash SILVERADO.\
SILVERADO\
\
<img src="jftyree.jpg" data-align="middle" data-border="0" />Jeff Fahey in "Silverado"\
CAST\
\
Kevin Kline - Paden\
Scott Glenn - Emmett\
Kevin Costner - Emmett's hell-raising younger brother Jake\
Danny Clover - Mal\
John Cleese - Sheriff Langston\
Brian Dennehy - Cobb\
Linda Hunt - Stella\
Jeff Fahey - Tyree\
Jeff Goldblum - Stack\
Brion James - Mr. Hobart - (a small part)\
\
\
Directed and Produced by Lawrence Kasdan who did RAIDERS OF THE LOST ARK and two of the STAR WARS movies.\
Rated PG-13\
approximately - 132 minutes long\
\
Well as you can see this is a full cast line up of great actors that star in this fun-filled Western. The Western is back, the first major Hollywood Western in nearly a decade. SILVERADO achieved overnight status as a Western classic.\
\
Four unlikely cowboys who band together to defeat a corrupt sheriff and his vicious posse.\
\
The opening scene catches you into a whirl wind of action and adventure as Emmett (played by Scott Glenn) is awakened by 4 outlaws shooting at him. After the fight, Emmett heads onto SILVERADO and meets up with a very hot and thirsty Paden (played by Kevin Kline) who was taken at gun point and was removed of his clothes (except his red underwear), boots, horse, gun, hat and was left to die in the heat of the desert. The first words out of Padens mouth to Emmett were "Pleased to meet you", which got to me since seeing what he went through, he still had the manners of a gentleman...remarkable.\
\
Together they head onto SILVERADO and stop in a little town for bedding and a good meal. Emmett was supposed to pick up his younger brother Jake and head onto SILVERADO to see their family before heading to California. But Emmett's hell-raising younger brother Jake (played by Kevin Costner) was in jail because of kissing a girl and then defending himself against another man with a gun. Paden did not want to help Emmett get his younger brother out of jail, but then ended up in jail himself after a shoot out in the saloon. They got out with the help of Emmett and high-tailed it to SILVERADO with a posse on their heels. Sheriff Langston (played by John Cleese) was a delight to see a sheriff who, when the tough gets going, you turn around and go home!\
\
Which was exactly what he does after getting shot at from Mal (played by Danny Glover) who was the best shot around them parts. Together they rode off to SILVERADO and with one stop in between. This is where we meet up with Tyree (played by Jeff Fahey).\
\
Paden was in a gun store trying to buy a gun to shoot a man who had stolen his horse, and when he ran out in this long red underwear still trying to load the gun as the man jumps onto the horse, rides towards Paden, shooting, missing, (in the most funny place), then Paden raises his gun and shoots once kills the man, and gets his horse back.\
\
This is where he meets up with an old friend Cobb (played by Brian Dennehy), who years before used to work for Cobb as an outlaw. Cobb gives him some money to buy some clothes, and asked him to see something he might find interesting. They walked up to the jail and that is when Tyree (Fahey)emerges. Scruffy and mean looking. He mounts a horse, rides over to Paden and asks "Where's the dog?" then rides off. Paden is shocked to have seen Tyree. Cobb smiles, asks him to join them, Paden refuses and Cobb rides off.\
\
They finally make it into to town, meeting more stars on the way, Paden meets Stella (played by Linda Hunt) in a saloon, and becomes instant friends. He admired her, and respected her. Cobb is the sheriff of the town and offers Paden a job at the saloon. Paden accepts...\
\
There is a lot in this movie to write, and even more stars to meet along the way. But I would like to leave something for you to see for yourself...\
\
I usually find something wrong with a movie, either it was too slow, acting was not good, needed more action, etc. But to be honest in this movie I like it all, and wished they would have made a SILVERADO 2. Jeff was great as a bad guy and was fun to hate. I respected all the characters in this movie and thought they did an awesome job of acting.\
\
I give this movie a 5 star rating for creativity, acting and entertainment. You will not be disappointed in this movie. With all the stars to watch, it is just fun seeing who played in the movie as well as how well they acted.\
\
By Tracy MacNab\

## Impulse - Review by Holly Wonder

<img src="impulse.jpg" data-align="middle" data-border="0" />Teresa Russell and Jeff Fahey from "Impulse"\
\
I had the opportunity to watch "Impulse" several times and truly liked it so here is my review of the movie. There are a few spoilers but I don't give away the ending.\
\
Stars:\
\
Theresa Russell, (Lottie)\
Jeff Fahey (Stan)\
George Dzundza (Lt Joe Morgan)\
Alan Rosenberg (Charley Katz)\
\
Directed by Sandra Locke.\
\
Screenplay by John De Marco and Leigh Chapman\
\
"Impulse" is the second feature directed by Sandra Locke. Her first feature, "Rat boys" quickly dropped from view into obscurity, but she seems to have learned a great deal from that first film. This movie is good to look at and painfully intense a times---especially when we are asked to identify with Lottie (Theresa Russel) when she is looking at making a decision that will land her in trouble. You know the feeling? It's something you're not supposed to do, and you could get in deep trouble if you're caught, but you want to anyway. The appealing and darkly impressive part of this movie is that her character cries out for a chance for love, but she must make a decision between love and the opportunity of a lifetime. Temptation, in it's most seductive form, has thrown it's arm around her shoulders and lead her into a dangerous situation.\
\
Lottie is a vice cop working undercover. She dresses in chains, predictable slutty miniskirts and body hugging dresses. Her job is to work the bars and sidewalks where the johns are and lead them lustfully into the arms of the law, and when her backup support arrives to read them their rights, she quietly slips away.\
\
The problem is that it's dangerous and lonely and frustrating. She is a tortured soul who lives for her job but also is coming to believe in the code of the streets. In many ways she begins to prostitute her own self confidence by putting up with the slurs and the woman hating comments of her boss Lt Joe Morgan (George Dzundza). His eyesight seems to be pavolvian; when he sees an attractive woman he begin to salivate. He thinks a woman who dresses like a hooker, wants to be a hooker. Yes, Lottie is a good cop, but would she take these chances unless she "gets off" because she looks like a hooker?\
\
Into the picture steps Stan (Jeff Fahey). He's the good guy of the picture and is appealingly naive and protective. Yet , Stan also has his complexities. He is the determined Assitant District Attorney, who is obsessed with getting a king pin drug Lord called Luna. Luna actually plays a very small part in the film. He only plays one scene, but it is pivotal to the plot. I'll come back to this point later.\
\
One of the nights that Lottie is "working" a drug bust, things get dicey. She almost ends up dead (there is a pretty good suspense action sequence at this point). To further complicate matters, she is romantically involved with Stan, who has to unwillingly send her into these situations and is tortured about it. He realizes that she is a tough cop, but still he wants to protect her. After the bust she takes off, and because of car trouble ends up walking into a bar, tired and depressed. A guy offers her a drink, and one thing leads to another. He offers her money for sex. Should she do it? The thing is, she loves vice undercover because of the power it gives her over men. In a world where she doesn't have much respect or power this gives it to her. But now, no one will know. And it is here the plot steps in. The man she goes with is none other than Luna.\
\
Through more twists and turns she ends up changing her mind about going through with it with Luna. She's ready to walk out on him when, BAM! he's killed, by a mysterious asassin who she eludes. Suspense increases as she takes steps to hide her presence from the killer. She makes some mistakes that continue to become increasingly unwise and complicated and paints herself further and further into a corner. To further complicate the plot, she stumbles onto drug money which tempts her to the opportunity of a lifetime.\
\
In the meantime, Stan is increasingly obsessed with Luna. Now he wants the killer of Luna and the mysterious witness who reports the murder. He also falls in love with Lottie. And Lottie might be falling in love with Stan. This is the weakest point in the plot. Why doesn't she tell Stan? The story gives the idea that she wants the money, but no-one really knows the money exists but her. She could still tell Stan and still have a dilemma about the money and Stan's place in her life. For some reason the writers continued with the total secrecy plot. Anyway, we are left with the question, will she stay ahead of the experienced cops who may see through the whole tissue of lies at any moment? Will she survive the search for the asassin and the witness? In the end, all the loose ends are neatly tied up, and we are left with a satisfactory ending. However, as a friend of mine said, "What if you gave into the impulse and got away with it? Isn't that the risk in life?" I liked the ending, but it was totally Hollywood.\
\
The performances of Jeff as the uptight, obsessed, and sexy DA was pure joy. He played the subtle nuances of a man obsessed with justice. His values and morals are above reproach, yet he wants adventure and Lottie offers that to his character. He wants to walk on the edge, but Lottie really does walk on the edge. Theresa's character is darkly held in check. She does a great job of showing the internal conflict of her need for power yet the emptiness of her life. Personally, the scenes with her seeing a psychologist, Dr Gardener, (Lynne Thigpin) was a bit overdrawn. If a client came in acting the way Lottie did she'd probably be pulled off the force immediately. I think it would have been more reasonable to have Lottie put on a Facade of "being in control" with more subtlety, like becoming the "perfect cop".\
\
Personally I would give this movie 4 out of 5 stars. It's a keeper!\
\
Holly J Wonder\

## White Hunter, Black Heart - Review by Holly Wonder

\
\
<img src="jfhunt.jpg" data-align="middle" data-border="0" />\
Clint Eastwood and Jeff Fahey in "White Hunter, Black Heart"\
\
Rated: PG , 1990\
Clint Eastwood (John Wilson)\
Jeff Fahey (Pete Verrill)\
Charlotte Cornwell (Miss Wilding)\
George Dzundza (Paul Landers)\
Marisa Berenson (Kay Gibson)\
Edward Tudor Pole (Reissar)\
\
Directed and produced by Clint Eastwood\
Screenplay by: Peter Viertel, James Bridges, and Burt Kennedy\
\
Film directors have to be a little like God, convinced that they have the correct answer to every question, and the responsibility to impose their opinion on everyone around them. Nice-guy directors---those praised for listening to their colleagues, keeping an open mind, and encouraging their actors to improvise---rarely make great movies. Great directors are routinely described as mean sons of bitches, but there is often awe in the voices that describe them.\
\
"White Hinter, Black Heart", is the story of a director who was not a nice guy. The storyline of White Hunter was, in fact, based upon the real life director John Huston. By most accounts John Huston was not a nice guy. He ran roughshod over marriages, friendships, and responsibilities, and he had a habit of losing interest in a film project half way through. He indulged his passions for horses, drink, gambling, and women as if he had the divine right to pursue whatever caught his interest regardless of consequences. Nevertheless, in his final years, crippled by emphysema, he did present his daughter, Angelica, with the role that launched her acting career in "Prizzi's Honor", and he grew closer to his children, from their own accounts, and helped their careers.\
\
It was one of Huston's best known films, "The African Queen", that supplied the subject and setting in which "White Hunter, Black Heart" takes place. The story in "White Hunter" is centered upon the reflections and viewpoint of the young Pete Verril. In fact, the character of Pete Verril, which Jeff Fahey portrays, was based upon the young writer Peter Viertel, who Huston at the last minute took along on location while filming "African Queen". It was Peter Viertel who wrote the final version of the screenplay of "The African Queen".\
\
Viertel was a privileged observer to what later became one of the legendary shoots of Hollywood history, and his novel "White Hunter, Black Heart", was obviously based upon that experience. It did not portray a flattering picture of "John Wilson", the Huston character, who was more concerned with shooting an elephant than shooting a picture.\
\
It is Cint Eastwood who swaggers into the role of Wilson., using the mannerisms and accent of Huston. It must have taken some nerve for Eastwood to take on this role. It is difficult enough for a famous actor to disappear into a role usually not associated with his "type"; it is even more difficult to take on the role of another famous personality and convincingly portray it. In the open scenes of "White Hunter", it is a bit distracting to hear Huston's words and mannerisms emerge from Eastwood's mouth. At times Eastwood's acting seems to be overblown and his character's bravado is almost irritating. Then the performance takes over, and the movie turns into a thoughtful film about the internal conflicts of an artist. Wilson emerges as a strong-willed, intelligent, manipulative man, who nevertheless, places his own desires above his ideals. He rails at Hollywood's dishonesty and yet is arrogant enough to keep an expensive film on hold while he goes on the obsessive hunt for an elephant.\
\
Why does he want so badly to kill an elephant? Perhaps because he has never killed one before. Perhaps to prove to himself that he is brave. Perhaps to give the elephant the privilege of being shot by John Wilson. The obsession grows and wraps around him like a snake while he justifies his actions with evasions and self-importance and rationalizations until the character's motive isn't really important anymore; he is so obtuse and selfish that he will do it simply because he wants to do it. He has a shallow, selfish, black heart. He doesn't care what it takes; he wants what he wants, when he wants it.\
\
There is a lot more to "White Hunter, Black Heart", then this description suggests. I especially liked the verbal fencing matches between George Dzundza (as the producer) and Eastwood when they have dinner in the small cabin in Africa. Of course, Jeff Fahey (as the Verrill character) does a wonderfully subtle counterpoint to Eastwood's character, by acting as the quiet, introverted writer who wryly takes note of the confessions and confidences of the flamboyant Wilson. I found Jeff Fahey's acting in this picture as a great example of his ability to use simple eye contact to convey an entire emotional response. In many of his scenes he seems to steal the scene with a lift of his eyes or a hard stare. In many ways, Eastwood appears to overact compared to the subtlety of Fahey's performance. Was that the intention of Eastwood's direction? Did he want to compare the flamboyant aggressive nature of Wilson to the quiet subtle thoughtfulness of Verrill? If so, he succeeded. What the film comes down to is the examination of a man's obsession and indulgence in determined ego-gratification.\
\
This film is beautifully shot on location in Africa and London. The cinematography is stunning with wide expansive safari scenes. In many ways this feels like a foreign film and the pace is slower than most American audiences are accustomed to. If you are looking for an action/adventure film, "White Hunter" will be disappointing. On the other hand, if you are looking for a thoughtful film about the internal conflict and senseless actions of obsession this is the film to see. It is also the chance to see Jeff Fahey in a very different role which, in my opinion he develops with subtle intensity.\
\
Personally, I've give this film 4.5 out of 5 stars.\
\
Holly J Wonder\

## Wrangler - Review by Holly Wonder

\
\
"Wrangler" is a movie which caught my attention about one year ago. I highly recommend it. Hope you enjoy this review.\
\
Holly\
\
Wrangler (Also known as "Outback" or "Minnamurra")\
\
No MPAA Rating, 93m, 1992\
Directed by: Ian Berry\
Written and produced by : John Sexton\
\
Jeff Fahey (Ben Creed)\
Tushka Bergen (Miss Alice Richards)\
Steven Vidler (Jack Donaghue)\
Sandy Gore (Aunt Maud)\
Richard Moir (Bill Thompson)\
Shane Briant (Allenby)\
\
\
The "western" is back but this time it's in Australia! Like those old westerns that I remember as a child, this story was simple and roles easy to identify with. But unlike those old westerns this is essentially a story about a young girl's transition from spoiled girlhood into womanhood. Alice, portrayed by Tushka Bergen, struggles to come to terms with her dreams of love and her desire to save her home, Minnamurra, from the villainous Allenby (Shane Briant). To add spice to the pot, there are two suitors which offer to help her while also fighting for her hand. So like all good westerns, this one had the good guy, the bad guy, and the woman caught up in the struggle between them. But unlike those simple stories this one has many twists and turns which keep you guessing until the end about who will win her hand.\
\
The opening sequence in the screenplay sets the tone of the rest of the movie. Horses driven against the backdrop of the raging ocean waves when boom! the barge they were driving the horses toward blows up. Here we are introduced to Ben Creed(Fahey) and Jack Donaghue (Vidler), men from two different walks of life working together. Yet, they build immediate respect for each other when Jack asks "Trust me?" and Ben has no choice, his stock is at risk of being stolen so he follows Jack into the ocean and they drive the horses dangerously into raging surf. Jack (Vidler) is the reckless drover who Alice admires with her rebellious heart. Ben (Fahey ) is the powerful entrepreneur/business man who throws caution to the wind as he falls deeper into love with Alice. For Alice he risks everything, even his business. (As a note, for those of you who are avid Fahey fans, there are wonderful scenes of him looking suitably tough while decked out in compete cowboy gear . He also looks very debonair in a dinner scene in which he is dressed in a formal tux with high-point cravat. Very handsome!).\
\
At the center of this conflict is "Minnamurra" , an old estate set in the beautiful hills along the snowy mountains. Allenby (Briant) has been conniving to acquire the estate. For some reason he hates the Richard family. Why? I don't know. This is not sufficiently explained in the story . Suffice it to say, Allenby is NOT a nice guy. He is in the mold of the "Robber-Baron" villains of the old westerns. The guy everyone loves to hate.\
\
I will not tell you much of what happens next, but then perhaps, I do not need to. If you are familiar with the Western, you will be familiar with this one. What is strange is that, if there is any nostalgia connected to this film, it will be found in our hearts and not necessarily on film. Too many Westerns in the past twenty years have been elegies to the dead past (with the possible exception of "Silverado") played out by actors playing out their memories of the cowboy roles of their youth (remember if you can the last Westerns of Robert Mitchum, William Holden, John Wayne, Joel McCrea, Kirk Douglas). "Wrangler" contains a group of young actors not associated with the Western. In fact, it is the Western in Australia which makes the story seem more real and less mythic. And like those old Westerns, "Wrangler" is set at time when the Australian continent was open to the ruthless and the opportunistic , and when the bad guys were still stupid enough to be trapped by a stampede of horses, and when feisty women stood up to ruthless villains, and when the good guys could be outmaneuvered but never defeated.\
\
So what does it prove, this movie about the love of a woman for the land and the two suitors who love her, this movie about cowboys held together by honor, and the power love has to change a man's focus from acquiring wealth to protection of what he loves. What does it prove? That the Western myth is best home in a setting of innocence, and "Wrangler" understands this, and that somewhere in our hearts we long for the love that will overawe our doubts, give us strength to achieve justice, and hold us together in the face of troubles. Long live the Western!\
\
I'd give this film 4 out of 5 stars.\
\
