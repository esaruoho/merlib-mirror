# The Letter~D

<span id="dark_brown_ink">Dark Brown Ink</span>  
Made from gallic acid --- a substance obtained from gall nuts --- and mixed with iron salts to produce a purple blank ink, that fades to brown.

<span id="de_slant">DE Slant</span>  
Upslant of between 126 and 146 degrees in Graphoanalysis.

<span id="decode">Decode</span>  
To reverse the method by which a message was hidden.

<span id="demotic_script">Demotic Script</span>  
Ordinary writing of Egyptian Hieroglyphics

<span id="dependent_variable">Dependent Variable</span>  
This is the variable that is related to the phenomenon of interest. In theory, this really represents the process of behaviour being measured.

<span id="descriptive_statistics">Descriptive Statistics</span>  
Procedures employed to organize and present data in a convenient, usable and communicable form.

<span id="deseret_script">Deseret Script</span>  
Writing system created by Bringham Young, for use by adherents of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter Day Saints.

<span id="despondancy">Despondancy</span>  
This is the fear of lonliness.

<span id="devanagari">Devanagari</span>  
Script used for most languages found on the Southern part of the Indian sub-continent.

<span id="deviation">Deviation</span>  
The difference between the score, and the mean.

<span id="diacritic">Diacritic Mark</span>  
A mark added to a character to indicate a modified pronunciation.

<span id="digraph">Digraph</span>  
1.  A pair of letters denoting a single sound, eg *ph*, *sh*.
2.  A encipherment in which the *plaintext* is written using letter pairs.

<span id="digram">Digram</span>\  
A pair of letters.

<span id="dr_fox">Dr Fox Effect</span>  
An impressive presentation, with zero meaningful content. To dazzle with science, eloquance and humour.

<span id="dnealian_script">D'Nealian</span>  
Copybook distributed by Scott, Foreman and Company in the United States of America.

<span id="ductus">Ductus</span>  
1.  An individual style of handwriting, characteric of a small group of scribes or sometimes a single person. ( Daniels & Bright 1996 )
