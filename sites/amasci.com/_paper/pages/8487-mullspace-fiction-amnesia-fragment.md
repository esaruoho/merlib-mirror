## Amnesia (fragment)

\

A woman in the right clothes is more seductive than one blatantly nude. Clothes which hint at what lies beneath, that flow as smooth and liquid as a caress and lie like moist breath across the ridge of a collarbone or the curve of a breast can raise a man's temperature to an ache more surely than a virus. Her dress becomes an erotic sentence, punctuated, perhaps, by a fleeting flash of thigh, a fold falling open over a delicate and vulnerable wrist.

He had never seen her like this. Mother-naked, yes--several times. Dressed to kill, absolutely--with various types of killing in mind. This....

He stood just inside the doorway, a step to the left in the contrastly darkened gloom beside the light which fell in from the hallway. The dim room was pierced by a soft double pool of illumination. She stood in that light, bending over the bed to look at something. Glimmering, black silk trousers poured over her hips and down her stretched legs. A matching jacket, very short and oddly cut, clung over her shoulders and draped down like a curtain over her torso. The collars fluttered.

He'd entered silently and did not believe she'd heard him, yet she seemed to become aware of him. But she did not stand. Instead, she reached down to the satin spread, smoothing her hands over it, then letting the mattress brace her weight. Bent, taut and fluid, she turned her head, tossing her hair with a quick flick.

The pale strands had been restyled: shorter, sleeker, with a more exotic slant. They lifted with her movement and flowed across the nape of her neck. Her face seemed sharp and she stared at him from under her brows, over her shoulder. He stared back, silent, arms crossed over his chest.

"What are you doing here?" she purred.

"Looking."

"Looking for me?"

"Whatever I can find."

"Better be careful of what you find; it might belong to someone else."

He quirked an eyebrow and unlimbered his stance to light a cigarette. The yellow flame of the lighter cast a moment's glow on his face, sparking in his ice-white eyes. He let his first mouthful of smoke rise between their locked stares.

"Someone like Tadesko?"

She scoffed and straightened. The short jacket revealed an instant of pale, bare ribs and waist before falling into place, the elaborate, pointed folds of the layered collars flowing into shimmering black cataracts around her slender throat and down to a hint of shadow at the base of the narrow opening.

"Someone like me," she corrected.

She strolled toward him, the shadows between them wrapping around her, making her eyes seem to glow with a banked, sapphire flame. He unbuttoned his jacket and his hand hovered near the flap as she drew near. She stopped just next to him, so close he could smell the warm, subtle perfume of musk, gardenia and roses that clung to her skin.

She leaned forward, reached up and plucked the cigarette from his lips. Her soft sleeve barely brushed his jaw. His skin tingled. She leaned back against the wall as she brought his cigarette to her own glossy, dark lips.

He watched her draw the smoke into her lungs, the jacket tightening slightly. She watched him watching.

The smoke flowed softly back out of her mouth, rising like a slowly shredding pall between them. They stared at each other.

"You don't smoke," he reminded her.

Her eyes flashed hard, blue ice. Viciously, she flicked the burning cigarette past him, into the hall. "And around me, you don't either," she snapped.

He gave her a slow, narrow grin. Her answering half-smile curdled to an icy sneer and she swung away.

"Get out." She began to stride from him.

His hand clamped onto her wrist. "No."

She spun back, stepping into him, her captive arm pulling down and back, her free hand swinging forward to shove him over her hip.

He didn't fall for it. He stepped around with her momentum, catching the second hand as it passed his shoulder and whipping her under and around. He pulled her arms tight across the front of her body, straight-jacket style. Her shoulders pressed against his chest and the top of her head brushed his earlobe.

He grinned as he spoke into her ear. "Nice move. Too bad you're so predictable."

She was indignant, tense in his arms, whipcord and steel.

"Let me go," she hissed. "Or should I start screaming?"

Expelling a disgusted breath, he loosened his hold a little.

She dropped down suddenly, ramming her shoulders into his stomach and yanking down violently on his wrists.

Breath exploded out of him and, surprised, he went over her head. She spun, dropping one of his hands as he thumped onto his back. Holding the remaining wrist tight, she stepped over him and put one foot on his chest.

"Nice move. Too bad you're so cocky."

He snorted. "You haven't forgotten some things." His free hand closed lightly on her ankle.

She threw his other hand down and stepped back from him, suddenly, pushing off with the foot on his chest. He let her go.

She walked back toward the bed. He picked himself up and watched her go, with the slightly stiff pace of an irritated leopard. He didn't quite smile.

"I know who you are. I can remember that much. So why don't you answer my question before I lose my temper?"

"I don't remember the question."
