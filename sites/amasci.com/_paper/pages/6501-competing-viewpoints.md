**Free enterprise** is the right to conduct a legitimate business for profit, under usual laws of supply and demand, without undue government interference. ***Black's Law Dictionary, Sixth Edition, page 665.***

Views on free enterprise vary widely. They encompass virtually every human experience. This compendium attempts to state the major conflicts in clear terms.

**Cornucopians vs. Catastrophists**. Stephen Cotgrove introduced the notion that two competing viewpoints dominated the environmental debate, *Catastrophe or Cornucopia: The Environment, Politics and the Future*, John Wiley & Sons, 1982 Cornucopians are those who believe that man and nature can live together in productive harmony forever, with the earth providing abundantly for all people and minimal damage to life support systems. Catastrophists are those who believe that modern civilization is destroying the earth's living systems, with a total collapse of all life support systems imminent.

Are environmentalists catastrophists? Not all, of course. Only the most influential and the most radical. Consider this excerpt from a speech by Patagonia, Inc. founder Yvon Chouinard at the Waterfront Inn in Traverse City, Michigan, on Oct. 28, 1999, to the Michigan Land Use Institute. Chouinard has given millions to environmental groups from company earnings.

> **Yvon Chounard:** The definition of a consumer is one who destroys or expends by use, devours, spends wastefully. Maybe that IS us. What if this new fashion catches on? What if Mid-America, Japan and France decide that the right thing to do is reduce their consuming? As it is now we have an overabundance and overproduction of every thing we need and dont need in the world. And still we have poverty and unemployment. A typical western European consumes only a quarter of what an American does now. So its entirely plausible that an economic crash could occur just from a drop in American spending habits. So were destroying the world with consumption, yet if we stop its going to be a huge crash. Thats kind of a catch-22 isnt it? For years Ive been tormented by the realization that my own company depended on the consumer economy and is responsible for making some of this overabundance of goods. Although Ive tried in the past to limit this runaway growth, Ive always failed....The hottest places in hell are reserved for those who in times of great moral crisis elect to take a neutral stand. Well, my personal hot spot in hell will probably be fueled with aviation gas, which Ive burned more than my share of in my lifetime.

And consider this interview with Wren Winslow Wirth, president of her family foundation, the Winslow Foundation, which donates millions to environmental groups. She is married to Tim Wirth, former Clinton administration sub cabinet official and current director of Ted Turner's United Nations Foundation. This interview with Wirth by noted author Alex Shoumatoff is on the website of Dispatches from the Vanishing World (a project of Shoumatoff's), <http://www.dispatchesfromthevanishingworld.com/loss/loss3.html>, and reflects a classic catastrophist view:

> The people who are best informed about what we are doing to the creation, or the environment, are extremely worried. As Wren Wirth, a veteran of the environmental wars of the last twenty years, whose Winslow Foundation has provided a start-up grant for the Dispatches, told me several years ago, None of the long-term indicators are positive. Basically, we just arent moving fast enough. Nothing works. Local doesnt work, global doesnt work. Everything has a red flag on it. The big thing now is compromise. The timber industry wants to compromise with the environmentalists in the Pacific Northwest. But how can you compromise, when only 5% of the old-growth forest is left? Wren rattled off some horrific stats: Humankind is presently using 40% of the plant matter made by photosynthesis and 52% of the fresh water, and our population is about to double, at which time mathematically we will need more than twice the plant matter and fresh water. So we are headed for certain catastrophe.\
>    But even our horrific realization of the destruction underlying the modern world, the terrible cost of all this, is projection and illusion, my old friend argued, like a good Buddhist. But this doesnt give us license to harm, either. Harm goes on, and for those who suffer it it is absolutely real and awful, but harm itself is relative, so its delicate.\
>  

**Risk-takers vs. risk-avoiders**. Entrepreneurs take risks, with money, with resources, with lives. They compare risks with benefits in making action decisions. They value grace under pressure. They are willing to face potential ruin in order to gain great benefit. They face failure with determination and persistence. They take command in their lives, their work and their personal relationships. Motto: Good Enough Is Not Good Enough. Risk-aversives seek the least dangerous path in their lives, their work and their personal relationships. They respond primarily to fearful communications and are deeply suspicious of enthusiastic acts. They rise to anger but not to enthusiasm. They do not consider benefits to be gained if risks stand in the way of achievement. They fear risk and demonize risk-takers. They justify failure, then give up and require everyone else to give up. They block those who would take command by raising alarms and fears. Motto: It Will Never Fly.
