## The P-T Effect (fragment)

\

A ball of sub-atomic muck appears to be floating above the stainless steel box effortlessly, and humming to itself, probably something by the band Tesla, just to demonstrate the sauvity of this particular wad of particles.

"What's that?" the young man asks.

Strange looks up from her notebook computer and peers in his direction. "Uhmmm.... P-T effect generator. Don't touch it, please. The field is stable so long as nothing enters the outer effect shielding and I'd hate to have you warped across discontiguous parsecs of space-time out of pure curiosity."

"Oh.... What's a P-T effect?"

"The Phillips-Thorne time/space discontinuity theory posits that, under the right conditions, quanta may exist in any coordinate of space-time from any starting point within a specialized set of electro-magnetic fields congruent to another set of such fields in any other coordinate of space-time."

He frowns and scratches his head. "Does that mean it's a time machine?"

"Well, not really, though that particular application of the effect is sort of acting like one at the moment. Those electrons and photons and such in there are normally very short-lived critters, but the field generation holds them static so long as the field is in motion. So they remain unchanged. Very useful for particle physicists, who normally have to extrapolate the existence of very small leptons and bosons by the fleeting effects they make on collectors in accelerators and other sensing equipment."

"Errmmm... so it's just some kind of stasis field?"

"No. You can't contain particles of that type in a stasis field, since the field can only affect comparatively large bodies. Minutely small bodies, like those, continue to move through time, even when movement through space may be hindered. Since the life of quarks and bosons and things like that is incredibly short by our measurement, they vanish from the stasis field, because they die out and decay into other particles which pass out of the stasis field. But the P-T effect operating there holds the particles in the same quanta of space-time and they do not decay because, effectively, time and space are standing still for them. In this case, the two congruent electro-magnetic fields are acting in the same coordinate of space-time simultaneously, so the particles are contained in one place and time."

He stares at her with a bemused look.

She sighs. "Both of the fields are in the same place, holding onto the same ball of goo. The ball of goo can't go anywhere or change because, from the goo's point of view, there is nowhere else and nowhen else, only here and now, forever. Or until I turn off the fields. Then the damned thing will go 'pop' and vanish into atomic decay."

"Oh, so what's the point?"

"For now, not much. Costs so much to produce a P-T effect right now that no one can afford to use it for much. Eventually, if anyone thinks it's worth while and we can get past the size barriers, the P-T effect could be used to move spacecraft across galaxies in the blink of an eye."

"What about time-travel?"

"You'd have to have a P-T effect field operating in another time for the quanta to shift to it, so, although you could go forward in Time and back to the starting place, in theory, it's doubtful that the field generation would remain stable under those conditions, because of other quantum effects that come into play."

"What about multiple fields? Couldn't you sort of steer the effect?"

"Some people think something like that may be possible, but, right now, using more than two fields in the same location causes a toroid shape to the field and everything inside it becomes a sort of atomic donut. Speaking of which: aren't we supposed to go to lunch?"

"Yeah," he answers.

She rises, shutting down her machine, and removes her lab coat which she hangs by the door as they leave. He looks back over his shoulder at the static ball of sub-atomic goo as the door closes behind them.

"So," he starts as they cross the street. "When can we expect to go shooting across the universe cloaked in the P-T effect?"

"Probably not for another 20 years at this rate. We can smack quanta around in a lab all we want, but human beings and space ships are not quanta."

"Well, I know a quantum is small, but how small?"

She laughs. "Sub-atomic, usually."

"What about all that other crud that was hanging around in the field, then?" he asks, holding open the door to the restaurant.

"Just crud attracted to the field."

"Is it inside the field, with the quantum packet?"

"Yes, but only the quantum is affected by the field."

"How do you know? What's happening to the rest of the crud?"

She stops and stands still by the table they have just been shown to. "I don't know." She frowns.

"Who does? You should ask him."

"Can't," she answers, her voice gone soft. "The guy who'd know is Cramer Thorne or Malcolm Phillips, but they're both dead."

"What's the matter?"

"It's just weird... No one's ever said anything about it, that I know of it. But Thorne or Phillips must have noted it. But they never published anything about it. It's too bad you didn't mention this a month ago when Thorne was still alive."

"What happened to him?"

"Died, of course," she answers, sitting down.

"Oh, come on! Was he old? I mean how did he die? When?"

"No, he was about fifty. About a month ago, he was killed in a car crash. But he would have known all about the crud in the field, since he's the mechanical guy. Phillips was the theoretician and he died, oh, more than twenty years ago."

"Didn't Thorne leave any notes or anything? He must have been working on something when he died."

"I could check the databases, I suppose, but I'd always heard that Thorne was, effectively, retired. He had a nervous breakdown a few years ago and hadn't worked in a lab since. A real loss to physics, but he would sometimes answer e-mail about the P-T effect generator. One of my collegues had some correspondence with him. He was pretty upset when Thorney was killed."

"Would your collegue have any ideas about the crud?"

"Miller? If he did, he wouldn't tell me about them. He's paranoid. And he just hates the fact that my experiments have control of the P-T effect generator at the moment."

"Maybe the crud is going along with the quantum packet in the middle of the field."

"There's no reason to believe that."

"Is there any reason to believe it's not?"

Conversation at the table halts. Strange blinks at him. Finally she begins to answer, very slowly.

"Common wisdom holds that only the quantum at the center of the field is fully contained and controlled by the field, since it is a quantum effect, but I don't know that anyone has ever studied or theorized about the other matter that may be contained in or drawn into the field...."

She jumps up suddenly from the table and dashes out the door.
