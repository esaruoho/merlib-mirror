## Flapping In the Wind

\

Apparently, Mick Jagger has been knighted, which makes him "Sir Mick," I guess. Though, as my husband pointed out, if he's started calling himself "Sir Michael" does that mean that the Royal Family's taken the Mick out of him?

When I told my husband that Mick had been knighted by Prince Charles, he replied "Oh, that must have been a sight: two of the ugliest men in Great Britain in the same room at the same time. Big lips and big ears. Somewhere in England, there's an orangutan who dons a suit and goes to work everyday, passing himself off as a rather hairy, short man because he was able to persuade Mick Jagger to wear his lips. And probably a very small-eared elephant, too."

"Do you suppose they're going to file suit?"

"Well, not the orangutan: he's got the better end of the bargain, by far."

Imagine him getting up and going to work every day, passing the conductor on the train....

"Oo!"

"Mornin', Mr. Oo."

"Oo!"

"Yes, yes.... Nasty weather, indeed."

"Oo, oo!"

"I said the same thing to my wife just this morning, I did. I looked her square in the eye and I said to her, 'Ethyl,' I said. 'Oo!' Just like that, I did. 'Oo, nasty weather, today, Ethyl.' What's that? Spare banana? Oh, thanks, Mr. Oo, don't mind if I do. Very good for you, bananas."

And when he gets home to his wife, he no doubt tells her all about his day and Mr. Oo.

"Terribly nice fella, that Mr. Oo, for a barrister. Always has an extra banana for me. Very nice... though I can't quite get over him peelin' 'em with his feet, like that...."

In court, this curiously shambling fellow in the wig and robe is probably asked to approach the bench "upright, if you please, Mr. Oo."

"Oo!"

"It is traditional to address the judge as 'My lord', but in your case, I suppose, 'Oo!' will have to do."

But while Mr. Oo is enjoying a comparatively normal and decent life, there's a very angry Mahout standing in an office at Buckingham palace attempting to redress a horrible historic and cultural wrong done to his charge and to his country, for the Indian elephant beside him has no visible ears at all. And it's all Charles' fault....

Y'see, back when Victoria was Empress of India, a sacred relic, known as The Most Regal Elephant Diadem (Cursed), was presented to her as a gift. But the monarch was too clever to don the thing, herself, and it was consigned to the Tower with the other excess jewels for, like the Koh-i-noor, it was a bit too flashy (not to mention ugly) for even the royals to want to wear.

Many, many years later, a young Charles Philip Arthur George was mucking about among the least-regarded bits of palace treasure--solid-gold mustache cups, pearl-encrusted chamberpots and the like--when he came across The Most Regal Elephant Diadem (Cursed), which had a strange pair of leathery bits hanging down along the sides. Being a rambunctious young sprat, he picked this compound object up and tried it on, but when he went to remove the assemblage, horror struck, for though he could remove the diadem, the leathery bits remained, stuck fast to the sides of his head.

Yanking at them uselessly, young Charles began to cry and shout and contort his face in expressions of pain and grief, until his nanny ran into the room and cuffed him dramatically across the head, then grabbed him by the ear to haul him away, saying, "How many times have I told you not to make such horrid expressions and pull your ears all out; they'll stick that way, if you're not careful."

And the Curse of The Most Regal Elephant Diadem was cast, leaving Charles with the flapping ears of an Indian elephant.

And now the elephant wants revenge.

He is stalking Charles, even now, through the streets of London and beyond, but, being an elephant, he tends to get caught in doorways and turnstiles. He has his trusty Mahout, however, to intercede for him and, someday, Justice shall prevail--if only the Royal family can find a particularly clever plastic surgeon.

Unless Elephant and Mahout get to Charles, first. (Step lively, Charles and keep your ears covered!)

## Building a Better Carol

\

I read an article, recently, which lamented that there had been no new "classic Christmas songs" written since 1955. I guess that anything which doesn't feature either classical descant or sweet-and-sleepy jazz structure straight out of Irving Berlin doesn't qualify as "classic." Apparently the use of musical aesthetic younger than your grandmother immediately disqualifies a song from being "classic" unless it's by Eric Clapton. (Although I did find <a href="http://www.nevada.edu/~blake/Christmas.songs.html#First%20Christmas" target="blank">one Christmas song site</a> which listed Stan Rogers' 1979 *First Christmas*. Lovely piece, but depressing beyond belief.) The article went on to whine that only "parodies and humorous songs" had been added to the repetoire in any significant numbers. While I can forgive (and even encourage) the skipping over of such modern Christmas "classics" as *Don't They Know It's Christmas-time?* by Band-Aid and that ilk, I must protest that the most awful lack in Christmas songs is not modernism, but realistic weather. (Well, realism of any kind, but I'll get to that.)

How many people really live where they might encounter a true "Winter Wonderland" during the holidays and not a car-splashed slush pile at best, much more a brown-edged lawn under-watered due to drought beneath a smog-tinged sky? And what of people in the Southern Hemisphere? Australians all over the continent surely look at each other with irony and wonder why there aren't more Christmas songs about surfing and barbecuing and opening your prezzies beneath a cloudless, blue sky while you slather on the sunscreen.

Where I live, it rains all Winter. But are there songs describing Santa's little rain-slicker or Nicky-the-non-skid-reindeer? No. Not a single one.

And what's so celebratory about snow, anyhow? *Especially* if you live with it all Winter long, as it is. Do you ever hear a real person talk about the joy of travelling through the snow to Grandma's House, anymore? Not too likely; most of them are complaining bitterly about the jackass in an SUV who cut them off and caused them to skid into a ditch hidden under 6 feet of semi-frozen sludge. Snow is for skiers, snowboarders and people who don't have to be anywhere.

Let's face it, the charm of Christmas songs is that they celebrate things most of us have never done, will never do, have no real interest in, and would hate like fire and skunk-juice in the eyes if we ever *did* have to do them. Do you *really* want to go trudging off into the frozen wastes to find a tree of sufficient size to chop down, drag home and burn continuously for 12 days? No? Me, neither: strike off the Yule Log, please. Wassailing? Frankly, I prefer to know what's in my glass and not to drink directly from the punch bowl along with the rest of the choir and who-knows-who-else. Bit unsanitary and disgusting for my taste. And that bunch of birds and guests and total weirdness in *The Twelve Days of Christmas* makes me very glad we've mostly cut it down to a week, now. (OK, it's only one actual day, but it feels like a week and New Year's is still damned weird, but that's for another day.)

Really, a lot of Christmas songs ought to scare the crap out of you. Yeah, sure, they "Wish you a Merry Christmas," but they also demand dessert, drinks and entertainment and say they "won't go until they get some!" I've sung in choirs and I wouldn't "give some" to your average scruffy caroler no matter how much they begged and pleaded, though, I know I'd want most of them out of my house long before any food arrived if I planned on eating, myself.

And Santa Claus is a frightening dude! (Is it any wonder most little kids are scared piss-less of him when encountered at the Mall?) Not only is he all hair and beard (gotta wonder what he's hiding under there...), but he's psychic: after all, he knows when you are sleeping and he knows when you're awake; he knows if you've been bad or good... and that could be a serious source of blackmail (a concept almost-instinctually understood by younger siblings from birth).

Not only that, he runs down little old ladies! Grandma got run over by a reindeer,y'know, and you can bet that it wasn't the deer's fault (let's not blame the vehicle for the driver's actions, now.) He's a philandering pig,too, running around letting Mommy kiss him when we know he's already married to Mrs. Claus. Good, God... what sort of diseases must be spread by *that*! (At least it's not Daddy kissing Santa Claus, is all I can say.)

Beyond all that, many Christmas songs are entirely unrealistic. Peace on Earth, Good Will Toward Men? Would be nice, but not very likely (at least not as long as Humans are involved.) Flying reindeer with glowing noses? Last time I looked into it, the FAA wasn't too sold on the safety of such a vehicle and neither was the SPCA. I'm not even sure that dashing through the woods in a one-horse, open sleigh is even *legal* any more. And some kid somewhere wants a hippopotamus, another wants his two front teeth? Who are these strange children? I want a piece of flashy jewelry and a night at a good hotel with room service, a huge tub and a fur throw on the bed. *Santa Baby* is a bit more my speed, I think, but only if Santa has been working out, lately, and lays off the cookies and milk for a while (and brings his own rubbers because, well... there is still that Mommy-kissing incident.... Ewwwww...!)

## By Wicked Words....

\

I have been seduced by books. This happened long ago, I grant you, but to this day, they seem to have an occasional power to fascinate and draw me from other things. As they have recently.

I come to rest between bouts of work or other necessary tasks and pick up a book for but a moment. But other things fall by the wayside as I wile away hours with my book. The moment stretches.

Yes, I should fix the page design and I should write Kat Litter and I should work on background information and columns and myriad projects which plead for my attention. But I don't. For I am captive to the promise of a good tale, well-told, and even games and friends and sex and food take a far second to the joys of a good book.

Excuse me, now, I have to finish this book....

\
\

It's very short, I know, but entirely true: I've spent most of my free time, lately, reading or working on a project I'm very fond of, rather than concocting Kat Litter. However, I expect I'll write more once things begin to perk a bit at work. The holiday doldrums have taken a certain toll. As this will be the last column for 2003, the temptation is to list all the things which have occured, but I will resist and, instead, wish you all a Happy New Year and better things in 2004.
