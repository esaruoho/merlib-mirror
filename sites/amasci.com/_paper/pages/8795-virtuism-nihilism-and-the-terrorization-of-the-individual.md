### Virtuism, Nihilism and the Terrorization of the Individual

Is not part of the philosophy of Nietzsche proven to be false time and time again in the outcome of so many people who seek to go beyond the morality of the "slave" and do things whatever way they please? Isn't this the beginning philosophy of men who become tyrants, of people who embezzle from their corporation or town, of men who seduce their neighbor's wives and end up in their own divorces, etc.? Was there anything really new in this philosophy? The philosophy of Nietzsche is not a rare philosophy, it is commonly held and it is easy to see its attributes. Common in all philosophy of life is a philosophy of what makes a man great. Solidifying that into academic philosphy can't be said to a true reflection of Nietzche's work. Most "great" people of today can't be said to take Nietzsche's philosophy verbatim. The great we see today are often highly involved in charitable works, of fighting for the rights of the "rabble," of making contributions to lives of others whose welfare is based on such that would not affect them. Yet, whether it be by Ayn Rand, or Satre, or Betrand Russell, it is said that Theists are lesser intellects, weaker willed, more suspectable to being ruled by tyrants, the cause of misery in human life, etc. Philosophically, in the logic and epistomology of what we know to be true, by the same force that also developed these minds and made them brilliant, this force shows many of their concepts be false now that we've had at least 200 years of strong aetheism pronounced from the philosophical pulpit. It is not needed to go back to something we had before the rationalist revolution, but instead it is time to come to something new, something that reflects the experiment and the proof that has come about after trying to free men from the "myth of God."

Virtuism also believes in aspects of the "teachings of Neitzsche" but these are ideas that didn't begin with him. If a Virtuist is also a Christian or has strong faith in God it doesn't mean that he is "like other Christians" or like others who have faith in God. I don't know how such an analysis of such as Neitzsche's can pertain to people like Pascal, Darwin, Martin Luther King, Jr., or Einstein, all of whom had a faith in God. Nietzsche's is probably the philosophy that has had the most wrong with it in modern philosophy, and luckily already the most legitimate criticisms against it. Witness the criticism of Camus.

What has to be explored is the many philosphies of the modern, knowing what many modern minds think. This becomes a philosophy of understanding the philosophy of people. Somehow it is a popular notion that theists or Christians are close minded. But what has changed in the last two hundred years? Wasn't it simply a public-relations campaign on the part of Nietzche, of Lenin and Stalin? And what is the result of what these people put in its place?

It is very interesting to see how old many philosophical statements are. In 1751, Hume said, "Moral Judgement is essentially a matter or sentiment, not reason." Virtuism unites the realm of the beautiful with the realm of something one may want to call a moral judgement. But it is inherently not a statement about sentimentality or the "metaphysics of morality" but about sociology, law, anthropology, psychology and aesthetics. Isn't anthropology the discipline which most often has a respect for man's need for deity? Isn't sociology and psychology good benchmarks for the analysis of the effects of a philosophy on an individual or society? And can not aesthetics be shown to display what belief in God can do to the artist? Look at the wholistic beauty that theistic painters showed in their works from the Pre-Raphealites school back to the original pre-Raphealites. Yet these areas still needed significant breakthroughs in 1751.

"Benevolence is approved partly because of human sympathy, partly because of its social utility, but justice is approved for it's utility alone." Hume

I want to coin a new term to give place to this influence that philosophy has outside of itself. I will call it, the Academic Wake. Somehow in time the influence the Academic Wake has engendered in many people is not an attitude which can be said to be a pure form of many of it's most influential people. It seems many people in the public have a way of taking things at their least common nihilistic demoninator. Nihilism and materialism seems to be justifiable and permissable in the Academic Wake.

Without religion there doesn't seem to be as much to spurn people on to social concern, and without this social concern there isn't the support of the many people who need guidance, and without this guidance lives are clearly falling apart. We can close our eyes to the building problems, or open them and say something is wrong, but we can't say that in the criticism of religion, chosing to see it all as fundamentalist and ignorant, we have put the effective measures in its place so that people are not breaking down and committing violent crimes, suicides, increasing their amounts of serious substance additions, obesity, etc. We may or may not have been able to prove Betrand Russel wrong at the very time when he wrote his works attacking Religion, but it seems fifty years later we can.

Philosophy is an avenue for mending these issues. It has been the concern of traditional philosophy. Although philosophy in the last 100 years has had two serious interuptions: the intrusion of the delusion that religion was responsible for man's ills and also the intrusion of reductionist positivism that tried to reduce the statemeonts of philosophy down to a linguistic study that had more to do with calculus than the past philosophy of the western world.

In the book The Rebel Camus stated that there were spiritual values that were valid. Just as Camus argued that Marxist ideology lead to Stalinist terror, it seems clear that aetheism and nihilism lead to a reduction in possibility that leads to the terrorization of the individual. Whether this is because aetheism and nihilism lead a man away from calling for God's grace and thus an "objective blessing" is bestowed, or whether the solacing takes place on a pure psychological level, is irrelevant to this study.

Virtuism takes no stance on which religion is true or not, but it does take a stance against the attack of religion as being non-philosophically justifiable. Attacking religion is not adequately supportable and not shown to be validly upheld by sociology or anthropology. The distribution of this attack and attitude ultimately has a negative effect on aesthetics.
