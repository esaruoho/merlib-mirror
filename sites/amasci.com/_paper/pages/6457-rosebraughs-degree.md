# Rosebraugh's degree

*Source page: `~rarnold/rosebraughs degree.htm`*

**The Center View: Profiles in ecoterror advocacy**

**Craig Rosebraugh**

**Muffin man's master's thesis advocates violent revolution -\
but does he live by his words? Nope.**

The *Willamette Weekly* headline said, "He Says He Wants a Revolution," with a subhead, "A former ELF figure defends the historic role of violence in social change."

Craig Rosebraugh, 30, a former vegan bakery operator, is also the former spokesman of the domestic terrorist network, Earth Liberation Front. The Portland, Oregon-based Rosebraugh told *Willamette Weekly* writer Amy Roe that violence may be necessary to achieve political change in America. "We must use a range of tactics, legal and illegal, violent and nonviolent," Roe quoted Rosebraugh as saying.

Rosebraugh once answered a congressional inquiry about how he supported himself with the single word: "Muffins."

*The Weekly* was covering Muffin Man Rosebraugh to publicize a talk about his recently completed master's degree, obtained through the Institute for Social Ecology, affiliated with Vermont's Goddard College, which awarded the degree. His thesis was titled, "Rethinking Nonviolence: Arguing for the Legitimacy of Armed Struggle."

Advocating bloody revolution, murder, rape, plunder and pillage, of course, is protected speech under the First Amendment to the United States Constitution - as long as you don't really *do* it - so the system that The Muff wants to destroy protects him while he pretends to be provocative.\
\
Bombing and assassination, Rosebraugh told Roe, can be legitimate forms of self-defense against political oppression: "Terrorism can be OK, can be justified. We use terrorism in the U.S. every day. Our government does it every day. It can be effective."

Revolution. Terrorism. "And that's a good thing." Sounds like Martha Stewart on steroids. Rosebraugh, the Martha Stewart Muffin?

The irony of Rosebraugh's tough talk to violent vegans is that he's as bloodless as his muffins. He's all talk and no do. He's never done a violent thing in his life. When questioned about actual violence he dissolves into evasion, equivocation, and misdirection of attention. His bluster and bravado melt into mumbles and mush when it's him that might get sent to prison.

Rosebraugh's rhetoric sets up violent vegans to take the fall, then he sympathizes when they're fleeing felons, he cries when they're convicted and sentenced - and he remains free. He rails against the system that protects him, but never takes his own advice about destroying it through violence and bloodshed. Do as I say, not as I do. The muffin strategy.

It's a good formula. Rosebraugh has become one of Portland's most celebrated political activists. He routinely gets coverage from the leftish alternative press and occasional spots in mainstream media. For example, Rosebraugh addressed a group at a Portland bookstore in mid-January, 2003, which Oregon Public Broadcasting covered. They broadcast him advocating violent and deadly tactics.

Rosebraugh is best known for the four years he spent as spokesman for Earth Liberation Front (ELF), a loose network of criminals who actually do what he only advocated from the safety of his muffin store. Since 1997, ELF has claimed responsibility for more than \$45 million worth of university arsons, pipe bombings and equipment destruction, none of it done by Muffin Man Rosebraugh.

The FBI has raided Rosebraugh's home and the vegan baking coop he used to run. He has also been called to testify before several federal grand juries. In 2000, he told jurors that he couldn't remember anything pertaining to a 1999 Christmas Day fire at Boise Cascade that caused \$1 million worth of damage. He pleaded the Fifth Amendment more than 50 times while testifying before Congress on ecoterrorism.\
\
Rosebraugh, who resigned his position as spokesman for ELF six days before Sept. 11, 2001, says he struggled to present a nonviolent message on behalf of the group while privately questioning the stance.\
\
"An internal dilemma," he told *The Weekly*. "I began to wonder if there is credibility involved in nonviolence at all."\
\
*The Weekly* asked Thomas H. Nelson, an anti-U.S. lawyer active in the Palestinian human-rights movement, about violence. He was surprised by Rosebraugh's attitude. To him, nonviolence isn't just one tactic; it's the only tactic.\
\
"The idea of nonviolence is to appeal not to the powers or muscles but to the sense of conscience," Nelson says. Still, he thinks a discussion of the origins of nonviolence is useful. "I'm glad somebody's asking that question."\
\
Nelson is glad because it always makes your extreme position look less extreme when somebody is more extreme than you.\
\
While The Muffin talks violence, the ELF does violence.\
\
In September 2002, the ELF released a communiqu about an arson, bragging that the group "will no longer hesitate to pick up the gun to implement justice." But The Muffin even equivocated about gun talk with no gunfire. Rosebraugh told *The Weekly* that the statement may have been the work of a single "cell" within ELF and does not necessarily represent the group's "entire policy." Mumbles and mush.

When ELF sympathizer Andrew McCrae confessed to gunning down an unsuspecting police officer who was gassing up his cruiser at a filling station in Red Bluff, California last November (for environmental and social justice reasons), The Muffin ducked. Didn't even fire a salvo of blazing adjectives. Woo, cop killing is a capital crime. That's too real. Not a peep from the guy whose master's thesis said assassination is a good thing.\
\
Amid this great display of bravery, Rosebraugh told *The Weekly* he endorses the work of the ELF, including the actions of fugitive violent vegan Michael J. Scarpitti, alias Tre Arrow, who is wanted on charges of arson and the firebombing of trucks from Ross Island Sand and Gravel, both of which occurred in 2001. Rosebraugh won't go to prison when Scarpitti is convicted.\
\
"I support his decision to not come forward," Rosebraugh says. "Anybody in their right mind, facing those charges, especially post-2001, anybody facing those kind of charges is screwed, whether they are guilty or not."\
\
Those muffinly nouns and verbs must give Scarpitti great comfort.\
\
Rosebraugh brags about getting his arm broken by a Portland Police officer during a skirmish at a 1999 protest and winning a \$47,500 settlement from the city.

So now The Muffin can add to his means of support: "professional victim."
