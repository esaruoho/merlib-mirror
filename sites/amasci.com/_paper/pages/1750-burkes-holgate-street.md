This is not intended to be an extensive historical site. Rather, this site will present some of the lesser known or less readily available historical information about alcoholism and A.A.</p>
<p>This site has no affiliation with Alcoholics Anonymous or any other organization related to alcoholism or its treatment.</p>

<h2 id="alcoholics-anonymous-related-historical-files"><strong>Alcoholics Anonymous Related Historical Files</strong></h2>
<blockquote>
<div class="list">
<img src="bk_dot.GIF" data-align="bottom" width="16" height="16" alt="bk_dot.GIF" /><strong><a href="history/daniels.html"><span class="text4">An 1878 View of the Washingtonians</span></a></strong>   The Washingtonians had all but disappeared when this temperance writer wrote about the Washingtonian Movement. Predating A.A., its rise was meteoric--and so was its downfall.
</div>
<div class="list">
<img src="bk_dot.GIF" data-align="bottom" width="16" height="16" alt="bk_dot.GIF" /><strong><a href="history/heatter.html"><span class="text4">The Gabriel Heatter Broadcast--April 1939.</span></a></strong>   The first national exposure for A.A. came with this broadcast of the popular "We The People" radio program.
</div>
<div class="list">
<img src="bk_dot.GIF" data-align="bottom" width="16" height="16" alt="bk_dot.GIF" /><strong><a href="history/liberty.html"><span class="text4">The Liberty Magazine Article--September 1939.</span></a></strong>   At last, national exposure that actually began a steady stream of inquiries into the Alcoholic Foundation's New York office.
</div>
<div class="list">
<img src="bk_dot.GIF" data-align="bottom" width="16" height="16" alt="bk_dot.GIF" /><strong><a href="plndlr/plndlrix.html"><span class="text4">The Cleveland Plain Dealer Articles--October-November 1939.</span></a></strong>   A series of seven articles by Elrick B. Davis which gives a comprehensive, and very interesting, view of the earliest days of Alcoholics Anonymous.
</div>
<div class="list">
<img src="bk_dot.GIF" data-align="bottom" width="16" height="16" alt="bk_dot.GIF" /><strong><a href="history/tablemate.html"><span class="text4">An Early Guide to the Twelve Steps</span></a></strong>   Before we had the "12 by 12" this little booklet was used as a study guide by many groups. A later, expanded version was published until quite recently.
</div>
<div class="list">
<img src="bk_dot.GIF" data-align="bottom" width="16" height="16" alt="bk_dot.GIF" /><strong><a href="history/cowles.html"><span class="text4">Chronic Alcoholism</span></a></strong>   A description of a bizarre approach to the treatment of alcoholism which was practiced in 1941. Bizarre, that is, from our current perspective.
</div>
<div class="list">
<img src="bk_dot.GIF" data-align="bottom" width="16" height="16" alt="bk_dot.GIF" /><strong><a href="post1950/post1950.html"><span class="text4">The April, 1950 Jack Alexander Article</span></a></strong> The Saturday Evening Post published a second, glowing article about Alcoholics Anonymous. Coming just fifteen years after the founding of A.A., this article is a fascinating snapshot of the early days of the Fellowship.
</div>
<div class="list">
<img src="bk_dot.GIF" data-align="bottom" width="16" height="16" alt="bk_dot.GIF" /><strong><a href="history/harpers.html"><span class="text4">The 1964 Saturday Evening Post Article Criticizing A.A.</span></a></strong>   The January 1963 issue of Harpers carried an article which harshly criticized A.A. This was only the first of several articles which appeared in national magazines in 1963 and 1964--including this one in the"Post". As a result, A.A. took its own inventory at the 1963 General Service Conference
</div>
<div class="list">
<img src="bk_dot.GIF" data-align="bottom" width="16" height="16" alt="bk_dot.GIF" /><strong><a href="history/wgwmem1.html"><span class="text4">Seattle Memorial Service for Bill W.</span></a></strong>   This article appeared in the February 15, 1971 issue of the Seattle Post-Intelligencer
