<h3 id="section"> </h3>
<h2 id="search-tips"><span id="tips"></span>Search Tips</h2>
<p>To get more specific search results when searching the Guitarnoise.com consider the following tips:</p>
<table data-border="0" width="95%" data-cellpadding="1" data-cellspacing="0" data-bgcolor="#574788" data-align="center">
<colgroup>

<td data-valign="top" width="50%"><p><strong>Check spelling<br />
</strong> Make sure your search terms are spelled correctly. The search engine will attempt to find words that sound similar to your search terms, but it is always best to try to spell the search terms correctly.</p>
<p><strong>Use plus (+) or minus (-)<br />
</strong>Use a plus sign when your search term or phrase must appear in the search results. Use a minus sign to indicate undesirable term(s). The plus sign tells the search engine that a certain word or phrase is required in the search results, and a minus sign indicates that a word or phrase must be absent in the search results.</p>
<p><strong>Use quotation marks<br />
</strong>Use quotation marks to find words which must appear adjacent to each other, for example, "can't play without noise." Otherwise, the search results will include the words but not necessarily in that order. The words may appear anywhere, and in any order, within the document.</p>
<p><strong>Use multiple words<br />
</strong> Use multiple words when performing your search. The search results will return more refined results from several words than from a single word. Relevant results are returned even if they don't contain all query terms.</p>
<p><strong>Use similar words</strong><br />
The more similar words you use in a search, the more relevant results you will get back.</p>
<p><strong>Use wildcards</strong><br />
Wildcard searches can expand the number of matches for a particular request. The * character is used as the wildcard character. For instance, searching for wh* will find the words what, why, when, whether, and any other word that starts with wh. Searching for *her* will find the words here, whether, together, gathering, and any other word that contains her anywhere in the word.</p>
