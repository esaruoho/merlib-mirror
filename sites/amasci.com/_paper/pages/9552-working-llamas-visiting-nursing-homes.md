Llamas are frequently used as therapy animals, Charlie loves visiting nursing homes. He has no problem moving comfortably around hospital beds, walkers, wheel chairs, narrow doors or sliding glass entry doors. (see photos below) Because llamas use a communal dung pile we have never had an accident when visiting. I guess you could say that Charlie is a naturally housebroken llama.</p>
<p><em>(Click on Photo to enlarge)</em> <a href="therapy2.html" onclick="window.open(&#39;therapy2.html&#39;,&#39;&#39;,&#39;width=253,height=310,scrollbars=NO,menubar=NO,toolbar=NO,location=NO&#39;);return false"><img src="images/therapy2a.jpg" data-border="0" data-align="RIGHT" width="51" height="62" /></a> •<br />
Charlie has the ideal temperament for being a therapy llama. He is calm and doesn't frighten or get easily excited. He seems to be very attracted to elderly people, especially the women.</p>
<p>Charlie learned how to walk on different surfaces in our house. When walking around inside our house he also learned how to move in and out of narrow places or rooms. Charlie never gets excited or uncomfortable and the elderly people just enjoy meeting him when he goes to visit the hospitals.</p>
