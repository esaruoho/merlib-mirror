> Subject: Re: Jeff Solo PART II
> Date: Thu, 16 Oct 1997 20:33:52 EDT
> From: Caballero Roberto
> &#10;Joe Ramsey writes:
> &#10;&gt;Gee, Roberto... if Reprise, in their great wisdom, didn&#39;t think that
> &gt;the time was right after Jeff produced the musical event of last year
> &gt;(A NEW Beatles single!!!), I don&#39;t have high hopes for an imminent
> &gt;release of the new one.
> &#10;Yeah, I knew someone was gonna bring that up.  I had considered it when
> I wrote my original statements, but was too lazy at the time to respond
> to it.
> &#10;IMO, releasing Jeff&#39;s solo album right after the Beatles new stuff
> would&#39;ve been disastrous.  Why?
> &#10;First off, as much as I love Jeff Lynne&#39;s music, it pained me to see
> many of the negative comments about him surrounding his production of
> the new Beatles tunes.  Jeff was taking quite a bit of the backlash for
> those tunes.  Many a review I read said they were &quot;over produced and
> too ELO-ish&quot;.  If Jeff had released his solo album then, he would have
> been accused of riding the coattails of the Beatles, which is not always
> a good position to be in.  I&#39;m afraid this would have adversely effected
> the acceptance of his album.
> &#10;Second, quite frankly, the new Beatles tunes were *not* a big success
> like the record company execs had hoped.  Sure they sold to the Beatles
> (and Jeff Lynne) fans out there, and there are a lot of them, but how
> many casual buyers were there?  Did either of the songs make Billboard&#39;s
> top ten?  Did the CD&#39;s?  (I&#39;m speaking of the USA charts here.)  And how
> much of this lack of success got blamed on Jeff?  (Not that I&#39;m saying
> it was Jeff&#39;s fault).  How would Jeff&#39;s new album faired under these
> circumstances?
> &#10;Well, these are just my opinions as to why Jeff&#39;s new album was not
> released following his production of the Beatles.  Feel free to find
> the flaws in my logic.

> Badfinger&#39;s version of &quot;Come And Get It&quot; MUCH more than anything ELO has
> done.  Come on, there was no way that Jeff was going to make it sound
> like ELO!  He was picking up where &quot;Double Fantasy&quot; left off.  When I
> watched its debut on the ABC Anthology special, I actually said out
> loud, &quot;Way to go, Jeff.&quot;
> &#10;There were many critics who wanted the new Beatles songs to fail.  It
> wouldn&#39;t have mattered who produced them.  I never saw such unjustified
> bitterness and resentment.  And Jeff took the heat.  Yes, he has a
> trademark sound that&#39;s easily detected in certain Tom Petty and George
> Harrison songs.  But I&#39;m sorry, I just don&#39;t hear it on &quot;Free As A
> Bird.&quot;
> &#10;Still disgusted...

> but with the technology employed to make the project happen.
> &#10;It is my understanding that the original voice track was recorded
> &quot;free-form&quot; with piano accompaniment. To make this fit with a click track

> the rather metallic &quot;affect&quot; on the vocal.
> &#10;I completely agree with Roberto - the critics had their knives sharpened for
> this one - and I&#39;m sure that they would have fried Jeff for trying to parlay
> his association with the Beatles into a solo promotion. Sad.
> &#10;I&#39;m sure that the artists themselves don&#39;t feel this way, but as I get
> older, I take comfort that things that I really like musically are really
> *not* for mass consumption. The people can have Elton John, Madonna and
> Michael Jackson.
> &#10;I&#39;ll be honest, ELO-people, I really lost it for Jeff for awhile. &quot;Xanadu&quot;
> embarassed me and I stopped telling people I even liked ELO. But with later
> projects that may even be seen as commercial &quot;failures&quot; (&quot;Armchair Theatre,&quot;
> The Beatles and even the Wilbury stuff), Mr. Lynne has reminded me of why I
> thought so highly of him in the first place.
> &#10;It would be great if these older guys (sorry about that, over-forties!), who
> have already had big success, would totally follow their muse and release
> new music independently - without interference from record companies. It
> would take guts because there&#39;s a lot less money coming in. But there&#39;s no
> reason in the world that Jeff&#39;s new album sits unreleased except that some
> failed musician who may run &quot;Bigwig&quot; Records has decided that due to
> &quot;Armchair&#39;s...&quot; poor sales, the new one may flop, too. Bummer!
> &#10;Sorry to be so agreeable...
