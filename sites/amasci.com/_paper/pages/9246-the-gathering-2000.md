<p>*<br />
<strong><em>Day One -- Tues., August 1</em></strong><br />
<strong><em>Travel and Arrival</em></strong></p>
<p>The shuttle trips to and from the airports said it all in terms of the differences between where we<br />
were starting from and where we were going -- the driver that picked us up in the rainy dark of a<br />
Seattle pre-dawn had a latte in his cupholder, and the driver taking us to the hotel through the<br />
muggy heat of an Orlando afternoon had a slushie. That seemed to sum it up nicely ...</p>
<p>My carry-on bag was a large bright periwinkle <em>Gargoyles</em> tote, and of all the ones on the conveyer<br />
belt, they had to pick it for a "random" by-hand inspection. Of course, maybe it just looked<br />
strange going through the machine, crammed as it was with all my junk plus Becca's six X-Men<br />
action figures. They also hand-groped Becca's bedraggled old Tigger toy, the one she's had since<br />
her first Christmas and takes with her everywhere. Maybe they thought the hard rattly ball in his<br />
tummy was a grenade.</p>
<p>At any rate, they let us on the plane and our long day of touring scenic airport concourses began.<br />
All three of us slept for a goodly chunk of the first leg of our trip, though I was awake for enough<br />
of the in-flight movie to notice a familiar face -- Keith David. Had I been using the earphones, I'm<br />
sure I would have caught the voice instantly, but as I had zero interest in listening to the gripping<br />
saga of the pregnant chickie in Wal-Mart, I didn't. As it happens, later when talking about it with<br />
Greg, his situation was just the opposite. He had the headphones on but wasn't watching, and<br />
when Keith's voice came on, snapped his head right up.</p>
<p>Boarding our plane for the second leg, Covington to Orlando, a young woman right behind me<br />
pointed to my bag and remarked how she "loved that show!" So I told her where we were going,<br />
to the con and all. Would've abducted her and dragged her along to the hotel if we hadn't been<br />
coming in early to go to Universal Studios.</p>
<p>Our reasoning was that none of us had ever been to Florida before, would probably not have<br />
reason to go again (and after experiencing the heat, if we ever <em>do</em> go again, it'd by-gosh better<br />
be in January!), so we might as well hit all the parks and make a real vacation of it. I told my<br />
boss that we'd come home sunburned, broke, and exhausted. As it turned out, I was right on<br />
only two counts, thanks to diligent application of sunscreen.</p>
<p>We finally arrived at the Ramada, and sorry to say it was quite a bit more drab than I was<br />
expecting. Maybe seeing the other hotels as our shuttle dropped off our fellow travelers had<br />
something to do with it. Our room was right by the lobby, which was convenient for check-in, but<br />
it was almost as far as we could have gotten from the convention areas, which was something of<br />
a drag. I understand why it was chosen and all, but the initial reaction was on the <em>enh</em> side.</p>
<p>So there we were, three weary, sweaty, crabby people. But there was one burning thought in my<br />
mind preventing me from either collapsing or jumping in the pool with all my clothes on -- had<br />
my books arrived or not? We'd called the hotel and they'd said no, we'd gone to the UPS online<br />
package-tracking thingie and <em>that</em> said they'd arrived the previous Wednesday, we'd called the<br />
hotel again and they said the books <em>were</em> there ... all of this process hampered by<br />
communication difficulties and general ineptitude of the front desk staff.</p>
<p>Now we were right there at the desk, and talked to three successive people who had no idea<br />
what we wanted. Books? Boxes of books? Simple cardboard boxes? No, no boxes ... Morgan? Are<br />
you sure that's the name (pretty sure, yeah). I was an eyeblink away from losing it, which is<br />
never a good thing because underneath this cute charming exterior beats the heart of a ravening<br />
ogre, and by then all my safety systems were straining and hissing and jetting steam. But the<br />
disaster was averted by the desk-dolts <em>finally</em> finding the books, and we escaped to our room<br />
with the cartons in tow.</p>
<p>The hotel had two pools, one covered over by the convention area and one open right near us.<br />
The sight of the pool instantly revived Becca and she began clamoring for a swim, but Tim and I<br />
were just too damn beat. We got unpacked, found out that Tim's miniature diorama of the Weird<br />
Sisters casting their spell had been damaged in transport (this began a long quest for a<br />
convenient hobby shop or game store so he could get some paint, a quest that proved fruitless<br />
because they were all too far to reach without an hour and a half bus ride each way or one<br />
whopper of a cab fare; he ultimately ended up doing a temporary patch job with some of Crzy's<br />
body paint).</p>
<p>At the center of the hotel is a little mall-type arrangement, with an arcade and a grocery and<br />
some touristy knickie-knackie shops. That's also where the con suite and one of the panel<br />
meeting rooms turned out to be. There's a deli between the lobby and the outdoor pool, and the<br />
hotel restaurant is right beside the convention area. We decided just to eat at the restaurant that<br />
night, for their buffet. The dinner one turned out to be nothing special, though we would later<br />
find out that the breakfast one was much more passable.</p>
<p>That ended Tuesday, with all of us turning in early and the alarm set for 5:30 AM so we could<br />
catch the bus to Universal Studios.</p>
<p>*<br />
<strong><em>Day Two -- Wed., August 2</em></strong><br />
<strong><em>Islands of Adventure</em></strong></p>
<p>With the Universal Studios park-hopper pass, you get in two hours before they open the gates to<br />
general admission. That is definitely the way to go, well worth waiting for the sunrise (in already<br />
80+ degrees) for the bus. That bus <em>nice</em>, with posh comfy seats and air conditioning and video<br />
monitors showing an endless loop of commercials for U. Studios, Sea World, Wet &amp; Wild, and<br />
Busch Gardens.</p>
<p>None of us had ever been to a Universal Studios park. We chose Islands of Adventure for our first<br />
day, thinking that Becca would like it better, and then the first thing we did to the poor kid was<br />
scare the hell out of her by going on the Jurassic Park ride. We were the only ones, all alone in<br />
the front of our boat, and at first all was well, toodling along, but then there were raptors, and<br />
the ones she calls "spitty dinosaurs," and then the big T. Rex, and then a much steeper scarier<br />
flume-plunge than even I was expecting.</p>
<p>That was when I was reminded that she's only five ... Becca is a bright and clever kid, but I'd<br />
forgotten that she wasn't automatically going to know, as I did, what was part of the ride and<br />
what wasn't. When they were sounding the alarms because our boat had gone "off course" into<br />
the raptor area, she really believed it. The rest of the day, she kept asking me why that had<br />
happened, and I had to keep trying to explain that they did it that way on purpose, that it was<br />
<em>supposed</em> to happen.</p>
<p>But we got through it, though the photo monitor showed Becca with such a look of abject terror<br />
that I couldn't bear to buy it and remind her forever of how scared she'd been. Sure, she'd laugh<br />
about it someday, but she didn't need that souvenir. Besides, we spent enough on other stuff.</p>
<p>The only trouble with the early admission is that many of the attractions, shops, and food places<br />
still don't open until nine, so we had to make several trips around to do all the things we wanted<br />
to do. Like the pteranodon ride, zipping people along a raised track with their feet hanging. We<br />
did the small Flying Unicorn coaster, and then Becca and I waited as Tim went on the Duelling<br />
Dragons, one of those hanging-under coasters that you'd never get me on because I don't like<br />
upside-down rides. Tim, though, isn't one for spinny rides, so he sat it out as Becca and I helped<br />
Storm spin up enough electrical power to defeat Magneto. Skipped Spidey's ride and the Hulk<br />
coaster.</p>
<p>Seuss Landing was a big hit with Becca and helped her get over the trauma of Jurassic Park. One<br />
of the attractions, based on If I Ran the Zoo, was a little water-squirty area, and one of these<br />
days I am going to learn that it does no good to tell that child she can only get wet up to her<br />
knees. She was drenched, and rode through most of the rest of the day with her shoes and socks<br />
trying to dry out on the roof of the stroller.</p>
<p>The Sinbad show was loads of fun, and of course the evil sorceress Miseria had the coolest<br />
costume (that black and purple combo with red trim works so well for so many villains!) and best<br />
lines. The princess (Amora, naturally) whupped some butt for a change, and while the show was<br />
peppered with pop-culture references, they weren't as annoying as those usually are. After,<br />
Becca got a plastic scimitar, and when we came out of the gift shop one of the cast baddies was<br />
there for a photo op, and she ran right up to him.</p>
<p>I completed a quest eighteen years in the making that day ... long ago, when I started gaming, I<br />
wanted to have a bunch of fake gems to carry in my dice bag. But for the longest time, the only<br />
ones I could find were the junky ones with flat backs with silvery stuff on, and while I tried gluing<br />
those together, it just never worked. But when we walked into the Sinbad store, eureka! A big<br />
bin, fill a bag with as many fake gems as you can for five dollars, and my mission was at last<br />
complete. Well, not <em>complete</em> ... I wound up doing the same thing a few days later at Disney<br />
because they had it too and their gems were even better ... I'm such a child sometimes! But I<br />
finally had my bag of gems, and gloated over them shamelessly.</p>
<p>Tim got his neat souvenir near there too -- a tent featured guys hand-striking coins with your<br />
choice of designs. He got one with a gryphon on one side and an M on the other, as the Morgan<br />
coat of arms for his branch of the name features a gryphon. The guys at the tent were great,<br />
bantering and seeming to be having genuine fun.</p>
<p>The Poseidon show, one of those hologram-water-fire extravaganzas, freaked Becca out a little<br />
but was otherwise neat, though I confess I spent most of it not paying attention to anything<br />
except trying to assure myself that yes, that was the voice of Tony Jay we were hearing. First<br />
Keith, then Tony (voiced Anubis).</p>
<p>We had dinner at the Mythos restaurant, which had fantastic decor and a great view, but the<br />
food seemed to be making too great an effort toward fancy and came off as pretentious. And<br />
many of the wait staff were on the surly side, but I bet if you had to go around all day with a<br />
nametag reading Ares, Pan, or Hera, you'd get cranky too ...</p>
<p>When we'd done all we wanted to do and were ready to go, it turned out we were just in time.<br />
The clouds had been massing, and as we reached the bus shelter, the skies opened. Now, I've<br />
seen rain before ... we live north of Seattle for crying out loud, but it had been a while since I'd<br />
seen rain like this. Had been, in fact, since the 20-Year Celebration of the SCA, held in Texas back<br />
in '87 or '88 (and I was cowering in a tent the whole time).</p>
<p>This was a <em>thunderstorm</em>, with huge jagged sheeting bolts of lightning, thunder that shook the<br />
bones, and rain that really did come down in curtains. The street went from dry to flooded in<br />
about ten minutes, and we sluiced through water all the way back to the hotel. So, obviously, we<br />
didn't go swimming that day either! But we were home in time to catch <em>Survivor</em>, a show on<br />
which Tim and I are both utterly hooked.</p>
<p>Then it was to bed, because we were going to do the same thing the next day. To bed, but not<br />
to sleep, thanks to several noisy passers-by.</p>
<p>*<br />
<strong><em>Day Three -- Thurs., August 3</em></strong><br />
<strong><em>Universal Studios</em></strong></p>
<p>Oh lordy, this was a hot one. The previous day, we were just wussy complaining Pacific<br />
Northwesterners (when it hits 80 degrees here, people whine), but this one was hot even for the<br />
locals. And our feet were aching already, but this time we were smart and got the stroller right<br />
from the front gate so Becca at least got to ride in style.</p>
<p>This park wasn't as good for small kids, and seemed to have much less in the way of attractions,<br />
much more in the way of shows, tours, and the like. The Back to the Future ride was bumpy,<br />
choppy, and not generally very good (especially when compared to Star Tours, which is great!).<br />
Men in Black was more fun, letting you score points by shooting aliens, though our car didn't do<br />
very well since Becca was only looking and I think one of the people behind us wasn't shooting<br />
either.</p>
<p>I dragged them on the classic Jaws ride, expecting it to be hokey and it was ... but it was also a<br />
lot more suspenseful, a lot more adrenaline-pumping, than I thought it would be. There's just<br />
something about that music, and about the idea of the unseen sharp-edged death coming up all<br />
sudden-like from below! Becca loved that one and got herself a big stuffed shark, so for the rest<br />
of the day I'd suddenly hear her going "duh-dun, duh-dun," and then she'd hit me in the rear<br />
with it -- big target, I guess!</p>
<p>For her, the best things were the E.T. ride and Feivel's (sp?) play area. That one included a sort<br />
of waterslide thing that she got Tim to go on with her twice, so she was soaked again. We also<br />
went on the Nickelodeon stage tour, and thankfully she was <em>not</em> the kid chosen to get slime<br />
dumped on her head, though not for lack of volunteering. She did get picked to help them play a<br />
game based on <em>The Wild Thornberrys</em>, throwing cheese doodles at someone with a velcro Darwin<br />
mask, and enjoyed that tremendously.</p>
<p>I missed out on the Terminator show, since I have never had much luck getting 3-D to work with<br />
my glasses (yes, I know they <em>say</em> it fits over regular frames, but all I ever get is blur and<br />
headache). Tim enjoyed that one while Becca and I cruised around. We saw the <em>Ghostbusters</em> car<br />
but they left before we got a photo op with one of the 'busters.</p>
<p>We packed it in early that day, Tim's pockets jangling with squashed pennies because he and<br />
Becca wanted to try every penny machine we found. I think we left Universal Studios with ten,<br />
and then would get more at Disney. The clouds were threatening, but by the time we got back it<br />
was clear enough to <em>finally</em> take Becca swimming.</p>
<p>Tim didn't join us, spending that time trying to find hobby shops. He said he ran into Denis and a<br />
few other con people, and at one point while I was in the pool I thought I might've seen Denis go<br />
by but without my glasses I couldn't be sure, and didn't want to rear up out of the water and yell,<br />
and scare a stranger (duh-dun, duh-dun ...).</p>
<p>Later, though, showered and dressed, we went over to see what was happening at the<br />
convention area, since the signs said registration would start at six, or eight, or whenever. We<br />
started running into people, some familiar faces from previous years and some names I knew<br />
from online.</p>
<p>Starsinger was there, and it was good to see her again. Maybe she'll come back to the comment<br />
rooms now? She'd left because of the atmosphere in there and her tempering influence has been<br />
missed, I think. The guy in the wild winged hat turned out to be Vig, so I finally got to meet him<br />
and would later drool over his work in the art show</p>
<p>Demona May gave us a sneak peek at the art she'd brought, including many pics of characters<br />
from my fics and some Barbies modeled after T'Chambleau, Aiden, and the twins Kenneth and<br />
Finella from my Guardians series. Those wound up winning a prize in the art show.</p>
<p>A bunch of us then wound up taking over the hotel lobby, and I feared we were going to get<br />
thrown out before the con even began when the talk turned to South Park and some folks<br />
seemed on the verge of breaking into song. Tim and Becca and I went back to our room for a<br />
while to rest from our busy day, and then went over to the deli for a very late dinner.</p>
<p>Of course, the very moment we were done, as we were about to go to bed, that's when Crzy<br />
(henceforth referred to as Jen because that's how I know her best) and Thom and the Weisman<br />
family rolled in. They'd been delayed, and Jen had nearly been bumped from the flight but<br />
whipped up a convincing sob story that she and Thom were getting married at noon on Friday<br />
and she <em>had</em> to be on the plane, she just <em>had</em> to ... it worked, though I'm sure her husband<br />
Alan will be surprised to learn he's been replaced!</p>
<p>It was the moment Becca had been waiting for all year, because she's Thom's #1 Fan (junior<br />
division; senior division goes to Lexy). We met Beth Weisman and the kids. Erin, whose sixth<br />
birthday was coming up on the 8th, had lost a tooth in Kellie Fay's car on the way from the<br />
airport, and 3-year-old Benny is just a doll ... I told Jen that if it ever came down to a cute-off<br />
between Benny and her son Fox, I don't know who would win!</p>
<p>They all got checked in, and then Beth and the kids went to bed while Greg joined a crowd of us<br />
on a trek for food (we Morgans, having just eaten, tagged along anyway because Becca wouldn't<br />
be pried away from her two fave people -- Thom and Jen -- for anything). It took us forever to<br />
get everyone collected, and then Denny's couldn't seat 15 for nonsmoking, so we ended up at<br />
IHOP. Playing with one of those damnable triangle peg-jumping games. And then Tim went and<br />
bought one to bring home!</p>
<p>A word on jaywalking in Florida: one of the bus drivers said that Orlando leads the nation in<br />
pedestrian fatalities, and I believe it! But the lights were so far away ... we crossed in a mob at<br />
least, and survived. In the daytime, it's all the more tempting because when the place you want<br />
to be is air conditioned and right across the street, and the light is way down there shimmering in<br />
the heat like a mirage ... yeah, it's tempting!</p>
<p>*<br />
<strong><em>Day Four -- Fri., August 4</em></strong><br />
<strong><em>Convention Begins</em></strong></p>
<p>The con really got underway well before the opening ceremonies, which weren't until late that<br />
afternoon. Registration went much more smoothly than in previous years (for instance, the<br />
badges were already made up).</p>
<p>The entrances to the dealer's room and art show were accessible either from the outside or<br />
through the video room, so we did a lot of tromping through the video room. After all, it was hot<br />
out there! Every time I stepped outside, my glasses tried to fog up.</p>
<p>There turned out to be a total of three dealers. At our table, Sabledrake Enterprises sold loads of<br />
<em>Gargoyles</em> merchandise as well as copies of my books. Across the way, Abram and his team had<br />
the complete Wintersmith Dreams as well as some calendars (I never did get around to buying<br />
one because I was saving my money for the auction and the Villains store at Disney World, but<br />
the calendars looked great and had terrific art). The last booth belonged to a lady whose name I<br />
never caught, who was selling jewelry and little sculptures and things. She also did caricatures<br />
and face painting.</p>
<p>The art show was as stunningly impressive as ever. I wandered through in pained awe of the<br />
incredible talent on display. In addition to the paintings and art on the walls, there was quite an<br />
array of 3-dimensional stuff. Crystal's mugs were fabulous (I got Goliath! I got Goliath), and<br />
Sara's stained glass, and Kyt's miniature diorama, and Demona May's soap sculptures and puzzles<br />
... very well done, people!</p>
<p>But it brings me to a bitchy little thing that's been on my mind for a while now, so we interrupt<br />
your regularly-scheduled essay for Christine's Whiny Rant:</p>
<p>How come the artists can sell their work and we writers can only give it away? Just as much<br />
effort and creativity must go into a fic as into a pic, they still many times involve characters from<br />
the show ... there's something that seems strange and more than a little unfair about that. Of<br />
course, artists do tend to have more of a materials bill than writers, but still ... it strikes me odd.</p>
<p>Many of the pieces for sale or auction in the art show are already available online, and anyone<br />
could have printed them out on just as nice paper. Could I, for instance, bring bound copies of<br />
my stories, done up pretty on parchment-style maybe, and sell them? I'd been under the<br />
impression that I couldn't do that.</p>
<p>What would happen if I did? If I tried to sell the complete printed collection in booklet form? I<br />
know if I tried to self-publish my fics, I'd get in hot water with the Disney people. How come the<br />
artists can get away with it? What would happen if I did something like what Stephen King is<br />
doing (though on a much more limited scale, obviously) and asked people to send me money on<br />
an honor system for fics? I can't <em>do</em> that. Can I?</p>
<p>I do take commissions for stories, sure. The Silver Flame thing has been interesting and fun, but I<br />
have had to be very careful how I get around characters that belong to Disney. I know artists,<br />
though, who take commissions not only for original characters but also for ones from the show ...<br />
they can charge for that, but I can't?</p>
<p>It isn't that I'm in the fanfic biz for money. I've written these stories because I love what I do and<br />
I love hearing from people who enjoy reading them. But I work hard on those fanfics. I put a lot<br />
of time and creative energy into them that I <em>could</em> be putting into something that might<br />
someday be publishable.</p>
<p>It just rankles a bit, that's all. And in an indirect way, yes, I <em>have</em> profited from writing fanfic. I<br />
have profited because it has given me the chance to practice my skills and get feedback, and also<br />
because people who have read and liked my stories are the people who trust me enough to buy<br />
my <em>MageLore</em> books.</p>
<p>Where it really gets weird, at least for me, is when someone else is selling artwork of <em>my</em><br />
characters. <em>I'm</em> not allowed to make any sort of money off of characters like Jericho or Aiden,<br />
but the artists can? I mean, you go artists, more power to you and all ... I admire your talent and<br />
skill, and am flattered as all get out that I've come up with characters compelling enough to<br />
draw.</p>
<p>But how come an artist can profit from my creation when <em>I</em> can't? LOL, I mean, where's my cut?<br />
Where's my complimentary copy? At the risk of tooting my own horn here, some of those pics<br />
aren't just selling because they are neat-looking gargoyles ... they're selling partly because of<br />
who the characters are!</p>
<p>I wonder if Greg and the gang feel that way about the same thing happening with the official<br />
characters. I've been to plenty of cons and seen portraits of Star Trek, Star Wars, Highlander,<br />
Xena, you name it ... I wonder if their people feel that way too.</p>
<p>Well, that's enough of that; I'm supposed to be reporting on the con! Onward!</p>
<p>The Radio Show auditions began that morning. Becca and I went to Dunkin' Donuts in the hot<br />
and ucky weather, and generally hung around the dealer's room meeting people and selling stuff.<br />
At one point, we saw Beth and the kids out in the covered pool, which the convention lobby<br />
overlooked through a set of floor-to-ceiling windows. We went out to say hi, and Becca really<br />
really wanted to join them but we didn't have the time just then. I also managed to slip on the<br />
wet bricks and bang my knee, always the same knee, and earned a big ugly purple bruise.</p>
<p>Coming back in, I got to meet Craig, one of the gang from Steve Jackson Games' <em>Pyramid</em><br />
magazine boards. I mentioned that I was going to the con, Craig said he lived near Orlando, so<br />
he came over for the afternoon! Luckily, he's a fan of the show, and of my fic, so he wasn't<br />
surprised at my reputation (well, he also knew me for my GURPS Sex article, which even pre-<br />
dated my fic, so I suppose there was nothing at all to worry about!)</p>
<p>After that, we headed over to the con suite for the Ice Cream Social, and Becca made a huge<br />
mess of her face. We caught the very end of Thom's talk on acting, and he gave out sheets that I<br />
assume (since I know nothing of the business) were a photo resume sort of thing. I got a signed<br />
one, even!</p>
<p>My reading was scheduled for 4 PM, which was conveniently the very time the hotel desk finally<br />
got organized enough to start letting people into their rooms. Until then, everyone had been<br />
hanging around waiting. Then they all disappeared, and I thought I'd be in there by myself. But a<br />
few people trickled in, and then a few more and more, until I had a nice crowd of folks. Many<br />

<p>At the end, the G2001 team set up in the back to take more pre-registrations, and there were<br />
things to sign for the con staff, and a lot of mingling. It was during this mingling that one of the<br />
best parts of the con for me happened. I was approached by Ashlee's mom, who told me<br />
something that made my day.</p>
<p>Ashlee had come to the con thanks to the Make A Wish Foundation. Her initial Wish, according to<br />
her mom, had been to bring back a new season of the show, because watching it had helped her<br />
when she was feeling low with her illness and she thought it might have the same benefit for<br />
others. But even Make A Wish can't do everything, it seems, so her second choice was to come to<br />
the con. Her parents were a little dubious -- how did they know we were legit, we were okay<br />
people, we were worth it? So Ashlee tracked down my essays from previous years, and those<br />
helped ease her parents' worries.</p>
<p>Hearing that made me feel so amazingly good ... I know that many who haven't been able to<br />
attend the Gatherings have taken some small comfort in being able to read all my ramblings<br />
about them, but to learn that they actually were able to help bring someone to join us, to<br />
convince someone's parents that we were good folks ... that is a great honor and compliment,<br />
and I am so very glad it worked out! I hope Ashlee had a wonderful time, and that we'll see her<br />
again next year!</p>
<p>It was Tim's turn to stay out late, so he went wine-tasting with Mitch and then, when Karine and<br />
her fella (our other Patrick) didn't want much, the two of them wound up polishing off three bottles. This on top of the drinks they had at dinner must've meant my man came stumbling home, and if <br />
he did any midnight swimming or otherwise cavorting with Jen and company when he ran into <br />
them, he glossed it over in the re-telling (my mom, after seeing last year's pics, wanted to know<br />
who the hell was that woman hanging all over my family &lt;g&gt;).</p>
<p>Me, I slept. Like a dead thing. Becca kept wanting to talk to me, and I don't know what I may<br />
have agreed to ... I would have said anything if she'd only hush up and let Mutti sleep!</p>
<p>*<br />
<strong><em>Day Seven -- Mon., August 7th</em></strong><br />
<strong><em>Disney World MGM Studios</em></strong></p>
<p>I spent much of this week trying to remember if there was a technical name for the torture that<br />
involves beating the bottom of someone's feet with a rattan stick. Couldn't ever recall it, but by<br />
the time we were ready to go home, I sure knew what it might feel like!</p>
<p>We hauled ourselves out of bed with great difficulty in time to catch the 11-ish bus to Disney<br />
World. I think the plan was to meet in the lobby at nine, but for us at least that was unthinkable.<br />
We went over on the bus, which dropped us off way out at lonely slot 140 in the parking lot of<br />
Epcot, and from there caught another bus over to MGM.</p>
<p>The first order of business was a stroller, and these ones turned out to be a bit of a problem<br />
because Becca was too tall for the roof, resulting in a huge mangled snarl of hair every time she<br />
got in and out of it. She soon got tired of me chasing after her with my comb, and I soon decided<br />
that fine, she could look like a cavekid, I didn't care anymore.</p>
<p>We caught a lot of shows. The Indiana Jones stunt spectacular was a hit, and Tim almost spent<br />
fifty bucks on a leather hat but decided against it ... at least for now. He's always resented that<br />
hats have gone out of style, and would have been happy living in a film-noir world.</p>
<p>After she hated Back to the Future, I was braced for Becca to be disappointed with Star Tours.<br />
But that wasn't the case; she loved it. And those Fast Passes are a great thing! We zipped on<br />
through, and when you've got a kid with you, believe me, those lines seem all the longer.</p>
<p>The Hunchback of Notre Dame stage show was very good, but very frantic as they tried to rush<br />
the entire movie into a half-hour. Esmeralda was a dish, which pleased Tim as he tolerates my<br />
thing about Goliath (and Obi-Wan, and Agent Cooper, etc.) as long as I tolerate his drooling over<br />
certain sexy gypsy girls. I took plenty of pics of her for him, too, and bought him Marie Norre's<br />
gargoyle-Esmeralda from the art show.</p>
<p>As Becca was waiting in line to hug Tigger, along came Jen, Kenna, Patrick, Karine, and her Patrick. The bunch of us went to the animation show together -- rrahr, rrahr -- and then we had to go for our Fast Pass to the Little Mermaid show. Giant robot Ursula, very cool.</p>
<p>And then I spent all my money. We found the Villains shop. I got a Chernabog pin, a T-shirt, a<br />
set of pens, a notepad, a license plate, a windup walkie Maleficent dragon, oh, all sorts of stuff.<br />
And a sorcerer Mickey for Becca. While I was there, I started talking to the clerk about why they<br />
didn't have a plushie Chernabog. He laughed, said that if they did, they couldn't keep it on the<br />
shelf. Now, isn't that the idea? They'd sell like crazy!</p>
<p>Ooh, Chernabog ... it's partly his fault I got so hooked on Gargoyles anyway ... other girls my age<br />
had crushes on Speed Racer, but me, I liked Chernabog and the foxy version of Robin Hood.<br />
Warped from early on, that's me. I was delighted to get to see him on the big screen in the<br />
animation show, and then again in Fantasmic.</p>
<p>Fantasmic itself was horrendously crowded. An hour before the 9:15 show was due to start, they<br />
were already only to standing room only. So we stood. Well, initially we sat, on concrete steps.<br />
But when the show started, people began standing up, and some clown behind us kept hollering<br />
"Sit down! Down in front!" and I came very close to rounding on him and pointing out that they<br />
didn't call it "sitting room only." Sheesh.</p>
<p>The astonishing bit was, as the close to 8,000 people filed and crowded out at the end of the<br />
show, we spotted some familiar figures. Greg and Beth and the kids were retrieving their strollers<br />
at the same time we were looking for ours. The Weismans looked twice as beat as Tim and I felt,<br />
which is understandable as they'd been riding herd on two kids for a longer day. Benny came<br />
bounding out of his stroller jabbering excitedly about the show we'd just seen, and Greg gave us<br />
sort of a shell-shocked smile and announced wearily that they were going back to the hotel.</p>
<p>So were we, though they had chauffeur service via Kellie and we got to retrace our route back to<br />
Epcot and then out into the vast empty parking lot to wait for the 11:30 bus back to the Ramada.<br />
That one wasn't on the printed schedule, but had just been announced by the driver, so we<br />
joined a fellow group (including Kythera and Raven and a few others from the con) all hoping we<br />
hadn't misheard. And it got later, and later, and more and more deserted, and then finally one of<br />
the small busses showed up. For about twenty of us.</p>
<p>But we all piled in, three people having to stand up, four or five of us with unconscious children<br />
pasted sweatily to our laps. The bus shimmied and swayed alarmingly on the road, and bottomed<br />
out badly when we got to the driveway. But we got there in one piece.</p>
<p>*<br />
<strong><em>Day Eight -- Tues., August 8</em></strong><br />
<strong><em>Disney World Magic Kingdom</em></strong></p>
<p>We managed to get up and get going early enough to meet Jen and Patrick for a farewell<br />
breakfast in the hotel restaurant. Sara had given Tim a good deal on leftover merchandise (and<br />
here I thought we were going to go home with <em>fewer</em> bags, dopey me!) and also had a bunch of<br />
t-shirts from G99, and we had to send one of those home with Jen as we'd exceeded our luggage<br />
limit.</p>
<p>While waiting for the 9:45 shuttle, we saw Alison and Rene, and sat near Bud-Clare and a friend<br />
of hers whose name I can't recall but who very kindly gave us a ponytail holder so I could try and<br />
keep Becca's hair in line. As it turned out, they had a different kind of stroller so she didn't wear it<br />
long after all, but I do appreciate it!</p>
<p>This was the theme park I'd been waiting for, even if my feet felt like hell and Tim's entire left<br />
foot was one massive blister. I grew up going to Disneyland on a yearly basis and was dying to<br />
see the differences and similarities.</p>
<p>First off was Adventureland, and we did everything. I prefer Tiki Room Classic to the "under new<br />
management" one with Zazu and Iago, truth be told. Becca loved the jungle boat ride (back side<br />
of water, I used that in a fic) and the Swiss Family treehouse. I discovered the pineapple float,<br />
which was pinapple sherbet floating on pineapple juice, even more refreshing than a lemon<br />
slushie.</p>
<p>And then was my all-time favorite, the Pirates of the Caribbean! Yo-ho, yo-ho, a pirate's life for<br />
me! We wants the redhead, send up the redhead! Woo! And more jewels to gloat over in the gift<br />
shop! And all sorts of other goodies and trinkets. We opted for package pickup, and that proved<br />
to be our downfall, resulting in the only major disaster we Morgans experienced during the whole<br />
trip. Bad idea. We would have been better off toting the big bag with us all day.</p>
<p>It's funny but in all the times I've been, I have never once gone to the presidential show. This<br />
time was no different. The Haunted Mansion remains my runner-up fave, though it freaked Becca<br />
a little. Tim slept through the Country Bear Jamboree. We continued our collection of squashed<br />
pennies (the grand total was 27, plus two squashed <em>quarters</em>)</p>
<p>In Fantasyland, while we were in line for the Snow White ride, we glimpsed Greg and Company<br />
(including Siryn and Sara this time) over watching the Merlin thing in front of the carousel. But by<br />
the time we got out, we didn't see them ... until they walked right up to us. They were on their<br />
way over to the toon area but we had a Fast Pass for Splash Mountain and couldn't tag along. I<br />
also thought I spotted them while I was resting my feet as Tim and Becca were in line for<br />
Autopia (or whatever they're calling it these days), but they were gone before I could stir myself<br />
to go say hi.</p>
<p>By then, we were all starting to get more than a little tired and crabby. We got into one of the<br />
last possible fastpass slots for the Buzz Lightyear ride, went on the People Mover (though they<br />
don't call it that anymore either) twice, and lurched on our aching footsies back to Fantasyland to<br />
try and squeeze in a few more rides. I whirled with Becca on the teacups, and then we stood in<br />
the longest line we'd hit for Dumbo of all things. Which was aggravating, because we were under<br />
their tent when the fireworks started and missed the whole show. Then a couple of bratty kids<br />
pushed Becca and took her Dumbo, then held up the ride fidgeting around.</p>
<p>After that, the night utterly went to hell. We hurried for the gate through waiting-for-parade<br />
traffic, and made our fatal mistake. I went to pick up our package, while Tim and Becca went to<br />
drop off the stroller. We planned to meet outside the gate near the Monorail entrance that we'd<br />
take back to the transportation center and then to Epcot, to catch our bus.</p>
<p>That was the plan, anyway. The Monorail part of it also turned out to be the plan of about ten<br />
thousand other people. So I couldn't find them in the crowd. And it had taken me so long at the<br />
package pickup window (smooth efficiency everywhere else in the park, one dinky window for the<br />
packages) that I was sure they must have already gotten on.</p>
<p>They weren't at the transportation center. I got on the train to Epcot, and busted my butt racing<br />
for the bus stop, thinking they must be waiting there and we still had time to catch that 11:30<br />
bus. Saw <em>armadillos</em> in the flowerbeds, startling me. I reached the bus stop in time to see that<br />
they weren't there either, and agonized as the bus arrived. But I couldn't leave without them, so<br />
I watched it go.</p>
<p>Back I went to the Monorail station. And waited. As each successive train came and they still<br />
didn't get off. I chewed off most of my fingernails from stress, and when the last train showed,<br />
still no Tim and Becca. The track traffic controllers took pity on me and helped me call around,<br />
and we eventually found out that they were at City Hall back in the Magic Kingdom, had never<br />
left the park because they were waiting for me.</p>
<p>Those two guys were the nicest darn guys! After closing up their station, they drove me in their<br />
van all the way back to the transportation center, and Tim and Becca caught the last train to<br />
there. So, after two hours of agonizing and worry, we all got together again. Had to cab it back<br />
to the hotel to the tune of 35 bucks (probably more than it should have been because the cabbie<br />
got a call allegedly warning him of a sinkhole and he had to take an alternate route; sounded like<br />
a scam to us, so he didn't get much of a tip).</p>
<p>A wonderful vacation up until that whole mess. But better that it happened at the end instead of<br />
the beginning, and it all worked out okay. We did have to miss out on the McDonald's with the<br />
world's biggest playplace, to Becca's disappointment, because the airport shuttle would be<br />
picking us up at quarter to ten the next day and it was after two by the time we made it back to<br />
our room.</p>
<p>Thus concluded our vacation, except for sleeping on planes most of the next day and arriving<br />
home safely still in time to see Colleen get booted off the island.</p>
<p>*<br />
<strong><em>Additional Ramblings --</em></strong></p>
<p>These essays keep getting longer and longer ... a chore for me but one I love to do. I know<br />
there's plenty that I neglected to mention, because even though I was taking notes I must have<br />
missed a lot. Apologies to anyone that I failed to credit and to all those people whose names<br />
slipped my mind (or anyone I named erroneously!).</p>
<p>There were several things I didn't I get to do due to scheduling conflicts, and many people I<br />
didn't get to spend much time with. Among them were: Greg G.'s character design workshop,<br />
Vic's storyboard panel, the custom action figure workshop -- did that one even happen?, Win<br />
Xanatos' Money, and visiting with people like Noel, Coyote, and many other online pals. And somehow I managed to shoot nine rolls of film and still never got a pic of either Greg G. or Vic!</p>
<p>A few folks remarked on it, and yes, I have lost weight since last year. 60 pounds to be precise ...<br />
and I hadn't realized what a difference it made until I saw myself on last year's video. I did it,<br />
believe it or not, with the help of hypnosis.</p>
<p>I practically didn't need to pack any tops, since I came home with ::counts:: FIVE new t-shirts.<br />
Wow! They were: a Jericho Fan Club, a G2001 staff shirt, a MageLore shirt made for me by<br />
Jessie, my official con shirt, and the Villains one I bought at Disney World.</p>
<p>And one of the neatest things for selfish old me about these cons is that people keep giving me _stuff! Like Jessie's great MageLore shirt, or the Jericho magnet by Demona May, or new art by Coyote (Denis -- scan me that pic of Cat, okay?). I'm so spoiled! </p>
<p>Racked up a pretty good animal tally -- several roaches (maybe they were beetles but they<br />
looked like roaches) as big as my thumb, a dragonfly with a six-inch body length, two fast little<br />
lizards by the outdoor pool, and those two armadillos at Epcot. At first I thought those babies<br />
were ugly possums (there's a redundancy for you), but no, they were 'dillos.</p>
<p>All in all, it was a great vacation and a great con. I'm looking forward to next year, the 5th<br />
Annual Gathering in Los Angeles. The G2001 team is already hard at work planning events and<br />
arranging for guests. Watch for info soon, sign up early, and tell <em>everyone</em>! This is the Big Push,<br />
people, our chance to get the attention of the folks at Disney and show them that while we may<br />
be obsessive lunatics, we are <em>serious</em> obsessive lunatics!</p>
<p>Thank you for bearing with me through this entire mammoth essay! Don't miss the accompanying<br />
Photo Album!</p>
<p><em>Christine Morgan</em><br />
<em>August 11, 2000</em></p></td>
