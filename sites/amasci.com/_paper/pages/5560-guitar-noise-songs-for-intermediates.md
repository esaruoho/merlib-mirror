<td width="100%"><h1 id="songs-for-intermediates">Songs For Intermediates</h1>
<p>These lessons take the next logical step moving from being a strict beginner to being a well-rounded guitarist. This covers a lot of different topics. If you’ve read any of the <em>Easy Songs For Beginners</em> pieces, you already know that I prefer to teach lessons by using specific songs as examples.</p>
<table data-cellspacing="5" data-cellpadding="0" width="97%" data-border="0">
<colgroup>
<col style="width: 50%" />
<col style="width: 50%" />
</colgroup>

<td data-valign="top"><img src="../about/images/thumbnails_david.gif" data-border="1" width="60" height="70" alt="David Hodge" /><img src="../images/shim.gif" width="8" height="8" /></td>
<td class="standard" data-valign="top"><img src="../images/includesmp3.gif" data-align="right" width="134" height="26" alt="Includes MP3s" /><a href="20030404.html" class="featureTitleLink">Lesson 10: America</a><br />
<span class="smauthorname">By David Hodge</span><br />
<img src="../images/shim.gif" width="50" height="8" /><br />
It's a travelogue so, appropriately, we take you all over the place with this lesson! There's something for everyone in this single guitar arrangement - walking bass lines, a mixture of strumming techniques and it's a waltz! If nothing else, increase your vocabulary by learning the word "gabardine…"<br />
<img src="../images/shim.gif" width="9" height="4" /><br />
<a href="20030404.html" class="smlink">Complete Lesson</a><br />

<p><a href="20010918.html" class="featureTitleLink">Lesson 1: Babylon</a><br />
<span class="smauthorname">By David Hodge</span><br />
<img src="../images/shim.gif" width="50" height="8" /><br />
This is the first installment of Songs for Intermediates. What we’re going to do one these pages is to take the next logical step in moving from being a strict beginner to being a well-rounded guitarist. This will eventually cover a lot of different topics. And, if you’ve read any of the Easy Songs For Beginners pieces, you already know that I prefer to teach lessons by using specific songs as examples.</p>
<p><a href="20011101.html" class="featureTitleLink">Lesson 2: Blackbird</a><br />
<span class="smauthorname">By David Hodge</span><br />
<img src="../images/shim.gif" width="50" height="8" /><br />
Today we’re going to start with that concept and then kind of warp it around a bit. The song is <em>Blackbird</em> by Paul McCartney (but being a Beatle at the time, it is officially a “Lennon/McCartney” piece). Not only is this a good “showing-off” song for the solo guitarist, it is also a great exercise for stretching one’s fingers.</p>
<p><a href="20020209.html" class="featureTitleLink">Lesson 3: Moondance</a><br />
<span class="smauthorname">By David Hodge</span><br />
<img src="../images/shim.gif" width="50" height="8" /><br />
Quite often, the next step is nothing more than relearning things you already know. To illustrate this, let's look at the song Moondance by Van Morrison. It's an intriguing piece in that while it is very easy to play (chord-wise), it does take a little thought to make it sound good.</p>
<p><a href="20020517.html" class="featureTitleLink">Lesson 4: Give A Little Bit</a><br />
<span class="smauthorname">By David Hodge</span><br />
<img src="../images/shim.gif" width="50" height="8" /><br />
Sometimes you sit down with a written piece of music and end up being disappointed because "it doesn't sound like the record." You may have the right chords, but you're playing them in the wrong place on the fretboard. Give A Little Bit by Supertramp demonstrates this concept.</p>
<p><a href="20020708.html" class="featureTitleLink">Lesson 5: Scarborough Fair</a><br />
<span class="smauthorname">By David Hodge</span><br />
<img src="../images/shim.gif" width="50" height="8" /><br />
Paul Simon's version of <em>Scarborough Fair</em> is something I try to teach my students fairly early on in their studies. Like <em>Blackbird</em>, it seems daunting at first, but it's truly not hard at all. Most of the song involves simple patterns that get repeated during the course of the verses.</p>
<p><a href="20020921.html" class="featureTitleLink">Lesson 6: Yesterday</a><br />
<span class="smauthorname">By David Hodge</span><br />
<img src="../images/shim.gif" width="50" height="8" /><br />
The version I intend to show you is not the original recording. While it is based on the Beatles' original song, my arrangement also incorporates elements that I like to use in my own playing, such as moving bass lines and the use of inverted thirds and chord shapes to use as vocal cues.</p>
<p><img src="../images/includesmp3.gif" data-align="right" width="134" height="26" alt="Includes MP3s" /><a href="20021019.html" class="featureTitleLink">Lesson 7: Fields of Gold</a><br />
<span class="smauthorname">By David Hodge</span><br />
<img src="../images/shim.gif" width="50" height="8" /><br />
I hope you're ready for a work out! We'll take this four chord song and make a breathtaking solo guitar arrangement for it. Lots of finger-style work and voice leading will teach us the importance of the guitar as an accompanying instrument. If you want to learn the secrets of finessing an arrangement, this is a great place to start.</p>
<p><img src="../images/includesmp3.gif" data-align="right" width="134" height="26" alt="Includes MP3s" /><a href="20021212.html" class="featureTitleLink">Lesson 8: Losing My Religion</a><br />
<span class="smauthorname">By David Hodge</span><br />
<img src="../images/shim.gif" width="50" height="8" /><br />
We continue on with another look at arranging a song you might normally just strum along with the chords. Here you are the guitar, mandolin and even the bass with one instrument. And believe it or not, this is way easier than some of our recent intermediate lessons. Did I mention that it also contain MP3 examples?</p>
<p><img src="../images/includesmp3.gif" data-align="right" width="134" height="26" alt="Includes MP3s" /><a href="20030206.html" class="featureTitleLink">Lesson 9: A Simple Twist of Fate</a><br />
<span class="smauthorname">By David Hodge</span><br />
<img src="../images/shim.gif" width="50" height="8" /><br />
<em>Simple Twist of Fate</em> offers a lesson in chord progressions for those would-be songwriters, and one on chord construction in open tuning for those of you still needing a challenge. While we're at it, let's throw in a little bit about string muting and maybe even touch upon strumming.</p></td>
