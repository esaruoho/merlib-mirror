The Original EVOPRAYER

(Containing Poems of the Homeless, and The Engine Burning Human Evil)

This is a collection of work that has languished unpublished for the last seven years or so. This collection contains attempts to write an anti-novel written in the same style as Motivated for the Cause, my anti-novel of 1985. A life dedicated to the spiritual, the philosophical, the charitable and to the artistic and creative in this world can lead one to some strange places. That is not to say there isn't a greater hand helping one out, somehow.

The problem with this collection I was working on is that it is on an integrative anti-novel like my previous anti-novel.....and I don't think the original work from 92-95 will ever become one. Most of it works better as non-fiction fragments. So then I realized, perhaps it is an anti-novel of fragments.

I stopped working on it many times because I became more and more alienated from what I was seeing happening in Western Christian churches, and the work explains themes of that religion. My perception was of an extreme lack of progressive thought: When many see Christians they think of people who could be molded into anything the elites wanted them to be: good consumers who tow the big corporate party line. Christianity was also becoming synonymous to many people for the terms judgmental, uncaring, offensive, narrow-minded. But the truth is, the people who see the abstract and meaningless semantic capacity term "Christians" as being only judgmental, uncaring, offensive, narrow-minded are probably also those very things themselves, just pointing their exclusivity in another direction. So, I no longer care how it looks to expose my inner creative life as it expresses itself in the Christian imagery of my childhood.

Some grow up being a part of that true upper class, the kind of person that has the good fortune to be able to ponder the deep mysteries of life hour upon end, who finds the spiritual meaning in life in love. But often, the philosopher is struck down by the needs of life, the leisure perhaps granted by kind parents is sucked like dirt by the vacuum cleaner that is the community -- we are forced to "earn our ride." But some of the motivation still must remain.

I'm looking for floating dialog that can be interpreted in many ways, but not like some abstract modern art I don't seek all meaning to be amorphous, because I myself see meaning in what I'm writing.

Self-Rudeness

Sometimes we tend to judge ourselves in rude ways that have nothing to do with the face of reality. We like to compare ourselves to characters who themselves try to be seen as "the very best" -- the kind who are driving the nicest cars and dress the nicest and who many would consider as the most attractive.

But unfortunately, these people are not so high, they have their failures, they "fall short of the glory of God," and even the idealism of many who might now seem to have anything going for them.

There are many who consistently hold themselves up against negative light -- criticizing themselves in awful ways. This essentially cripples them in their all parts of their life

What is the problem with these self-haters? What is the cure? It seems interesting that they tend to not measure themselves against the one-million people that are now in American jails, or the hundreds of thousands that are in various types of mental hospitals, or even the hundreds of thousands that are homeless.

They aren't measuring themselves against the homes that have problems with incest, physical abuse or addiction. Instead, they weigh themselves against the best, the most average images that they can find. Or they are weighing themselves against what they see on the highways -- the multitudes who are driving more expensive cars than they.

They don't realize that their old conceptions of normal and average were misleading. So many of these average people have something tragically wrong if you just get close enough. The wife has a brother who is a habitual criminal, in and out of jails. Someone's daughter committed suicide at age 22. The husband has a sister who was murdered. They don't have to look from a moral perspective, they don't even tend to see how the average is affected by disease, unemployment -- they don't want to weigh themselves against the unemployment rate, they don't want to get any higher self-esteem for some reason.

Granted, these people are often the victims of rejection that stems from parental or peer abuse. This might be very difficult to overcome -- but if it is to be overcome it will be by use of reason. There is no reason to have to measure up to someone's standard of normality -- normality is actually quite full of criminality, insanity, disease, and poverty. These are conditions of life that all of us find ourselves facing. I don't know who is painting a picture displaying hordes of perfect lives.
