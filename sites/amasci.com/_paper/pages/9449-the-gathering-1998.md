<td>      I made some mistakes last year that I vowed not to repeat this year. But, <br />
as is the way of such things, I made up for them with all new mistakes! 
<p>      One blooper I managed to avoid was the lack-of-sleep one. Last year, I <br />
worked the night before I departed and told myself I'd have time for a nap before <br />
packing and a snooze on the plane. I knew better this time. And so it was that, <br />
well-rested, I boarded my flight for New York. </p>
<p>      And then one of the all-new mistakes came up -- I flew into JFK instead <br />
of Newark, thinking it would be a shorter ride to the hotel. Ooh, was I ever wrong! <br />
 <br />
     I was planning to meet up with Christi, whose plane was arriving 45 <br />
minutes after mine. But it took almost exactly 45 minutes for my luggage to get <br />
unloaded, and only then did I realize that Christi's gate was in an entire other <br />
building, a good 1/4 mile away. </p>
<p>      So I scrambled around trying to have her paged and never did manage it <br />
(but if any of you were wondering why I had an 800-number written on my palm <br />
for a day and a half, it was the Delta info line and I used a permanent pen). </p>
<p>      By now it was 5:00 PM, and I got on a Gray Line shuttle with five other <br />
people. We cruised around the airport for a while, at one point getting stuck <br />
behind a 3-taxi accident, and eventually pulled into the parking lot in front of <br />
another terminal (still in the same airport, one of those other buildings I <br />
mentioned). Whereupon the driver told us she'd be back in a couple of minutes, <br />
and departed. </p>
<p>      There we sat, with the air conditioner on full blast (in retrospect, I should <br />
have relished it, given how the hotel's climate-control would turn out to be). For <br />
fully 20 minutes, with no clue where our driver had gone. A bathroom stop? we <br />
wondered. A latte? A bikini wax? What the hell was taking so long? </p>
<p>      At last she returned with 4 German guys in tow, at which point one of my <br />
fellow detainees began haranguing her about the delay and wanted the <br />
manager's name, etc. But we eventually got on the road again. In the middle of <br />
what I assume was rush hour. </p>
<p>      The thing that most amazed me about my various trips on the streets of <br />
the city was that, given the way people drive, so few of the cars are damaged! <br />
Nearly two hours of horns, hair-raising close calls, and pedestrians who are either <br />
convinced of their own immortality or have a serious death wish. <br />
 <br />
     Sometime after 7:00 PM (and just my luck, my hotel was the last stop!), I <br />
finally dragged my suitcase through the doors. I was stressed, headachy, freezing <br />
from that darn air conditioner, and generally not at my best (plus my bag got stuck <br />
in the door and nearly gave me whiplash). I was just hoping that, after all this, I <br />
wouldn't have gotten the hotel wrong. </p>
<p>      Four people leaning over the balcony calling to me, cheering, and taking <br />
pictures assuaged my fear. I recognized Missy, and the next thing I knew, Doug <br />
and Thomas had pelted down the stairs. They carried my luggage, escorted me <br />
to the registration desk, and generally made me feel like a real celeb. </p>
<p>      While I was standing there waiting for the clerk to figure out something <br />
about a fax I had supposedly received (it turned out to be another Morgan), <br />
Christi showed up, to my intense relief. I was terrified she would be stuck at the <br />
airport waiting for me, even though I knew she would figure out that I wasn't going <br />
to make it. So we both arrived, a little weary, and with our entourage in tow, went <br />
up to our room. </p>
<p>      Our very small room. That was one thing in which last year's con <br />
surpassed -- the rooms last year were actual suites. But the view from our <br />
bathroom was exceptional: the Empire State Building and the Chrysler Building. <br />
Quite a thing to see in the mirror while brushing one's teeth. And a good thing, <br />
too, because the bathroom light was choosey about when and if it worked, so if <br />
not for the glow of the skyscrapers, we would have been answering nature's call <br />
in the dark. </p>
<p>      I wrote "The Christine Morgan" on my badge, pinned on my Jericho <br />
button, and went around to say hello to familiar faces from last year and new <br />
faces to go with familiar names. I just had time before the opening ceremonies to <br />
freshen up and make myself look human. </p>
<p>      Some of the artwork was already on display in the Crystal Room (so <br />
named because there were chandeliers and crystal-dripping wall sconces <br />
everywhere, not to mention the three enormous ones hanging in the lobby -- <br />
overdone, and with the gold-flocked wallpaper, perhaps just a tad on the tacky <br />
side, but I took a picture all the same). </p>
<p>      The opening ceremonies got underway, and Christi was given a T-shirt <br />
emblazoned with her own deep-voiced smooth-talker of the night, Spike, as a <br />
thank-you for her work in creating the gorgeous portraits of Keith, Salli, and Greg, <br />
which would be presented later in the weekend. </p>
<p>      Then I was whisked away by Mitch (aka Pogo, fandom hunk) to a <br />
romantically-lit French restaurant and plied with wine and champagne ... but I <br />
suppose I should point out that Christi and Mitch's lady Karine (aka Kanthara) <br />
were also part of the group. </p>
<p>      After that, we went back to the hotel and learned that Greg had made an <br />
appearance while we were gone -- wearing his official Gathering striped shirt! I <br />
got to finally say hi to Denis, who'd come all the way from Belgium, and finally met <br />
Jennifer (aka CrzyDemona) face to face and presented her with her own Jericho <br />
button, as she is president of the fan club. </p>
<p>      The con suite was on the 4th floor, originally in one room but then the <br />
hotel staff had us move to another room so they could re-carpet or something, <br />
and there seemed to be a lot of aggravation and confusion caused by all of that. </p>
<p>      I roomed with Christi, Missy, and Mandi, and we arranged for a 7:00 <br />
wake up call on Saturday. Somehow, it all worked out with four ladies and one <br />
dinky bathroom (though with an incredible view, did I mention that?). Christi has <br />
mastered the art of stealth, since by the time the rest of us woke up, she was <br />
already dressed and ready to go -- she attributes this to plenty of practice trying <br />
not to wake up her boys so that she can get things done in the morning. </p>
<p>      I had volunteered for a shift at the registration desk from 9:00 to 10:00, <br />
but Jordan showed up shortly after I did, and since he knew what he was doing, I <br />
sat there and tried to be supportive. At 10:00 was one of the events I'd been <br />
anticipating, the fanfic panel. </p>
<p>      It was originally supposed to be Christi, Missy, and myself, but we <br />
managed to pull a few other folks in so we filled all six chairs. Leva, A Fan, and <br />
Scott (aka Abram Wintersmith) were persuaded to join us. </p>
<p>      Now, I don't know about my co-panelists, but I had a great time. I love <br />
attention, and I love to talk about myself and my stories. And plugging my book! <br />
Which, sadly, was not ready in time for me to have actual copies on-hand. But <br />
some people just couldn't wait to give me money, so I got several advance orders <br />
that will be shipped out as soon as I get and sign the copies. Which is supposed <br />
to be by Friday Aug. 21; we shall see. </p>
<p>      I ran into Greg and gave him the Goliath figure to replace the one that <br />
had been stolen from him. While it was tempting to see what I could get in trade, <br />
figuring that if anybody had Garg goodies that I couldn't get anyplace else, it <br />
would be him, my more diplomatic side prevailed. </p>
<p>      After that, I told myself to get down to the gift shop and buy Becca a <br />
present before I spent all my money on stuff for me. But just as I was about to do <br />
that, the dealer (Jubilee?) began setting up, and I spotted a bunch of things that I <br />
didn't have in my collection. The thing about collecting is: you're content until you <br />
are aware of something you don't have, and then it becomes a constant <br />
maddening urge. </p>
<p>      I walked away from that table with lots of stickers, issue 9 of the comic, <br />
some static-cling things to go in the window (of my new car, hee-hee!), various <br />
other items, and most amusing of all, a "locker bag" which was a plastic <br />
drawstring bag containing Gargoyles brush, toothbrush, toothbrush holder, and <br />
even official Gargoyles Kleenex. I also bought a bunch of raffle tickets, because I <br />
had my heart set on the big vinyl play tent. </p>
<p>      After that, it was time for part of Greg's Secret Event (GSE), so I did that <br />
and hung out in the con suite, and finally visited the gift shop. Bought Becca <br />
some presents -- a snow globe with the Statue of Liberty in it and a boxed set of <br />
toy cars including a taxi and NYPD patrol car. I also got a postcard of the Empire <br />
State Building and a stamp, and mailed it off to my mom. </p>
<p>      There were also a couple of workshops going on -- fanfic, webmasters -- <br />
that didn't pan out because so many people were trying to get in on the GSE. I <br />
also poked my head briefly into the art workshop but didn't stay long, because <br />
what some of those people draw with their off hand with eyes shut is better than <br />
anything I could ever hope to do. There was also a filk workshop, and I confess, <br />
filk hurts my brain so I try to avoid it whenever I can (to the relief of Missy and <br />
Constance, who first tried to encourage me and then feared I'd be too good at it!) </p>
<p>      I eventually realized I should get something substantial to eat because I <br />
kept noshing on munchies. So I popped across the street to McD's, where I found <br />
the food more expensive than in Seattle and the customers considerably ruder. <br />
Then it was a quick stuffing of the face before my stint guarding the art show. </p>
<p>      While I was keeping tabs on the artwork -- lots of beautiful paintings and <br />
drawings, a stained-glass of Demona using the Phoenix Gate, a sculpture of <br />
Goliath, and three fantastic reproductions of the magic items (Grimorum, Gate, <br />
and Eye of Odin) -- I was also able to listen in on part of the TGS panel. </p>
<p>      I'd lost track of Christi at some point in there; I think she was whisked <br />
away by Mitch again for Indian food; the man seems to eat constantly and yet just <br />
look at him! (which I did, whenever I got the chance!). </p>
<p>      Around 8:00, it was time for GSE, which was great. Lots of talent on <br />
display, not only Greg's writing and narrating ability but the acting skills of many of <br />
our own beloved friends and acquaintances. </p>
<p>      The GSE ran long, though, until after 10:00 PM. And then there was a <br />
scheduling conflict, because the Gathering '99 World Tour Bid Party was going on <br />
in the con suite at the same time as the auction in the Crystal Room. And, <br />
although I am fully in support and hoping to be very involved with the G99 team, <br />
the siren song of merchandise I didn't have yet was too strong to resist. </p>
<p>      So it was that I stayed for the auction, and let me tell you, the bidding was <br />
out of control! I had brought plenty of money, but could not bring myself to go that <br />
high on several items! I dearly wanted one of the Gargoyles floormats, for <br />
instance, but I guess I didn't want it enough since I wasn't going to bid 60+ <br />
dollars. </p>
<p>      Seth (aka IRC Goliath) must have come with a wad o' cash, because he <br />
outbid me more times than I care to remember. Even on the final item, the big <br />
cardboard stand-up of Goliath (okay, there was some other video-game Hunter <br />
guy in the lot too, but I only wanted Goliath). But if he was going to take it away <br />
from me, he was going to have to pay! Which he did. </p>
<p>      The highest bid, as I recall, was $105 for a pair of T-shirts. Wow. </p>
<p>      Speaking of T-shirts, I was disappointed because I had pre-ordered and <br />
paid for a specific size, and was planning to buy one of another specific size for <br />
Stephen, but due to some sort of problem with the T-shirt makers, there were <br />
only shirts of one size to be had. So I have to wait until mine gets made and <br />
mailed to me. </p>
<p>      The auction wrapped up at sometime after 2:00 AM, and by then most <br />
everybody were punchy from exhaustion. I dropped by the con suite to see if the <br />
party was still going on, and the tail end of it was. Apparently Greg had been there <br />
for a while, giving some good advice and suggestions to next year's organizers. </p>
<p>      As late as it was, I was still the first one to bed, and must have zonked <br />
out the minute my head hit the pillow, because the next thing I knew, Mandi's <br />
mom was calling bright and early Sunday morning. </p>
<p>      I came downstairs just in time to photograph the two big lines of people <br />
heading out on the Central Park tour. I wasn't going this year; my feet still <br />
remembered the blisters from last year and the thought of a ride on a real New <br />
York subway somehow failed to appeal to my sense of adventure. </p>
<p>      I got some breakfast (juice and a pastry from the place in the hotel lobby) <br />
and hung out in the con suite with Brian, Alison, Constance, Thomas, Shari, and <br />
some other folks. Then there was a slight snafu, because Constance thought she <br />
was supposed to wait until the Central Park tour came back before taking the <br />
Empire State tour out, but some of the other con staff thought Constance was <br />
taking the Empire State tour during the Central Park tour. </p>
<p>      So anyhow, eventually the tour thing worked out, and we set off to the <br />
building that had once been the world's tallest. A walk of many blocks along the <br />
humid, noisy street. Made noisier by the fact that there was some sort of a parade <br />
about to start (someone said it was a Republic of Jamaica celebration?), and <br />
there were spectators and flag vendors all over the place. </p>
<p>      There were 26 of us that lined up in the cool marble foyer while Jordan <br />
bought our group tickets. The lines surprised me -- it was like being at <br />
Disneyland! Waiting in line for the elevator (twice, as there were two), battling the <br />
crowds ... but the view, even though it was hazy, was just as impressive as <br />
advertised. We could see our hotel -- I meant to hang a towel out the bathroom <br />
window and see if one of those coin-operated telescopes showed anything <br />
alarming, but forgot -- and the Statue of Liberty, Central Park, and the Jamaican <br />
parade route. </p>
<p>      After shooting some film, I was ready to head back, so Shari, Jen, Patrick <br />
and I told Batya and Jordan (who might have freaked if their headcount came up <br />
four short) and we returned to the hotel. I took my lunch to the Crystal Room and <br />
watched Merav's (sp?) costume workshop, which had done a materials-run to a <br />
thrift shop and had fabric and stuff strewn all over the floor. </p>
<p>      Alison chose that moment to gift me with a Scooby Doo doll, and if you <br />
don't know why that made me scream, Dear Reader, you're very lucky indeed! <br />
Let's just say that even I have written at least one fanfic I regret! </p>
<p>      Meanwhile, the great "Where is Christi?" panic had gone on. Seems like <br />
everybody wanted to talk to her, and nobody knew when she was leaving, only <br />
that she had a flight sometime that afternoon. Turns out she'd toured the art <br />
museum and then dashed off to the airport, so there wasn't much chance for <br />
goodbyes. </p>
<p>      Then it was 4:00, and time for Greg's talk. Last year, nearly every picture <br />
I took of that man was either the back of his head or had the worst case of red- <br />
eye I've ever seen. So hopefully they'll come out better this time. </p>
<p>      Much of the info was the same -- we viewed the promotional video, the <br />
leica reel of Bad Guys (there may be hope for that one again, now that the chain <br />
of command at Disney's been revamped), and Greg told the jalapena story and <br />
the "better than Barney" story. </p>
<p>      Some new treats included a pre-Gargoyles plan for the New Olympians <br />
as a series (and oddly, hearing all about that and seeing the pics took a lot of the <br />
sting out of an episode that really gave me a hairball), and a letter from Salli Elise <br />
Richardson. There had been a rumor flying about that she was going to do the <br />
same surprise appearance like Keith David did last year, but no such luck, as she <br />
was filming in Vancouver B.C. (and he was in Australia, apparently). </p>
<p>      Eric (aka Gorebash) missed most of the talk, and word was it was <br />
because he was dying his hair, in preparation for going as Elisa to the costume <br />
ball. </p>
<p>      All in all, though, I think my absolute favorite part of the talk was toward <br />
the end, when Greg broke off, looked toward the back of the room, and declared, <br />
"Jesus, there's a lot of people making out in here!" </p>
<p>      At that point, everyone whirled. And because Karine (Kanthara), sitting <br />
with Mitch, had bent forward suddenly to hide, effectively submerging herself <br />
behind the people in front of her, all eyes found Jennifer (Crzy) and her husband <br />
Alan springing apart with a guilty look. Hoots and whistles and ribald howls came <br />
from all corners of the room. Everyone thought it was just them until Karine began <br />
making a noise that was half anguished-shamed wail, half hysterical wheezes of <br />
laughter. She surfaced briefly, showing a face as red as Brooklyn, then vanished <br />
again. </p>
<p>      Needless to say, it took quite a while to restore order and get on with the <br />
talk! Then it was autograph time, and I took the opportunity to tell Greg how much <br />
I liked the essay he posted in "Ask Greg" about sex in fanfic. Up until then, I'd <br />
been operating with an attitude of "as long as I don't know for sure he'd hate it, I <br />
won't feel so bad." And then, after reading his thoughts on the subject, I was very <br />
relieved. I'm confident that if he were allowed to read my stuff, he'd probably like <br />
it. Whew! </p>
<p>      After that, we had an hour and a half to get ready for the masquerade <br />
ball. I called home and chatted with Tim and even Becca (who could only be pried <br />
away from the computer by telling her Mommy had bought presents). Then I went <br />
up to the 32nd floor and got into my Una-suit. The horn had broken in transit, but <br />
a little tape patched it back up. </p>
<p>      All things considered, for a costume I put together with a hot glue gun, it <br />
wasn't half bad! Una with a great big caboose. But I do wish that the elevators <br />
worked a little differently! One set went from the lobby through the 20th floor, the <br />
other went from the lobby only to the 21st through 40th floors. So all of us in our <br />
costumes had to make an appearance in the lobby before escaping to the relative <br />
safety of the 2nd floor Crystal Room. </p>
<p>      The costumes this year were spectacular! I doubt anybody was surprised <br />
that Jennifer walked away with first prize for her Demona outfit. It was perfect. <br />
The feet, the teeth, everything. If only she hadn't broken off her wing talons <br />
perching in the window and scaring passers-by! Her husband Alan made a great <br />
MacBeth, having grown a beard specially for the occasion (though, IMO, he <br />
should keep it!). </p>
<p>      Shari and her sisters (or is one an in-law; I can never keep track) came <br />
as the Weird Sisters again, but this time in their little-girl-sailor-suit guises. We <br />
had two Unas, myself and Noel (and Siryn was _not_ Una; don't call her that or <br />
she might ram that long golden spiral horn of hers right through your head!). <br />
Kathy was a very professional Dominique Destine, Seth was the Hunter. We had <br />
Future Tense Brooklyn and Demona, a couple of Xanatoses and Foxes, <br />
Alexander as a grown-up, an evil Ninja, a Quarryman, lots of gargoyles, Dominic <br />
Dracon (when Thomas got rid of the moustache, he looked eerily like Jay Leno <br />
minus the trademark chin; the Green Baron is too cute to be a priest and I must <br />
admit to having some very evil thoughts about corrupting him!) and even a guest <br />
appearance by one of the Scarlet Angel band members, portrayed by Denis. </p>
<p>      And then there was Gore. His entrance was a complete showstopper. If <br />
Jen as Demona wowed the socks off us, Gorelisa left us stunned. </p>
<p>      Now, a lot of guys can't pull off dressing as a woman convincingly. If <br />
nothing else, the wig ruins the whole effect. But Eric is blessed with a lush fall of <br />
thick, gorgeous hair, and the best eyelashes I've ever seen on anyone, male or <br />
female. With the hair darkened, the goatee gone, and Aimee's make-up artistry, <br />
not to mention the outfit and the balloons, it was an amazing transformation! </p>
<p>      Deciding that if he was gonna do it, he was gonna go all out, there was <br />
much pouting and preening for the camera. And maybe just a little too much of a <br />
fixation on the boobs. Someone should have loaned him a bra, because they <br />
were wandering all over every time he moved. But that gave him the excuse to <br />
fondle them back into place, something that he might have enjoyed just a wee bit <br />
too much! I heard that Alison accidentally popped one, and how she got her <br />
talons on his cha-chas is something upon which I'd rather not speculate. </p>
<p>      The promenade was really something. Each contestant had to parade <br />
back and forth down the aisle to show off the costumes. And oh, was there ever a <br />
lot of wiggling and prancing! I thought Seth was going to hurt himself! </p>
<p>      At some point during all this, Greg snuck in unnoticed. I have no idea <br />
when or how, but Jordan eventually spotted him (hiding behind Jennifer's wings). <br />
Well, at least by then he couldn't have possibly thought we were any less sane <br />
than before! </p>
<p>      I managed to get a few chances to talk to Greg, about the GSE and my <br />
book and all that, and was tremendously flattered when he told me I impressed <br />
the hell out of him with my writing. Not that he's read any of my fanfic, too bad, <br />
but because of the book. Wish I'd had a copy to send him. Something he could <br />
actually read! </p>
<p>      So I'll have to send one, a thank-you gift because in a way, the book <br />
owes a fair amount to him, seen as how all this practice on fanfic has helped my <br />
"real" writing quite a bit. If anybody knows the address for Dreamworks, I'd <br />
appreciate it. Much safer to send it to work, rather than track down his home <br />
address and make his family worry about crazed stalker-fans! </p>
<p>      One thing I did notice about the masquerade -- as the hours went by and <br />
more and more bits of costume were shed (wings, wigs, and tails mostly), the <br />
cha-chas remained! </p>
<p>      I was thrilled to win the play tent in the raffle, although between that and <br />
my plastic Una-wings, packing everything into one bag was quite a challenge! <br />
Wish I'd stayed up later. I heard there were some wild conversations in the wee <br />
small hours after I'd showered away that white makeup and collapsed. </p>
<p>      Monday morning was a time for lots of hanging out in the lobby and <br />
saying goodbye. I bet the hotel people were glad to be rid of us, since they booted <br />
us out of the Crystal Room and con suite as early as they possibly could (though <br />
they were peeking in the windows at us all through the masquerade). I wheedled <br />
a sketch out of Karine, figuring I'd better ask before she reads my account of <br />
what happened, as she'll probably want to kill me. And got more hugs than I've <br />
had all year! </p>
<p>      It began to rain shortly before my shuttle was due. The rain softened the <br />
city with a grey-gold light, making it prettier than I would have expected. But the <br />
rain, buckets of it, also flooded the intersections and made the pedestrians' <br />
movements even more likely to get them run over. </p>
<p>      The ride back was much better. No getting stranded for 20 minutes. A <br />
few honks, yeah, but not too many. We reached the airport with plenty of time to <br />
spare before my flight, so I settled myself down and wrote out titles and brief <br />
notions for my next dozen fanfics. </p>
<p>      I was even inspired to do a little sketching, once I was safely away from <br />
people who really knew what they were doing. Though one would think that the <br />
last thing I'd want to do was depress myself seeing how my feeble skills stacked <br />
up to all the art I'd admired this weekend! </p>
<p>      Overall, last year was fun and this year was even more fun, and I'm <br />
looking forward to next year! Dallas in '99! <br />
