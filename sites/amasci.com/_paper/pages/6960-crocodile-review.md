# Crocodile Review

*Source page: `~redone/swrong/rev2.html`*

Sick and Wrong at the Crocodile Cafe (Seattle) 11/2/95

      I think I'm starting to get a handle on this group.   Maybe...

      The Crocodile is a nice venue.  There's a real bandstand and enough room
    for a show.  I like the way the lighting in the band room makes a gin and
    tonic glow blue in the dark.  The sound here was much better than my first
    encounter with this group.  The vocals were audible over the instruments if
    not always intelligible.  My lack of comprehension is due more to the
    nature of the music than any technical fault.

      Sick and Wrong led off a four band show.  Maybe the first spot on the
    bill is best for a week night show.  My writing has left me with a daytime
    job so I was obligated to leave halfway through the set by Get It Gurl who
    were second.  They were pretty and wore nice costumes but I didn't mind
    leaving while they were playing.  If I'd had to wait around for the last
    set to hear Sick and Wrong it would have made the following morning a
    torture.
      Thursday is not a day to expect a big lively crowd for a relatively
    unknown bill and this one wasn't.  They were moderately appreciative but I
    doubt many of them knew beforehand what to expect.  They flatly refused to
    get involved in the show despite the best efforts of the band.  Perhaps
    after listening to Get It Gurl, they regretted their lack of demand for an
    encore.  I know I wanted Sick and Wrong to play longer.

      I don't know where Sick and Wrong gets their material but I'm impressed.
    Songs like "Sex Change", "Slap Yer Meat" and "Hung Like a Horse" are
    perfect for this group.  Their new song "Uncle Buck" is a piece of work.
    Somebody here has a real handle on how to create an effect and push peoples
    buttons.  This is definitely adult material but it's done with flair and
    total lack of self-consciousness and a healthy dose of humor.  I've heard
    it said that "there's nothing as ridiculous looking as people having sex"
    and everyone in this group knows it on some level.

      Adam Bratman's lead guitar remains on second hearing, much better than
    what passes for competent in most local bands.  Static stays seamlessly
    with him on rhythm guitar while they maintain a suitably frantic pace and
    avoid sliding into the murky sound that ruins the best efforts of many
    bands that try to play high energy punk rock.
      Muddy Checker's bass playing was a definite asset - walking all over and
    laying down a solid bottom to the melodies.
      I would be a poor reviewer it I didn't point out problems when I heard
    them so I have to say I was disappointed with the performance of Young John
    Pimp the drummer.  His beat was competent but the sound and tone of his
    skins was a bit flat on this night.
      I will also object to Adam's closing antics.  Destroying a setup after
    the set is a sort of tradition in rock music but if it's going to be an
    effective part of the show it has to include real destruction.  Anything
    less than flaming amplifiers, broken guitar bodies and feet through drum
    heads is just sham.

      Little Miss Farrah was once again a bundle of pure energy and mischief.
    The childish demeanor combined with her costume achieve the aura of a very
    naughty 11 year old wise beyond her years.  An utter tease and flirt on
    stage I marvel at her ability to retain a sort of innocence and decency in
    the midst of Mr. Wendy.

      Mr. Wendy's stage presence defies description.  The only thing I can
    think of that might be comparable is Wendy O. Williams of The Plasmatics.
    I don't know how much Mr. Wendy is consciously influenced by Wendy O. but
    judging from the similarity of the names I have to suspect she is fully
    aware of the legacy.  I know The Plasmatics by reputation only, but even to
    my uneducated eye Mr. Wendy is not just an imitator.  Where Wendy O's
    trademark was bits of electricians tape Mr. Wendy's costume includes
    professional pasties - she has her own distinctive erotic accessory.

      The overall effect gives the music powerful sexual energy without being
    just sexploitation.  It's my opinion that the humor and the quality of the
    performances are what make it work well.  Sick and Wrong put on a first
    rate show yet remain friendly and accessible - refusing to put themselves
    above the audience.

      Based on my second encounter I have to renew my recommendation.  This
    group is getting better, go see them before they have to clean up their act
    so they can get discovered and make some money.  The only question left in
    my mind is which one is sick and which one is wrong?

                                            Ambrose McNibble
                                         a_mcnibble@halcyon.com
