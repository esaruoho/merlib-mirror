## Explore these Massive, time-sucking indexes of CoolSci:

> [Cool Robot of the Week](http://www.cs.cmu.edu/~pprk/index.html)
>
> A large collection maintained by [NASA telerobotics](http://ranier.hq.nasa.gov/telerobotics_page/telerobotics.shtm). With archives going back to 1996.
>
> [The Memepool: Science Archive](http://www.memepool.com/Subject/Science/)
>
> The [Memepool](http://www.memepool.com) is an excellent, eclectic "cool sites of the week" page with all links to all sorts of somewhat warped websites. Browse their collection [by subject](http://www.memepool.com/Subject/).
>
> [Exploratorium's](http://www.exploratorium.edu/learning_studio/sciencesites.html) "Cool Sites" archive
>
> The Exploratorium's Learning Studio maintains a monthly "Top Ten" page for science sites. They've ferreted out the cream of webscience cool sites. Don't miss their past months' archive.
>
> [Dr. Matrix' Weird Web World of Science - Awards Page](%0Ahttp://www.scientium.com/drmatrix/award.htm)
>
> Dr. Matrix cruises the www and collects the coolsci sites for his Awards page. Seventeen catagories, hundreds and hundreds of science sites. Cruise the rest of his site for fascinating, thoughtful commentary
>
> [SCIENCE CITY](http://www.keysites.com/keysites/hotspots/hotspots.html)
>
> Science hotlist from [New Scientist](http://www.newscientist.com%0A) magazine. They've got Scientific American trounced but good. (...not necessarily! Sci Am just got a late start, see below...)
>
> [The Editors Selections](http://www.sciam.com/bookmarks/editselect.html)
>
> Science hotlist from [Scientific American](http://www.sciam.com) Magazine.
>
> [Science Friday: HOTLINKS](http://www.sciencefriday.com/pages/misc/hotlinks.html)
>
> NPR Radio's TALK OF THE NATION: [SCIENCE FRIDAY](http://www.sciencefriday.com) is not to be missed. They've also built a nice collection of eclectic science links.
>
> [Netsurfer Science: Backissues](http://www.netsurf.com/nss/backiss.html)
>
> NSS is a free email newsletter of cool-science links. Peruse their back-issues links for a gigantic collection of fascinating explorations.
>
> [ZOOLAND Artificial Life Resource](http://alife.santafe.edu/~joke/zooland/)
>
> The famous/infamous (delete one) Santa Fe Institute brings us this brainmeltingly extensive collection of links to sites involving Artificial Life; a-life. Zerosum breakage and titfortat dealings. Simulations and animations and appletbased growings. Fractals and fishtanks and emergentboid wings. These are a few of my favorite things!
>
> [Cool Science Archive](http://www.nap.edu/csarc.html) from the [Nat. Acadamy Press](http://www.nap.edu)
>
> Also check out the NAP [reading room](http://books.nap.edu/info/browse.htm) for all sorts of science books online. Yes, complete actual illustrated books on the internet for free.
>
> [The Virtual Laboratory](http://physicsweb.org/TIPTOP/VLAB/) at Pilot to Physics
>
> An excellent (and large) collection of hands-on Java applets, shockwave, and VRML programs for the visual learning of physics concepts.
>
> [ENC Digital Dozen Archive](http://www.enc.org/classroom/dd/nf_ddarchiv.htm)
>
> Eisenhower Nat. Clearinghouse picks a monthly top-12 science education sites. No dry textbook sites, this archive of past picks is the really cool stuff.
>
> [Omni Magazine Cool Site ofthe Day](http://www.omnimag.com/cool_science/old.html)
>
> OMNI bases their 'cool site' picks on a bit less normal criteria than other index sites... with excellent results!
>
> [Hands on Science Centers Worldwide](http://www.cs.cmu.edu/~mwm/sci.html)
>
> Science Museums have the coolest websites. Here they all are, all at once. Take it slow, don't get brainburn.
>
> [Sci/Math Education](http://www-hpcc.astro.washington.edu/scied/science.html)
>
> Alan Cairn's vast and wonderful collection of sites for Science and Math educators.
>
> [CCI MAGAZINE'S](http://www.currents.net/magazine/reviews/scirevws.html) site of the week reviews: science Computer Currents Interactive has a growing "site reviews" page online, including a Science section with worthwhile links.
>
> [PITSCO COLLAB. PROJECTS](http://www.pitsco.com/p/Respages/collab.html)
>
> From the Pitsco Science Catalog site, a collection of "collaborative projects," a cool-science link collection by any other name.
>
> [Interesting Devices](http://dir.yahoo.com/Computers_and_Internet/Internet/Interesting_Devices_Connected_to_the_Net/) on the Net
>
> From Yahoo, this is an immense and venerable collection of sites with live stillcams, interactive robots, and all manner of instrumentation connected to the net. Spy on people's offices all over the world. See the coffee pot that started it all. Watch 24hr timelapses at the UW. Talk to the cat. See if the pop machine at MIT has any warm Jolt cola left.
>
> [SAS WWW Index](http://www.chebucto.ns.ca/Science/SAS/sas-sub1.html) for Science hobbyists.
>
> The [Society for Amateur Scientists](http://www.sas.org) maintains this index of sites of interest to science hobbyists worldwide. Become a member, get on their listserver!
>
> [Franklin Institute's hotlist of online exhibits](http://sln.fi.edu/tfi/jump.html)
>
> A list of highly interactive Cool Science sites.
>
> [Franklin Institute's monthly hotlist](http://www.fi.edu/qa96/favesindex.html%0A)
>
> [Yahoo's SCIENCE index](http://www.yahoo.com/Science/)
>
> Hours of surfing pleasure. If you find something here that should be in Cool Science, drop me a note!
>
> [Omni Magazine's](http://www.omnimag.com/cool_science/old.html) index of cool science
>
> From their "Cool Science of the Week" site. A collection of strange and fascinating science links.
>
> [Physics Servers and Services Around the World](http://physicsweb.org/TIPTOP/)
>
> A must-see site for the physics buff. The WWW offerings of all physics departments everywhere, all in one place.
>
> [SCIENCE MUSEUMS](http://www.yahoo.com/Science/Museums_and_Exhibits/), Yahoo
>
> More and more museum sites.
>
> [NDSQ Science Links](http://www.nd.edu/~scienceq/scilinks.html)
>
> Science sites recommended by the Notre Dame Science Quarterly.
>
> [Other people's hotlists](http://www2.islandnet.com/~cwalker/other.html)
>
> Not science, but not to be missed. A big compendium of "cool sites" sites.
