# English for Filipinos - Some Words

*Source page: `~robertf/eng4fili/e4f_wrds.html`*

> **English for Filipinos**\
> ***Some Words That Don't Mean the Same Thing***\
>
> In the Philippines, some English words have taken on a different meaning than what they have in other countries. For these reason an American or Canadian may misunderstand what you say, or just get confused. I will list words that have different meanings and try to explain the meaning in different countries and maybe other words that mean what you want to say. Of course there are other problem words than the ones listed here. I will add to this list from time to time.
>
> **comfort room, or C.R.**\
> "Comfort room" has no meaning in the U.S. and Canada, and will usually leave somebody looking at you wondering what you mean. If they don't know what "comfort room" is, then they won't know that's what "C.R." stands for. Some people will think you mean "carriage return" (as on a typewriter or computer), others will think it means something else, and many just won't know what you're trying to say.
>
> There are many terms people in Canada and the U.S. use and some really aren't nice to say, but three that you can use are "rest room", "bathroom", and "men's (or women's or ladies') room".
>
> **pass by**\
> For many Filipinos "pass by" means to stop at a place while on the way to another place. But in the U.S., "pass by" means to go past a place without stopping, almost the opposite meaning. In the U.S., "stop by" means to stop at a place while on the way to someplace else, or maybe not when on the way to someplace else.
>
> **throw**\
> In most countries, including the Philippines, "throw" mean to use your hand to throw something for a distance, for example to throw a ball or throw a rock. But many Filipinos also use "throw" to mean putting something in the garbage or to get rid of something no longer wanted. For example, "This toy is broken, so let's throw it." But that is strictly a Filipino useage. If you say that to an American, it sounds like you will take the toy and throw it across the room or out the window or something. Americans use the phrase, "throw away"; for example, "This toy is broken, so let's throw it away."
>
> **tom boy**\
> In the Philippines, this word is used for homosexual women. But in the U.S. "tom boy" means a girl child that likes the things a boy likes, like to climb trees, play rough games, etc. It has nothing to do with sexual preference. In the U.S. and Canada there are many words used to mean a homosexual woman, but most of the words are not nice to use with strangers. More polite words are "homosexual woman", "lesbian", and "gay woman" (but many people think "gay" should just mean men).
