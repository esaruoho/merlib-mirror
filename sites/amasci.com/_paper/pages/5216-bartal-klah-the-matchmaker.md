<td data-valign="top" width="580"><h1 id="klah-the-matchmaker">Klah, the Matchmaker</h1>
by Jr. Harper apprentice <a href="../bartal.html">Bartal</a>
<p><em>(Sung to the tune of "Strangers in the Night" by Frank Sinatra)</em></p>
<hr />
Bartal launches quickly into a ballad-like song, chords strong and lengthened as he pretends to croon. The smirk on his face is a bit unsettling, given the presumed nature of the song.
<p><em>"Seacrafters on the deck, exchanging glances<br />
Wondering in the night, what were the chances<br />
They'd be sharing klah before the night was through?"</em></p>
<p>Bartal winks, and continues with the slow, purposeful background chords of the gitar,</p>
<p><em>"Something in their Craft was so exciting,<br />
Something in hot klah was so inviting,<br />
Something in the Sea decided it was time to blow..."</em></p>
<p>Bartal modulates up, the tempo increasing just enough to notice,</p>
<p><em>"Storm was brief but strong, it did the damage... they were<br />
Crafters on the deck, two scalded people...</em></p>
<p>The tune moves to a short bridge section, losing some of its seriousness and adding the hint of a sea shanty as Bartal begins again:</p>
<p><em>"When the boat rocked to and fro, little did they know,"<br />
How one innocent mug of klah could be<br />
A warm embrace on their pants this way..."</em></p>
<p>Bartal stifles a giggle, keeping his expression even as he returns to the magestic chords of the beginning,</p>
<p><em>"And ever since that night, they'd been together<br />
Conversing in the night, love growing stronger<br />
It turned out so right, for Seacrafters in the night..."</em></p>
<p>Bartal falls silent, letting the gitar music tell the story of the love between the crafters itself. Finally, he begins singing again,</p>
<p><em>"Was it just circumstance, hot klah spilled on their pants that day...<br />
Ever since that night, they loved each other<br />
Alert to any plight, to befall their partner<br />
It turned out so right, for Seacrafters in the night..."</em></p>
<p>Bartal's playing goes to the forefront, playing the background to the last verse over and over again until it finally dies out.</p>
