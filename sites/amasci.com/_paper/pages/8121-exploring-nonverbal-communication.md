<td data-valign="top">This web site gives you a chance to learn more about <strong>nonverbal communication</strong>. You can even <strong>test</strong> your own ability to "read" samples of real nonverbal communication.
<p>Research shows that clues in the nonverbal "channels" of communication (<strong>how</strong> something is said) are often more important than words alone (<strong>what</strong> is said).</p>
<p>There are many different "channels" of nonverbal communication: facial expressions, the clues in our voices ("vocal paralanguage"), hand gestures, body movements ("kinesics"), touch ("haptics"), and personal space. These and other "channels" are explored in a new University of California video series on <strong>Nonverbal Communication</strong>. Each video is about 30 minutes in length and comes with a detailed <strong>instructor's guide</strong>. These University of California videotapes are produced by Dane Archer, a Professor at the University of California at Santa Cruz (Email: <a href="mailto:archer@cats.ucsc.edu">archer@cats.ucsc.edu</a>).</p>
<p><strong>GETTING STARTED:</strong> To learn more about nonverbal communication, including a chance to <strong>test</strong> your ability to read real samples, click on any of the following titles. Each title takes you to a different topic in <strong>nonverbal communication</strong>. You can also ask to see a <strong>description</strong> and <strong>reviews</strong> of each videotape.</p>
