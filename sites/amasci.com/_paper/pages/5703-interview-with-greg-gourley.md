## Interview with Greg Gourley

Greg Gourley is a registered immigration assistant at [New Americans](http://www.newamericans.com) in Redmond, Washington. He is holding naturalization classes, and recently got involved into DV Lottery submissions.\
Greg, is not an immigration lawyer, he is an immigration assistant which is more of a facilitator. By Washington State regulations immigration assistants are not allowed to give legal advice, but he works together with a couple of immigration lawyers he refers his clients to should legal advice be needed.

Greg, thank you for agreeing to this interview. You are a registered immigration assistant in Redmond, WA. In that function you help a lot of people participate in the DV lottery. About how many "winning", as in being drawn by the computer, DV cases have you dealt with?  
Really very few compared to the many entries I have submitted. Probably 17 winners in the last two years. That doesn't mean that 17 people got green cards. Some decided not to submit their paperwork to the National Visa Center (hard to believe) and others failed their interview for a variety of reasons.

You can find a lot of horror stories on the net about DV cases which have been lost due to the INS not being able to process the adjustment of status in time. Others succesfully adjusted their status, but went through a lot of grief and had to hire a lawyer. Do you advise your clients to opt for consular processing, if flying home is not a hardship for them?  
That's a judgement call that depends upon the winner's circumstances. Because the State Department tends to interview people faster then the USINS and the State Department forms are more user friendly then INS forms, I generally encourage people to opt for a consul interview if they can do so. The INS has a heavy workload and DV processing is not a high priority. Remember that the DV program belongs to the State Department and by law the Department must issue up to 55,000 immigration visas annually and by the end of the fiscal year. The DV program is not so critical to INS.

Of the cases you were involved with, what percentage chose adjustment of status with the INS? Of those, how many were successful? Was it necessary to get a lawyer involved in any of those cases, did anybody do it completely by themself?  
Most of the winners I have worked with have chosen to go through the American Embassy for their green card application. They seem more comfortable. Some have found it to be cheaper and less frustrating. Still, the decision is theirs to make.\
I have recommended that stateside lottery winners obtain the services of an immigration attorney if they decide to file their papers through the INS. Sure, you can struggle with the papers and do it yourself, but there is a loss of time and a high risk of making mistakes that can jeopardize your chance for permanent residency. Because a green card is so important to so many people, I believe winners should use an immigration attorney. The cost of an attorney is a necessary expense.

A DV winner case should be a slam dunk case, yet the forms can get intimidating and experienced help might be appreciated. How much do you charge to assist in such a "slam dunk" DV adjustment of status case? At what point would you refer a case out to an immigration lawyer, and can you give us an idea of what costs are to be expected when hiring a lawyer.  
I do not do DV paperwork. When asked to help, I will make sure the person has the right papers and supporting documents, follows the National Visa Center rules and understands why certain documents are important. More then anything else, I facilitate the process. People pay me for my time and the cost varies, but I am not in the business of giving legal advice, especially if someone is dealing with INS. You asked about the general cost figure if someone hires an attorney. Remember that applications must be done for every member of a winner's family, not just the winner. In Seattle a DV winner with a "clean" case - no problems - may pay an attorney around \$1200. Remember, also, that there are government filing fees in addition to what an attorney charges. These fees amount to several hundred dollars.

What advice do you have for the do-it-yourself filer who decides to go through the INS?  
If you are not a patient person, learn to be patient. It is my experience that most DV interview at INS are scheduled late in the fiscal year. When you file your papers, INS will issue you a work permit while your papers are being processed, but it is only temporary. If your visa number never comes up and you are not called for an interview before the end of the fiscal year, your work permit will not be renewed and you will be expected to return to your country. I do agree with the suggestion made by others that you should red flag your paperwork as "DV Lottery Processing." Yes, use a bright red marker. Also, photocopy everything. Keep a file copy for yourself. INS does lose paperwork. INS officers like to say they never lose papers, they just misplace them. Either way, the result is the same. You are the person on the short end and in trouble.

Gregory L. Gourley\
Immigration Assistant Registered,\
State of Washington
