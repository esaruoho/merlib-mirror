Recently Microsoft released a new version (5.0) of Automap. Information about what was changed or enhanced will be outlined and impressions will be presented.

*This comes from about 8 hours of hands on operation*

### <span id="Overview">Overview</span>

The new Automap Road Atlas (now named Microsoft Automap Trip Planner -- `not to be confused with Rand McNally's TripMaker`) has undergone a complete facelift and much has been added in things to see, places to stay and eat, and facilities. Little has been sacrificed to bring all the additions. In fact the routing capabilities have improved with better control for times at a stopover and more ease at making an overnight stop.

The maps are a lot cleaner and don't easily get cluttered and unreadable as with version 4; but that does come at the loss of some control. You no longer have the dozens of features and overlays to pick and choose from. Instead you chose from a map with or without detailed land features; then the zoom level determines what you see. There is no longer the more/less detail/places buttons. To see places and features, you have to get close enough, or have asked to find them.

The relief map feature is still present. It, with land details, is the default. You may chose its plainer counterpart, or a detailed/no-details non-relief road map. The new relief map will bring mixed reviews. My first impression was it looked better (view of entire US). But, as I zoomed in close, the resolution was not as good as version 4. You do not get down to the "miles wide" pixels as in version 4 and it is a lot smoother, but you lose the sense of elevation difference. My guess is there is not enough contrast between adjacent elevation color selections.

Route planning is much the same as version 4. The route dialog box is gone and you must use the wizard or plant flags as before. The wizard gives more information and only has a few steps to go through. And, you can now set stop-over times in the wizard.

Moving around and selecting functions is less cluttered and hopefully more intutive. Left click on the map and it presents you with nearby places to go or information available. Right click and it presents you with things you can do. The scroll bars are gone and you just move the mouse to the appropriate margin and click & hold to pan. The "hand pan" is still present. And a fast zoom has been added where you can just move a slider.

One big addition is street level detail around major cities. You still cannot route throught the minor roads, but you can see them and their names; and businesses can be located.

A second big addition is places to stay and eat. You may select eating establishments, hotels/motels and Bed & Breakfasts; see where they are located, and sometimes prices and features. You can also make online reservations for lodging. Things to see and do have been expanded along with their placement on the map. And information about a region is also on the CD. Plus, there is additional information available on The Web. When you go to Streets, restaurants are further catagorized by ethnicity.

A third big addition is pushpins. You can mark any location on a map and add your own comments or data. The pushpins can be catagorized into groups and you can route to your pushpins.

I suspected the Trip Planner and Streets shared the same data as pushpins created in one are visible in the other. However, the ethnicity breakdown for restaurants is in Streets and not in Trip Planner. Plus, I looked for restaurants within 5 miles of my town. Trip Planner showed one; whereas Streets showed 184.

Trip Planner requires Windows95 with 8MB memory or NT 4.0 with 16MB memory. It requires 15MB of hard disk; or you can install 45MB for better performance. You will need a double speed CD and 256 color VGA.

### <span id="Deficiencies_reduced">Deficiencies Reduced</span>

As before, a desired daily driving time (starting hour:minute, ending hour:minute) can still be set in the driving preferences. It is then used in conjunction with the overnight stop-overs.

The controls for time at a stop-over have improved. You may specify time in nights, hours or minutes (all integers); or you may specify a desired arrival and departure time. If you specify nights, the journey will resume at the desired daily starting time after the specified number of nights have passed. Hours or minutes must be integer values, so 2.5 hours must be specified as 150 minutes. If you specify desired arrival and departure times, you can extend a day beyond the normal day end. Unfortunately, you cannot specify both; e.g. an overnight stop-over and pick a departure time that differs from the normal daily starting time.

Stop-overs can be edited from the routing wizard. You may pick a stop-over and change its stopping times, remove it, or reposition it up or down in the list of stop-overs. A new stopover can be added to the end of the list, then moved up in the list to its desired position in the order.

Driving Profiles (prefered times of day, speeds, fuel varables) are now saved with a journey. Whenever you recall a saved journey, these parameters are set to those in affect when the journey was last stored. This does mean that whenever you start a new journey, you need to make sure these parameters are set as desired. *I have not checked to see what happens if you pull up a classic journey*. The "points of Interest" do not appear to be saved and reset, nor is the type of map viewed.

When you print, the region you see is pretty close to what is printed and it fills a page. Version 4 would either give a partial page, or expand the map area to fit the paper. To just show a particular area, one had to pick a smaller area and guess. Version 5 is a definite improvement.

### <span id="Enhancements">Enhancements</span>

Instead of just 800 numbers for lodging, they can now be seen on the map, routed to, and you can sometimes see additional information such as price range and facilities and acceptance of pets; etc. There is also an online reservation offering. Besides hotels/motels, bed and breakfasts are also listed.

Things to see have expanded into museums, shops, festivals, ski areas, etc.

Moving around on the map is different. The hand for grabbing the map and moving is still there. But, the scroll bars are gone. Move the mouse to the edge of the window and the pointer turns into an arrow. Click and it will scroll a little ways. Click and hold and it scrolls continuously.

The map and itinerary views are handled better. Instead of having the itinerary view dominate when a route is calculated, the screen is split showing both the map and itinerary. Different preferences may be selected.

The driving times of the day may be overridden by specifying a drive straight through and the departure or arrival times may be specified. *In this case, the stop-over times still seem to be honored, but an overnight stop seems to be from arrival until midnight.*

### <span id="New_Features">New Features</span>

Places to eat have been added to the database and they can be located on the map. If you switch to Streets, they are catagorized by ethnicity. The available list seems to differ between Streets and Trip Planner. A search within 5 miles of my town showed one for Trip Planner and 184 for Streets.

Things to see and do have been added. You now have local events and festival, aquariums, factories, shopping areas, parks, landmarks, etc. All of these (and restaurants and lodging) are under the "points of interest" dialogue box. You can bring up the box for the area you are viewing then click on the different items you want. They are listed in the dialogue box. You may show theses on the map, and/or select ones of interest and bring up more information. And, some of these may offer more information on The Web.

Travel Guide Regional information is now available for many areas. Left click on the map and it shows selections for that region. Chose one and a Travel Guide dialogue box comes up with loads of information that will keep you busy for some time. This may also lead you to The Web for even more information. You can also elect to see pictures of interesting features in a region. A mosaic of pictures appears. Select one and it expands in size and has text associated. And it may lead to more information. Some of these pictures also have sound.

360 degree views. For selected areas, they have panned a camera in a complete circle. Select this and a list of places appear. Select on and a picture appears on the screen. It slowly pans, probably taking a few minutes for the complete circle.

Online information. As previously mentioned, many items also lead to additional online information. Reservations are also possible online for lodging and flights (I have not checked any of this).

Put your own information on the map. The Pushpin feature allows you to mark a place on the map, give it a name, and load it with information. A short few lines may be entered and printed with you maps, or extensive information with pictures and sounds provided for interactive viewing. You may route to any of the pushpins. The pushpin is how you put your home on the map -- and all your relatives. You may pull information out of the yellow pages (if you purchased them) and put them on the map as pushpins. If you are a traveling salesman, put all your clients on the map with any information pertenant. The pushpins can be seen in both Trip Planner and Streets.

Fast Zoom. Besides the previous zoom features of stepping in/out a step at a time, or windowing a region, there is a zoom slider. It pops up a picture of the earth, a platform above it, and shows the height of the platform. You may slide the platform or step it with a fine adjust. With this, you can quickly go from a view of a city to covering the entire state; or out to the entire US. I find this feature very useful. The only drawback is it is not dynamic. You have to let go of the mouse button and give it time to repaint the map.

Optimize stop-overs. Like before (and other planning products), your route will be in the same order that you entered the stop-overs. If this is not optimal, you may ask Trip Planner to reorganize these into the quickest/shortest route. *I have not tried this, yet*.

Classic Drives. I have not explored this yet; but it looks like they have taken dozens of scenic/interesting drives and pre-canned them. For a quick check, I chose "mt. St. Helens, circling the mountain". It gave me a route from Portland up around Mt St. Helens with several stopping points and a travel guide. I changed the route to leave from and return to my house (Seattle area). So, you can use these as a suggested travel guide and tune to your desires.

Street level detail. For approximately 30 metropolitian areas, streets as detailed as Automap Streets are visible under Trip Planner. I live in the Seattle area and I can see all the streets in my neighborhood and they are named. This allows locating a business without going to the Street Atlas. However, you cannot route at the street level.

Printing options for a route have expanded significantly. Instead of just printing the current view, one can select: 1) each leg on a separate page; 2) each day on a separate page; 3) x hours on a separate page; 4) x miles (KM) on a separate page. There are a few print quality options, but I have not yet explored their differences.

GPS. It is reportedly supported, but I have not investigated.

### <span id="Deficiencies_not_Addressed">Deficiencies not Addressed</span>

Exit numbers. Many people have commented that they would like to see exit numbers listed on interstates and in the driving instructions. The new Trip Planner does not show exit numbers; nor does Streets. But, I did see one exit number listed on an itinerary for a 3000 mile trip.

The real (estimated) time for a trip comes from specified speeds on the various types of highways plus explicit stop-over times. Gas fillups take no time and there is no provision to indicate rest stops every so often. This deficiency is pointed out in the User's Manual with the instructions to deflate travel speed by a few miles/hour.

Suggested places to refuel are indicated on the itinerary. If one plans to fill at some stop-over, there is no way to indicate and have the following suggested refuel stops recalculated.

*Points of interest are not saved with a journey*. Whatever is in effect when you load a journey stays in effect. Driving preferences are saved with a journey.

The itinerary delineates "day 1", "day 2", etc. It would be nice if I could also include Monday, Tuesday. etc; and even better if I could include the date besides the day of the week.

### <span id="Lost_Capabilities">Lost Features or Capabilities</span>

Destination Campgrounds, Destination Golf, Destination Ski, etc. Version 4 and version 5 are not compatible. These databases have been lost. They did include Ski in points of interest; but for the others, they expect you to use the Yellow pages and import Pushpins. The Yellow Pages are not an acceptable replacement as they do not list features and ratings.

Rest stops are no longer indicated on the map, nor searchable.

*Journey start and/or end can no longer be specified unless you are driveing straigt through*. The only controls are through the stop-over times. For example: I leave Seattle with Salt Lake being the first overnight stop location required and with desired driving times of 8 AM to 6 PM. I will let it pick an overnight stop between Seattle and Salt Lake. It has me leaving Seattle around 1:30 PM and stopping in Oregon at 6 PM, then continuing on at 8 AM to Salt Lake. BUT, I wanted to leave after work at 6 PM and drive until near midnight the first day. To do this, I have to plan a stop in Oregon and show desired arrival as 11:30 PM and depart at 8 AM. It then has me leaving Seattle at 6 PM.

Version 4 showed calculated overnight stops on the map and the itinerary, and it seemed to try to stop you near a town. Version 5 seems to be anywhere and often it is not near a city and the location is hard to ascertain from the itinerary. Version 5 shows on the Itinerary, but not on the map. Clicking on the itinerary will expand that region of the map, but it is still hard to pinpoint.

Version 4 showed refuel locations on the map and in the itinerary. Version 5 just shows the itinerary.

Version 4 showed time of day markers ocassionally along the route. They were a nice touch; but they are gone in version 5.

*We may have lost detail for Canada. Places I go in British Columbia seem to have less roads and towns*.

### <span id="Performance">Performance</span>

It is a lot slower in calculating a journey and possibly in printing. To compute a 3000 mile journey with 15 vias took 4.5 minutes. Version 4 only took 15 seconds. And I did load in the 45MBytes to make it go faster; and I was not asking for any points of interest. It does give a bar chart and indicates what leg of the journey it is working on.

Printing was a lot longer than I remembered. I did not do a timing comparison, yet; but I did time spooling the 3000 mile journey with 15 vias. To spool (prepare the print and start printing) the one page map and 4 page itinerary took 3 minutes with the detailed relief map, and 2:20 with the non-detailed flat map.

I have a 486/120 with 32MB memory.

###  <span id="Bugs">Bugs</span>

I have a NEC 4x 4 CD changer with the driver that treats it as one drive letter and I select the slot to be read. When instructing Trip Planner to switch to Streets, or Streets to switch to Yellow Pages, it will not release the drive to let the NEC driver change slots. The NEC driver reports busy. Canceling all causes Automap to exit. WORKAROUND: if I only use one slot for the Automap products, I can eject the disk of the product I am leaving, then insert the disk of the product I am going to. NOTE: Version 4 did not have this problem when switching between Road Atlas and Streets, or the two disks of Streets.

When printing my 3000 mile journey with each day being on a separate page, one page had all my via symbols overlaid in the middle of nowhere. They were laid out in the same arrangement as the overall trip map, but apparently at the scale of the individual day. I have not reprinted to see if it can be duplicated.
