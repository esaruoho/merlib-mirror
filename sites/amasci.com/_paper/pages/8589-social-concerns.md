<p>Adopt-A-Stream Projects</p>
<p>Prior to construction of the church a small wetland existed on the northwest corner of the property. Construction and subsequent improvement activities seriously degraded this small habitat. In 1998 a project was launched to restore the area to its original wetland status. The effort was a joint project of the Social Concerns Committee and the children's Religious Education program. Click here for some <a href="Wetlands2.htm">pictures</a> of the start of the project.</p>

<p><strong>Cedar Valley School Tutoring and Book Projects</strong></p>
<p>The Cedar Valley Community School, part of the Edmonds school district, is located in an economically depressed area and many of the children come from families who have recently immigrated to the United States. Some of these children's English language skills are poor or almost non-existent. As a result of these conditions there is a great need for teacher assistants and tutors. EUUC members already make up a significant portion of lay people helping at the school, but more volunteers are needed.</p>
<p><strong>Guatemalan Literacy Project</strong></p>
<p>The Social Concerns brunch held on February 27 raised over $700 to benefit the Proyecto Bibliotecas Guatemala library and literacy program in Guatemala.<br />
- Why Guatemala?<br />
- Why literacy?<br />
Guatemala has endured 36 years of civil war. All domestic services, including education, were disrupted during this time. A peace accord was signed recently, and democratic elections were held in late 1999. While the government is operating schools throughout the country, many people, especially the indigenous people, cannot afford to send their children to school. Schools have dedicated teachers, but few supplies and resources. A seminary student. Rigoberto Zamora Ckharuc, realized he could do more for his country by establishing libraries, including a traveling library, to bring more reading materials to the small villages. By supporting this project we are helping assure that the formerly oppressed people of Guatemala can meaningfully participate in their country's democracy and future.</p>
<p>Unitarian Universalist Service Committee Update</p>
<p>Last year our Social Concerns brunch supported a summer youth work camp in Yakima, focusing on farm worker housing issues. Rachel Long, a graduate of our Youth Group, who helped with this project reports, "We were appalled by some of the substandard housing we found, in particular a trailer park without basic plumbing or hygiene. A local UU church member became aware of this problem due to his involvement in this project. He recently told us that he took this situation to the Health Department. They have now bought the trailer park, and are bringing the units up to code and making them livable. Thanks to UUSC and the Social Concerns brunch, people now have decent housing."</p></li>
