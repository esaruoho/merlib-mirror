**Eco-Imperialism's Deadly Consequences**

<img src="foxnews_story.gif" data-border="0" width="62" height="10" />

<span style="font-size: 11.0pt; font-family: Times New Roman"> The United Nations global warming bureaucracy is meeting (vacationing?) in Milan this week pondering how to revive the beleaguered international global warming treaty known as the Kyoto Protocol. This weeks news that Russia might say nyet to the treaty all but seals its doom.   </span>

<span style="font-size: 11.0pt; font-family: Times New Roman"> A number of questions have been raised about the link between carbon dioxide and climate change which do not appear convincing and clearly it sets very serious brakes on economic growth which do not look justified, said an aide to Russian President Vladimir Putin.</span>

<span style="font-size: 11.0pt; font-family: Times New Roman"> The treatys scientific and economic shortcomings are both excellent reasons for rejecting it. Another reason not mentioned nearly often enough, however, is exposed in compelling fashion by Paul Driessen in his new book "Eco-Imperialism: Green Power, Black Death."</span>

<span style="font-size: 11.0pt; font-family: Times New Roman"> Driessen, a senior fellow at the **Atlas Economic Research Foundation** (<a href="http://clickit.go2net.com/search?cid=307797&amp;site=srch&amp;area=is.clicktracking&amp;shape=link&amp;cp=info.foxnws&amp;clickurl=http%3A%2F%2Fsearch.foxnews.com%2Finfo.foxnws%2Fredirs_all.htm%3Fpgtarg%3Dwbsdogpile&amp;ext_qcat=web&amp;ext_qkw=Atlas%20Economic%20Research%20" style="color: blue; text-decoration: underline; text-underline: single">search</a>) and a former member of the **Sierra Club** (<a href="http://clickit.go2net.com/search?cid=307797&amp;site=srch&amp;area=is.clicktracking&amp;shape=link&amp;cp=info.foxnws&amp;clickurl=http%3A%2F%2Fsearch.foxnews.com%2Finfo.foxnws%2Fredirs_all.htm%3Fpgtarg%3Dwbsdogpile&amp;ext_qcat=web&amp;ext_qkw=Sierra%20Club" target="_blank" style="color: blue; text-decoration: underline; text-underline: single">search</a>) and **Zero Population Growth** (<a href="http://clickit.go2net.com/search?cid=307797&amp;site=srch&amp;area=is.clicktracking&amp;shape=link&amp;cp=info.foxnws&amp;clickurl=http%3A%2F%2Fsearch.foxnews.com%2Finfo.foxnws%2Fredirs_all.htm%3Fpgtarg%3Dwbsdogpile&amp;ext_qcat=web&amp;ext_qkw=Zero%20Population%20Growth" style="color: blue; text-decoration: underline; text-underline: single">search</a>), reveals how the ideological environmental movement -- essentially comprised of wealthy, left-leaning Americans and Europeans -- wants to impose its views on billions of poor, desperate Africans, Asians and Latin Americans.</span>

**<span style="font-size: 11.0pt; font-family: Times New Roman">Eco-imperialism</span>**<span style="font-size: 11.0pt; font-family: Times New Roman"> (<a href="http://clickit.go2net.com/search?cid=307797&amp;site=srch&amp;area=is.clicktracking&amp;shape=link&amp;cp=info.foxnws&amp;clickurl=http%3A%2F%2Fsearch.foxnews.com%2Finfo.foxnws%2Fredirs_all.htm%3Fpgtarg%3Dwbsdogpile&amp;ext_qcat=web&amp;ext_qkw=Eco-imperialism" target="_blank " style="color: blue; text-decoration: underline; text-underline: single">search</a>) violates these peoples most basic human rights, maintains Driessen, and denies them economic opportunities, the chance for better lives, and the right to rid their countries of diseases that were vanquished long ago in the U.S. and Europe.</span>

<span style="font-size: 11.0pt; font-family: Times New Roman"> Hollywood actor and eco-imperialist Ed Begley, Jr., for example, preaches that the two most abundant forms of power on Earth are solar and wind. Its much cheaper for everybody in Africa to have electricity where they need it, on their huts. </span>

<span style="font-size: 11.0pt; font-family: Times New Roman"> Driessen points out, however, that while solar panels would be a major improvement over current conditions in many areas of the third world, they are but a band-aid approach to the developing worlds critical electrical deficiency.</span>

<span style="font-size: 11.0pt; font-family: Times New Roman"> They cannot possibly provide sufficient power for anything more than basic necessities, and large-scale photovoltaic electricity is far more expensive than what is produced by coal, natural gas, nuclear or hydroelectric pants. Wind power has the same shortcomings. For impoverished countries where few have access to electricity, these are not idle considerations, writes Driessen.</span>

<span style="font-size: 11.0pt; font-family: Times New Roman"> The environmental movement has repeatedly used the alleged threat of global eco-catastrophe  e.g., global warming  to override the wishes of people who most desperately need energy and progress, he adds.</span>

<span style="font-size: 11.0pt; font-family: Times New Roman"> In Indias **Gujarat Province** (<a href="http://clickit.go2net.com/search?cid=307797&amp;site=srch&amp;area=is.clicktracking&amp;shape=link&amp;cp=info.foxnws&amp;clickurl=http%3A%2F%2Fsearch.foxnews.com%2Finfo.foxnws%2Fredirs_all.htm%3Fpgtarg%3Dwbsdogpile&amp;ext_qcat=web&amp;ext_qkw=Gujarat%20Province" target="_bl " style="color: blue; text-decoration: underline; text-underline: single">search</a>), a dam project was halted after eco-activists pressured international lending agencies to withdraw financial support. The dam had to be stopped because it would change the path of the river, kill little creatures along its banks and uproot tribal people in the area, one eco-activist smugly intoned.</span>

<span style="font-size: 11.0pt; font-family: Times New Roman"> The local tribal people, however, dont appear to appreciate her intervention, offers Driessen. One resident angrily called the activists handiwork a crime against humanity, because the project would have provided electricity for 5,000 villages; low-cost renewable power for industries and sewage treatment plants; irrigation water for crops; and clean water for 35 million people.</span>

<span style="font-size: 11.0pt; font-family: Times New Roman"> Driessens book isnt limited to global warming and third world energy problems. The chapter Sustainable Mosquitoes Expendable People describes the ongoing tragedy of the eco-activist crusade against DDT.</span>

<span style="font-size: 11.0pt; font-family: Times New Roman"> Our family and community are suffering and dying from \[malaria\], and too many Europeans and environmentalists only talk about protecting the environment, says Fifi Kobusingye, 34-year old Ugandan businesswoman with malaria. But what about the people? The mosquitoes are everywhere. You think youre safe, and youre not. Europeans and Americans can afford to deceive themselves about malaria and pesticides. But we cant, she added.</span>

<span style="font-size: 11.0pt; font-family: Times New Roman"> The Ugandan woman is only one of more than 300 million annual victims of malaria in the third world. Between 2-3 million die every year. Over half the victims are children, who die at a rate of two per minute or 3,000 per day the equivalent of 80 fully loaded school buses plunging over a cliff every day of the year, explains Driessen.</span>

<span style="font-size: 11.0pt; font-family: Times New Roman"> Despite this ongoing public health horror story, the United Nations Environment Programme, World Bank, Greenpeace, Pesticide Action Network, World Wildlife Fund, Physicians for Social Responsibility and other eco-imperialist groups oppose the use of **DDT** (<a href="http://clickit.go2net.com/search?cid=307797&amp;site=srch&amp;area=is.clicktracking&amp;shape=link&amp;cp=info.foxnws&amp;clickurl=http%3A%2F%2Fsearch.foxnews.com%2Finfo.foxnws%2Fredirs_all.htm%3Fpgtarg%3Dwbsdogpile&amp;ext_qcat=web&amp;ext_qkw=DDT" target="_blank" style="color: blue; text-decoration: underline; text-underline: single">search</a>)  the only practical solution to the malaria crisis. The eco-imperialists disturbing attitude toward the third world is perhaps most frighteningly described by Robert S. Desowitz in another must-read, **"The Malaria Capers,"** (<a href="http://clickit.go2net.com/search?cid=307797&amp;site=srch&amp;area=is.clicktracking&amp;shape=link&amp;cp=info.foxnws&amp;clickurl=http%3A%2F%2Fsearch.foxnews.com%2Finfo.foxnws%2Fredirs_all.htm%3Fpgtarg%3Dwbsdogpile&amp;ext_qcat=web&amp;ext_qkw=%22The%20Malaria%20Capers,%25%20" style="color: blue; text-decoration: underline; text-underline: single">search</a>) (W.W. Norton, 1991). </span>

<span style="font-size: 11.0pt; font-family: Times New Roman">            Desowitz reports a U.S. Agency for International Development official named Edwin Cohn as saying,       The third world didnt require a healthy labor force because there was a surplus of workers; better some people should be sick with malaria and spread the job opportunities around. Even more bluntly, Cohn reportedly said people in the third world were better \[off\] dead than alive and riotously reproducing.   </span>

<span style="font-size: 11.0pt; font-family: Times New Roman"> There should be no question, then, about Eco-Imperialisms subtitle: Green power does indeed mean black death. </span>
