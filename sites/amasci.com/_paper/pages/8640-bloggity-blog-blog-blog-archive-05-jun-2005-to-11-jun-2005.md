<strong>The Mysterious Beep Was Solved...</strong>
<p><span class="rss:item">On May 27th I posted a link to the TIVO community's thread about a <a href="http://www.tivocommunity.com/tivo-vb/showthread.php?t=239068">mysterious beeping house</a>. The guy who posted the original plea said:</span></p>
<blockquote>
Something is beeping in my house and I cant find it for the life of me. I have tried everything. I have spent hours looking for it and I cant find the source.<br />
<br />
Its not a smoke alarm. I have taken off each one. Its not the alarm system. I have completely disconnected it and taken it off battery power. The beep remains.<br />
<br />
The power on my whole block went out a while ago. Nothing was working. Except the beep. I figured I would let whatever battery it is running on to just run out....well it has been beeping for about a year now...who do I call to help me with this?<br />
<br />
It lets out a single beep once every 45-60 seconds. Its so annoying!!!
</blockquote>
<p>The folks in the thread had some suggestions, but one fellow actually went to the house. He was unable to find the source of the beep.<br />
<br />
So a whole crew of beepbusters went out to the house, arriving on May 28th. They quickly found the culprit: <a href="http://www.tivocommunity.com/tivo-vb/showthread.php?p=2910372&amp;&amp;#post2910372">"...an old, forgotten carbon monoxide detector buried under a pile of CDs in an end table with really squeeky drawers that nobody ever looks in."</a><br />
<br />
The men were ready to tear up the floorboards or wall, as the sound detector indicated that the sound was coming from behind the TV set. The woman in the group just pulled open a drawer that nobody had looked in for over a year and left it open long enough to find out that the beep was coming from the drawer of the metal chest being used as a end table... which apparently has some pretty odd acoustic properties.<br />
<br />
The monster thread on the subject has <a href="http://www.tivocommunity.com/tivo-vb/showthread.php?p=2910720&amp;&amp;#post2910720">pictures</a> of the offending detector, the "end table" and the folks celebrating the destruction of the beep. And from the guy who started it all:</p>
<blockquote>
The CO detector was placed in that drawer over 2 years ago by my old roommate at my old apartment. I never used that drawer and I had no idea that detector was in there. I dont know how I missed it when I was looking earlier.
</blockquote>

<em>"You should take a look at this Aquaman thing. It's pretty cool for a comic book."</em>
<p>Vince, a Hispanic actor, is hunting for a new role after some sort of success in the first season of <a href="http://www.tvtome.com/Entourage/">the show</a>. His buddy Eric is trying to get him a role in a big movie, but his agent Ari has already found him a role... in the upcoming Aquaman movie.<br />
<br />
The show itself is fairly normal situation comedy about an actor who was doing ok for himself, but has to keep on top of the heap. Of interest to me was the Aquaman comic book prop used in the show. As far as I could tell, this was a prop made for the show itself, as I can't see any resemblance to any Aquaman story I can recall off the top of my head.<br />
<br />
We get a couple of very quick looks at the cover, once when Ari tosses the book to Vince, and a moment later as Vince looks at it.</p>
<img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/entourage01_02.jpg" width="177" height="92" /> <img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/entourage01_03.jpg" width="201" height="109" />
<p>The cover doesn't appear to match any of the <a href="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/aqua/gal/00.html">Aquaman covers</a> that I've seen, though it's possible I've missed a few, and some foreign language editions of Aquaman have different covers (usually taken from interior artwork).<br />
<br />
Of more interest is later in the story, when Eric is paging through the comic book just before he makes the comment I started this post with. We get several interior shots, at least one of which shows an unfamiliar character.</p>
<img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/entourage01_04.jpg" width="71" height="147" /> <img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/entourage01_05.jpg" width="57" height="182" /> <img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/entourage01_06.jpg" width="63" height="151" />
<p>Who's the guy with the "V" on his chest? Anyone recognize him?<br />
<br />
I find it difficult to accept that the show designers would create a whole new comic book, complete with interior artwork that has Aquaman, for the show. But for the life of me I can't recognize what little I see of the book. Maybe I'm short-circuited or something. But neither the cover nor interiors ring any bells. So if you recognize it, give me a shout out.<br />
<br />
Update: Looking at the above screen caps, I think all three images of Aquaman are the same drawing, just reversed and cropped in the interior shots. Since you don't see much of the interior, it's possible that this is just a nice cut and paste job. I still want to know who V-Man is.</p>

<strong>Rapid Reviews - 2 June 2005 - Part I</strong>
<p><span class="rss:item"><strong>Aquaman #31</strong>: "Kiss of Death part 2: The Nine": Ok. Let's start with the most obvious. What's the gun-thing on the cover? It doesn't appear to be much of a handheld, with those handles out to the side. Then there's the title. It's exactly the same as the first part. Usually writers tend to change the bit after the part number. Either this writer didn't, or there was some sort of production mistake. I can't tell which, as the writing has been pretty sloppy from start to finish on this fill-in.</span></p>
<a href="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/aqua/"><img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/aqua/pics/cvr2003/cvra431.jpg" data-border="0" width="200" height="300" /></a>
<p>Next problem is the "posse" that Aquaman and Chandra round up. What kind of loon wears a suit, complete with noose, underwater? By my count, there are four such loons on pages seven and eight. And one of them even follows Chandra. I can see clinging to the old and familiar, but who would waste time learning how to knot a tie around your neck underwater?!??<br />
<br />
Then there's one of the main concepts of this storyarc. The police stopped being police during the disaster. Bull. I know one or two people who work in emergency services, and they would simply switch over and start pulling together a new system. That none of these cops did in the DCU is nearly unthinkable. I can see a handful of people being so shocked that they become useless, but <em>every single cop</em> in the sunken area?<br />
<br />
Pet peeve with the artwork... more bubbles than on a Lawrence Welk special. Every single panel does NOT have to have tons of bubbles to indicate that the action is taking place underwater. If you need bubbles to show that the action is underwater, then you just aren't drawing it right.<br />
<br />
There are other plotholes, some big enough to steer a whale through. As <a href="http://absorbascon.blogspot.com/">Absorbascon</a> points out, this is better than the last issue. But considering that the last issue was utter gibberish, that's just not saying much. <a href="http://realtegan.blogspot.com/2005/03/new-ratings-system.html"><img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/0020.jpg" data-border="0" width="75" height="15" alt="2 starfish" /></a></p>

<strong>Aquaman Sighting</strong>
<p><span class="rss:item">Oh dear. I'm not at all interested in this show, but according to the TV reporter of the Seattle Times, the overall storyarc of the second season of "Entourage" on HBO involves Aquaman. Here's the crucial bit of the <a href="http://seattletimes.nwsource.com/html/artsentertainment/2002296996_kay03.html">Friday article</a> from the Seattle Times, written by Kay McFadden:</span></p>
<blockquote>
<em>Though the ultra-dudes of "Entourage" may appear emotionally and socially frozen in time, they're slowly learning to finesse that complex, wily place known as Tinseltown.<br />
<br />
Back from three months of doing an indie film, rising star Vince (Adrian Grenier), business adviser Eric (Kevin Connolly), Vince's half-brother Drama (Kevin Dillon) and jack-of-all-trades Turtle (Jerry Ferrara) return to Los Angeles.<br />
<br />
Some things are the same. Drama continues to reek of macho desperation, unable to forget his brief flirtation with fame. Turtle is still an inept wheeler-dealer.<br />
<br />
The episode plots are loose and the overall story arc about Vince being cast as "Aquaman" in a movie rumored to be directed by James Cameron isn't high art.<br />
<br />
And that's the pleasure of "Entourage." The series is pure situational humor, moving at the same pace and attention span as its testosterone-driven characters. The biting depictions of Hollywood are a mean thrill; as agent Ari Gold, Jeremy Piven makes us revel in his harassed cynicism and devious joy.</em>
</blockquote>
<p>I am just imagining all the stupid lame Aquaman jokes that tired TV writers are likely to come up, and cringing. Yeah, I'm obsessed enough I'll probably hunt down the episodes, especially the third one, which <a href="http://www.tvtome.com/Entourage/">TV Tome</a> says is titled "Aquamansion".<br />
<br />
I can't say I'm really looking forward to it, but it's there.<br />
<br />
Update: The first episode of the new season has aired, and yes, the lame Aquaman jokes have already started. Joy.</p>
