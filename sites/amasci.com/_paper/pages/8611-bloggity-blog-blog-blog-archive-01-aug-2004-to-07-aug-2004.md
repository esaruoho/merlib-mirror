<strong>Rapid Reviews - 4 August 2004 - Part III</strong>
<p><span class="rss:item">A western, a sci-fi tinted western, and pure 1960s science fiction. <em>THIS</em> is what comics can be. Not all superheroes, but just about anything the imagination can pull off. I love superheroes, but I love seeing good books in other genres, too.<br />
<br />
<strong>The Ballad of Sleeping Beauty #2</strong>: The first issue was enough to pull me in to getting the second issue, and I think I'll stick with it. It's good. Not much about the sleeping beauty part in this one, but enough to get me to come back for more. I like the art a lot, it fits the story well. All-in-all, very good. <a href="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/rapidreviews.html"><img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/4_0_starfish.jpg" data-border="0" width="75" height="15" alt="4 starfish" /></a><br />
<br />
<strong>Daisy Kutter: The Last Train Chapter One</strong>: Is it really a western? I don't know, but it definitely is good. The story of a woman who has "quit the life" but misses it deeply. This is just wonderful. The artwork is expressive and neat, reminding me mostly of Mark Crilley's Akiko, but the subject matter is more serious... and the humor is still there. Worth getting. <a href="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/rapidreviews.html"><img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/4_0_starfish.jpg" data-border="0" width="75" height="15" alt="4 starfish" /></a><br />
<br />
<strong>Star Trek Key Collection Vol 1</strong>: The giggling started on page 8 of the collection, which happened to be the first real story page of the book, when Captain Kirk says, while looking at a vegetation-covered planet, says "If there's life in this galaxy, we'll find it there." Duh Captain. Vegetation IS life. Still, I understood what he really meant. It was just incredibly funny. As were a lot of the goofs in this book. Heck, the ending of the first story alone is guaranteed to appall most modern audiences. The introduction makes it very clear that the publishers of this collection are aware of the goofs and funky science, but those goofs are part of what makes this book so cool. I agree. It's a wonderful little time capsule, and well worth getting for either Star Trek fans or fans of the history of comic books. The first eight issues, including slightly reduced images of the covers, are included. <a href="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/rapidreviews.html"><img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/4_0_starfish.jpg" data-border="0" width="75" height="15" alt="4 starfish" /></a><br />
<br />
I don't have next week's shipping list handy, so you'll just have to wait to see what I'm picking up this time 'round.<br />
<br />
And on other topics, I seem to have missed <a href="http://eatmorepeople.blogspot.com/2004_08_01_eatmorepeople_archive.html#109176424862651949">Rick's Eleven</a>. Shame on me.<br />
<br />
Also recently posted is <a href="http://oakhaus.blogspot.com/2004_08_01_oakhaus_archive.html#109189601442888252">Bill Sherman's Eleven</a>.<br />
<br />
It has also been noted that my opinion on the subject <a href="http://www.comicon.com/thebeat/archives/00000168.htm">that started the whole thing</a> hasn't been seen. That's because I didn't feel like repeating myself. But, if you insist: There are lots of comics for kids out there, but marketing is still the problem. Comics for kids aren't seen where kids can get at and read them. Manga is changing this, and if other publishers follow that trend I think we might have an opening. But the trick is getting the good American stuff into the hands of Manga readers... and convincing them that it IS good enough to try. I'm watching, and I'm doing my part by spreading news of good comics for kids around to people near me in real life. If I even come up with a brilliant idea that will definitely work, I'll be sure to share it.<br />

I think I had noticed it, but I was so busy checking out everything else, I forgot to mention that <a href="http://www.dccomics.com/comics/dc_display.html?cm_dc_itemCode=ttitans17&amp;month=October"><em>Teen Titans #17</em></a> starts a 3-issue story arc that includes "Aquawoman". I still cannot give it a high recommendation, but I liked it. <a href="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/rapidreviews.html"><img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/3_5_starfish.jpg" data-border="0" width="75" height="15" alt="3 1/2 starfish" /></a><br />

I'm still not interested in the long crossover story arc about to start in the Batman books, but if I was into Batman much at all, this story would have done its job of drawing me in. <a href="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/rapidreviews.html"><img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/3_5_starfish.jpg" data-border="0" width="75" height="15" alt="3 1/2 starfish" /></a><br />

Good stuff overall. <a href="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/rapidreviews.html"><img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/4_0_starfish.jpg" data-border="0" width="75" height="15" alt="4 starfish" /></a><br />

<strong>Random Thoughts</strong>
<p><span class="rss:item">Steve Lieber comes up with <a href="http://mercurystudio.blogspot.com/2004_08_01_mercurystudio_archive.html#109171929769385025">Lieber's Eleven</a>, books that should be carried by libraries, and challenges all us comic bloggers to come up with our own lists. Ouch. This'll be both tough and easy. Easy to come up with enough books, tough to decide which really qualify for inclusion on the list. So here, in no particular order, are eleven comic books that I think libraries should have on their shelves.<br />
<br />
</span></p>
<ul>
<li><a href="http://www.randomhouse.com/pantheon/graphicnovels/persepolis.html">Persepolis: The Story of a Childhood</a> by Marjane Satrapi (Pantheon) - Excellent story covering a culture that most of us don't understand. This book makes it clear that we aren't as different as we sometimes think we are. I'm looking forward to the second volume, due out this fall.<br />
</li>
<li><a href="http://www.pedroandme.com/">Pedro and Me</a> by Judd Winick (Henry Holt) - Whatever you think of Judd Winick's other works, this book is something everyone ought to read. This tells the story of Judd's roommate on MTV's Real World, Pedro Zamora, who was HIV positive while on the show, and later died of AIDS. It's educational as well and painful and funny.<br />
</li>
<li><a href="http://www.theinterman.com/">The Interman</a> by Jeff Parker (Octopus) - Great action story with a small hint of super powers to satisfy the fanboys. Very well done and worth getting.<br />
</li>
<li><a href="http://www.gt-labs.com/dignifying.html">Dignifying Science</a> by Jim Ottaviani and various (G.T. Labs) - A hall of fame worth of artists covers a hall of fame of scientists that are usually overlooked, even in this enlightened age.<br />
</li>
<li><a href="http://age-of-bronze.com/aob/index.shtml">Age of Bronze: A Thousand Ships</a> by Eric Shanower (Image) - I've been praising this one since before it came out. Literally. This isn't for children, but anyone with any interest whatsoever in the history of the Trojan war ought to get this one.<br />
</li>
<li><a href="http://www.jayhosler.com/Cartoonist.html">Clan Apis</a> by Jay Hosler (Active Synapse) - I used to hate bees with a passion. I still hate bees, but I'm not nearly as passionate about it, thanks to this book. I love science comic books.<br />
</li>
<li><a href="http://www.ps238.com/">PS238: With Liberty &amp; Recess for All</a> by Aaron Williams (Henchmen Publishing) - Probably the best superhero book out in the last couple of years. Well worth reading, and something that more people ought to read.<br />
</li>
<li><a href="http://www.hellboy.com/">Hellboy: Seed of Destruction</a> by Mike Mignola with John Byrne (Dark Horse) - Mike Mignola's artwork is just something else altogether. It's an amazing book to read, but even more amazing just to look at. I haven't seen the movie yet, but the source material is certainly good.<br />
</li>
<li><a href="http://www.ait-planetlar.com/ursula.shtml">Ursula</a> by Fabio Moon and Gabriel Ba (AiT/Planet Lar) - Already a classic, and it just came out. This one talks about relationships and growing up and somehow manages to capture both at once. Well worth a a read, and well worth a spot in a library.<br />
</li>
<li><a href="http://usagiyojimbo.com/">Usagi Yojimbo Book One</a> by Stan Sakai (Fantagraphics) - Twenty years of this character, and I'm still thrilled by reading about him. This first book is a very good place to start... although you can safely pick this series up just about anywhere and enjoy it.<br />
</li>

<strong>Travels With Artie</strong>
<p><span class="rss:item">So I've got a new website. It was a direct result of getting a digital camera, acutally. At first, I thought it would be cool to take one of my Aquaman action figures and take pictures of him on our trip with us. I could do all kinds of stuff, like take his picture in front of the state capital in Olympia:</span></p>
<img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/artieolympia.jpg" width="225" height="300" />
<p>or maybe in Oregon's capital in Salem:</p>
<img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/artiesalem.jpg" width="250" height="300" />
<p>or maybe at some touristy Vista Point in California:</p>
<img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/artieshastavista.jpg" width="300" height="250" />
<p>We had Artie check out the facilities at our hotel in Red Bluff, where he declared the shower acceptable and the pool a little deep for him:</p>
<img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/artiemotel6.jpg" width="300" height="200" /><img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/artiepool.jpg" width="300" height="200" />
<p>And he visited the Dunes of Asilomar, not to mention the beach, as seen in a <a href="http://realtegan.blogspot.com/2004/07/unhappy-hollisters.html">previous blog entry</a>:</p>
<img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/artieasilomardunes.jpg" width="300" height="200" />
<p>But when all was said and done, the Aquaman action figure just didn't work out so well. I got a number of good shots, but other shots were hopelessly blurred. And he also often looked washed out in bright light. Worse, he had an annoying tendency to fall down, usually just as I was about to take the picture. His trident didn't help hold him up at all, and often got in the way of the shot. I needed a perfectly flat surface to pose him on. And while flat surfaces are common, they aren't everywhere. We resorted to using the box I was carrying Artie in or the top of the car:</p>
<img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/artieweed.jpg" width="300" height="230" />
<p>While I was pleased with a great many of the pictures, overall I would have to declare the Aquaman experiment a failure.</p>
<p>But not to despair! Although I took Artie, I also had a moment of brilliance and decided to take another figure along as well. At each tourist spot in California my long suffering husband dutifully pulled out each figure and posed them, and I got the shots.</p>
<p>The other figure has some distinct advantages over Aquaman for picture taking. First off, he's made of wood and has a larger base, so he's easier to pose and less likely to appear washed out in pictures. Second, he has a part in family history, so his travels will be much more interesting to the Gjovaag part of my audience than Aquaman would be. Third, he's handmade (I think) and comes from Norway, so the likelihood of someone else managing to take pictures with an identical figure is slim.</p>

<p>Sunday, August 01, 2004 <span id="109141206042234852"></span></p>
<strong>Ballard Locks Photo Workshop</strong>
<p><span class="rss:item">I got a little nervous this morning as I looked at today's forecast, and the first time I checked it said 78° F (which is pretty warm for Seattle), then the second time it was 79° F, then the third time it was 80° F... I figured if I kept watching it would get up into the 90s, so I turned off the TV (the actual high temperature was 83° F).</span></p>
<img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/locksfish.jpg" width="350" height="300" />
<p>I got to the <a href="http://www.nws.usace.army.mil/opdiv/lwsc">Lake Washington Ship Canal and Hiram M. Chittenden Locks</a> about 15 minutes early and wandered down to the locks to take a look. It's been a little while since I was <a href="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/20021027-20021102.html#83642056">last there</a>, but not so long that I'd forgotten what they looked like or anything. There were a lot of tourists there, including the <a href="http://www.nwfuchsiasociety.com/">Greater Seattle Fuchsia Show</a> in the garden, a live band (Greenwood Concert Band) playing near the locks, and somebody told me that there was also a wedding although I saw no sign of it. In any case, having such a nice day in Seattle brought out tons of folks to the water.</p>
<img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/lockstrainboats.jpg" width="300" height="225" />
<p>Virtually every second person at the Locks today had a camera, so I wasn't sure who might be there in <a href="http://www.brownequalsterrorist.com/">support of Ian</a>. I was an obvious target for folks who were part of the group, though, as I was wearing my <a href="http://www.zazzle.com/link.asp?associate_id=238498794449118802&amp;redirect=product&amp;product_id=235205464839947603">"I'm blogging this." T-shirt</a>. Several people came up to me as I wandered to check if I was with the group. My husband had suggested getting a t-shirt that says "I'm a photographer, and I vote" but there wasn't time to make them before the event. I looked for people carrying tripods. Eventually we started <a href="http://www.blueneedle.com/wordpress/archives/2004/07/13/ballard-locks-photo-workshop-sunday-august-1st-2004-1pm-4pm/">to congregate</a>.</p>
<img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/lockspeople1.jpg" width="300" height="225" />
<p>There was never a huge group, more of a milling group of like-minded people looking for good shots. We did pull together at one or two points for group photos, but I don't think everyone was there any time we did. One guy was going around getting names and home towns... I hope he wasn't with Homeland Security. We also had the presence of Louis The Freedom Pug:</p>
<img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/lockslouisthefreedompug.jpg" width="300" height="335" />
<p>I wandered over to the other side of the Locks with my fellow Bothell blogger, <a href="http://blog.donnael.com/">Garrett Fitzgerald</a> to get pictures of the fish ladder and wave sculpture. My pictures of the fish ladder didn't come out, but I got two good shots of the waves.</p>
<img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/lockswave1.jpg" width="300" height="200" />
<img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/lockswave2.jpg" width="300" height="220" />
<p>I ended up with a good set of just over 100 pictures, about 80 of which I'd be willing to share if someone asked nicely. I'm putting many much reduced versions up in this blog. The larger shots are nicer, but I've got limited disk space for all my pictures.</p>
<img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/locksboats4.jpg" width="300" height="400" />
<p>Here's a shot from the Lock gates, which pedestrians and bicyclists can walk over when they are closed. This shot is of boats headed towards Lake Washington.</p>
<img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/locksboats1.jpg" width="400" height="235" />
<p>A boat named "Outlaw" heading into the ship canal after being raised in the Locks. The name seemed appropriate for the event.</p>
<img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/locksboats2.jpg" width="400" height="215" />
<p>We had many different people and many different cameras. This is the <a href="http://www.aclu-wa.org/">ACLU</a> rep with a camera that some of us wondered if he'd borrowed from a museum. Very cool. The ACLU guy had a shirt that read: "It's a Bill of Rights, not Suggestions".</p>
<img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/locksaclu.jpg" width="235" height="350" />
<p>Here's another cool camera there for the event. As fun as digital cameras are, there is something deeply satisfying about seeing a good old-fashioned looking camera at a photographers' workshop like this. It looked like he got some really incredible pictures out of it, too.</p>
<img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/lockscoolcamera.jpg" width="300" height="250" />
<p>We lined up between the two locks and started taking photos. At times we looked really quite professional:</p>
<img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/lockspeople5.jpg" width="300" height="250" />
<img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/lockspeople4.jpg" width="400" height="250" />
<p>However, whenever we got together, we couldn't think of anything more enjoyable than taking pictures of people taking our pictures. Oh c'mon, you would've taken these kind of pictures, too!</p>
<img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/lockspeople2.jpg" width="300" height="175" />
<p>The local wildlife wasn't impressed with us.</p>
<img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/locksseagull.jpg" width="300" height="200" />
<p>When the <a href="http://www.argosycruises.com/publiccruises/locks.cfm">Goodtime III</a> came up to the lock, I shouted and waved, and we got a little bit of a response from the folks enjoying the sun on the deck. I noted that the more I waved, the more they waved back, but it was hard to snap a picture and wave at the same time.</p>
<img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/locksboats3.jpg" width="350" height="300" />
<p>I got a lot of good shots (for me) and was pleased with the event. I think my favorite shots came right at the end of the event, before I left to avoid another killer sunburn. I noticed that the train bridge had been raised, and then saw a lot of boats headed toward the locks. I snapped a couple of shots. This is the best one. Note that it was taken from about the same spot as the opening shot on <a href="http://69.93.170.43/">Ian's Artist's Statement</a>.</p>
<img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/locksbridgeboats.jpg" width="350" height="235" />
<p>After a lot of milling around and taking cool pictures, we got together for one last attempt at a group picture, then the group kind of broke up as everyone wandered off.</p>
<img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/lockspeople3.jpg" width="400" height="150" />
<p>As we were leaving, one of the <a href="http://www.blueneedle.com/wordpress/archives/2004/07/23/day-of-photography-at-ballard-locks-re-defined/">marshalls</a> pointed out that Ian had apparently been taking his photos from <a href="http://www.cityofseattle.net/parks/parkspaces/commodorepark.htm">Commodore Park</a>, across from the main body of the Locks and part of the City of Seattle instead of the Army Corps of Engineers. It was proposed that we do a follow-up event and take pictures of the Lock from Commodore Park sometime in the future. I'm for it. I had a blast and learned quite a bit from talking with actual photographers.</p>
<img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/locksflags.jpg" width="300" height="225" />
<p>I'll also note that I picked up two brochures in the Visitor's Center, for free, which give a lot of details about the Lock and its history. It is not at all difficult to get information about the Locks, and taking pictures from Commodore Park seems like a less dangerous spot than the Locks themselves for such an activity. <a href="http://69.93.170.43/">Ian was harrassed</a> for no good reason. We already knew that, but I've got even more proof.<br />
<br />
If you were at the event, and blogged about it, please leave a comment with the link. I'll update this entry to include links to other coverage.<br />
