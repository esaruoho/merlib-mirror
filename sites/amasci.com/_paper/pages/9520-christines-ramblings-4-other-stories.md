<td><p><strong><u>Others:</u></strong> </p>
<p><strong>1. For It May Come True (TGC rewrite)</strong> <br />
<strong>     Overall, most of us considered The Goliath Chronicles</strong> <br />
<strong>something of a letdown. Which is putting it mildly. The general</strong> <br />
<strong>opinion seemed to be that while there were a few episodes</strong> <br />
<strong>that had at least tried and carried some snippets of merit, most</strong> <br />
<strong>of the rest ranged from mediocre to just plain reeking.</strong> <br />
<strong>     I am not the only writer who chose to pretend that TGC</strong> <br />
<strong>never happened (though I did try to incorporate some of the</strong> <br />
<strong>events, and held onto a few things such as the Quarrymen -- if</strong> <br />
<strong>for no other reason than because the name with its dual</strong> <br />
<strong>meaning is just splendid!).</strong> <br />
<strong>     A group of us on a mailing list got to thinking we might</strong> <br />
<strong>be able to do a better job ourselves. So, taking the official</strong> <br />
<strong>episode synopses off the Buena Vista site to use as guidelines,</strong> <br />
<strong>each of us chose a story and went for it.</strong> <br />
<strong>     It was suggested that I take For It May Come True,</strong> <br />
<strong>because I had gotten a reputation as being something of a</strong> <br />
<strong>romantic (don't know <em>where</em> that could have come from &lt;g&gt;).</strong> <br />
<strong>    At the time, I hadn't even seen the episode because my work</strong> <br />
<strong>schedule conflicted with the wee-small-hours airing of TGC. I</strong> <br />
<strong>had been taping them, but after watching the first couple, had</strong> <br />
<strong>lost some of the eagerness that had initially made me rush right</strong> <br />
<strong>home from work to watch.</strong> <br />
<strong>     This was a depressing endeavor, because the premise</strong> <br />
<strong>had <em>so</em> much potential and the actual episode failed to touch</strong> <br />
<strong>on dozens of things that could have made it a wonderful and</strong> <br />
<strong>memorable one. I felt, egomaniacally, that I could do better with</strong> <br />
<strong>one side of my brain tied behind my back.</strong> <br />
<strong>     My biggest problem, a problem that has haunted me</strong> <br />
<strong>throughout my editorship of Avalon Mists and my projects for</strong> <br />
<strong>TGS, was keeping it tame, clean, and within the boundaries of</strong> <br />
<strong>what would be seen on the show. I may have slid over the line</strong> <br />
<strong>a time or two, but I tried, I really did ; )</strong> <br />
<strong>     I saved the finished product for presentation at the</strong> <br />
<strong>Gathering 1997, when I read it aloud to a small but appreciative</strong> <br />
<strong>group (extraordinarily appreciative; they kept listening to me</strong> <br />
<strong>even when Greg himself walked into another part of the room</strong> <br />
<strong>halfway through the reading!!!).</strong> <br />
<strong>     Overall, I am very pleased with this story. Sneaking in</strong> <br />
<strong>little in-jokes and references, such as naming the kids Keith and</strong> <br />
<strong>Salli, or Owen's Mr. Smithers line, was only part of the fun. As</strong> <br />
<strong>with What Might Have Been, thinking through the implications</strong> <br />
<strong>and changes was the best part. Such as having Demona be the</strong> <br />
<strong>leader of the corrupted clan, Fox and Xanatos <em>not</em> getting</strong> <br />
<strong>together, MacBeth locked in the dungeon, Derek never</strong> <br />
<strong>becoming Talon, etc.</strong> <br />
 </p>
<p><strong><u>Some thoughts on TGS:</u></strong> <br />
<strong>     I am consistently in awe and admiration of the people</strong> <br />
<strong>who make this possible! This is a <em>huge</em> effort by many</strong> <br />
<strong>talented writers, artists, editors, organizers, and watchdogs, all</strong> <br />
<strong>working together to create a cohesive series of stories based</strong> <br />
<strong>on Greg's vision. That they have been able to do it so well and</strong> <br />
<strong>so long with a minimum of infighting and bloodshed is worth</strong> <br />
<strong>an ovation! (Applause sign blinks on and off in the background).</strong> <br />
<strong>     We save our highest esteem for those who tackle the</strong> <br />
<strong>job we ourselves wouldn't want, and do it well. That's what I</strong> <br />
<strong>believe, anyway. These folks work their <em>asses</em> off, and I am</strong> <br />
<strong>honored to have been invited to be a peripheral member of the</strong> <br />
<strong>team.</strong> <br />
<strong>     Why peripheral? Well, way back when, I was invited to</strong> <br />
<strong>join up. But I am not a good team player. I do not work well</strong> <br />
<strong>with others. I am a selfish, demanding, strong-headed Aries who</strong> <br />
<strong>always wants things my way. I mix passive-aggressive</strong> <br />
<strong>techniques with just being a plain ol' bitch, leaving a trail of</strong> <br />
<strong>bodies in my wake. About the only person I could realistically</strong> <br />
<strong>collaborate with is my husband Tim, and that's partly because</strong> <br />
<strong>he does plot while I do story, and partly because after ten</strong> <br />
<strong>years together, he knows better than to get in my way ; )</strong> <br />
<strong>     Therefore, I knew I would not be of benefit to an effort</strong> <br />
<strong>such as TGS, except in the capacity of writer-for-hire. Besides, I</strong> <br />
<strong>had my own stories happening, and didn't want cross-</strong> <br />
<strong>pollination or cross-pollution of ideas.</strong> <br />
<strong>     And there was the not inconsiderable problem of S&amp;P.</strong> <br />
<strong>Ah, Standards and Practices, the bane of my existence. These</strong> <br />
<strong>are the aforementioned watchdogs whose grueling task it is to</strong> <br />
<strong>make sure that the writers don't get too naughty, violent, or</strong> <br />
<strong>commercial. As you might imagine, we butted heads more than</strong> <br />
<strong>a little even over the few projects that I took on ; )</strong> <br />
<strong>     I have actually read very little of TGS, I must confess.</strong> <br />
<strong>This is partly due to time and partly due to preference. But I</strong> <br />
<strong>don't have to be a dedicated follower to appreciate the hard</strong> <br />
<strong>work that has gone into this series. To all of the staff, from the</strong> <br />
<strong>planners who brainstorm the initial story ideas to the editors</strong> <br />
<strong>who give it the final polish, I salute you!</strong> </p>
<p><strong>The Seduction (TGS Dark Ages project)</strong> <br />
<strong>     With a title like that followed by the words "by Christine</strong> <br />
<strong>Morgan," I bet a lot of TGS readers were pretty darn worried! ; )</strong> <br />
<strong>     Dark Ages was the spinoff that most intrigued me. Here</strong> <br />
<strong>was a whole cast of characters, vibrant and fascinating ... and</strong> <br />
<strong>they were all going to be slaughtered in the Wyvern massacre.</strong> <br />
<strong>     I think this may be why Dark Ages ultimately suffered</strong> <br />
<strong>some popularity problems. Everybody knew that the staff were</strong> <br />
<strong>going to kill off a clan that we had come to know and love.</strong> <br />
<strong>These weren't just generic background gargoyles seen in</strong> <br />
<strong>flashbacks. They had names, personalities, hopes, dreams. And</strong> <br />
<strong>they were doomed. I know of several fans who have contrived</strong> <br />
<strong>ways of trying to spare their favorites, letting them live on in</strong> <br />
<strong>their own fanfic -- fanfic of fanfic, wow!</strong> <br />
<strong>     Then there was the name thing. It is damn hard to write</strong> <br />
<strong>for characters that don't have any names. A name can sum up</strong> <br />
<strong>the essence of a character, is vitally important. Yet the early</strong> <br />
<strong>clan didn't have them, except as assigned by Brother Edmund.</strong> <br />
<strong>This was a dodge that let the writers get past the stumbling</strong> <br />
<strong>block, though sometimes it didn't seem to work as well as it</strong> <br />
<strong>could have. Those names were only known to Brother</strong> <br />
<strong>Edmund, and thus really only should have been used from his</strong> <br />
<strong>point of view. But doing that would have been well nigh</strong> <br />
<strong>impossible.</strong> <br />
<strong>     In Something Old, Something New, I ran into this myself.</strong> <br />
<strong>You can only do so much with "brother," "leader," and</strong> <br />
<strong>descriptive terms. It is far easier to come up with names, or at</strong> <br />
<strong>least nicknames. Hence Joy and Crimson. But I never did feel</strong> <br />
<strong>quite right about it.</strong> <br />
<strong>     So, when I took on The Seduction, I wanted to see if I</strong> <br />
<strong>could do it without names. And it was a pain in the butt. I did it,</strong> <br />
<strong>but not without much gnashing of teeth.</strong> <br />
<strong>     Writing to someone else's outline was a new challenge</strong> <br />
<strong>for me, one that I initially dreaded and wound up actually</strong> <br />
<strong>enjoying.</strong> <br />
<strong>     Overall, I really like this story. I like the interactions</strong> <br />
<strong>between Hudson and his mate, and young Demona's kitchen</strong> <br />
<strong>misadventures still make me grin. I liked getting to torment her</strong> <br />
<strong>on all sides, the pressure she was under from the second-in-</strong> <br />
<strong>command, the teasing she got from her siblings, her own</strong> <br />
<strong>internal worrying. I know all too well what that can be like.</strong> </p>
<p><strong>Reprisals (TGS Gargoyles project)</strong> <br />
<strong>     Christi made me do it.</strong> <br />
<strong>     She flattered, bribed, and browbeat me into taking this</strong> <br />
<strong>one on. I was hesitant -- I hadn't even been reading most of the</strong> <br />
<strong>stories, and here I was supposed to do the season finale?</strong> <br />
<strong>     But she was persistent and made me offers I couldn't</strong> <br />
<strong>refuse, so I agreed ... and then I got a look at the outline. Aye</strong> <br />
<strong>caramba! A veritable truckload of characters that I knew nothing</strong> <br />
<strong>about! The Ultra-Pack (and here I was, having hated Jackal and</strong> <br />
<strong>Hyena ever since Upgrade)! Building up to an apocalyptic war!</strong> <br />
<strong>Eeeeek!</strong> <br />
<strong>     I knew I was in way over my head. Way, way over. My</strong> <br />
<strong>initial submission, the first draft of the opening scene between</strong> <br />
<strong>Demona and Canmore, just about gave the S&amp;P people cerebral</strong> <br />
<strong>hemorrhages because of the gore. Thus began a lengthy ordeal</strong> <br />
<strong>that sorely tested my faith in myself and my ability to keep my</strong> <br />
<strong>cool.</strong> <br />
<strong>     Among other things, I got raked across the coals for</strong> <br />
<strong>Broadway's question to Angela about Mavis making a pass at</strong> <br />
<strong>her, and Lex's "incredibly rude gesture involving both hands</strong> <br />
<strong>and his tail." Those both stayed in, but boy was it a big to-do!</strong> <br />
<strong>     To this day, I get angry letters about Lex. How could I</strong> <br />
<strong>do that to him? How could I strip away all his hope and leave</strong> <br />
<strong>him wallowing in freakish misery forever? Hey, I cried in</strong> <br />
<strong>protest, it wasn't <em>my</em> idea! I just wrote from the outline! But</strong> <br />
<strong>still, wow, the guilt! Some people came across like I had ruined</strong> <br />
<strong>their lives forever, they were never going to read another line</strong> <br />
<strong>of TGS, they couldn't believe I did such an awful thing!</strong> <br />
<strong>     Wow. All that, and S&amp;P was worried because I made</strong> <br />
<strong>some mild joke about Mavis?</strong> <br />
<strong>     To further complicate matters, I was supposed to have a</strong> <br />
<strong>couple of co-writers, who assured me that they would take care</strong> <br />
<strong>of the Unseelie stuff. They knew the scoop, particularly on</strong> <br />
<strong>Maeve and Umbriel. Hakuna matata. No worries. But then time</strong> <br />
<strong>went by and time went by and I wound up having to muddle</strong> <br />
<strong>through on my own (with a couple last-minute tiffs, snags, and</strong> <br />
<strong>to-dos that sent me scurrying for cover while some of the staff</strong> <br />
<strong>duked it out).</strong> <br />
<strong>     Because of my opinions on Jackal and Hyena, I had a</strong> <br />
<strong>hard time with their scenes. Their fight with Lex was supposed</strong> <br />
<strong>to be serious, and I just could not do it. I wanted it quick,</strong> <br />
<strong>cinematic, and over fairly fast. I wound up coming out of that</strong> <br />
<strong>with one of my favorite images, that of Hyena rising from the</strong> <br />
<strong>fountain like Venus. I was really proud of that one.</strong> <br />
<strong>     I could not have done this without Patrick. This man</strong> <br />
<strong>saved my sanity. Usually "editor" is a word spoken with the</strong> <br />
<strong>same tone as "lawyer" or "scumbucket," but not Patrick.</strong> <br />
<strong>Supportive yet firm, like one of those expensive mattresses on</strong> <br />
<strong>TV (I mean that as a compliment, Patrick, honest!). When I was</strong> <br />
<strong>out of line, he told me so as kindly as possible, when I was</strong> <br />
<strong><em>way</em> out of line he smacked me around like I deserved, and</strong> <br />
<strong>when he believed in me he went to bat for me and stood his</strong> <br />
<strong>ground against the fire and fury of all others. I love ya, man!</strong> <br />
<strong> </strong> <br />
<strong>Signs and Portents (TGS Pendragon project)</strong> <br />
<strong>     I wasn't planning to participate this season. I had too</strong> <br />
<strong>much else to do, and still was reeling from the furor over</strong> <br />
<strong>Reprisals.</strong> <br />
<strong>     But Stephen cajoled me into at least taking a look -- talk</strong> <br />
<strong>about persistent, this guy tried so hard to get me to agree to a</strong> <br />
<strong>collaboration that he deserves a medal (or a psych eval; I kept</strong> <br />
<strong>telling him what an impossible-to-work-with harridan I am and</strong> <br />
<strong>either he didn't believe me or he has nerves of steel ... or he's a</strong> <br />
<strong>masochist &lt;g&gt;).</strong> <br />
<strong>     While the collaboration never worked (I out-stubborned</strong> <br />
<strong>him, which was <em>not</em> easy), I did take a look at the posted list</strong> <br />
<strong>of TGS episodes, and found one that instantly appealed to me.</strong> <br />
<strong>In high school and college, I got into reading Tarot cards and</strong> <br />
<strong>was pretty good at it ... someday, if I can't make it as a writer, I'll</strong> <br />
<strong>hang up my counselor hat and go into business as a psychic</strong> <br />
<strong>advisor. So when I read the outline that had Una consulting the</strong> <br />
<strong>cards, my imagination was sparked.</strong> <br />
<strong>     And as I read on and found out there were basilisks</strong> <br />
<strong>involved -- well! The middle book of the ElfLore series is</strong> <br />
<strong>supposed to be called Knight of the Basilisk, so I knew all</strong> <br />
<strong>about them already. And then the confrontation in the chicken</strong> <br />
<strong>coop ... the chance to write slapstick with King Arthur ... I</strong> <br />
<strong>couldn't resist.</strong> <br />
<strong>     I immediately went begging to Kathy, who had written</strong> <br />
<strong>the outline, and when she agreed, I got writing. Well, first I got</strong> <br />
<strong>out my deck and figured out what cards I could use to make</strong> <br />
<strong>Una's reading meaningful and effective.</strong> <br />
<strong>     Sharp and Peake ... these two appear as DSA agents in</strong> <br />
<strong>the Leigh Nichols (pseudonym of Dean Koontz) book</strong> <br />
<strong>Shadowfires.</strong> <br />
<strong>     Really bad joke that nobody ever got ... or if they did, it</strong> <br />
<strong>killed them so they could never reply ... the original name of</strong> <br />
<strong>the farmer in the outline was not Cabot. I gave the other name</strong> <br />
<strong>to the vet so I could use Cabot. Henry Cabot. The action takes</strong> <br />
<strong>place in Henry Cabot's henhouse (and don't forget the gangly</strong> <br />
<strong>red-sweatshirted Fred who knew the job was dangerous when</strong> <br />
<strong>he took it ...)</strong> <br />
<strong>     Henry Cabot Henhouse III was the secret identity of</strong> <br />
<strong>Super Chicken ... "when you find yourself in danger, when</strong> <br />
<strong>you're threatened by a stranger, when it looks like you will take</strong> <br />
<strong>a lickin' ... there is someone waiting who will hurry up and</strong> <br />
<strong>rescue you ... just ca-all for Super Chicken (buck-buck-buck-</strong> <br />
<strong>buck) ... just ca-all for Super Chicken! (buck-awk!)"</strong> <br />
<strong>     Oh, I'm so old, nobody remembers that but me, Super</strong> <br />
<strong>Chicken and Tom Slick and all those cartoons ...</strong> <br />
