<strong>Sketchbook - Todd Nauck</strong>
<p>How I got this sketch is detailed in my <a href="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/aqua/sandiego/#4d">San Diego 2000 Con Report</a>. Todd Nauck is one of the nicest artists I've ever met, and that's saying something because I've met an awful lot of artists now. This is also one of the very, very few sketches in my collection that I didn't actually ask for. Todd drew this without asking after looking at all the other sketches in the book. And it's still one of my all-time favorites in my collection, and the sketch that cemented Todd as one of my all-time favorite artists. I was bitterly disappointed when Young Justice was cancelled, and I've supported his projects, particularly <a href="http://www.wildguard.com/">WildGuard</a>, even more fervently since then.</p>
<a href="http://www.wildguard.com/"><img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/sketch/sketch_NauckT.jpg" data-border="0" width="347" height="400" /></a><br />
<strong>AQUAMAN</strong><br />
<strong>by Todd Nauck</strong><br />
22 July 2000<br />
(permission to post given 29 Feb 2004 in person)<br />
<a href="http://www.wildguard.com/">WildGuard</a>
<p>To see all the sketches I have permission to post so far, check out my <a href="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/sketch">Sketchbook Page</a>. If you have any contact information for any of the <a href="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/sketch/index.html#wanted">other artists I'm trying to contact</a>, please <a href="mailto:tegan@eskimo.com?subject=Bloggity-Blog-Blog-Blog">e-mail me</a>. Click for a <a href="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/sketch/random.html">random Aquaman sketch</a>.</p>
<p>As a reminder: Almost every sketch in my sketchbook was non-commissioned and done on the floor of a con. These are not the best works of the artists. These are only representative of what the artist can do under pressure in a loud, crowded, and often stressed-out environment. Most of them were done as quickly as possible, to prevent huge lines from forming. Don't judge any of these artists negatively by the artwork you see, instead be as impressed as I am by what they accomplished in far-from-ideal conditions.</p>
<p>The Saturday Sketch ™ is brought to you by the letters "A", "Q" and the number "27", and also courtesy the fine artists who pour their lives into producing wonderful comic books, then come to conventions only to get asked by geeky fangirls for a sketch of Aquaman. Support an artist, buy a comic book.<br />

<strong>Rapid Reviews - 17 March 2004 - Part IV</strong>
<p><strong>Amazing Spider-Man #505 (#64)</strong>: Mary Jane is having doubts, so why doesn't she confide in Peter? That sort of indicates a flaw in their relationship, that she isn't confident enough to let Peter know what she heard. I wonder if anything will develop out of this? In the B-plot, I like Peter's new-found adulthood. Being able to talk with kids and get them to listen is a rare skill, and one that I admire greatly. Showing Peter as a mature adult is nice, especially since he hasn't lost his sense of fun. Overall a satisfying issue. <a href="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/rapidreviews.html"><img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/4_0_starfish.jpg" data-border="0" width="75" height="15" alt="4 starfish" /></a></p>
<p><strong>Abadazad #2</strong>: Being very familiar with Oz, I think I was expecting a longer and more difficult journey to the Emera--- er, to Inconceivable. These books traditionally start out with a bunch of quests before they make it to see the Queen, so I was thrown off by Martha showing up right away and The City being at the start of the quest instead of the end. And where's the obligatory party held in the honor of the guests arriving for the first time? *ahem* Anyway, other than that, I really enjoyed the issue, and was a bit disturbed by the foreshadowing. This is a darker place than the Abadazad of the books, as Kate will soon learn. Other nice aspects of this issue included the lengthy recap of the first issue at the end of the book, the crossword puzzle, information on the Bridges program, including some hints for lesson plans, and the great "Abadazad readers may also enjoy..." bit, which I was delighted to see included <em>Leave It To Chance</em>, <em>Scary Godmother</em>, and <em>The Courageous Princess</em>. Very nice! <a href="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/rapidreviews.html"><img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/4_0_starfish.jpg" data-border="0" width="75" height="15" alt="4 starfish" /></a><br />

<strong>Free Comic Book Day - July 3rd</strong>
<p><a href="http://www.newsarama.com/pages/FCBD3.htm">Newsarama</a> reports on the offerings for this year's <a href="http://www.freecomicbookday.com/">Free Comic Book Day</a>. As in years past, this is NOT free for the retailers.</p>
<p>I urge serious comic book fans who have a good relationship with their local stores to offer to pay for a set of free comics for your store. Yeah, you heard me. Offer to pick up some of the tab so the burden <em>isn't</em> on the store itself. In return, ask for a copy of each free comic that the store gets. Make the deal, get more "free" comics, and allow the store to give away more comics. It's a win-win situation.</p>
<p>And, <a href="http://www.cornercomics.com/">Paige</a>, if you're reading this, I'd like to pay for some of the comics this year. I said I would last year, and I mean to keep my word. I'll pitch in to help you pay for the "free" stuff.</p>
<p>Now, as <a href="http://johnnybacardi.blogspot.com/">Johnny B</a> did, here's my thoughts on the offerings this year:</p>
<p>Gold Sponsors:</p>
<p><em>Archie: I Was a Teenage Comic Book Character</em>: Always good for the kids. Archie always puts out something of interest.</p>
<p><em>The Ballad of Sleeping Beauty #1</em>: Hmmm, don't know. Looks interesting, from Beckett, which is a company I'm unfamiliar with. We'll have to see.</p>
<p><em>American Power: Prequel</em>: From CrossGen. A Prequel instead of one of their proven series. Interesting. I'd be more impressed if it didn't seem like Chuck Dixon is single-handedly writing every CrossGen title at the moment.</p>
<p><em>Star Wars Clone Wars Adventures</em>: Yeah! I like seeing this. The Clone Wars cartoons were the best Star Wars I've seen since the original trilogy, so it's nice to see a new adventure based on them.</p>
<p><em>Teen Titans Go! #1</em>: This ought to be popular, if the success of the show is any indication. I have to admit, it's growing on me, and I haven't watched many episodes. Just the Aqualad ones.</p>
<p><em>Duel Masters #1</em>: No idea on this one. Looks like a standard bit of Manga-ish fare. We'll have to see when it comes out.</p>
<p><em>Walt Disneys Mickey Mouse</em>: It's Mickey, It's good.</p>
<p><em>IDW Sampler</em>: This will be popular, as it's got a CSI story in it. I seem to recall my little sister responding to the CSI comic with a hurt look of "why didn't you tell me!" at me. In other words, push this one on the TV show fans.</p>
<p><em>Image Comics Summer Special</em>: This one look like its mostly a pass. No <em>Age of Bronze</em>, no <em>Wildguard</em>. Boring.</p>
<p><em>Marvel Age: Spider-Man #1</em>: New art from old story? We'll have to see.</p>
<p>Silver Sponsors:</p>
<p><em>Wild About Comics #1</em>: Hey, it's got Licensable Bear, how bad can it be? Seriously, About Comics is a good company, so this ought to be a fun one to read.</p>
<p><em>The AdHouse Books FREE Comic Book Day Comic Book</em>: The word "floppy" in their ad annoys me. I don't know why. Still, this appears to be a collection of 24-hour comics, which ought to be fun.</p>
<p><em>Alternative Comics #2</em>: MATURE READERS. These anthologies always have at least one cool thing. I hope I get this one.</p>
<p><em>Arcana Studio Presents #1</em>: Hmmm... This might be the book that finally gets me to look at <em>Ant</em>. With three different stories, this one will offer good value.</p>
<p><em>Astonish Comics #1</em>: I want this one. Herobear alone makes it worth it, then add in the others and we've got something that should be a lot of fun for lots of people.</p>
<p><em>Back in Black: Brian Pulido Unleashed at Avatar</em>: Another prequel, this time with three different titles. We'll have to see about this one.</p>
<p><em>A Bunch of Baboons</em>: Six short stories. Looks like it could be very good.</p>
<p><em>Del Ray Manga Sampler</em>: Looks like this one also offers a couple of sneak peeks. Retailers take note. Manga is good right now.</p>
<p><em>Best of Dork Storm Press #2</em>: Me want. This will have a PS238 story, and that's enough to get it.</p>
<p><em>Dr. Chaos Comic Cavalcade</em>: The solicit sounds fun: "Santa Claus, the Easter Bunny and the rest of the gang arent too happy about Free Comic Book Day stealing their holiday thunder". Hope I get it.</p>
<p><em>Reggie-12</em>: Robot kid fighting giant robots. Humor book. Ok.</p>
<p><em>Johnny Raygun Classics #1</em>: Another attempt to bring back the Silver Age? We'll have to see how well it succeeds.</p>
<p><em>Keenspot Spotlight 2004</em>: This is a 100+ pager, which suggests value for money. However, it is Keenspot, which has ... um... slightly lower standards. I would be prepared to be surprised <em>or</em> disappointed in this one.</p>
<p><em>Sore Thumbs Election Special</em>: Hmmm. A liberal activist and a conservative video game shop owner. Like we need to be reminded of the civil cold war. We'll have to see.</p>
<p><em>From Pixels To Paper 2004: Keenspace.com's Third Dead-Tree Sampler of Independent Webcomics</em>: Webcomics on dead trees! The title says it all, doesn't it?</p>
<p><em>The NBM Sampler: Boneyard, Rumble Girls, Dungeon</em>: Another book, another sampler. I can see this year's FCBD taking a LONG time to review if I somehow manage to get everything.</p>
<p><em>The Adventures of Barry Ween, Boy Genius: Secret Crisis Origin Files</em>: MATURE READERS. A reprint of an earlier tale which will let my hubby finally get a good look at Judd Winick's non-Green Lantern work.</p>
<p><em>Love Fights #1/Everest: Facing the Goddess Preview</em>: MATURE READERS. After reading <em>Whiteout</em>, I'm interested in seeing what Rucka can do. I'd definitely like this one.</p>
<p><em>Amelia Rules! Special Edition</em>: Must get this. Amelia rocks, and this is a great one for girls. Get lots of this one, retailers!</p>
<p><em>Delicious Seasons</em>: I almost bought this one when it was solicited in Previews. I would love to check out this preview to see if I made the right choice.</p>
<p><em>Slave Labor stories #2</em>: Looks like this one is going to be another anthology, and I'm kind of interested in seeing the <em>Pirate Club</em> story. So I hope this one gets into my hands.</p>
<p><em>Top Shelf Tales</em>: Four of the fascinating creators out in comics today, playing in an anthology. Another one I'd like to get.</p>
<p>Bronze Sponsors: A Fine Line, Arcana Studios, Claypool Comics, Colburn Comics, Little Rocket, Lone Star Press, Mirage Studios, Nite Owl Comics, Strawberry Comics, and Street Legends Ink. I don't usually get much from the Bronze Sponsors, as I'm intent on getting the special books, so I don't know that I'll have anything to say about any of these guys.</p>
<p>You can still read my Rapid Reviews of last year's Free Comics: <a href="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/20030427-20030503.html#93730954">Part I</a>, <a href="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/20030504-20030510.html#93752826">Part II</a>, <a href="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/20030504-20030510.html#93806181">Part III</a>, <a href="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/20030504-20030510.html#93834930">Part IV</a>, <a href="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/20030504-20030510.html#93871664">Part V</a>, and <a href="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/20030504-20030510.html#93937603">Part VI</a>. Because of last year's Free Comics, I hunted down and bought all the <em>Leave it to Chance</em> I could find, which I've been loaning out to people.</p>
<p>So: Offer to help! Get those free comics out there! Promote this sucker! And try something new. That's what this day is for people who already read comics... give something new a chance. After all, it's free.<br />
</p>

<strong>Suggestions for Aunty</strong>
<p>I opened up <a href="#107942481750828369">a little thread</a> in the hopes of getting Aunty some reading suggestions if she wanted to try out comic books. Here are the rules: Aunty does not like "mature" books. Nothing that would get an "R" rating as a movie. The genres she enjoys are science fiction, spy novels, and historical novels.</p>
<p>Here are the suggestions:</p>
<p>From me: <a href="http://www.amazon.com/exec/obidos/ASIN/0972555307/theaquamanwebsit"><em>The Interman</em></a> by Jeff Parker, as it's a spy thriller. Samples are available at the website: <a href="http://theinterman.com/">here</a>. I also suggested <a href="http://www.amazon.com/exec/obidos/ASIN/0966712714/theaquamanwebsit"><em>Whiteout</em></a>, which I'd just finished reading. A free sample of issue #1 is available <a href="http://www.onipress.com/freecomic/">here</a> (scroll to the bottom of the page). I couldn't think of any Science Fiction, but I also mildly recommended <a href="http://www.amazon.com/exec/obidos/ASIN/1582402000/theaquamanwebsit"><em>Age of Bronze</em></a> despite there being a lot of mature stuff in there... none of it is inapropriate to the story, but there's nudity, sex, and violence like you would expect from a story about the Trojan War. The website is <a href="http://age-of-bronze.com/">here</a>.</p>
<p><a href="http://www.nearmintheroes.org/">Shane</a> came through in a big way, with a pile of suggestions and links: <em>The Ring of the Nibelung</em> adaptation by P Craig Russell. Gorgeous artwork, and you can get it as a <a href="http://www.darkhorse.com/profile/profile.php?sku=12-050">ltd. edition hardcover here</a> or as the cheaper <a href="http://www.darkhorse.com/search/search.php?sstring=P.+Craig+Russell">individual books here</a>. He also suggested the <em>DC's Big Book Of ___</em> series, which has a large variety of stories. Those can be found at Amazon <a href="http://www.amazon.com/exec/obidos/tg/detail/-/1563892170/104-8286099-2720764?v=glance">here</a>. For science fiction he recommended Jim Starlin's <a href="http://www.budplant.com/prod.itml/icOid/13287"><em>Dreadstar</em></a>, and <a href="http://www.amazon.com/exec/obidos/tg/detail/-/0743474767/qid=/br=1-/ref=br_lf_b_//104-8286099-2720764?v=glance&amp;s=books&amp;n=4390"><em>The Best of Ray Bradbury: The Graphic Novel</em></a>. He also recommended <em>Whiteout</em>. In another genre, he recommended <a href="http://www.amazon.com/exec/obidos/ASIN/1891830430/ref=pd_b_qp1t/104-8286099-2720764"><em>Blankets</em></a>. <strong>Dinner Tonight</strong>
<p>Tonight hubby-Eric and I went out. There's a new restaurant in the neighborhood, and even though it's part of a chain, we were very curious to see what the food was like. Today was the first day the restaurant was open. As far as I know, neither of us had ever eaten at <a href="http://www.applebees.com/">Applebee's</a> before, so we went down and tried it out. The wait was long, as everyone in Bothell seemed to be there to try it out.</p>
<p>Despite the wait, everyone was quite cheerful. Waiters came by and fed the waiting people with cheese stick samplers. The crew of the restaurant was still working on their skills, so they were constantly checking and re-checking the lists. One little girl waiting with us got... impatient. They got her a balloon, then a cup of water. And we learned that one of the employees was actively afraid of balloons, which isn't really that odd when you consider how many people claim to be afraid of clowns.</p>
<p>The restaurant was decorated with lots of pictures, everything from sailboats in the foyer to collages celebrating local high schools to movie posters. We spent a bit of time examining the pictures and the menu, and waited.</p>
<p>We were eventually seated, and I thought it might be a bit of fate that we were put right next to the <a href="http://www.eskimo.com/~tiktok/">Wizard of Oz</a> movie poster and memorabilia. I was also right under a picture of the classic Christopher Reeves Superman. Let's just call it a good sign to start the evening's meal.</p>
<p>I ordered up some kiwi lemonade, which is now my favorite flavor of lemonade. Eric got regular lemonade. They also talked us into appetizers, and so we got quesadillas. They were wonderful. And enough. I wasn't particularly hungry when dinner finally arrived. I got salmon and "riblets", while hubby got some steak and shrimp. They made a few flubs, which didn't bother me. They forgot to bring me mashed potatoes, and when the manager brought them over, she told us that we should have seen the restaurant during the "dry run" yesterday. "Of course, the customers weren't paying last night," she said.</p>
<p>There wasn't enough room left in either of us for dessert, but the dulce de leche cheesecake cried out to be eaten. So we ordered it to go. And still haven't eaten it yet. In fact, half my dinner is sitting in the fridge because I couldn't eat it. What I did eat was extremely good, but there was WAY too much of it.</p>
<p>I guess that's a good thing. Two years ago I would've cleaned my plate no matter how stuffed I was.<br />
