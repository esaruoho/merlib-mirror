<strong>Computer Woes</strong>
<div class="entries">
<div style="clear:both;">
&#10;</div>
The keyboard on my laptop has just completely died. Right in the middle of a sentence I was writing for the Aquaman website. I'm still extremely grateful to Eric's dad for giving me this used laptop almost two years ago for Christmas. It's done wonders since then, and has worked very hard. I daresay that I used it more each week than he used it during the entire time he had it before giving it to me.<br />
<br />
But now the keyboard is dead, and I'm using a very old and really uncomfortable keyboard from my long-dead desktop. I was thinking very seriously about getting a new laptop soon anyway, this has just made the need a little more urgent. I don't know exactly what I want to get yet, but it would be nice to have an internal wireless card and a fast processor.<br />
<br />
In the incredibly unlikely event that someone rich and generous is reading this, I put <a href="http://www.amazon.com/o/registry/1LQ0LSAUK0S6I">a computer on my wishlist</a>. I don't know if I'll be able to wait until Christmas, but I'll hold out with the annoying keyboard as long as I can. While some computers are good for just about anything (like the one I put on the wishlist), finding a cheaper computer that has everything I want might be a bit more difficult.

<strong>CSS Update</strong>
<div class="entries">
<div style="clear:both;">
&#10;</div>
I feel like I'm getting more and more adept at CSS as I play with my <a href="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/aqua/">Aquaman Website</a>. Every day I seem to learn at least one new aspect, and it's now to the point that I actually understand what I'm doing most of the time, and why it should work. Admittedly, I still don't understand a few things... but I'm getting closer.<br />
<br />
Here are a few of the sites that have helped me out so far:
<ul>
<li><a href="http://www.websitetips.com/info/css/intro1/">Converting Existing Content to Style Sheets</a> - a good primer for absolute beginners.
<br />
</li>
<li><a href="http://www.nypl.org/styleguide/">NYPL Style Guide</a> - Why converting to XHTML and CSS is a good idea.
<br />
</li>
<li><a href="http://www.w3schools.com/default.asp">W3Schools Tutorials</a> - Lots of great detail on every aspect of CSS and XHTML.
<br />
</li>
<li><a href="http://www.westciv.com/style_master/academy/css_tutorial/">Complete CSS Guide</a> - Anything that I don't understand the W3Schools explanation is usually explained here so I can get it.
<br />
</li>
<li><a href="http://www.htmlhelp.com/reference/css/">Guide to Cascading Style Sheets</a> - Another decent set of guides.
<br />
</li>
<li><a href="http://home.tampabay.rr.com/bmerkey/cheatsheet.htm">Cascading Style Cheatsheet</a> - This page actually explains stuff that nobody else does.
<br />
</li>
<li><a href="http://www.webcredible.co.uk/user-friendly-resources/css/css-tricks.shtml">Ten CSS Tricks You May Not Know</a> - I can't use <em>most</em> of these yet, but I'm beginning to understand them, and they look like fun.</li>
</ul>
<br />
Now I am getting a little sick of working on the Aquaman site, just because there are limits to what I'm willing to change at the moment, so I looked at the template for <em>this</em> blog and was stunned at how incredibly ugly and messed up it is. I shouldn't be stunned, but I am. It's just so much garbage. I suddenly have another project, cleaning up my main blog template.<br />
<br />
So if the blog looks really funny over the next couple of weeks, you know why.

<strong>Rapid Reviews - 9 November 2005 - Part II</strong>
<div class="entries">
<div style="clear:both;">
&#10;</div>
<strong>Aquaman #36</strong>: "The Failure of Success": I suppose the title could refer to Koryak's mistake. I found it a little strange that Arthur didn't criticize Koryak a bit more... he did kill someone, after all. But then, it's very much in Koryak's nature, and it would be understandable for Koryak to believe that an OMAC was a robot. Not that he'd care, mind you. But Aquaman would. Manta does as expected, but I really like the touch of him scaring the scientists. Kirk and Clarke's Vulko looks like a Munchkin. Anyway, nice cliffhanger. I'm not totally satisfied with this book, but Arcudi gets better with every issue. <a href="http://realtegan.blogspot.com/2005/03/new-ratings-system.html"><img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/0030.jpg" data-border="0" width="75" height="15" alt="3 starfish" /></a><br />
<br />
&#10;<a href="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/aqua/"><img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/aqua/pics/cvr2003/cvra436.jpg" data-border="0" width="200" height="300" /></a>
<br />
<br />
<strong>Infinite Crisis #2</strong>: "The Survivors": This is not a book you can summarize quickly. And honestly, I don't think I'd want to try, because it would mean recapping the entire DCU since the Crisis. Or should we call it the first Crisis now? The implications of Superman's speech at the end of the book are ... chilling and thrilling. And, you know what I, as an Aquaman fan, am thinking. Should Superman succeed, will there even <em>be</em> an Aquaman? What is the Earth-2 Aquaman? Yellow gloves? More myth than man? He's one of the few characters that made the jump from Gold to Silver without changing... but he's changed a lot since then. Will Atlantis be erased? The solicits for future issues suggest that, yes, it will. So what then? Have Aquaman wear yellow gloves and continue on? Something more dramatic? Regardless of anything else in this issue, Superman's speech puts my brain into overdrive. I don't know what's coming next, and that's both exciting and insanity-inducing. And, you know, if DC actually manages to pull this off, to make <em>all</em> their books change... IF they manage it, then this might be the first crossover ever I'll call "worth it." <a href="http://realtegan.blogspot.com/2005/03/new-ratings-system.html"><img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/0030.jpg" data-border="0" width="75" height="15" alt="3 starfish" /></a>
