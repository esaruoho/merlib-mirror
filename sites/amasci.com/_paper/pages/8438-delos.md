# Delos (Δήλος)

Due to its advantageous location in the center of the circle of islands known as the Cyclades, Delos was an important and wealthy trading post in ancient Greece.  It also had important religious and mythological aspects.  The myth goes as follows:

Zeus, though he was married to Hera, had a tendency to be unfaithful and at one time made advances towards a nymph named Asteria.  Out of respect for Hera, Asteria rejected Zeus' advances and flung herself into the sea, falling like a star.  Zeus punished her by turning her into a barren island which was not anchored to the bottom of the sea, instead being blown around by the wind.

Later, Zeus got the goddess Leto pregnant.  Zeus' jealous wife Hera decreed that Leto should be driven out of all the cities of Greece, so Leto was forced to wander until finally reaching the floating island that is now called Delos.  When she set foot upon the island, four columns rose from the bed of the sea, forever fixing Delos to its present location.

After nine days of labor (prolonged because Hera would not allow the goddess of childbirth, Eileithyia, to come to the island to assist), Leto finally gave birth to Artemis and her twin brother Apollo.

Delos today is uninhabited except for people working there - the entire island is essentially one big archeological site.
