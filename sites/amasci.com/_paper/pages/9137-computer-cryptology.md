# Computer Cryptology

## Who am I?

This is the World Wide Web site for Computer Cryptology, aka Turing (from the email address turing AT eskimo DOT com), aka CC. Besides these Web pages, my posts in [alt.privacy.anon-server](news:alt.privacy.anon-server) provide some idea of who I am.

## What is here?

As the first two names (Computer Cryptology and Turing) imply, one of my hobbies is computer cryptology, i.e., making and breaking codes and ciphers using the computer. At the moment, only two parts of the CC hobby are on the Web:

- The CC [Pretty Good Privacy](http://www.pgp.com/) [RSA](keys/cc-rsa.asc) and [DH/DSS](keys/cc-dsa.asc) public keys, and
- The CC [Anonymous Remailer Page](remailer/).

## Why is this here?

I was born to educated parents, with a father who told me his opinions but encouraged me to form my own. I grew up in a country that whose judicial common law and whose constitution reflect a belief in the freedom of speech and of the press, and the right to privacy. My introduction to those freedoms and rights was my library card as child. As a teen, I relied upon this access to satisfy my curiosity about everything--from the hotly-debated political issues to the unspoken mysteries of sex.

In the digital age, many interests oppose these freedoms and rights:

- Legislatures convinced of the righteousness of their laws seek to eliminate online information that challenges the legitimacy of those laws.
- Executive agencies unprepared for the expansion of technology seek to monitor communications that might foster activity outside their control.
- Judiciaries offended by the resistance of netizens seek to control the content that permits disobedience to their dictates.
- Corporations wanting ownership extended into cyberspace seek to possess the information that enables others to find enjoyment outside their markets.
- Marketers wanting instant wealth seek to prevent the personal data hiding that provides individuals with the opportunity to make choices unmolested by their pleas.

I hope my interest in learning new technological skills can instead support the freedoms and rights I value.
