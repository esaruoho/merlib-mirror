# Whole Foods

*Source page: `~rarnold/whole_foods.htm`*

**Fox News: Dispute over "organic" food purity and threats of lawsuits**

**Opposing viewpoints provoke lively debate and hot tempers**

 

<span style="FONT-SIZE: 7.5pt; COLOR: black; FONT-FAMILY: Arial">Friday, July 26, 2002</span>\
**<span style="FONT-WEIGHT: bold; FONT-SIZE: 9pt; COLOR: black; FONT-FAMILY: Arial"> By Steven <span class="SpellE">Milloy</span></span>**\
\
<span style="font-size: 10pt">Whole Foods Market can dish it out, but they sure can't take it. The largest organic foods retailer developed a mega-profitable business by scaring consumers about conventionally produced foods supposedly "contaminated" with chemicals and biotechnology. </span>

<span style="FONT-SIZE: 10pt"> Now Whole Foods is having a mega-<span class="SpellE">hissy</span> fit because someone says its products might not be so unadulterated after all.</span>

<span style="FONT-SIZE: 10pt">The American Council on Science and Health, a nonprofit group that addresses consumer health issues, notified Whole Foods on July 11 of <span class="SpellE">ACSH's</span> intent to sue Whole Foods for violating California Health & Safety Code section 25249.6, better known as Proposition 65. The law requires warning labels on products containing substances deemed "toxic" by </span>California.

<span style="FONT-SIZE: 10pt">ACSH says Whole Foods illegally sells its whole wheat organic bread without a warning label. Swedish researchers announced in April that baking carbohydrate-rich products like bread produces the chemical <span class="SpellE">acrylamide</span> - listed as a toxic substance under Proposition 65.</span>

<span style="FONT-SIZE: 10pt">ACSH doesn't really believe that any minute amount of <span class="SpellE">acrylamide</span> present in Whole Foods' organic bread is dangerous. But the state of </span>California apparently does.

<span style="FONT-SIZE: 10pt">Whole Foods no doubt caught on that ACSH was simply playing a practical joke to make a point about the silliness of Proposition 65 - a law that needlessly scares consumers and provides no safety benefit.</span>

<span style="FONT-SIZE: 10pt">The company apparently was ignoring ACSH when the *<span style="FONT-STYLE: italic">New York Times</span>* called. Food reporter Marian Burros wanted Whole Foods' reaction to the potential lawsuit for her article in next Wednesday's *<span style="FONT-STYLE: italic">Times</span>*.</span>

<span style="FONT-SIZE: 10pt">Whole Foods no longer thinks ACSH is funny and has unleashed its attack dogs (i.e., lawyers).</span>

<span style="FONT-SIZE: 10pt">"This is no longer a publicity stunt of limited impact.  I \[am\] requesting that \[ACSH\] confirm in writing (immediately, for release to the press) that it does not intend to pursue any claim against \[Whole Foods\] and that it apologizes for any embarrassment or other injury to \[Whole Foods\] on account of your notice and your statements to the press," threatened Gibson, Dunn & <span class="SpellE">Crutcher</span> lawyer Charles <span class="SpellE">Ivie</span> in an e-mail to ACSH associate director Jeff <span class="SpellE">Stier</span>.</span>

<span style="FONT-SIZE: 10pt">"\[Whole Foods\] does not intend to sue ACSH, if you will immediately rectify this</span><span class="GramE"><span style="FONT-SIZE: 10pt"> damage</span></span>.  It is incomprehensible to me why an organization such as yours would have done what it has done," added <span class="SpellE">Ivie</span>.

<span style="FONT-SIZE: 10pt">ACSH is not backing down, according to <span class="SpellE">Stier</span>. In any event, the irony is rich. </span>

<span style="FONT-SIZE: 10pt">Whole Foods papers its stores with advertising implying, if not proclaiming, that conventional and biotech foods are downright dangerous. </span>

<span style="FONT-SIZE: 10pt">Whole Foods issued a press release last September claiming organic food is healthier than conventional food because, "... organic foods are spared the application of synthetic insecticides, herbicides, fungicides and fertilizers. Many EPA-approved pesticides were registered long before extensive research linked these chemicals to cancer and other diseases. Now, the EPA considers 60 percent of all herbicides, 90 percent of all fungicides and 30 percent of all insecticides as potentially cancer causing."</span>

<span style="FONT-SIZE: 10pt">Unfortunately for Whole Foods' assertion, there is not a single shred of evidence that any application of pesticides has caused a single case of cancer anywhere in the world despite 50-plus years of widespread use. </span>

<span style="FONT-SIZE: 10pt">About biotech foods, Whole Foods says, "As consumers learn more about the genetic modification of crops, or <span class="SpellE">GMOs</span>, there is increasing concern that biotechnology may be moving faster than our government's regulatory system is currently able to monitor and evaluate.  The effect genetic engineering may have on our environment and on long-term human health issues has not been determined by health officials. </span>

<span style="FONT-SIZE: 10pt">The implication is simply false as regulatory agencies have conducted extensive reviews of biotech foods and deem them to be safe.</span>

<span style="FONT-SIZE: 10pt">Last November, Whole Foods issued a press release titled, "Guess Who's Not Coming to Dinner This Year?; Whole Foods Market Offers Solutions to Make Holiday Meal Memorable And Delicious Without Artificial Additives, Preservatives, Growth Hormones."</span>

<span style="FONT-SIZE: 10pt">Omitted from mention is that these technologies have been extensively tested and approved as safe by government regulators around the world and have been used for decades without any evidence of harm. </span>

<span style="FONT-SIZE: 10pt">But why let facts get in the way of a profitable marketing strategy?</span>

<span style="FONT-SIZE: 10pt">A final irony is that *<span style="FONT-STYLE: italic">New York Times</span>* reporter Marian Burros is no fan of ACSH and <span class="GramE">it's</span> facts-not-fear advocacy. Her upcoming article is more likely to depict ACSH, not Whole Foods, as the bad guy. </span>

<span style="FONT-SIZE: 10pt">Whole Foods is lucky the conventional foods industry isn't as thin-skinned. Otherwise, Whole Foods' lawyers would have a whole lot more to worry about than ACSH.</span>
