### Air Search - Outstanding Success!

8/25/2004

From [Tom Peterson](mailto:PeterTh@wsdot.wa.gov)\

On Saturday 21 August, the Washington State Department of Transportation - Aviation received notification from the Federal Aviation Administration that an aircraft had made a crash landing in the area between Kelso and Toledo WA. One of the victims was able to make a phone call to 911 with a cell phone shortly after the crash and fire. The caller described a triangular field that they were close to but was unsure of his exact location. Attempts to recontact the caller to have them assist rescuers were unsuccessful.

Actions were immediately taken to activate Search and Rescue assets in SW Washington. The crash site was assumed to be in Lewis County based on information from the cellular phone company and the range of the particular tower the call was connected through. The cell company technicians identified the tower location as being near Winlock and that the caller would have to be within a 15 mile range. Additionally they indicated that the tower only works for callers East of Interstate 5.

The initial reports had four persons aboard the Cessna 185F, two of them teenage children of one of the pilots, who were enroute from McMinnville OR to Everett WA. The weather in the area was reported as winds NE-3. Temp 64, dew point 63, sky condition overcast 200' at the Kelso airport. The closest report for Toledo airport was at 1948L indicating winds calm, Temp 63, dew point 61, sky condition overcast 700' with mist and light rain. Conditions at McMinnville around the time of departure were winds west at 16, Temp 79, dew point 56, sky condition few at 11,000'. The destination airport was reporting winds South at 14, Temp 65, dew point 65, sky condition Scattered 1,700'/Broken 2,600'/Broken 7,000' at the time of departure from Oregon.

Weather conditions were a NO-GO for fixed wing or helicopter searching. The King County Sheriffs Aviation unit was contacted for rotor wing support and use of the FLIR unit, but conditions were well below safe minimums. Ground teams were dispatched from District 5 to try and pick-up a signal from the aircraft's Emergency Locator Transmitter (ELT). They left the Vancouver area and traveled up and down Interstate 5 as well as across the valley on Hiway 12. The Air Force Rescue Coordination Center had no reports of any ELT's in the western part of the state during this time period. Chuck Williams, Rick Knapp, Tom Dubois, and Mike Lowery made up Ground Team 1 while Rick Blevins and John Keel, were Ground Team 2. The two teams were on the road from 2310L until around 0500L trying to detect a beacon but were unable.

At 0259L, Lewis County was contacted and an update was provided indicating that there were only three persons aboard the aircraft, Jim Londo and his brother Robert and Robert's 14 year old son Kaiser.

Around 0800L - Civil Air Patrol's Washington Wing Director of Operations was notified of the open mission and a target time was set to activate the Wing for expanded search operations if needed.

Around 0830L Sunday morning, Tom Jensen of Washington Air Search and Rescue received a phone call from a friend of the Londos indicating that they were organizing and impromptu search of their own. Tom advised them against this due to the safety problems this introduces to the situation. The caller indicated that they would not be held back and were preparing to launch several small helicopters into the search area.

By 0900L another WASAR aircraft was enroute to the search area to assist the other two aircraft that had been flying since first light. By this time the weather situation was still Marginal VFR or worse. Kelso was reporting winds west at 5, Temp 63, dew point 59, sky condition Scattered at 1,400'/Broken at 2,000' and overcast above with heavy rain showers.

At 0932L the crew of N8714T, Chuck Williams/Jardy Dawson/and Paul Donnelly, reported a sighting of smoke in some trees, sheered tree tops and red and white debris around the site. The location was approximately 5 miles east of Silver Lake at N47 17 09.68 / W 122 41 14.72. The search area was no longer in Lewis County!! The information was immediately relayed to Cowlitz County who then directed their SAR Teams to the location. The MAST helicopter was also preparing to launch from Fort Lewis at this time. After having trouble opening gates that blocked the roads in the private timber land, the Ground Teams arrived on scene. Two victims were found at the scene and one person tried to walk out leaving plane parts as a trail. He was later returned to the site by persons driving around the logging roads that morning. The pilot died on the scene and the passengers were airlifted out to a local hospital. The cause of the accident is under investigation by the FAA and NTSB.

It is with great pleasure that I congratulate the crew of 14T, Chuck Williams/Jardy Dawson/and Paul Donnelly, for the life saving efforts they put forth. Because of them, two individuals are still alive today. They are being credited with two SAVES each by the Washington Department of Transportation - Aviation Division. Saves number 6 and 7 for 2004.

In addition, I congratulate and thank Chris & Mac MacSpadden, Dave and Sally Luse, Rick Knapp, Tom Dubois, Mike Lowery, Rick Blevins, John Keel, Terri & Leigh Viertel, Tom Jensen and Rich Alldredge for their outstanding efforts and dedication to Search and Rescue in Washington State on this mission. These individuals have demonstrated what cooperation, effective training, and selflessness can accomplish.

Many thanks as well to King County Sheriffs Department Aviation Unit, Lewis County Sheriffs Office/Lewis County Search and Rescue, Cowlitz County Sheriffs Office/Cowlitz County Search and Rescue, Silver Star Search and Rescue, and the 832nd Med Co, MAST unit, and the Washington Emergency Management Division Duty Officers
