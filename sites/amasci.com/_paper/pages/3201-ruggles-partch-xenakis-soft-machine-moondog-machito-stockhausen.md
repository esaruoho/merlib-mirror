<span id="Ruggles"></span>

![Carl Ruggles](ruggles.jpg)

**Carl Ruggles (1876-1971) spent 5 years honing [Sun-treader](treader.ram), (Real Audio) his orchestral masterpiece. A roiling eruptive jagged sea of sound, Sun-treader sings with noble clarity. Ruggles forsook composing in the 1950s to devote more time to painting. For more, start with a short [biography](http://www.luminet.net/~rlindner/ruggles.html) and then visit Thomas Herlin's [Ruggles page](http://www.diac.com/~herlin/ruggles.html) which is packed with links and an excellent bibliography. This excerpt, from Deutsche Gramophone's outstanding CD release DG 429 860-2, was performed by the Boston Symphony conducted by Michael Tilson-Thomas. Now why won't Sony/CBS release Tilson-Thomas' *complete* Ruggles on CD?**

<span id="Partch"></span>

**Harry Partch (1901-1974), fed up with Western music, incinerated his compositions in a pot-bellied stove in 1930. Creating, adapting and constructing many beautiful [instruments](http://www.eyeneer.com/CCM/Composers/Partch/Instruments/index.html) tuned to a 43-tone octave, his compositions feature shimmering glissandi, ritualistic rhythms, hobo sayings and much that remains beyond my feeble powers of prose. Try an excerpt from Partch's [The Bewitched](bewitch.ram) (Real Audio) recently remastered and reissued on CRI. I also heartily recommend [INNOVA's](http://www.composersforum.org/noframe/innova/partch.html) magnificent 4 CD set, *Enclosure Two*. To hear what the instruments sound like visit the invaluable [Corporeal Meadows](http://corporeal.com), a site dedicated to the life and works of Harry Partch. You can also visit the [Harry Partch Special Collection Archive](http://www.music.sjsu.edu/Partch/harry_partch.html) which has some good pictures, John Loffink's admirable [Partch site](http://freeweb.pdq.net/jloffink/partch.html), and [PARTCH](http://hamp.hampshire.edu/~ngzF92/partch/partch.html), which presents material from *Genesis of a Music*. CBS should be compelled to release *The World of Harry Partch* and *Delusion of the Fury* (with the accompanying instrumental record!) on CD.**

<span id="Xenakis"></span>

![Iannis Xenakis](xenakis.jpg)

**Iannis Xenakis has probed the frontiers of acoustic and electronic music for decades. He was the first to explore the musical possibilities of granular synthesis. Listen to the gloaming granular textures of [La Legende d'Eer](legende.ram) (Real Audio) from the cd *La Legende D'Eer* MO 782058. Recently, I unearthed few Xenakis resources on the WWW: you can read an intriguing [conversation](http://www.rogerreynolds.com/xenakis1.html) with Xenakis, peruse an incomplete [list](http://157.27.1.40:80/germano/eme/compos/xenaia.htm) of his works, wade through some [citations](http://www.mala.bc.ca/~mcneil/xenakis.htm) or read a brief [bio](http://www.u.arizona.edu/~jkandell/music/xenakis.html). [Tell me](mailto:foont@eskimo.com) about any Xenakis links I may have missed. Also, don't miss *Electro-Acoustic Music* now [on CD](http://www.cdemusic.org/electronic/emf.html). Hurray!**

<span id="softs">Only the Miles Davis groups of 1968 - 1975 surpassed the soft machine in jazz-rock.</span> ![the soft machine, circa 1970](softs2.jpg)

\
This glorious excerpt of [Slightly All The Time](slightly.ram) (Real Audio) is from *Third* - CBS CGK 30339. The "softs" reached their apogee in 1972, and afterwards succumbed to the trends of the times: cop-show jazz, synth-fusion, etc. Here's a [photo](softs.html) of the band circa 1968-9. Their early albums through *Fourth* are all worth investigating, along with the Peel Sessions and the Live At the BBC discs. Find out more in this informative [band history](http://www.alpes-net.fr/~bigbang/bands/soft.html) or start with a detailed [account](http://www-2.roughguides.com/rock/entries/entries-s/SOFT_MACHINE.html) of their early days. You can also read about them in [Facelift](http://www.ponk.com/flindex.htm), an on-line 'zine devoted to the Canterbury scene. Fans of soft machine's peak years may also want to check out these intriguing [reissues](http://members.aol.com/Cuneiform4/softmachine.html). If you're a Robert Wyatt fan, visit this [tasty site](http://www.progrock.net/Bands/Gongwyatt/wyatt.home.html). While I am pleased that CBS saw fit to reissue *Third* on cd, why didn't they do a digital remix? Such a seminal album deserves better sonic treatment!

<span id="Moondog"></span>

![Moondog](moondog.jpg)

**The visionary Moondog blends canons, street poetry and elegant off-kilter rhythms into his compositions. A popular denizen of New York City's street life, he recited his poetry on the corner of 54th Street and 6th Avenue in Manhattan. His magnificent [Theme](moondog.ram) (Real Audio) from his eponymous two-fer CD - CBS MK 44994 offers a good introduction to his style. I don't have the strength to post his most incredible work, Symphonique \#3, which leaves me exhausted after each listen.**

<span id="Schoen"></span>

**Arnold Schoenberg (1874-1951) remains misunderstood more than 40 years after his death. Aside from his legacy of beautiful compositions, he taught many of this century's most influential composers: Anton Webern, Alban Berg and others: even John Cage took a lesson or two! I'm currently deciding which clip to feature, so please be content with visiting the [official Schoenberg web site](http://www.schoenberg.org), which is packed with photos, documents, a list of works and other items or settle for a [quickie bio](http://w3.rz-berlin.mpg.de/cmp/schonberg.html).**

![Arnold Schoenberg](schoen.jpg)

<span id="Machito"></span>

![Machito](machito.jpg)

**Tito Puente may be the King of Latin jazz, but Machito (1908-1984) reigns as Eternal Emperor - Lord of Screaming Brass and Thundering Percussion. Machito's Afro-Cuban Jazz Orchestra dazzled audiences for decades with the dextrous arrangments of Mario Bauza, Rene Hernandez and others. Their hair-splitting ensemble work fused steely precision with incorrigible bop-styled swing. [Wild Jungle](jungle.ram), (Real Audio) from *Kenya* or *Latin Soul Plus Jazz* is available on Palladium PCD-104 or preferably the grittier Caliente CD CHARLY 149, respectively. I demand a boxed set!!!!**

<span id="Stock"></span>

**Karlheinz Stockhausen's [official Web page](http://www.stockhausen.org) is packed with nifty articles, features, and some sound clips, so get thee thither! You can also visit the [Licht page](http://www.geocities.com/Vienna/2047/records.html) for information on the opera *Licht,* read reviews of some recent Stockhausen cds and more! You can also get a clip of one of his electronic masterpieces, [Gesang der Junglinge](http://arts.ucsc.edu/ems/music/music/landmarks/stockhausen.html). A leader of the post-war avant-garde, he pioneered serial techniques in electronic music, the combination of live instruments with electronics and multimedia performance. Stockhausen is unduly neglected here in America, so I urge you to unearth his records at your local library and write for his *free* catalog:**
