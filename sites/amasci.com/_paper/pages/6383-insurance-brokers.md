**Why we care: America's faith in the free enterprise system has been seriously shaken by corporate scandals in the headlines, from Enron and Tyco to Shell's overstating its petroleum reserves. The insurance industry is essential to the well-being of our nation, but it has come under scrutiny for potential scandals over conflicts of interest and self-serving practices. The insurance industry is highly regulated with the goal of providing <u>consumer choice</u>, but two practices have aroused concern, one called Placement Service Agreements and the other called Leveraging. While it is rare for the Center to support government regulation, it is also typical of the Center to <u>examine each issue on its own merits</u>. Insurance companies have sensed public unease over these two practices and some have called for closer public scrutiny. <u>CDFE su</u>pp<u>orts the insurance industry</u> in its effort to avoid scandals and provide the best service possible to the public.**

 

**<u><span style="FONT-WEIGHT: bold; FONT-SIZE: 10pt; FONT-FAMILY: Arial">I</span><span style="font-weight: bold; font-size: 10pt">SSUE</span></u>**

<span style="font-size: 10pt; color: navy; font-family: Verdana">T</span><span style="font-size: 10pt; font-family: Verdana">he Washington Legal Foundation recently wrote to attorneys-general and insurance departments in New York and California requesting that those offices investigate and consider regulating questionable practices. </span>

<span style="font-size: 10pt; color: windowtext; font-family: Verdana"></span><span style="color: windowtext; font-family: Verdana">         </span>Recently scrutinized in the *<span style="FONT-STYLE: italic">Financial Times</span>*, **<span style="FONT-WEIGHT: bold; COLOR: windowtext">Placement Service Agreements,</span>** or PSAs, <span style="COLOR: windowtext">inherently </span>compromise a broker's duty to <span style="COLOR: windowtext">act in the cli</span>ent's best interests by encouraging brokers to refer business only to companies that pay them contingency fees. As a result, customers run the risk of paying more for coverage they do not need, and <span style="COLOR: windowtext">potentially </span>receiv<span style="COLOR: windowtext">e</span> less in terms of overall policy benefits. PSAs also referred to as revenue sharing and contingency agreements in the mutual funds industry have already drawn criticism and have been investigated by the SEC.

<span style="font-size: 10pt; font-family: Verdana"></span>         <span style="font-size: 10pt; font-family: Verdana">A related practice, known as **<span style="FONT-WEIGHT: bold">leveraging or tying</span>** has also drawn fire from consumer groups.  Heres how it works: Insurance companies get referrals from brokers who can also sell reinsurance (insurance for insurance companies).  In leveraging, brokers will threaten to withhold referrals, unless an insurance company agrees to fill its reinsurance needs through that broker.  This can lead to inflated prices for reinsurance (which get passed along to insurance customers) and a market that unfairly shuts out competition (because captive companies cannot search for the best prices).  </span>
