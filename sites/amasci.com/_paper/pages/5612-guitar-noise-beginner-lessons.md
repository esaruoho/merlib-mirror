<td width="100%"><h1 id="beginner-lessons">BEGINNER LESSONS</h1>
<p>Just getting started? No problem! We have lessons covering guitar basics (holding your guitar, tuning it), buying a guitar, learning chords, reading tab and more.</p>
<p><a href="../columns/beginner1.html"><strong>Absolute Beginner Part 1: Chords</strong></a><br />
<span class="smauthorname">By David Hodge</span><br />
You've got a guitar but you haven't got a clue? We've been getting a lot of email about this and before I go off on some rant about how it takes more brains to figure out how to use a computer than it does to play a guitar, let me introduce our new series: Absolute Beginners. Part one gives you a general introduction and teaches you how to play eight chords that will get you playing in no time flat. If you can turn on your computer and navigate the web, then playing the guitar should be just as easy!</p>
<p><a href="20020912.html"><strong>Busy Beginner on a Budget</strong></a><br />
<span class="smauthorname">By Blue (Cathi Pearce)</span><br />
What annoys me most is how much discouragement there is out there for folks wanting to take the plunge and learn to play guitar. If you're someone other than a young man expressing a wish to learn, you can expect to run a gauntlet of criticism, confusion and plain bad advice.</p>

&#10;<p>Without a doubt our most frequently asked question concerns buying equipment. We have already answered this question many different times in many different ways. Whether you need information on certain brands and models, or you have a question about new or used gear, shopping online, choosing effects and accessories you will find the answer somewhere here.</p>
<p><a href="../columns/2000/20000328.html"><strong>The Rites of Spring</strong></a><br />
<span class="smauthorname">By David Hodge</span><br />
Buying a new or used guitar can be an emotional ordeal, especially if you haven't the faintest idea of what you are looking for. This is why it's important to do a bit of thinking and investigating before even setting foot inside a music shop. It doesn't matter if it's going to be your first or fortieth guitar, take a moment to gather your thoughts.</p>
<p><a href="../amps/20010919.html"><strong>How To Buy A Guitar Amp</strong></a><br />
<span class="smauthorname">By Jeremy Ledford</span><br />
This is the first installment of two articles that will try to help you choose an amplifier. I will focus on beginning players today and intermediate players in the next installment. You go into the store and buy it right? Yes, it could be that simple. But, without proper information, one could end up making a very costly mistake.</p>
<p><a href="../bass/20010708.html"><strong>How To Buy A Bass Guitar</strong></a><br />
<span class="smauthorname">By Dan Lasley</span><br />
This column will review some of the numerous things that go into the design of a bass guitar. Some matter more than others, and different people have different tastes. Hopefully, when you are done, you’ll have an idea of what to look for the next time you’re in the mood for a new bass.</p>

<p><span id="basics"></span></p>
<h2 id="guitar-basics">Guitar Basics</h2>
<p><a href="../articles/20020525.html"><strong>Stringing and Cleaning Your Guitar</strong></a><br />
<span class="smauthorname">By Alan Horvath</span><br />
I don't know why anyone would string a guitar by putting the end of the string through the tuner-hole, and then winding and cranking and winding and cranking ... Have you ever seen the wad of string that sits around the peg of a guitar strung that way? That is *not* the reason they give you all that extra string.</p>
<p><a href="tuning/20000801.html"><strong>How To Tune A Guitar</strong></a><br />
<span class="smauthorname">By Paul Hackett</span><br />
Tuning a guitar is the single most important concept for a beginning guitarist to learn. If your guitar is not in tune, you will never sound good.</p>
<p><a href="classical/20000801.html"><strong>Posture</strong></a><br />
<span class="smauthorname">By Logan L. Gabriel</span><br />
How many of you really understand the importance of proper posture, it's effect on your playing and risk of injury?  More and more I see beginners sit in the way their favorite player does, without regards to their body.</p>
<p> </p>
<h2 id="playing">Playing</h2>
<a href="../beginner/index.html"><strong>Easy Songs for Beginners</strong></a><br />
<span class="smauthorname">By David Hodge</span><br />
If you already have a grasp of guitar basics (holding your guitar, tuning it) then the next step is to start building a repetoire of songs. This series of lessons will teach you a bit of music theory and technique while you learn to play some pretty easy and well known songs. For best results you really should begin with the first lesson and work your way up.<br />
&#10;<p><a href="../maximum/20020405.html"><strong>How to Improvise: A Guide for Beginning Guitarists</strong></a><br />
<span class="smauthorname">By Darrin Koltow</span><br />
There's nothing magical about improvising, except the great feeling you get when you hit the right notes. In this article we show you how to hit those notes.</p>
<p><a href="riffs/20000718.html"><strong>Easy Guitar Riffs</strong></a><br />
<span class="smauthorname">By Paul Hackett</span><br />
My fascination with guitar riffs goes back a long way. I was still only playing the air-guitar and washing dishes in a restaurant when I ordered a tape from TV called Guitar Rock. This lesson contains examples of some of the most popular and easiest rock guitar riffs, including: Smoke on the Water, All Right Now, Layla, Purple Haze and more.</p>
<p><a href="riffs/20000830.html"><strong>Easy Guitar Riffs Of The 80s</strong></a><br />
<span class="smauthorname">By Paul Hackett</span><br />
Some things get better with age. Old sneakers, a well worn pair of jeans, classic cars, art, cheese, and sometimes even music. Oldies, retro, classic rock - whatever you call the music that you are nostalgic for - is likely to be a perennial favourite. Whatever can be recycled and resold eventually will be. And history will always repeat itself.</p>
<p><a href="riffs/20020112.html"><strong>Easy Riffs of the 90s</strong></a><br />
<span class="smauthorname">By Matt Heger</span><br />
While many guitarists complain about the lack of solos in 90s alternative rock, they contain many intense and memorable riffs. These songs have become essential to any guitarist's playing library.</p>
<p><a href="19990207.html"><strong>Getting the Most Out Of Your Practice</strong></a><br />
<span class="smauthorname">By Jimmy Hudson</span><br />
Never use tablature as a critch again. Follow Jimmy Hudson's exclusive lesson on Getting the Most Out of Your Practice and you will be well on your way to success with your musical goals.</p>
<p><a href="../articles/20000822.html"><strong>Reaching the Limits</strong></a><br />
<span class="smauthorname">By Abel Petneki</span><br />
Tried everything but still can't play that solo no matter how much you practice? This seems to be a common problem for beginners - lets try a different approach. The problem might not be you; it could actually be your guitar.</p>
<p><a href="../articles/20020309.html"><strong>So You Want To Play Guitar, Huh?</strong></a><br />
<span class="smauthorname">By Alan Horvath</span><br />
My desire was so strong that you couldn't stop me from learning if you tried! What else is there but desire? Can anyone teach you without it? Well, I'm assuming since you're here, you're of the same cut ... and are here, looking for ways of learning some stuff on your own.</p>
