<td data-bgcolor="#FFF9F4"><strong>T</strong>he word “marriage” packs a lot of meaning. Because it contains two widely divergent definitions, it has also been widely misunderstood. This is due to that fact that marriage is both a <em>legal</em> as well as a <em>ceremonial</em> event.
<p><em>Ceremonial</em> marriages, sometimes called “weddings,” are performed by churches, temples, and within other social events. They can be tailored to reflect religious and philosophical views.</p>
<p><em>Legal</em> marriage — only obtained through a license in the United States — is entirely the responsibility of each state. This legal status has very specific rules and procedures affecting between 120-300 rights and responsibilities, depending upon one’s resident state.</p>
<p>On the federal level, more than 1,049 rights and responsibilities are triggered. [Please see <a href="mar-fed.html"><strong>U.S. Federal Laws for the Legally Married</strong></a>.</p>
<p>[The federal law disingenuously called <a href="doma.html">DoMA</a> tramples on the rights of states to decide the criteria of legal marriage. No other federal law has ever defined who can get a marriage license. Nor has it ever regulated who’s marriage license it will honor or disallow.]</p>
<p>Opposite-sex couples may combine both the legal and ceremonial events, but they are entirely distinct under our nation’s constitutional separation of church and state.</p>
<p>In order to simulate only a small fraction of these rights, a same-sex couple would need to spend as much as $3,000 for legal and related fees. And these documents need to be renewed about every four years. Wills, powers of attorney and relationship agreements give same-sex couples some say in the conduct of their personal affairs in the event of major life changes.</p>
<p>Because same-sex couples are considered strangers in the eyes of the law — no matter how long one is in a committed relationship — same-sex couples must go to extraordinary lengths to protect their families. [Please see our <a href="protect.html"><strong>Legal Precautions to Protect Your Relationship</strong></a>.]</p>
<p>And these documents can all be challenged by blood relatives. In many jurisdictions, the relatives would have the weight of the law — and the prejudice of judges — on their side. Also, while the documents are as much as couples are allowed to legally create, they still cannot cover, for instance, the rights to:</p>
<ul>
<li>recover damages based on injury to a partner,</li>
<li>have joint custody over the children of one partner,</li>
<li>receive survivor’s benefits,</li>
<li>have a foreign partner become a U.S. citizen,</li>
<li>live in housing for married persons or neighborhoods zoned “family only,”</li>
<li>collect unemployment benefits after leaving a job to relocate because of a partner’s job move, or</li>
<li>refuse to testify against one’s partner in court.</li>
</ul>
[Please see our extensive <a href="mar-list.html"><strong>Marriage Benefits List</strong></a>.]
<p>Because same-sex couples love each other, they naturally want to be able to care for each other. This could all be accomplished with but a single legal document — usually available for about $35 — that could settled definitively the right of same-sex couples to protect their families.</p></td>
