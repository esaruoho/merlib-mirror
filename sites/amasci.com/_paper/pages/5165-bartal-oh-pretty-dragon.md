<td data-valign="top" width="580"><h1 id="oh-pretty-dragon">Oh, Pretty Dragon</h1>
by Senior Harper Apprentice <a href="../bartal.html">Bartal</a>
<p><em>(Sung to the tune of Roy Orbison's "Oh, Pretty Woman")</em></p>
<hr />
Bartal absently tunes his gitar. "This was inspired by a friend of my father's, R'orbison, at Benden Weyr," he says. "It's a tale of feeling inadequate and how that can't always stop people from appreciating who you are in spite of any failings." As he pauses to look around at his listeners, he says "I call it 'Oh, Pretty Dragon.'"
<p>The gitar in Bartal's hands emits a steady, slow-tempo backdrop as he plays the introduction. At the end of the first line of song, he starts to sing, with a mellow, crooning voice, the first verse:</p>
<p><em>"Pretty dragon, creeling in such distress<br />
Pretty dragon, the kind I won't Impress<br />
Pretty dragon<br />
</em></p>
<blockquote>
I don't believe you, you're not the truth<br />
I could not look good enough for you..."
</blockquote>
<p>Bartal's face stretches into an expression of pure longing. He growls more than sings the word, "Mercy!" like a call for help, but it is soon drowned by a repetitive eight-note phrase in the lower register on his gitar.</p>
<p>Bartal quiets down the gitar, and his voice cuts above the instrumentation. He continues his insistent sound of longing as he sings,</p>
<p><em>"Pretty dragon, would you pardon me<br />
Pretty dragon, I could not help but be<br />
in your path</em></p>
<blockquote>
You were as sure as can be<br />
Could you be lonely just like me?
</blockquote>
<p>Bartal waits a moment, and then emits a growl-like sound, though it resembles the softer, more expressive &lt;&gt; of a young dragon. His gitar kicks it with the eight-note phrase again.</p>
<p><em>"Pretty dragon, stopped on by<br />
Pretty dragon, whirled and whirled its eyes<br />
Pretty dragon, gave her heart to me!</em></p>
<p>Bartal strums more forcefully on his gitar strings, the major chording evoking a sense of relief and joy, which he echoes with a soft smile.</p>
<p>Bartal's fingers move swiftly across the frets before returning to the soft, easy tune of the other verses. He sings,</p>
<p><em>"Green dragon named Ognath<br />
Pretty dragon, call me T'lay<br />
Pretty dragon, say you'll stay with me!</em></p>
<blockquote>
'Cause I need you ... I'll treat you right<br />
Come with me young one ... curl up toniii-iii-iiigh..."
</blockquote>
<p>Bartal's gitarwork becomes more insistent as his voice continues to sing the end of the last line. His eyes closed, wistful.</p>
<p>Bartal sings some more, his face a picture of relief.</p>
<p><em>"Pretty dragon, didn't walk on by<br />
Pretty dragon, even so you made me cry<br />
Pretty dragon</em></p>
<blockquote>
Don't walk away babe ... okay?<br />
This is the way it must be, okay?<br />
I guess you need some meat on a plate<br />
Oil on your skin -- it'll feel great<br />
We'll be fighting Thread, just you wait<br />
Because you'll always be with me ..."
</blockquote>
<p>The catchy eight-note phrase is played first softly, underneath the previous verses, and then louder and a little quicker as he reaches the climax of the song. He repeats,</p>
<p><em>"Yeah you'll always be with me ...</em></p>
<p>Oooh-Oooh ... Pretty dragon."</p>
<p>The last two words are said quickly and with emphasis, echoing the gitar's final notes. Bartal hushes the gitar and looks up, a dreamy look in his eyes and an easy smile adorning his face.</p>
