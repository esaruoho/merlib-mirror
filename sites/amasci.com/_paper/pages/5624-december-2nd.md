The List of Many Things!

Here's where we start to see the car coming back to life even more...

I have a list of about 83 things now and we'll tackle those from this point forward...

**November 18th, Tuesday 2003:** Home sick with a fever today but laying on you back all day can make you itchy so I did stumble out into the garage and fabricated a lower alternator bracket to replace the lower generator bracket.  I used 1/4 inch steel to ensure it will hold well.  Buying a fan belt is not as easy as you might think.  **PARTS CHALLENGE! ** Okay here we go...I am looking for a lower fan belt for the Edsel, it would be the same size as the original since the alternator and generator are in the same spot and have roughly the same pulley size.  So we off to find a 42 inch (or so) fan belt.  Napa didn't go that far back however they were willing to let me look through their belts and find the right size.  Schuck's didn't have anything but metric sizes so after some quick math, I now needed a 1300cm fan belt.  Their books also didn't go that far back and they wouldn't let me look through their belts, they seemed to busy to help.  Autozone who have great people entertained the kids with cartoon voices and went out of their way to find a belt that would fit the car, they plowed through a number of theirs and found one for me that worked perfect!  I like those guys.  So I picked up a **KellySpringfield 17435, \#13AV1105** and it worked great.

**November 19th, Wednesday 2003:** Home sick with a fever still!  Sheesh.  My daughter felt better and she was able to go to school so I guess by tomorrow I should be okay too.

**November 22nd, Saturday 2003:** Feeling better and ready to work, I tackled cleaning the door regulators for the power windows.  The person back in the 70's that sand blasted the car (somewhat) filled the regulators with a lot of particulate and these bad boys were fairly stuck...not now, I was able to remove the regulators with little hassle and while leaving the windows in the car.  I ran new wiring to the windows using the stock switches after a good cleaning and they work great both up and down.  The large red wire (10 gauge) with blue stripe lengthways supplies each switch with main power that allows the local switch to function when used...Green-Red down, Green-Yellow up and black is grounded just like the original wiring.  I found a guy on the internet that has a compass for the car since the original is gone, leaving behind a mounting plate only, and he's sending that my way in a day or two so my dash will be 100% done, yeay!

**November 23rd, Sunday 2003: ** Didn't do much today...I tucked and zip stripped the wires neatly under the dashboard so they are out of the way.  I am going to use the power window leads left and right from the Painless Wiring harness to work the front power windows.  The rear windows need a circuit for each and Painless smartly supplies two power leads for power door locks, since the Edsel has manuals these will work perfectly for the rear two windows with just the addition of a few feet of wire.  So far, that Painless Wiring Harness has been the best investment for the car I could have hoped to toss at it.

**November 26th, Wednesday 2003: ** I ran to a local parts store known as Bonney Lake Auto Parts, where I have never done a parts challenge just because they have treated me wrong in the past...but they had a pulley to fit the stock crank pulley so I picked that up, now I can get the power steering pump hooked up.  Three bolts later the pulley was on and in perfect position to accept a new belt.  Also, today arrive the compass, it looks great!  The chrome is not the best and is a little pitted on the bezel however the gauge itself works great and has plenty of fluid and no leak.  G'night!

**November 29th, Saturday 2003:**  I found a local Radiator shop that are willing to take a hard look at the two radiators I have for the car.  They both look used but not bad, but visuals on a radiator are nothing compared to flow and cooling ability let alone the infamous pressure test.  So with that in mind, Auburn Radiator and Muffle is going to check them and see which is usable if either of them are usable at all...so I am hoping that at least one is good enough to rebuild.  I don't know when they will call with their assessment but I expect a few days.  I cleaned the rear window regulators today too, these were also very clogged with particulate from that sandblasting job 30 years ago...with some work, they are going up and down just fine!  Almost done with the interior wiring, now I just have the power seat to work on...oh and thanks to the fan that sent me the update on the joystick question...it's for the power seat...this will be interesting to get working hehehe.

**November 30th, Sunday 2003: ** **PARTS CHALLENGE! ** Okay here we go again...I am looking for a lower fan belt for the Edsel to go from the crank to the power steering unit, round trip must be between 35 and 37 inches...AutoZone was able to pop this part up quickly...well, I had to actually look through their rack with the help of the guy there and got the right one...so the lower belt driving the power steering unit was a **KellySpringfield 17390, \#13AV0990** and like the previous one, it fit great and gets me another step towards starting the engine.  I will have to record the sound of the first start and share that on the site, should be fun!

**December 1st, Monday 2003:** Today on the way home from work I picked up the lower and upper radiator hoses from Napa, they had them in stock and there for me, and they were a perfect fit. Summit Racing sent me an electric fan and temp sensor to mount against the radiator. **Auburn Muffler and Radiator** in Washington did a great job on the radiator and at a great price, good folk, ask for Josh or Tom: 253.979.1979 (yeah, lots of 9's). So with all that on I began to fill the radiator and of course, I began to hear splashing, the tell tail sign that somewhere along the way something went wrong. I grabbed a flashlight and as I was about to look for where the leak was coming from, the power went out because of the storm outside. So even with those odds and in the dark, I grumbled and began to take in what I needed to do to get the water pump off and see what's happening. It looked to me like the plate that acts as the backing on the pump with a seal was leaking...I would start there. Pulled the fan belts, and four bolts holding the water pump and the two hoses (one to the manifold and the one to the heater core) and it was off it ten minutes...a quick view revealed nothing however, the backing plate was the culprit so I pulled it off, turns out the folks that I had rebuild the water pump or rather exchange it since my core was poor, didn't put any gasket sealer on the paper gasket in the water pump so it was leaking easily...wha la, off to Freddies for a tube of Copper RTV (now around 10:45pm). I had to re-make the gaskets for the pump and I keep a bag of various gasket material around for just such an event. Made the gaskets, coated them generously and re-installed the brand new pump...I'll have to let this cure for a couple of days though...I am tired and it's after midnight now. G'night!

**December 5th, Friday 2003:** Well...I ended up going with a normal fan for the car, turns out that I had a few pangs of technical angst and felt that I didn't want to change out the manual fan so I will head off tomorrow for a parts challenge to obtain a fan, spacer, and four stainless bolts to hold the mess together hehehe.  The engine holds water now, it's filled and ready to go.  I also added an impromptu fuel line so I can give the car a start sooner rather than later...hehehe.  Later this evening we also had the chance to compete in the Boy Scout pinewood derby car races and my boy took second place, would have been first but we lost some weights in the next to last race and had to run light, what a difference that made, and my daughter took first place overall in the siblings races, her car pulled out ahead of everyone's easily on each race, and yes, the kids helped cut sand, and paint the cars.  G'night!

**December 6th, Saturday 2003:** Installed the transmission lines today and tightened up the filler neck and added enough fluid to get things going, about nine quarts...I also added enough oil to meet the recommended 6 quarts.  After spending the day in a Cub Scout parade I was able to get back to the car and completed the window regulator cleaning for the passenger side...here is a great tip, **!!!TIP!!! WINDOW GLASS: ** when putting the window back in, pre-load the window slots with the assembled retailers (four pieces make a retainer, the inner washer, the spring, the concave washer, and then the outside cylinder with it's built in edge for the window track...put these four pieces together, lube them and slip it into the track ahead of time.  manually raise the window regulator to about 7/8ths of the way up...at this point insert the window and slowly lower it onto the pins of the regulator.  From here start with the rear pin...reach your hand up into the door and push the arm with it's pin against the inner side of the door (it will be easy at this height and jockey the pin into the retainer that has already been slipped into the track and press it into place.  Now do the same for the front retainer...there is a convenient bulge in the door metal that allows this to work for the front retainer.  Poof, very quick and very very easy...enjoy this bit of a trick, certainly beats trying for a LONG time to do this on the pin itself instead of pre-loading.\

**December 31st, Saturday 2003: ** I am finally getting the site updated today and I need to focus my efforts on the remaining items left to work on for the car...my list of 83 is down to around 50 or so now but without money, it is hard to get motivated and see the car coming to life.  Ahha!  I will tackle the skeleton for the power top today...thankfully I have a box under the bench that has a golden set of perfect arms and doohickeys for a new convertible skeleton.  You can see in some of the pictures that the skeleton is shabby and broken in all the important spots and needs to be replaced.  It took me about an hour and a dozen long bungee cables hanging from the rafters to do but I replaced one side then the other, they look dirty but mechanically are great...here's a tip, a hammer and a nail you would use to nail a roof tile will knock out those pins that hold the skeleton together.  I have everything done but am missing one of the chrome rivet pins that acts as a bolt at the major joint, for now I will start looking for one but the skeleton is being marked off the list...guess I need to give it a good scrubbing and enamel coating in the old beige.

**January 3rd, Saturday 2004:** All the pictures are update on the site, wow, I had some from back in October too so if you missed anything such as some of the dash restoration or door cutting activities, please take a look, they turned out good I think.  Cheers...and yes snow snow today!  We've got about six inches and growing on the ground...Close to a white Christmas but makes for a very cold experience out in the garage with the car.  Besides, I now have a new bass amp and am able to practice a bit more so that amp took my money for this month on the car...sigh...I will update here in January when I can but don't expect much this month...\*heavy sigh\*.  G'day!

**January 9th, Friday 2004: ** So with a few bucks in my pocket today, I trundled off to the local Napa for a few quarts of Electrolyte or Sulfuric Acid to some.  This caustic liquid is added to the dry battery, allowed to percolate for a bit then you charge the battery.  I put the large slender batter on the deck, opened up the "Wine Box" of acid, yes, it came in a large box with a long hose you could take out to pour the acid, similar to a Box of Wine in some restaurants.  I filled the cells as described from Classicautobattery.com, where I got the battery from, and just put the charger on it at 10amps for an overnight cook.  So tomorrow we'll see how it works and weather or not the starter is bad or just draws a LOT of amps.  G'night!

**January 10th, Saturday 2004:** Today I feel ambitious so I began with Cheerios and some tea, watched some Powerblock TV on Spike TV and dropped the battery into the car after putting on the nice looking bright green vent caps.  The battery looks great in there, antique looking with a modern rubberized case, very very cool.  I painted the raised letters for that attention getting look, turned out good.  I re-installed the starter just a bit ago and tightened everything up (almost, you'll find out...).  I grabbed the kids playschool recorder because where the heck and I gonna get a microphone tape recording unit around here...I got in the car, stepped on the brake and the engine cranked over perfectly except a bit slow...I tried and tried...ran that poor new and freshly charged battery down...here's the sound from that:

[Cranking the Ford 410 Engine Over \#1, recorded courtesy of a preschool kids Playschool Toy recorder.](edselpics/13_crank_over1.mp3)

So with a sign, I began checking wiring, first thing to check is to see if you are getting spark to the plugs.  The wife helped out and cranked it over while I had the \#1 plug out and touched to a grounded source (don't use the batter for this).  I lit up very brightly so I knew I was getting spark...here's the catch.  I checked each plug to make sure the wires were on good and I found that during the engine build, I only finger tightened the drivers plugs and loosely at that.  I tightened them up and thought I would try it again.  Here are the results of that:

[Cranking the Ford 410 Engine Over \#2, recorded courtesy of a preschool kids Playschool Toy recorder.](edselpics/13_crank_over2.mp3)

So with a good solid turn over and rumble from the engine I was ecstatic and very happy.  The engine cranked over on the low battery smoothly, especially now that the cylinder pressure was even across the engine ... doh!  And of course, the engine fired on the first crank!!!!  Yes!  We've covered another great milestone and thanks for all your emails of support as this has been going on.  Stay tuned (is that an outdated clich or what) as I try to get through this month on little projects that don't cost much.  I will keep you posted and have a great day, happy motoring!  ... maybe this will be on the road for a test by summer...muwahahaha!
