## Done to Death

\

Supposedly, when you're a kid, especially a teenager, you don't really have a conceptual understanding of death; you believe that you're immortal or at least pretty much untouchable. This is total bullshit. I've always had a pretty good understanding of death, at least in a physical context (the metaphysical still confuses the crap out of me). This is why I've always had a fear of falling off of bridges or being hit by cars and why I've mostly led a rather dull life; having broken a few things, I figured that dying probably hurt a lot more and you didn't get to walk away from it.

But I don't have a morbid fascination with death or dead things, like my friend Michael had, who collected dead gophers and things of that nature. I simply don't wish to become part of the collection, though I don't doubt Michael has, himself, been collected by now.

On the other hand there is Glenn, who, if told that he had a date with Death, would show up at Death's door with flowers and chocolate and charm the shroud right off the ol' reaper. Most likely he'd take Death out for dinner and dancing. I suspect he has already studied all the smoothest moves of the Dance of Death and they, collectively, would cut quite a swathe, though it might be a bit hard on the rest of the clientele.

Glenn has long searched for a nice set of Grim Reaper checks, though he now is looking for a custom credit-card with a tasteful Ars Mortis depiction since, as we know, checks are dead.

Glenn, of course, cavils at the phrase "Grim Reaper." "Why should he be grim? I mean, doesn't he *like* his work? If you're stuck doing it for all eternity, you really ought to get some satisfaction out of it." Discussion of which lead to various ideas for postcards and cartoons, such as inserting Death into Edward Hopper's famous "[*Nighthawks*](http://www.ibiblio.org/wm/paint/auth/hopper/street/hopper.nighthawks.jpg)" with a cigarette and espresso cup.

When Death wasn't Death, what would he be doing? A whole series of "Jobs of Death" were proposed, always with the ubiquitous espresso cup and cigarette (being from Seattle, we, of course, cannot imagine Death without caffeine and tobacco--after all, he's Death, so what does he care?): Death as a Gardner; Death pumping Gas in Oregon, complete with paper hat; Death as a Waiter; Death as a Commentator; Death as a Bus Driver; Death as a Cook, in which, naturally, all food would have been warmed over; Death Clerking at K-Mart.... 'Tis pity neither of us can draw.

## Pudge

\

All right, so I gained weight.... Actually it distresses me to a large degree that I'm now only a few pounds below my ultimate pudge. I'm working on reducing the bulge-factor, but I'm afraid I'm a lazy beast and getting my spreading ass into the gym is not as easy as it may seem. Actually, it's not my ass which seems to the be problem, buy my severe lack of a waist. I look great from the tits up and the knees down and I've got great arms, but that middle bit... has to go. (A few other things need a bit of an adjustment, that's all.)

I used to run until I racked the knee, which was actually rather fun and I miss it a lot (the running, you animals, not the racking). Now I spend an hour or 2 a day (when I don't fluff off) pushing weights around and elliptically going nowhere. It's very boring. I went swimming last night and discovered that it's hard to swim when you are out of breath. Wearing yourself to a frazzle with chores and errands does not count as aerobic exercise.

So, I started to consider my diet. I used to be really good about what I ate, but I've fallen off the wagon lately and taken to eating the evil french fries and having a few drinks, and even dessert, all too frequently. (And one must be very careful with those drinks, as Nautilus machines definitely qualify as the sort of "heavy equipment" one should avoid when squiffy.) The problem with being good about what I eat is that my brain and my stomach seem to have a conspiracy and a secret communication link.

Brain: She's going to start feeding us rabbit food again.

Stomach: I hate that.

Brain: All right, here's the plan, Tummy; you growl at everything you don't like and I'll convince her that she's hungry, no matter how much of that veggie stuff she puts in you.

Stomach: All right. Do you think you can persuade her that Double Chocolate Cake with whipped cream is health food?

Brain: Umm... no. She's too smart for that, but I think I'll send out a psychic message to her mother to come and visit and the stress will break her down.

Stomach: Gotta be careful, Brain. I can hear that "Oh, Gods, don't let me look like her when I'm her age" whining from here.

Brain: Yeah... could be counter-productive. I'll have to think about it. Meanwhile, start grumbling.

Stomach: Gotcha!

How much easier life would be if only I had been raised on Purina People Chow. I could switch to the low-cal type and hardly know the difference. Although my cat seems to be less than satisfied with Eukanuba's attempts to "Contrôle du poids", I suspect it is more a matter of not wanting to eat what I put in front of him than of actually caring what it tastes like (this is the cat who eats vinegar and salt potato chips, after all.) Ah, the ease of waking in the morning, having a quick cup of low-cal People Chow, then going about my day, maybe a nibble of Chow now and then as a snack (hey, everything I need to know about life I learned from ferrets: nap, eat, and hide snacks, just in case).

But no. I have to go shopping and go to restaurants and see all that yummy stuff I had better not eat. It's not fair. Stores should hide that stuff in locked cases, like cigarettes. Then we'd all be too embarrassed to ask for it. Either that or there would be a lot more chocolate smuggling going on.

Belgium, Switzerland and the Netherlands would become more publicly vilified by the US Government than Columbia. Soon we'd all be urged to turn in our Swiss watches in a show of public support for the anti-chocolate campaign. Cows would be raised on low-fat feed to produce low-fat meat and milk and Possession of Whipped Cream would get you a minimum of 10 days in jail, eating Muesli the whole time. Soon PETVe (People for the Ethical Treatment of Veggies) would be the biggest food-terrorist group in the world, attacking vegans and Californians eating salad in sidewalk cafes by shoving illicit burgers and steaks down their throats. We would be subjected to recordings of the psychic screams of grass being mowed and asparagus being harvested. Pineapples would be shown to be on the verge of group sentience, if we would only stop cutting off their heads. Amnesty for Broccoli!

Oh, the carnage! Where is the People Chow when you need it?

Shut up, Tummy....

## Time Flies....

\

Whoa.... While I wasn't paying attention, it seems I've been married for 7 years this Monday. Now, how did that happen?

It's sometimes amazing to me that I, a demanding, neurotic loon, has managed to get a mate in the first place, much less keep one, especially with the insane schedule which has befallen us over the past 3 years. There are times it has seemed more like a trip through the carnival Fun House than anything else. But I am still here, still married, and not regretting it, except for the occasional cry of "why can't I run around like a crazy single-girl?!" when I'm really depressed or bored.

But, being a crazy single-girl wasn't all that fun for me. I wanted to have someone I could count on being around when things got bad, and for a long, long time beyond the parties and the fun and even the work. Someone who would be around because they wanted to, even when I was old, crotchety, and less-than-gorgeous. And who wasn't a cat.

And I got that. I had to trade a few things, but they really don't matter that much, except when I am being very selfish. There is someone who will always be around for me and always tell me I am wonderful, pick me back up every time I land on my ass and encourage me to go out and risk landing on my ass again and not think badly of me if I *do* land on my ass again.

This married-thing, it has its bad points: like bearing the weight of someone else's expectations, desires, and dreams as well as my own, accommodating myself to schedules and necessities and personal quirks. But, over all, I think my husband has the harder job: He has to put up with me. And he doesn't seem to mind.

It must be Love....
