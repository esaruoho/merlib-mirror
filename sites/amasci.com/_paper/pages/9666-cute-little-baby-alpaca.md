<td><strong>This photograph was supplied by Pine Ridge Alpacas, their baby Alpaca is in a Kush position. When they are in a kush position...there legs fold under like a card table and they look like they are FLAT against the ground. In the Winter time when the weather is cold, this position keeps them warm. There is very little wool on the underside of Llamas or Alpacas, the long fiber from the back drapes down over them as if they were wrapped in a nice warm blanket, and hold the body heat in. Remember these animals originally lived in the Andies with snow and subzero temperatures. They are designed to handle the cold temperatures. In this country the bigger concern is not how to keep them warm in cold temperatures but how to keep them cool in warm temperatures. Heat stress is a real problem for these animals, many ranchers now shear them in the Spring to prevent heat stress related illness.</strong></td>

<td><h2 id="what-are-some-of-the-differences-in-alpacas-and-llamas">What are some of the differences in Alpacas and Llamas</h2></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><table data-border="0" width="80%" data-align="CENTER" data-cellpadding="5" data-cellspacing="10">
<colgroup>
<col style="width: 50%" />
<col style="width: 50%" />

<tr data-align="CENTER" data-valign="TOP">
<td width="40%"><strong>ALPACA</strong></td>
<td width="40%"><strong>LLAMA</strong></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><ul>
<li>Weight on the Average is 100-180 lbs. about 1/3 to 1/2 the size of a llama</li>
<li>Weight of the average newborn is 15 - 19 pounds</li>
<li>The fiber, for clothing has been the traditional use of these</li>
<li>Do not require a lot of acerage, 5 to 8 animals per acre</li>
<li>Alpacas have single births, twins are very unusual</li>
<li>They can start breeding as early as 12 months of age</li>
</ul></td>
<td><ul>
<li>Weight on the Average is 250-450 pounds, nearly twice the size of the Alpaca</li>
<li>Weight of the average newborn is 20 -35 pounds</li>
<li>Llamas were originaly used as packing animals</li>
<li>Because of the larger size, 4 to 5 animals per acre</li>
<li>Llamas also have single births, twins are very unusual</li>
<li>Usually a female isn't breed until the age of 2 years</li>
</ul></td>
