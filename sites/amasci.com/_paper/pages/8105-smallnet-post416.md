<div class="green">

There's another choice of fan unit for the model design you have -- could that be a Berkeley Skyray, or maybe a full-size plan from Model Airplane News? Anyway, HiLine sells a couple of electric- powered D-F units, with 2 1/2" OD ducts. One is called "Blue Flame Blaster", and is suitable for free flight models of 150 to 250 square inches of wing area and a weight of 8 to 12 ounces.

A higher-powered version (with the same physical size) is the "Red Flame Blaster". Andy Clancy built and flew a 2-motor R/C scale Messerschmitt Me262 with these for power. He reported that it was a mighty potent performer.

As for your PAW .03 stopping when you lanch your Dakota, that sounds to me like a "surge problem". I've always advised hand-launching Dakotas smoothly & gently, pushing them gradually into flight at their normal climbing attitude: banked to the left. Too hard of a toss not only makes the fuel surge away from the engine -- it also invites a series of power stalls and recoveries, from too much "initial launch energy". \[JW\]

Bernard Cawley \< 

 \> reports:

I just have to tell the SMALLnet guys about my most recent project, even if I steal some of my own thunder from a yet-to-be-written piece for the Ezone.

As some SMALLsters know, the LiteStik/Tiger Moth people, Grand Wing Servo-Tech (GWS), released a tiny set of mechanical retracts a couple of months ago. These are 90-degree retracts with 1/16 wire legs, a positive downlock but no uplock. They weigh almost nothing. The claimed weight is 0.98 GRAM per mechanism (not counting the legs). And, they're available from places like AeroMicro, Balsa Products, and Todd's Models for six bucks!

At that price I had to buy a set just to speculate what I would do with them.

A few weeks after I did so, Doug Binder of Mountain Models in Colorado Springs (known for the DuskStik slowflyer, at \< <http://www.mountainmodels.com> \> produced his third design. It's a low-wing four-channel sportster called the Switchback, in two versions: one with a 31.5 inch span symmetrical wing with no dihedral (called Switchback 3D); and the other with a 35-inch span wing with about 5 degrees of dihedral and an airfoil that looks suspiciously like the real Clark Y (called Switchback Sport). As designed it uses a GWS geared 280 or 370 motor, a 9-inch prop, and 8 720AAA NiMH cells.

Well, it didn't take me long to decide to put those retracts in a Switchback Sport, and an email to Doug got the project launched.

The kit is all laser-cut and for the most part can be assembled without even using pins, the fit is so good on the parts.

Fitting and actuating the retracts took some doing, because I had to angle them inboard to compensate for the dihedral; and forward to get the wheels out where they ought to be, yet into the wing when retracted. I had to use some smaller wheels (from Chris Hansen at Anything R/C) to get them into the wing.

At any rate, my Switchback Sport RG, which weighs 9.4 ounces plus battery, or 13.1 ounces with the 8-cell AAA NiMH pack, first flew on March 4th. This is a COOL airplane and capable of much more than I'm any good at doing. It is the first electric I've owned that can do consecutive OUTSIDE loops effortlessly (near the beginning of the charge, at least).

I wound up with the following equipment list:

Motor/gearbox: GWS EPS-300C-BS (4.43:1 geared Mabuchi 370) Prop: APC 9X6 slowflyer, mounted to gearbox with a neat aluminum hub I got from Todd's Models\
Battery: 8X720AAA Sanyo NiMH\
Receiver: GWS R6N (this is the 6 channel version of the tiny GWS receiver commonly used in Lite Stiks and such)\
Servos: Hitec HS-55's on elevator, rudder and retracts. GWS Pico BB on ailerons\
ESC: Great Planes C-10\

The plane is covered with the included Doculam (something else recently discussed here on this list).

I've got 6 flights on it so far, including two at a schoolyard near Doug's house in Colorado Springs on the 9th. It flies almost as well at 6000 feet above sea level as it does here in the Seattle area, with not even my customary prop change.

So far the retracts are holding up well, and it even gets off of thick grass in about 10 feet.

I've posted a couple of pictures in a thread about it on the Ezone discussion boards at \< <http://www.rcgroups.com/forums/showthread.php?s=&threadid=31317> \>

After getting this morning's posting and reading Sam Brauer's missive about his Cannonshot, I realized I hadn't told SMALLnet about the Switchback Sport RG. Now I have.

Bernard Cawley, Jr.

I have a company that is willing to mix fuels for me and would like for them to make a few gallons. However, they need to know what to mix.

<div class="Joe">

...BlitzK, here's a fuel blend that works well in Coxes: 25% castor oil, 18% to 25% nitromethane, and the rest methanol. The nitro percentage varies, depending on whether you're running Tee Dees or reed valve engines -- also the weather and the size prop you're flying with.

Tee Dees spinning small props in cool weather need the most nitro content. Black Widows turning 7-3 1/2" props in hot weather run better with lower nitro. Another factor involved is how many head gaskets are installed. The more of those you use, the higher the nitro content your engine can handle... \[JW\]

</div>

Mike Gillihan \< 

 \> wrote:

The last time I went flying, one of my radios did a funny thing. For no reason at all the rudder control became the elevator control, and the elevator control became the rudder control. This happened in the middle of the day, and no one had swapped any plugs in the receiver.

Granted, the radio was a 1992 vintage AM unit that had been hammered, rain-soaked, and fuel-soaked over the past 10 years, but still odd...

However, this left me with the opportunity to try a new radio. Last year at the SMALL gathering in Maumelle I won a \$50 gift certificate to FMA Direct at the raffle, which I used to purchase one of the new Xtreme5 receivers. The receiver impressed me, so when I got the latest issue of Flying Models and saw an advertisement for a new radio system offered by FMA, my decision was made. I ordered the "Micro Gas" system with full NiCads (about \$160 delivered). I thought others might be interest in my experience with this one (so far).

The system arrived within a week, and consisted of the compact T80RF transmitter, an Xtreme5 receiver, a 180 mAh airborne battery, two PS30 micro servos, a micro switch harness, the proper charger, and both crystals. The information on the web site says that the system ships with S80 servos, but I received the PS30's, which appear to be better quality servos. (I think they may be phasing out the S80's.)

At first brush, this system looks like a gem. The transmitter offers tri-rates (50%, 75%, 100%), elevon/V-tail mixing on channels 1 & 2 (right stick), and servo reversing on all four channels. All of these functions are accessible via recessed DIP switches on the face of the transmitter. All of these functions must be set before flight, unless one is dexterous enough to flip a switch with a small screwdriver while flying.

The transmitter is molded plastic, and very solid. The TX crystal is on the front, and came already installed. I'm already sold on the receiver, which is one of the lightest dual-conversion jobs out there. The crystal for the receiver came in a separate package within the airborne system box, and is the low-profile variety.

Be aware that the servos are tiny (0.28 ounce), and produce between 12 and 14 inch-ounces of torque. That might not be enough for a hot 1/2A application. No doubt one could substitute the more powerful PS100 mini servos (24-28 inch-ounces, 0.67 ounce weight) if one asked. As I am still developing an HLG, I'm happy with the micro servos.

Packaging was beautiful, if not overdone. The transmitter came in one box, and the airborne system in another, nestled into Styrofoam cutouts. The overall presentation was much better than expected (I'm keeping the boxes). So far this company has impressed me with their products and their service. Their site can be visited here: \< <http://www.fmadirect.com> \>

And no, I'm not receiving a kickback from FMA. I just get tickled when I get good service and products.

Mike S. Gillihan
