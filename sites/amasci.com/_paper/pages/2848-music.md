# music

*Source page: `~drmrdave/music.html`*

> Here are some of the activities that I do, explained in greater, boring detail. Yep, mine is a site of words, not pictures, or , scratch n sniff thingies....:D
>
> **MUSIC**
>
> As anyone might tell you, music is very subjective. What is noise to someone, is music to another. My musical interests vary to a wide degree. I am trying to expand my expirence, so if there is something you think might pique my interest, by all means, tell me!
>
> I do happen to me a muscian...I'm a drummer, and therefore, like bands who feature, or highlight their drummers... I like the complex, challenging drumming found in, Metallica, Rush, Creed -- those, among others. Kiss, of course, is near and dear to me hehehe :-) It is especially rewarding to me, to pick up some transcribed music from, say Metallica, and work on it -- and get to the point of being able to play it, to speed. The expertiese in which Lars throws in triplets, the thundering double bass just really does it for me :-)
>
> I am trying to expand to other styles, which is truely a challenge for me -- playing lighter, softer, limiting fills, makes me have to really think while I'm playing -- a good thing for me ha! It never hurts to be capable of being able to play several different styles -- and, in playing other styles, I will find ideas I can use that I might not have come across.
>
> I -- I am tired of beginning all these paragraphs with \>I\< heehee -- I am in a band of sorts, you would probably classifiy the stuff we do as industrial/metal type stuff. I used to be really into playing covers, but, as the guys I play with come up with original material, I am getting more and more into it...because, I can put my own signature into the playing style...I can be creative... I'll be posting tunes as we get them recorded. I do tend to pratice on my own about 2-4 times per week, and, I take drum lessons once per week -- working on getting my ability to sight read music, and to work on rudiments, which will translate into being able to get around the kit more fluidly.
>
> The Drum Instuctor I take lessons from is totally kick-ass! His name is Scott Schroedl. He can do all styles, is a great teacher (paitent, good at explaining things) and in taking the lessons, I have seen a huge improvement in my playing ability.He has transcribed many popular/famous bands muisc into drum books -- Metallica, Red Hot Chilli Peppers, to name a few. He also plays in a rock band (70's/80's/90's tunes) called Heartbreaker [(http://www.heartbreakerrockband.com)](http://www.heartbreakerrockband.com) on weekends, and in another band called Tom Green and the Blues Machine -- they do blues :-) They are both great bands to listen to.
>
> Update! The group of guys I've been playing with on and off, have recorded a demo CD, which I just got! It is cool, progressive stuff. My job is to add the acustic drum tracks to the songs...which I've been slowly working on...I'll post some stuff once I have it together...I know next to NOTHING about recording music -- I'll muddle through it somehow.hehehe.
>
> Also, I am jaming with yet another group of guys, who are into more of a metal sounding style.. these guys seem to have endless energy, and lots of ambition...we'll see what develops :) At least I get to play a few times a week...so thats a good thing.
>
> I never described the gear I use, and thought it might be of interest :) I currently play a Remo MasterTouch series drumkit with a flame finish. Its an 8 piece kit :
>
> \
> Toms : 8, 10, 12,13, 16 and 18\
> Bass : 22 inch
>
> I have the deep 14 X 7 inch snare, which is good for some stuff..But I feel in love with the Chad Smith signature snare drum, that my friend uses on his kit, so I mainly use that!
>
> My hardware is quite a mix: 2 DW double cymbal stands...Remo Tom Stands, Pearl Highhat stand and AXIS double pedal. I sit on a Rock-n-Sock throne, with backrest.
>
> I've learned alot about what I like and don't like for cymbal sounds...so, I'm still changing out my cymbals. I have currently :
>
> \
> 20 inch Paiste 802 Ride\
> 18 inch Paiste Alpha thin crash\
> 18 inch Paiste 802 crash\
> 16 inch Paiste Alpha power crash\
> 18 inch Paiste 802 china\
> 20 inch Paiste Alpha china\
> 18 inch Sabian BB pro crash\
> 16 inch Paiste Dimensions crystal crash\
> 6 inch Paiste Alpha splash\
> 8 inch Paiste 802 splash\
> 10 inch Sabian hand-hammered splash\
> 14 inch Paiste Sound Edge hihats
>
> As you can see..a wide variety of stuff. The 802's were the first set of 'decent' cymbals I got...and I reccomend them to any person who is just starting out!! They are great for the money. The Alpha's , for a little more money, have a more professional sound...the high-hats I have totally rock. The Dimensions crash is awesome...and of course, this is Paistes next to the top-of-the-line stuff...I'll be sure to upgrade to more of those as time goes on.
>
> Lets see...any boring detail I've left out ? Yep....Sticks! I play with Mainline mostly -- the big ole 620B's...these sticks are made from Kevlar...you know..the bullet resistant material they use in vests. The sticks ALWAYS feel the same, have a nice , solid feel to 'em...and boy do they last! If you are a hard player..these are for you! I've tried the Ahead sticks...made by the Easton Bat company...and they are ok, but they don't have the 'feel' that the mainlines do...and, they buzz slightly when playing a practice pad...they have a good, balanced feel to them, but overall, they just didn't feel comfortable.
>
> For heads, I use Remo Emperors on all my toms, but the 18 inch...which has a Pinestipe. I use an Aquarian Force 2 on my Bass..and a silverdot on my snare.
