## Well-Fed Research

\

It is very important to recognize the contribution and impact of Pizza on science and technology. Without leftover pizza, many an undergraduate would have starved to death and died in collegiate obscurity before they ever had a chance to make their mark on the world.

American Pizza is the perfect food, especially for geeks, because of its easy User Interface. No special equipment needed, no special approach, just grab it and stuff it in your mouth with one hand while keyboarding madly away with the other. It's good hot or cold, requires no plate that only gets in the way (since it comes with both a serving platter *and* a built-in plate), and, when you're done, you get a snazzy pizza box to stack in the corner, use as a seat, to shore up wobbly tables with. Additionally, there's the Special Rule of Pizza Incorruptibility, which, due to the curious interaction of toppings and cheese, allows pizza to be edible and entirely safe to consume, no matter how long it's been sitting in the box, be it hours or days, so long as it's not yet stiff as a board or growing mold. And it has the cachet of being the one food obsessed over by science geeks.

Not only is pizza nearly perfect for these reasons, but you can have anything you like on it and eat it at any time of day without getting sideways looks from your companions. You can have it for breakfast. You can have it for lunch. You can eat it for dinner, or even desert. In Japan, they like corn on their pizza. One of my friends likes Bacon, Lettuce and Tomato pizza. One of our local places makes chocolate-chip-cookie pizza and serves it with ice cream on top (which, being a dairy product, will stand in for cheese just fine, in this case). I've had Cheese Burger pizza and White Pizza and then there's the "gourmet" pizzas. I have a friend who claims to hate "the P-word," though she's very willing to dine on these. Yum... roast gourmet on crust *avec fromage*....

Endless variety, easy to use, and no plates to wash! How great is that? In this, Pizza is kind of like the original Sandwich, invented by the Earl of Sandwich, who didn't want to leave the card table to eat dinner. He had dinner served to him between two thick slices of bread and then ate the whole thing. Not that eating off slabs of bread was unusual, since people had been doing it for ages, but they didn't usually eat the bready dishes, themselves. They threw them to the dogs or servants. No one had ever smashed their dinner between two "plates" and snarfed it down, trenchers and all without utensils, before, and certainly not while doing something other than sitting in the dining hall. And then came Pizza....

Even better than sandwiches, since you can have it hot or cold with equal convenience. A nice, stiff bready "plate" with hot goo on top and you get to eat it with your fingers. No extra bread on top, just mounds of steaming, sloppy, sticky toppings and cheese to hold it on. Unless you're from parts of the Midwest or East Coast, where cheese is non-standard. As a Western Pizza Grrl, the cheese holding the stuff onto the crust is what defines pizza to me. It doesn't matter what the stuff in the middle is, so long as there's a crust on the bottom and cheese on top: it's a pizza. Whatever you put in the middle is up to you.

There's a [book of science essays](http://www.rbookshop.com/engineering/t/Thermodynamics/The_Thermodynamics_of_Pizza_0813517745.htm) out there called *The Thermodynamics of Pizza*, published in 1992 by a Rutgers University Professor named Harold Morowitz. The title was taken from a student essay submitted, if I remember correctly, to a California Institute of Technology class sometime in the 80s, which explained, in terms of Thermodynamics, why the cheese on pizza always burns the roof of your mouth. Last year, applying this concept, the Oklahoma Climatological Survey [published a paper](http://www.arm.gov/docs/education/lessons/nsa/pizza.pdf) using the Thermodynamics of Pizza to explain atmospheric heat retention. So useful.

Now, because of the thermodynamics of pizza, it is necessary to have copious amounts of quenching liquids around in case of the infamous Pizza-Cheese Syndrome (PCS) which prompted the original paper (y'know: the sticky, melted cheese adheres to the roof of your mouth while the insulating bread and boiling-hot sauce keep it fired up to raise instant blisters on your hard palate). In the event of a PCS event, cold liquids must be applied immediately to the site by the most efficient method. I don't know about you, but smearing aloe vera on the roof of my mouth just doesn't seem reasonable. Something far less noxious is needed if I'm going to quaff it in cheese-cooling quantities. And since we know that alcohol has a contact cooling effect as well as being an antiseptic, it seems to me that something cold and alcoholic, but not too alcoholic, or it'll hurt, is perfect: beer! (If you can't drink beer, I'm afraid you're going to have to settle for second best: root beer, still a tasty salve, though.)

So, not only is Pizza the near-perfect food, it's scientifically important. So, the next time you're lazing about, eating pizza and drinking beer, and your parents, roommates, significant other, or whatever stick their heads in to complain about your slacker-hood, just remember that you're doing important research and experimentation in the Thermodynamics of Pizza. After all, a large scientific sample is necessary for valid research. So do your part for science: eat pizza and drink beer! Right now might be good....

## Horns of the Dilemma

\

Faced with a challenge, what does one do? Run and hide? Jump out and scare it to death? Wrestle it to the ground and make it squeal? Some challenges seem easy to meet or obvious that you will at least try to wrestle them. Others present an ambiguity. Or cold-sweat inducing terror.

I went sailboat racing this past Tuesday. It was my first time (yes, you freaks, I was a virgin!). I wasn't sure about this; what if I screwed up and we lost the race horribly because of it. We didn't. I did my job and not badly, if I say so myself. (We didn't win, but we did come in seventh in a field of 30, which is pretty damned good, considering that some of the boats were serious race competitors. And it was damned fun, too!)

But other challenges, I haven't met so well. I'm still puttering about with the novel revision. Still haven't finished this site, really, and it's 8 months since I re-did the design. Let my HTML and CSS studies slide. Dropped out of a couple of projects, lost one to circumstances, haven't been chasing down work (I know, some of you are still saying "what is it you do, anyway, you silly bitch?" Someday I'll get some kind of bio and resume up and then you'll say "ahhh, now I see...".) Now there is a little project out there which intrigues me. It's big and scary and the deadline is really short. Can I make it? Is it worth trying? It's not my usual thing at all, but it is in a field I'd like to be deeper into, especially professionally. And, it pays, if I (we, actually; a friend of mine is helping me out, at this point) make the cut.

There's always a scary moment once I've committed to something. That "oh, shit, what if I screw up?" moment. But, it's sort of like riding a bronco, I guess. Just hold on tight and be prepared to dodge the trampling feet if you fall off.

Maybe this should be a rodeo event: challenge-wrangling.

You're all set in the stall, astride your trusty challenge-steed (in my case that will be a deceptively mild, six-legged computer--four legs for the CPU and two for the monitor).

The alarm sounds.

The gates swing open.

The rampaging Challenge charges into the ring! It's a wild, intimidating creature, snorting and tossing its huge, frightening head and making the ground tremble with the thunder of its hooves! It has the head of a dragon, or at the very least a giant sort of frilled lizard, on a snake-like neck. Its mouth is filled with venomous fangs and razor-edged teeth and a forked tongue which spews legalese. Its body is rawhide and smoke with a cat-o-nine-tails on the rump-roast end. And Gad, does it move fast! Eels have nothing in this guy for slippery.

I know that if I just leave it alone, it will leave me alone until the "what a wimp" buzzer sounds. But I'm in the ring now, and anything I do may attract its attention, not to mention the attention of all my friends and detractors who are sitting in the stands eating popcorn.

I kick my trusty computer into a trot and head for the Challenge.

It whips around at my approach and paws the ground. Small continents drown in the tsunami caused by the disturbance. It lets out a fearsome bellow and begins to charge!

Argh! It's attacking! Run away!

But, no! I must wrangle the rampaging Challenge. My stalwart computer quivers, but holds its ground. I grip my mouse and prepare for the Challenge. I spur my computer forward. "Heigh-ho, Linux-box!"

The keyboard cord sings across the distance and loops around the neck of the Challenge. My CPU rears back on its legs, holding the Challenge still as I leap off its back and head for the beast, mouse and cable in hand. The monitor gambols about on the other side, attempting to distract the monster.

I run to the creature as it pauses a moment to watch an amusing mpeg of my ferret dancing. I use my cordless mouse and double-click the Challenge on the head. While it is dazed, I leap upon it with my faithful keyboard and wrestle the monster to the ground. I must type very fast to outpace the flood of legalese from the creature's mouth and shut it up and tie its flailing, knife-shod hooves. A handy ethernet cable does the trick.

And the Challenge is on the floor. Hah! It's really just a kitten. It was nothing!

I laugh and wave to the crowd. Victory is mine.

But the Challenge shrugs off the cables at the last minute, leaping to its feet in rage.

Oops.... Maybe I should have plugged the stupid computer in, first....

## Catty

\

My cat sounds like Alan Rickman. Or at least he would if he could talk. Until someone comes along who is willing to pet him and make a fuss over him, then he acts, and probably sounds, just like a five-year-old.

The cat is firmly convinced that he is the center of the universe and it may be that he is right. Thousands of cat-centric universes are probably whirling around us as we speak, holding the larger meta-verse together, and all of them with the various, muttering voices of cats holding them on course.

Cats, as you may be aware, are the actual balancers of the universe(s). This is very hard work, which requires much concentration, followed by intensive napping. Occasionally, a small crises happens and then the cat in charge of that particular slice of meta-verse must dash frantically about and try to catch the various parts of the cati-verse which are now flying about randomly. This nearly always happens at night or very early in the morning, when careless humans do something called "sleeping" (which always includes "at the switch" in the mind of any cat nearby).

Recently, I came home to discover Kitts, the black cat under cover of a large, white spot, running frantically back and forth and pinging off the furniture.

"Excuse me," I said. "Just what seems to be your problem?"

"Poop!" the cat replied while dashing maniacally.

"Pardon me? What about it? Have you done something bad?"

"Of course not, woman! Don't be ridiculous! But, somehow, the poop has gotten out of the box and the whole universe is in danger!"

"Because you can't hit the target?"

"Oh, my, God! Don't be so very dense! Do I have to do everything around here? I'm far too busy keeping the immediate universe from collapsing into a black hole to be having this ridiculous conversation with you. Why don't you use your terribly insensitive nose, find the poop and then employ those opposable thumbs to manipulate some instrument to return it to its proper place?! Hmmm?"

Eventually, I found the errant poop and returned it to the litter box. Kitts immediately sat down and started to wash.

"Hey... I'm the one who needs to wash, Mr. Fastidious," I pointed out. "You're just the one who dumped the stuff out of the box in the first place. I'm the one who had to put it back in."

"Well, that's your job, isn't it?" the cat replied, looking up from licking a forepaw. "Really, I don't know how the universe goes on as well as it does with such slackers as you lot in the mix."

"You can get a job as a pair of mittens, you know...."

"Oh, I'm terrified...." The cat rolled his eyes at me and went out to the cockpit to sit in the sun.

"Look, you," I started to say, emerging from down below, but at that moment, George came by.

"Hiya, kitty!" he said.

"Oh, George!" the cat meowed and ran to the edge of the deck to be petted. "George, George, George! My favorite human!"

"I thought Boyd was your favorite human," I muttered.

"Well, he's not here, is he?" Kitts sniffed in my direction. "George is!"

The cat jumped down onto the dock and began to rub against George, who, not being completely attuned to cats, missed the entire conversation and didn't hear the childish whining of delight which the cat emitted. He thought it was purring.

"Gosh, he's such a friendly cat," George said. "I'd think you never petted him or paid him any attention, if I didn't know better."

"Oh, he gets plenty of attention...," I replied.

And another neighbor walked up with a grocery bag in hand. "Oh, is this your kitty?"

"Yep, that's him. Affection-Sluts-R-Us."

"Oh, he's just so cute!" She put down her bag and started to pet Kitts, also.

It's very difficult for a cat whose brain has just reverted to total kittenhood to decide what to do when faced with the choice of two humans wanting to pet him and a tempting grocery bag. Attempting to have it all, Kitts tried to straddle the bag and rub on the legs of both humans simultaneously.

Unfortunately, the universe does occasionally have a sense of humor. The only space between the humans and the bag was occupied by a few cubic inches of air overlying several cubic yards of water.

The cat fell in.

After the splashing, Kitts emerged and glared at me. "This is somehow your fault."

"Don't look at me, Mr Pet-Me-Pet-Me," I laughed. "You're the greedy attention-hound."

"Hmph! I'm not a hound of any kind. Besides... it's hot. I needed to cool off...."

The cat stalked back on board the boat with much injured dignity.

That made up for the poop, pretty well, I think. Next time, I'll have to toss him in myself....

## Let Them Eat Cake

\

She places it down between us: black-brown and both as wide and as thick as an industrial door-stop. Three cream rosettes rest on the crumbling edge of the wedge. We stare at it in trepidation.

"Is there anything else I can get for you?" she chirps.

"Uhhmmm... yeah: two starving orphans with their own forks."

The waitress giggles and leaves us alone with the killer cake. I suspect her giggles are on account of our dismay at realizing we have just made a pact with Pure Evil. Is that a pointed tail I see flirting under her uniform? This cake may be the death of us....

This is the sort of dessert which one hopes for in childhood dreams of avarice. It is the chocolate cake from Hell, the contract-sweetener which induces the gluttons of the world to sign in blood, the camel-breaking calories of Faustian nightmare.

We stab it with our steely forks and hope it will remain inert. One never knows with a cake of this type; it might become ambulatory at any moment, in an effort to ensnare other victims. If it had eyes it would be looking lust and promise at us (of course what it is lusting for is our souls...). The pastry equivalent of bedroom eyes from a femme fatale (gateau fatale?).

In spite of valiant efforts, we just can't kill the beast. Half of it remains on the plate after much forking-off.

Childhood memories of "starving children in China" make us wonder if we should ask for a to-go box to be shipped to some deserving country. Or possibly we should take it with us until accosted by a hungry homeless person, when we could turn it over to the needy. But is anyone really *in need* of chocolate cake?

But, in the end, we leave it behind. It is a far, far better thing to leave such temptation aside. After all, it might entrap less-hardy souls than our own and enslave them to an eternity of Chocolate Damnation. And what would they do to fight against it if they don't have either milk or coffee? It's straight to Sticky Chocolate Hell for them and we can't be responsible for that.

Of course, I feel terribly guilty about not eating all of the cake, in spite of the constant battle to keep my waistline significantly smaller than my bustline (which is a considerable measurement). Should I have had the cake in the first place? Probably not, but that's sort of the nature of sin: you shouldn't have indulged in it in the first place. But, on the other hand, if I never indulged in any sin at all, I'd be a terribly dull person.

Maybe I should just eat cake and skip dinner... and guilt. Hmm....

Oh, demoness--er, I mean waitress... about that cake....

## Perpetual Holiday

\

Well, we're heading for our particular National Holiday. Last year, someone said they found it significant that Americans were the only people he knew of who celebrated their national character by blowing things up. Apparently, he doesn't know the French, the Mexicans, or the English, come to that, and certainly not the Chinese. (Letting aside the "Irish question," please.)

But I will admit that there is often a sort of naivete to Americans about this weird holiday. I overheard a guest at a party asking a visitor from Canada "Do you have the Fourth of July up there?" Well, duh! Unless they bounce right from the Third to the Fifth without a pause (a sort of backwards leap-year-day thing) I suspect that the Canadians do have a fourth day of July, too, since most Westerners use the same calendar. I suppose it might be a rather more interesting calendar if only the nations who celebrated something on a particular date "had" that day. Think of the holes!

July looks pretty bad for most people. Non-Canadians will please recline for the First of July. Obviously the Fourth is a loser for anyone not American. Bastille Day (the Fourteenth of July) is out for anyone who isn't French.

August is a bit punctuated, too. Outside of Jamaica, there will be no Sixth day of August. The Fifteenth of the month is out if you are anywhere but Korea or Congo. August Nineteenth will be a day of rest for anyone outside of Afghanistan (and here's hoping it will be one for everyone inside it, too).

September, October and November don't look much better than July and August. Any place which is not Qatar will be very quiet on the Third of September. Non-Mexicans will please siesta for the entirety of the Seventeenth of September (and also May the Fifth, just for fun). And please keep on sleeping through the week of September the Nineteenth, unless you are in St. Kitts or Nevis. The entire month of October will be a blank for everyone who isn't Communist Russian (except for Hallowe'en, which will be celebrated in shopping malls), ditto November Fifth (Guy Fawkes Day) for those outside of Britain, and the Eleventh for all non-Poles.

Randomly, the Fourth of April will be too busy with parades and canoe races in Senegal to be available anywhere else, June Tenth is a non-starter outside of Portugal, the Sixth of December will only be active in Denmark, everyone who isn't Norwegian is required to sleep through the Seventeenth of May, and if you were planning on doing anything outside of Greece on the Twenty-fifth of March, you can just forget it.

And on and on. By the time you're done counting up the days which will happily drop from your calendar into oblivion by reason of being "owned" by another nationality, you'll hardly have to work at all. "It was a National Holiday... somewhere" will become a viable excuse for non-attendance at almost anything. Personally, I plan on collecting the whole set....
