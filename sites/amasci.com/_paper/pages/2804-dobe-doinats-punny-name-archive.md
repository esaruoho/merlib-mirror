*Mission Statement*

</div>

\
The <u>P unny N ame A rchive</u> is dedicated to the study, collection (archival), management (database interactions), growth, usability, publishing (display), and enjoyment, of <u>only</u> those <u>common names</u>, admittedly a relative term, which have some <u>literal meaning</u> implied by the sounds the name produces when <u>spoken aloud</u>.

We think the "<u>Punny</u>" part of the name is located somewhere in the inner ear, although this is in great dispute among modern scientists. PNA will be a repository of <u>slack rules of pronunciation</u>, such as in my "[Pun'O'Nyms](PNApunonyms.htm)" page. Nearly <u>any</u> [common phrase](PNAliterals.htm) (literals) can be mapped to a Last Name in the <u>U.S. Census database</u> , which is known, however, <u>alas</u>, to be <u>incomplete</u>! Yet how many more than 88,000 names do I want to deal with? Probably fewer if I had a choice! Anyway, each name will then have an \_underscore and a number, like Jones_4, meaning that Jones is the <u>4</u>th most common name in the United States, you see?

While awaiting Census 2000, the PNA recordsets will be made available in a variety of different formats and platforms as technology progresses. Input is requested, and regular updates are expected. Feedback is appreciated. As time and resources allow, the database will continue to grow in usability. Tools will soon be available for the "manufacture" of new Punny Names, using an application soon to be released through these pages, written by <u>John Hartman</u> using combo boxes and either VB or Java. Watch for a download page to appear in the coming months!
