<td width="100%"><h1 id="section"><img src="images/equipment_large.gif" width="504" height="55" /></h1>
<p>Without a doubt our most frequently asked question concerns buying equipment. We have already answered this question many different times in many different ways. Whether you need information on certain brands and models, or you have a question about new or used gear, shopping online, choosing effects and accessories you will find the answer somewhere here.</p>

<p><a href="../guitar/20020912.html"><strong>Busy Beginner on a Budget</strong></a><br />
<span class="smauthorname">By Blue (Cathi Pearce)</span><br />
What annoys me most is how much discouragement there is out there for folks wanting to take the plunge and learn to play guitar. If you're someone other than a young man expressing a wish to learn, you can expect to run a gauntlet of criticism, confusion and plain bad advice.</p>
<p><a href="../guitar/acoustic/20010722.html"><strong>What To Look For In An Acoustic Guitar</strong></a><br />
<span class="smauthorname">By A-J Charron</span><br />
This article will educate you about the different parts of the acoustic guitar, what they are made of, and most importantly what you should look for when choosing which guitar to take home.</p>
<p><a href="../amps/20010904.html"><strong>GAS Powered</strong></a><br />
<span class="smauthorname">By Stefan Leonhardt</span><br />
Most of us guitarists suffer from an affliction called GAS - Gear Acquisition Syndrome. That means we are buying gear nearly compulsively - more and more often than we really need. What do we basically need to play guitar?</p>
<p><a href="../columns/2001/20010715.html"><strong>Sittin' On The Dock of eBay</strong></a><br />
<span class="smauthorname">By David Hodge</span><br />
In this article I’d like to do is offer up some observations and advice to those of you who are thinking about exploring the idea of getting your next guitar online.</p>
<p><a href="../songwriting/2001/20010725.html"><strong>Where To Find Great Prices</strong></a><br />
<span class="smauthorname">By A-J Charron</span><br />
“The pawn shop is a musician’s best friend.” I’ve heard that phrase many times before. The worst part of it is, it’s true. Many musicians end up pawning old instruments and old equipment there. They get out of the game or they simply change their instruments or equipment for something newer. This is where you can profit from their losses.</p>
<p><a href="../otherside/20010701.html"><strong>Shopping</strong></a><br />
<span class="smauthorname">By Laura Lasley</span><br />
I find that shopping for a guitar is much like shopping for anything else.  I have a few simple shopping rules that I like to follow which apply to clothing, TV's, cameras, cars, houses and yes, guitars and guitar accessories.</p>
<p><a href="../otherside/20010727.html"><strong>Looking And Listening</strong></a><br />
<span class="smauthorname">By Lee Budar-Danoff</span><br />
When selecting a guitar, the most obvious advice will be to select one with the sound you like best. But on walking into the mega guitar store, you are confronted with hundreds of guitars, and sound is not the thing that makes you take one off the rack to try. You are not a shallow person for selecting something based on looks, although hopefully it will not limit your final choice of guitar.</p>
<p><a href="../articles/20001129.html"><strong>The Little Things</strong></a><br />
<span class="smauthorname">By David Hodge</span><br />
If you are considering buying something for a musician you know and are a bit worried about buying the "wrong" thing, have no fear - there are no wrong things! You may not know this, but there are a lot of things that guitarists <em>always</em> need, but rarely purchase. Guitar players go through stuff that needs to be replaced fairly often. While there is still time left before the holidays let us go through some of the things you can buy for the guitar player on your list.</p>
<p><a href="../articles/20000822.html"><strong>Reaching The Limits<br />
</strong></a> <span class="smauthorname">By Abel Petneki</span><br />
Tried everything but still can't play that solo no matter how much you practice? This seems to be a common problem for beginners - lets try a different approach. The problem might not be you; it could actually be your guitar.</p>
<p><a href="../bass/20011103.html"><strong>Buying Your Second Bass Amp</strong></a><br />
<span class="smauthorname">By Dan Lasley</span><br />
Just about everyone who picks up the bass as a beginner has a marginal practice amp to start with. Which is why I’m going to talk about what to look for when you buy your second amp – your first real “play it loud and proud” bass amp.</p>
<p><a href="../amps/20011216.html"><strong>How to Buy a Guitar Amp - Beginning The Quest For Tone II</strong></a><br />
<span class="smauthorname">By Jeremy Ledford</span><br />
This is the second installment on how to annoy your parents, siblings and/or neighbors. We will focus on the intermediate player and go a bit deeper into the quest for tone. It is both easier and more difficult to choose an amplifier at this level of skill. It is also a bit more expensive, in most cases.</p>
<p><a href="../sound/20020209.html"><strong>Recording on a Budget</strong></a><br />
<span class="smauthorname">By Scott Hysell</span><br />
So, you've written and perfected a bunch of original songs and you're ready to lay them down in a permanent form. The problem is, you're probably worried or maybe even a little discouraged by the potential costs involved. It can be incredibly expensive, but have no fear; it doesn't have to be that way.<br />
[from <a href="../sound/index.html">Sound Engineering</a>]</p>
<p><a href="../songwriting/2000/20000606.html"><strong>Building a digital studio</strong><br />
</a><span class="smauthorname">By A-J Charron</span><br />
The digital recording environment, although not the best, offers some cheap solutions for musicians. Digital is the conversion of naturally occurring phenomena into a series of electrical pulses, i.e. 0's and 1's. In this article A-J shows you how to use your PC as a home digital studio. Topics include what you need to do and how much it will cost.</p>
<p><a href="../bass/20030116.html"><strong>British Bass Amps - Oh, the Majesty!</strong></a><br />
<span class="smauthorname">By Steve Smith</span><br />
Here is the lowdown on British bass guitar amplifiers. From Trace Eliot to Marshall, Steve Smith details the similarities and differences between the various amps and also gives you some help as to deciding which may be the right one for you.</p>
