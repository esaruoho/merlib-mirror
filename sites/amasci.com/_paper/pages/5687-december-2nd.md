**April 22nd, Tuesday 2003:** Thought I would add another page to the frame restoration since it was getting a little long for those users on a dial-up connection :)

**May 1st, Thursday 2003:** Finally sent off the Motor Mounts to be revulcanized...so they are off to Coonrapids Minnesota for a scrubbing and resurrection.  This coming Sunday, Oscar, my 58 Ranger is going to move to Oregon and continue life there with a good family.  All of the brake lines are done and I am now looking to get the transmission taken care of then I will be able to settle the engine and transmission back onto the frame...sigh...need to send the power steering pump somewhere to be rebuilt...maybe ProSteering in Oregon can do that.

**May 03, Saturday 2003:** My granddad is near death so we've been getting together from many states to say goodbye so I may have a number of busy days ahead that will preclude any Edsel work...wish me luck.

**May 04, Sunday 2003:** Granddad passed away today, my work sent a great setting of flowers.

**May 11, Sunday 2003:** Today I was finally able to get some time to work on the car and with the weather sunny and nice, it was a great time to work.  Returned the transmission mount and brackets to the frame, the look great sitting nestled between the frame rails finally.  Eaton sent me the rear shackle upper bushings.  Eaton has about any sort of Edsel bushing you could imagine, check them out if you need any sort of bushing and spring sorts of goodies for your Edsel.  [Eaton Suspension Goodies](http://www.eatonsprings.com/).  So the rear suspension is finally in place and synched up, all the brake lines look great, so really the next thing is to get the transmission rebuilt...that'll have to wait until next payday.  Night!

**May 18th, Sunday 2003:** Still waiting for the motor mounts...other than that things are slow and work is heavy so I am getting little time with the car.  At least I was able to get a couple of good pics of the front suspension

| **May 25th, Sunday 2003: ** The motor mounts arrived today, man oh man, they look great and are solid and perfect as can be.  Metro Rubber Co helped out with these.  They typically work with weather stripping but do dabble, and with some \$\$ on the line, I was able to persuade them to take on the job of restoring the mounts.  They are wonderful and I will get a pic here soon of them.  So remember \*here is the plug\* **Metro Rubber {<http://www.metrommp.com>}**for any classic car rubber needs.  They did ask me to keep it a little hush hush as they aren't in the business of doing motor mounts however, since there aren't really that many citation class Edsels around any more, I don't think they will get a slew of orders.  They really are beautiful (for a motor mount).  For now, nite! | <img src="edselpics/08_edsel_mounts.jpg" data-border="0" width="684" height="533" /> |

**June 4th, Wednesday 2003: ** Bought a new truck, "The Sheriff" as named by the kids...a 2003 F350 with the V10, four doors and long bed (no duly in the rear)...gun metal gray and leather interior...oh yeah!  But, that will slow down the restoration process a bit due to the second mortgage it is costing me (Close to it).  But stay tuned, I have lots of other things I am doing to the Edsel.  My next goal is to get the transmission rebuilt in Tacoma and then finish the underbody metal repair, then TA DA, the frame will go back under the body for good.

**June 7th, Saturday 2003:** Finally began some of the underbody work.  Before I put the frame (engine and tranny too) back under the bulk of the Edsel, I wanted to repair the rust damage and age that seems to hold onto the underside.  What a mess, there are four structural supports that run crosswise under the car, these stiffen the floorpan and provide some rigidity between the rocker panels and the underside.  Nearly all the spot welds are separated and rusted, all the support braces that reach from the floorpan onto the rocker panel are rusted away or a shell of their stronger former self.  Wow the undercoating is a mess too...go figure.  Someone did undercoat the car...long ago, much of it is thin and brittle, really, most of it is gone.  Nearly finished scraping and de-rusting the passenger underside and included a nice coat of primer.

Once I am done with the drivers side, I can measure replacement sheet metal, grab the clippers and welder and go to town.  Ideally when I am done, I will coat the stuff with this thick Rhino-Hide like rubber coating for protection and sound deadening...I will be getting a case or two of this stuff from **Saria International Inc. {**[**http://www.sariainternational.com/autoprod/undercoat.htm**](http://www.sariainternational.com/autoprod/undercoat.htm)**}**...night!

**June 14th, Saturday 2003:** The transmission is on it's way to the local shop for a buff and rebuild...it could take weeks according to some, longer according to others, who knows but I am not really in a hurry right now, I have learned a good deal about patience on this project.

**June 18th, Wednesday 2003:** Crawled under the car today and started the long process of rust and grim removal.  Not to my surprise, nearly every support under the body fell away like dust and road dirt with each chip and grind.  I really should have saved all the Edsel-Dirt that has fallen off and been scrubbed off the car...could be good trivia, "How much dirt does a neglected car collect over forty years".  Not much left of the supports, in fact little.  Finished primering the underside and treating it with rust removal acid and cleaner.

**June 19th, Thursday 2003:** Ran over to Tru-Value today and bought some sheet metal, screws, and more acid cleaner.  I need to get going on some cardboard mockups of the support replacements...too tired tonight.

J**une 20th, Friday 2003:** Wedding anniversary today...so it is NOT a day to work on the Edsel.

**June 21st, Saturday 2003:** Got a LOT done today in restoration of the underside of the Edsel. Started with the passenger side front support, man this was a bad one...you can see where the rust ate away the support member and through the side sill.  This is typical rust damage for these cars, the welds were notoriously sparse along the seams of the ribs.  Water and dust would filter through the seams and collect in the rib and run down to the side of the car against the sill...made for a great environment for rust and decay.  I'll be filling this rib areas with an insulating rubberized foam which will keep this from reoccurring.

Next, on to getting these things in place and temporarily screwed in, once that's done then onto some metal melting!  Have a good night!

**June 24th, Tuesday 2003:** Over the last three days I have lived under the old gal, drilling, grinding, sanding, primering and patching. At last, this evening around midnight, I finished the restoration of the underbelly of the car, finally! Crack out the Champaign. I still need to order the rubberized undercoating and foam insulation that will come together and nicely seal the belly for a very long time. Once that is done, the tranny and engine can be remounted on the frame and slid back under the hulk...night! \
\
**June 25th, Wednesday 2003:** Okay, hold onto the bill folds, just got a message from the transmission place. Turns out that too many years have been at the transmission and the shop would not be willing to stand behind it's rebuild if they attempted it on the trashed unit. The folks there already sought out two additional cores (very cool on their part) and relayed the news to me. It's going to cost around \$2500 to have a new transmission for the old gal, holy smokes! Well, it does come with a great warranty so I told them go for it...they mentioned it would take about three weeks plus or minus so I am okay with that. I just need to sell my old 1954 Chevy to support the transmission effort. So if you live in Washington State and want to buy a 54 four door Chevy in good shape (just needs an interior and that's about 1K), drop me \$2000 and we'll consider it done. (**Sold in July 4th**)

**July 3rd, Thursday 2003:** Got a call from the transmission shop, the estimate is close, turns out the original transmission has solidified into a solid metal mass sometime back in the mid 70's when it was in the junk yard in Canada...big surprise.  So with that in mind, the shop had already grabbed two cores and had me choose the next path, rebuild of another core.  I told them to take their time because I really wanted a great job done (and needed a month or two to get the money together).  The shop will call me now and again to update me on the progress of the transmission rebuild and alert me to any surprises...(been plenty of those so far).

**July 14th, Monday 2003:**  Spent the last week and a half on vacation and away from work and home projects...ah...sweet rest.  Called the tranny shop today because I haven't heard from them yet as to progress.  Turns out they are nearly done and waiting for a switch (neutral safety as Ray recalled)...so they might be done and ready to hand it back in a couple of weeks.

**July 19th, Saturday 2003:** Called the transmission shop to see how things are going...no switch yet but they feel confident they can find one...this has given me time to get most of the money together so things are looking good.  Later!
