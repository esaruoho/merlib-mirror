From "Larry Renger" \< <larry_renger@earthlink.net> \>\

In answer to Jerry Cashman's request, you can indeed scale down models to whatever size you want. For optimum performance, I would make the tail moment a smigen longer or the tail a bit bigger on a small model for better damping of the response. Airfoils and stability scale well, but you have to be careful of weight.\
Weight = k x (wingspan x wing area)\
This is a good guide for scaling models. Find "K" for the model you like, and then adjust weight in the smaller size to match. The most useful units seem to be ounces, feet, and square feet. The numbers for K then come out somewhere between 1 and 10 no matter how big the plane (dig up the data on a full scale P-51 or Boeing 747, and compare it to a hot pattern ship)\
Best regards,\
Larry Renger

From "Andrew Donatelli" \< <andrew@donatelli.net> \>\

Jerry Cashman reqeusted info on designing BDA Planes in SMALLnet post RR05. Here are some rules of thumb that I use when designing BDA planes.

I have found that a fuselage length of about 24" to 27" (610mm to 685mm) and a wing span of 28" to 35" (711mm to 889mm) works well. I like to use 30/70 moments. That's where, you measure the length from the prop to the elevator hinge and set the CG of the plane is 30% back from the prop line. I usually use a 30% balance on a 5.5" to 6" (140mm to 152mm) chord wing. I use a vertical stabilizer of 3.5" to 4.5" (89mm to 114mm) and a horizontal stabilizer of 12" to 13" (305mm to 330mm). The ailerons and elevator are 3/4" to 1.25" (19mm to 32mm) depending on how responsive you want the plane to be. If I use a rudder, I make it large... about 2" (50mm) or more. If you are going to bother with a rudder, it might as well be large enough for knife edge flight. You can check out some of my designs on my web site. I also have plans drawn for a =BDA Tiger 2 style plane and a BDA Ugly Stick, which aren't on the site.\

From \< <jawa@ies.net> \> (Joan Wilson)\
It's good to have SMALLnet back on line again isn't it! Thanks to Randy for picking up the torch. A few months ago I was looking for some Free Flight plans when I came across a reduced drawing from a British magazine (RCM&E?) for a model fitted with a Rogallo wing - interesting!\
A large black plastic garbage bag, 4 lengths of 5/16" dowel 48" long, some double sided sticky tape, a few feet of fishing trace, some plywood scraps, a short length of 1" x 1" aluminum angle & a few nuts & bolts was all it took to have a kite-like structure to fit on the model instead of the conventional wing. I used the fuselage of the OBOGO as that was all I had. The engine is an early Thunder Tiger 20 R/C with a 9x4 prop, Rudder & elevator are fitted. I waited a long time for the wind to drop below 25 mph, yesterday I flew the model with the normal wing fitted as the wind had finally slowed somewhat - finally after about 3 months.

I was not too rusty so today I tried the Rogallo wing. It works! What more can I say, much to my delight it flies quite well. There is a large increase in drag over the normal wing, the climb out is steady & stable. I had to add up elevator trim even at full throttle but it was fun. At fast idle it flies backwards & at slow idle it sinks down like a parachute, then when the throttle is opened it goes forward & starts to climb again. The turns can be very tight, it would probably be OK on a small field. I hand launched the model without any problems (the field is rough & covered with low scrub) & landed it a few feet away with more throttle than usual to control the height. However it blew over after landing as the wind had begun to pick up & broke the fuselage behind the bulkhead at the front of the wing. Inspection showed that fuel & oil soakage had finally taken their toll so a new fuselage will be needed. I will increase the rudder area & the elevators for more response, the original control surface were OK but increased area might help at the lower speed the model now flies.\
A fun time, CHEAP & a good way to make use of a fuselage & tail waiting for the wing to be repaired. Anyone who would like a copy of the plan contact me direct & I will scan it to send.\
A car larger than a Suzuki Swift would also be an asset, the wing takes up a surprising amount of space.\
Regards from Windy Brownsville, Tx\
Tom Wilson
