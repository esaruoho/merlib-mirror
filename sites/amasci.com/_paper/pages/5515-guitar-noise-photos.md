<td width="100%"><h1 id="photos">Photos</h1>
<p>These pictures and accompanying stories will give you some idea of how we typically spend our summer vacation.</p>

<td><img src="images/riverside2002.jpg" width="200" height="147" alt="Riverside Jam 2002" /></td>
<td><p><strong>Riverside Jam, New Jersey</strong> - August 2002<br />
From left to right: Laura, Dan, Paul, David and Lee. This is our first group shot of the Guitar Noise crew. We had a great get together for Riverside Jam 2002, which took place in New Jersey this year. It was a pretty big jam with about 20 musicians attending from all over. Thanks to everyone who put it together and kept us fed.</p></td>
</tr>
<tr data-valign="top">
<td><a href="../articles/20001019.html"><img src="images/paul_ili_valley.jpg" data-border="0" width="200" height="133" alt="Click here for the full story" /></a></td>
<td><p><strong>Tales From the Ili Valley</strong> - <em>Autumn 2000</em><br />
Often it is not so obvious, but what David Hodge says in "On Gifts and Giving" is true: "Music is a medium that really works when it is shared." This became apparent when our multi-lingual and multi-national film crew of four found itself in a remote part of the world where there was no common language for communication. When wefinished recording musical performances we would immediately give a set of headphones to the performers and let them listen. It was the first time they had ever heard themselves sing and play. They were delighted at what they heard.<br />
[read <a href="../articles/20001019.html">Tales From The Ili Valley</a>]</p></td>
</tr>

<td><a href="http://www.cyberlaz.com/rj2k/RJ2Kpost.html"><img src="images/RJ2Kpic19.jpg" data-border="0" width="200" height="89" alt="Click here for the Riverside Jam website" /></a></td>
<td><p><strong>The Riverside Jam 2000</strong> - <em>August 2000</em><br />
If you invite a bunch of musicians who are also reasonably fun people, it will all work out just fine. So long as there is some time structure to the different sessions, everyone will get what they want, and some wonderous music will be made. So have fun!<br />
[visit <a href="http://www.cyberlaz.com/rj2k/RJ2Kpost.html">The Riverside Jam Website</a>]</p></td>
</tr>
<tr data-valign="top">
<td><a href="../columns/1999/19991216.html"><img src="images/19991216c_small.jpg" data-border="0" width="200" height="137" alt="Click here for the full story" /></a></td>
<td><p><strong>On Gifts and Giving</strong> - <em>December 1999</em><br />
In August, I hosted a "last Friday the 13th of the century" jam at my home. Some old friends and new students got to sit in together. But to me the best part was having some of my friends' kids getting in on the act - playing bongos or triangles or maracas while the "adults" jammed away on the "big person's instruments."  Nothing is as thrilling as being part of the band, no matter what age you are.<br />
[read <a href="../columns/1999/19991216.html">On Gifts and Giving</a>]</p></td>

<td><p><strong>All Along the Watchtower</strong> - <em>August 1999</em><br />
In July and August 1999 Paul Hackett and Robert Leblanc were putting together a website about the unplugged music of Pink Floyd called Acoustic Floyd. Before going live they went to the Great Wall of China for a guitar summit. It proved to be a great venue for performing Pink Floyd classics from The Wall. For interested in the next guitar summit, "I'll see you on the dark side of the moon..."<br />
