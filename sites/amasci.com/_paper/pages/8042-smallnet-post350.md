Jeff Carter \< <jcarter@hot.rr.com> \> offers a correction:

Sitka Spruce for full-scale aircraft has to have a minimum of 6 annual growth rings per inch of board space (quarter-sawn for high strength to low weight ratio), and the grain deviation can be no more than 1:15. That's a 1" drop to 15 inches of board length, per the current FAA regulations. The type certificate for the aircraft can change that, but not go below the minimum FAA standards.

Sincerely, Jeff Carter

Mike Goliber \< <mjgoliber@earthlink.net> \> addresses that topic:

I use many Norvel BigMig R/C .061's and picked up a Thunder Tiger GP 07 as soon as one was available. I substituted it on a modified Q-Tee (Semi-symmetrical, no-dihedral wing; aileron, elevator, throttle) -- and here are some of my observations.

The Thunder Tiger turns less rpm with an APC 6x3: approximately 16,500-17,000, as opposed to Norvel's 18,500-19000. It weighs more (about 3.3 ounces vs 2 ounces) but is much quieter and much cleaner.

It uses a standard short glow plug, although a Norvel with a standard glow plug offers similar rpm. Throttle transition and Idle (around 4000) are very good. I use Omega 15% with 4 capfuls/gallon extra castor. I estimate fuel consumption at about 12 minutes per 2-ounce tank.

I cannot start my Thunder Tiger as well as others can who use the 07, but I suspect my fuel tank placement. I am one of those with no problems starting Norvels (lucky?) and often flip start, but I have never been able to flip start the 07.

Bottom line for me is if you have an application where extra nose weight, quieter operation, and scale-like or slower-flying characteristics are wanted, the TT07 is ideal. If you want higher performance, less cost, and more mess, then the Norvel .061 is the pick. Mike

As I understand it, glow plugs have an uncommon 1/4-32 thread.

...Quite right, Ian. By the way, since nobody responded to Tom's original query directly, here's some information from my own Cox experience. The main reason for performance decline with "converted glowheads" in Coxes is that the heads are thick, and just drilling & tapping 1/4-32 isn't good enough. The glow plug element ends up way higher than the original's location.

Counterboring Cox glow heads deep enough to put the glow element in its original location is not an easy task. I've done that more than a few times, and almost always need to cut considerable metal off the top, to let a short-reach glow plug seat deeply enough.

Ken Elder \< <kenelder@concentric.net> \> chimes in:

As an addition to Mike Gillihan's foam Ultra-Flyer, I thought everyone should know that the "other" foam gliders work well also. I've been powering the \$6 foam gliders with Cox .049s for over twenty years!! My first one was a foam 747 with highly swept wings, full- size radio, and .049 pusher. Boy was it heavy, took a hard throw for hand launch, and most of the time achieved flying speed just about a foot from the ground. Exciting for the 1970's, but not a lot of fun since the glide was rather like the space shuttle's.

My last one used the "all-flying tail" like Mike's did, but I didn't like the sensitivity; so my next one will have a conven- tional tail. These things don't go away easily, I usually have to throw them away after too many crashes and the epoxy just starts to make it look really ugly.

One thing is for sure, the swept wing gliders will tip stall with full size radio gear on board!! In fact, that's how I get mine down when it climbs out of sight: just yank full up and wait for a while and pretty soon you'll see a fluttering white thing rolling like mad on its way down. Haven't tried one with mini-radio gear yet, but I think they'd fly much better. Never broke a wing, the strapping tape is great.

I agree with Mike, the straight wing should fly much better. You just can't get a cheaper R/C plane!

Ken Elder, Largo, Florida
