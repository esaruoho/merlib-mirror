<td colspan="2" data-valign="TOP" width="80%" data-bgcolor="#ffffff"><p><strong><em>Spirit</em> concert review</strong></p>
<p>Time to rave about last night's concert... it was fabulous! It's really nice to sit in the audience and get to hear my chorus, now and then, and last night was no exception.</p>
<p>For the past few years we had been performing at Benaroya Hall in Seattle, but due to financial constraints the concert was moved to the large and beautiful sanctuary at the Plymouth Congregational Church. As the theme of this concert was <em>Spirit</em>, it was actually quite an appropriate move.</p>
<p>Act One of the concert - traditionally SLGC's "more serious half" - was chock-full of beautiful spiritual music from many traditions. Divided into five themes of elemental spirit, earth, air, fire, and water, the songs embraced Native American, Christian, non-denominational, and "general spiritual" (in the sense that it was spiritual, not religious, and often nature-based) traditions. My favorites included <em>This We Know</em> (a capella, words attributed to Chief Seattle, music by Ron Jeffers) and <em>Keep Your Lamps</em> (a capella spiritual, arranged by Andre Thomas).</p>
<p>Special guest percussionists Om Bili added a great beat to several of the pieces, although unfortunately with the change of venue there were times when it was difficult to hear the chorus over the drumming. But overall, the contribution by the group was worth having them there.</p>
<p>At the intermission, current chorus president Brian Watson walked to the podium for the usual President's Blurb, aka State of the Chorus Presentation. This is the chorus' 13th year, and so I've heard a lot of chorus presidents' speeches. I have to say, however, that Brian's was the best of all those that I can remember. It was humorous, it was touching, it was honest, and it was of an appropriate length. The chorus is in pretty dire straights right now, financially, and Brian was forthcoming about the realities and the need for donations to keep it going. He did it with both humor and sensitivity. He noted Chris Smith's recent passing, and dedicated the concert to Chris. "We know he'd appreciate the attention," he said. And oh yes, Chris would. :)</p>
<p>Intermission, and then it was on to a much shorter second act. This is the "fun half" of our concerts, and they didn't disappoint. I was entranced by <em>Once Upon a December</em> (from the movie <em>Anastasia</em>) - I love that song, and had forgotten it was on the program. (I rehearsed with the chorus this season for about a month before taking leave, so had sung most of the music I heard last night.) There was <em>When I Grow Up, I Wanna Be</em>, a medley of songs arranged by the director, John Kelleher. The chorus members drew on prop clothing and accessories to become one of four, either a cowboy, princess, superhero, or baseball player. Gender bending all around (with an appearance of some magnificent boobies by Ryan, someone I never expected would wear them so well), a lot of fun and hilarity, and all had a great time.</p>
<p>The concert closed with <em>Prayer of the Children</em>, a wonderful piece. The lyrics are serious and questioning in nature (...can you hear the prayer of the children?) but the music itself is soothing and beautiful.</p>
<p>Our traditional closing, with <em>We Are Everywhere</em> (by David Maddux, a former director of the chorus), featured a twist which we've done in the past but that we hadn't used in a few years: having past chorus members join the chorus on stage. So out of the audience I came, along with probably twenty other chorus alumni, and stood with my friends to sing the last song. It was wonderful!</p>
<p>Good job, guys. I'm proud of all of you!</p>
