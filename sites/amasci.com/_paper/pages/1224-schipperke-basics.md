# **Schipperke Basics!**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

**Click <a href="schiphis.htm" target="main">here</a> to go to text a friend in Europe translated for us.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

**First and foremost, Schipperkes are a very energetic breed, originally bred as working dogs. If you intend to get yourself a nice, calm lapdog, a Schip is not for you. They are full of energy, and always ready to run and/or play. Perhaps the bluntest (and most humorous) way to put it is that you simply cannot wear these dogs out. If you are a physically active person and like to have your dog along with you as you exercise, a Schip might be ideal.**

**Don't get me wrong... I love my Schip!  If you're a Schip owner and you're offended by the above list... send me your additions and I'll put them on there, too.  Remember, it's all meant in good fun.\
But Schipperkes seem to have behaviors that most other dogs and dog owners simply do not have to deal with.  To get a better idea of what I'm referrring to, I suggest you subscribe to the Schipperke email list and read the comments of owners for a while before deciding to purchase one of these darlings.**

**But rest assured, there are many, many positive aspects associated with having a Schipperke own you.***\*
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

------------------------------------------------------------------------

**If you have a house, a fenced yard is almost a must when you have a Schipperke. However, simply letting your Schip into the yard does not give him or her enough exercise - you must play and exercise with them. If you have an apartment, the small stature of the breed is attractive to your environment - but be prepared to walk your Schip several times a day!**

**Although they are small in stature, Schips are in no way a "toy" breed. They seem to have no true idea of their size, and will loyally guard their family and possessions. Although small, they make good guard dogs. By nature, they are completely devoted to their families and are suspicious of strangers. Schipperkes are very intelligent - the larger breeds have nothing on this lively little dog! They want to be involved in all your family events... don't bother to close doors around a Schip, because he/s he will want to be on the other side very soon. Most Schips seem to love riding in cars, as well.**

**Also, know that Schipperkes traditionally have docked tails. Whether you have your pup's tail docked is up to you. Make sure you speak to your breeder about it - before the litter is born, if possible. If you have questions about docking, talk to your veterinarian about the procedure. Also, take a look into the [Frequently Asked Questions About Dogs](http://www.zmall.com/pet_talk/tittle/tittle.html) homepages maintained by Cindy Tittle More. You'll find FAQs there regarding the pr os and cons of docking. Some owners prefer the natural look of Schipperkes with tails. If the tails are left intact, Schips have beautiful plumed tails that curve up over their backs.**

**Recognized Problems (from"Medical & Genetic Aspects of Purebred Dogs" by Ross D. Clark and Joan R. Stainer, published by the Veterinary Medicine Publishing Compnay, Edwardsville, Kansas): *The Schipperke is remarkably free of breed abnormalities. Entropion and narrow palpebral fissure have been reported in the breed. There is some dermatitis that may appear in the fall, caused by allergy, but it responds to treatment well. A hay fever asthma occasionally appears in the fall in some individuals ... The one problem that is serious and is occasionally seen is Legg-Perthes disease. Even it is not common enough to be known and recognized by all Schipperke breeders.* Cases of hip dysplasia are also reported.**

**Clark and Stainer also comment: *Dewclaws, which appear only on the front legs, and tails are removed at 3 to 5 days of age, in the normal healthy puppy. The tail is properly removed to the last possible joint, leaving no stub, despite the Standard which allows up to one inch of tail. The Schipperke that appears in the ring with an inch of tail will wind up last in the class, no matter what other good points it may possess... Tail docking is major surgery, requiring expert handling, stitching of the w ound, and attention to serious bleeding. Veterinarians should keep puppies in their care for at least an hour after surgery to watch for bleeding. A small dose of local anesthetic seems to give considerable comfort to puppies, which can cry and fuss for 12 to 18 hours after surgery.***

**If you decide that the Schipperke breed is for you, with loving, firm, and careful training you are in for years of delightful enjoyment with your dog! Remember, buying a pup such as a Schipperke is a committment of many years... Schips can live to 16-17 years in good health, and sometimes longer. It is recommended that you seek out a responsible breeder and spend some time with a Schipperke before making your decision to own one of them. You will find that responsible breeders will want to know about your living style and environment before agreeing to sell you one of their pups. More information about the responsible purchase of puppies is available on the "Other Dog Related WWW Sites" section of the main Schipperke page. It is strongly urged that you read the information regarding puppy purchasing before you even consider having a puppy in your home.**
