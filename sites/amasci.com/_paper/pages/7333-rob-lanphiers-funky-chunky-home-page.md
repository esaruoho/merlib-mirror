### About Me

I currently work at [RealNetworks](http://www.real.com), the home of RealAudio, RealVideo, and all things Real(tm) (no, we don't really have a trademark on "Real", though given the choice...). My main role is resident Standards Nerd, working on things like [SMIL](http://www.w3.org/AudioVideo) and [RTSP](http://www.rtsp.org). I'd talk about what I do in my spare time, but that usually involves staring at a flashy box in my living room with pretty pictures, or nerding out in front of the computer. Whatever it is I do, one of the things you'll see is that I don't spend a lot of time editing these web pages, because most of the stuff here dates back to 1996.

I wrote an article a while back for Issue \#3 of *[The Perl Journal](http://www.samag.com/documents/s=1282/sam01030002/)*, a quarterly magazine dedicated to the Perl programming language. The article itself is about [Condorcet's method](http://www.eskimo.com/~robla/politics/condorcet.html), which is a really elegant way of electing folks in contests where there must be one and only one winner, such as president, governor, mayor, or Best Supporting Actress in an Elvis Presley Movie. It's particularly well suited to elections with three or more candidates (and thus provides a means for eliminating primary elections). Actually, the article centered on the [program I wrote for calculating Condorcet winners in a single seat election](http://www.eskimo.com/~robla/politics/condorcet-front.html).

My parents run [Rebound Rubber Recycling](http://www.reboundrubber.com). They shred up used automobile tires to make rubber granules and sell them for use in playgrounds and for raw material for other products. They will eventually produce flat-roof roofing made from the granules. They also sell used tires in large quantities, if you are interested in buying a few hundred.
