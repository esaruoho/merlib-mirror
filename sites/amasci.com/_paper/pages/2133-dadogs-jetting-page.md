DADDOG DOGRIDER WR0450 CHAIN UPDATE

MAKING THE CORRECT TIMING CHAIN FOR THE STROKER 512 DADDOG MOTOR SEE BELOW

What has been happening with the Dogriders?

July 5 2004 LATEST UPDATES July 5 2004 LATEST UPDATES

 

LATEST INFORMATION: In an earlier report, we stated that the Yamaha WR-450 chain could be used for the YFZ-450 Dogrider 512 cc motor, We would like to add that the length of that chain is perfect but the links don't quite fit. After tightening the chain a bit, we noticed it was high riding and the chain did not fit the grooves exactly and we were a bit worried. The engine builder said it SHOULD work but if you want to try it, then please let us know how it goes.

 

Because of all the money invested, we did not want to run this chain if there was a better option, one slip and the timing would be off and the valves could be damaged. and maybe the expensive crank and stroker rod. So we decided to make our own chain.

 

NEEDED: TWO YFZ-450 timing chains at 14 dollars a piece. Dremel tool with metal cutting disk. Concrete nails (3-4) you can buy at the hardware store, a small piece of 1/4 inch steel 2x3 inches or so, with a 3/8 inch hole drilled through it.A TIG welder, .040 tip and \#4 shield.

 

Procedure. Take one of the YFZ-450 timing chains and using eye protection, dremel off about 12 pins on both sides of the chain, Grind off the little pins just until they are flush with the plates, but try not to scratch the plates.

 

Put the chain sideways over the metal plate with the hole drilled in it. using a hammer and a concrete nail and eye shield. put the concrete nail on the drilled off pin and hit it with the hammer, the pin should go into the metal hole in the plate underneath it (position the pin so that it WILL go into the hole)

 

Knock out about twelve pins and save the outside plates and the inner metal plates you will need them.

 

Now, the remaining chain, take a nice section of the chain and using the dremel, you need to cut through the outside plate RIGHT NEXT TO THE PIN on two sides REAL CLOSE! but do NOT hit the pin. Stay a little away from the pins you can also dremel closer later if you need to. each plate has two pins on each plate you will make four cuts until the plate falls away. Now take pliars and pull the remaining metal (a square piece) still surrounding the pins, and if the cutting was close enough, it will break away. If it is too tough, carefully drill and cut closer to the pin until it falls away. Now you easiy see the pins sticking up.The plate is gone. The plates underneath are probably damaged, just dont worry about that.

 

Now take a cone shapped diamond drill bit (\$9 dollars wall mart) and carefully shave the side of the mushroom pins exposed down until the pins are no longer muchroom shaped, but rod shapped, this takes a little time. Don't drill too much or the pins get too thin and wont work. anyway, when you get two pins thin enough YOU CAN PULL THEM OUT! You must get two of these, (preferably three of them.(ONe plate with two pins attached - ushaped. We lost one and had to make another make some extra.)

 

Now you are ready to make the chain. Take the brand new good timing chain and carefully grind off with the dremel two pins on the same plate. and on the other side also and drive out those pins so the chain falls apart. Now carefully put in one of your NEW U shaped plates and the nice little plates and then ANOTHER u shaped plate (we are LENGTHENING THE CHAIN BY ONE LINK. when it is all together , which takes a little work, now you are ready to finish it.

 

AFTER you put your chain together, take two PLATES you took from previous effort and slip it on the pins, the chain is now done. But it will fall apart if you do not mushroom the pins. Put your TIG welder on 20 amps pulse 4.5 using a sharpened clean 0,040 tungsten tip and a \#4 shield,and ground the chain, If you have an inverter welder, set frequency to around 200 so it hits hard and sharp. placing it on a heavy anvil. Get another person ready with a flat nosed happer and a 1/4 inch flat punch ready. Have them close their eyes. Hit the TIG WELDER PETAL with 12 liter/flow argon, give it 5 to 10 pulses and the pin sill start to glow red and when the TIP of the pin just barely turns molten yell GO!

 

The helper now OPENS his eyes cause you have shut off the welder, we hope, opens his eyes, QUICKLY places the punch on the hot pin and hits it three times quickly with a hammer, and the tip will mushroom nicely. This does not weaken the chain since the plate was not heated and the pin was not melted beyond the tip, the weight bearing area of the pin is still strong. So now you have a chain that cost you about \$2000 including ARGON GAS and including the welder cost, Or 14 bucks if you can havbe a welder friend to do it for you.

 

P.S. Yamaha is a pain in the neck they won't tell you where the chain was made, But after relentless searching I found BWA in Thiland as the source for our chains, NO THANKS TO YAMAHA. Yamaha makes a great quad but they are absolutely no help to the little guy. I bet they would have told Kory Ellis where they were made. But now you know. and I could buy 5 chains min order if I want BWA to make them for me. I figured FORGET IT I can make it faster myself. We wanted to go riding. So, there is how to make your 120 link chain (stock is 118) Happy CHAIN MAKING!
