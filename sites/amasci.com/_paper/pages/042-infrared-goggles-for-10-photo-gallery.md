<td width="1000"><h1 id="i.r.-filter-goggle-photos-shot-with-sony-dsc-p73-non-infrared-camera-with-opaque-congo-blue-filter-stack">I.R. FILTER-GOGGLE PHOTOS<br />
Shot with Sony DSC-P73 non-Infrared camera, with opaque Congo-Blue filter stack</h1>
<p>For these infrared photos I simply held one of the lenses of my "<a href="http://amasci.com/amateur/irgoggl.html">$10 IR goggles</a>" against the lens of my unmodified camera. I shot them in "moonlight mode" at f2.8, and around 2.5-sec exposure time. Cameras without an internal IR-cut filter will shoot <a href="http://www.flickr.com/photos/zachstern/sets/72157594229366903/">much better IR photos.</a> If you actually build your own IR filter-goggles, the view will be <em>somewhat</em> similar to those shown below. Also it will be very different, since human eyes aren't the same as cameras. Even more important, the IR goggles allow you to walk around in the infrared world. These photos don't communicate the odd experience of seeing through infrared eyes.     <a href="http://amasci.com/amateur/irgoggA.html#dn1">[SCROLL DOWN]</a><br />
