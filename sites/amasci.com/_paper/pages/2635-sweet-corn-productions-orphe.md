<td>The great poet Ophée seeks only the truth. However, he stumbles when seeking truth from a horse. He is convinced that the horse uses his hooves to tap out secret messages.
<div data-align="center">
<img src="s-o-1.jpg" data-border="0" width="432" height="335" alt="Orphe him self." /><br />
William Skurski — Ophée’s opening soliloquy.<br />
&#10;</div>
Upon the death of Eurydice, his wife, Ophée vows to seek his lost love in the underworld. The angel, Heurtebise (who works as a glazier for a living), guides Ophée through Hades.
<div data-align="center">
<img src="s-o-2.jpg" data-border="0" width="288" height="310" alt="Orphe takes the plung." /><br />
William Skurski, Richard White<br />
Ophée is shown the way into hell by the angel.<br />
The portal is the mirror.
</div>
Finding his wife, he is allowed to bring her back to the world of the living, only on the condition he never look at her again. If he does, she will be instantly transported back to Hades.
<div data-align="center">
<img src="s-o-3.jpg" data-border="0" width="432" height="270" alt="Orphe makes a boo-boo." /><br />
William Skurski, Patricia Coomey<br />
Ophée inadvertently looks at his wife and thereby kills a dead woman.
</div>
<div data-align="center">
<img src="s-o-4.jpg" data-border="0" width="432" height="271" alt="Cast of Orphe." /><br />
John T. Sheedy (Azreal), Michael Leininger (Raphael), Katherine Thomas (Death),<br />
Richard White (Heurtebise), Patricia Coomey (Eurydice), William Skurski (Ophée),<br />
Richard Lizza (Commissioner of Police), Jean Zampiceni (Secretary to the Police)
</div>
<strong>Ophée</strong> is a first-class example of surrealist-dada theater. Jean Cocteau wrote this and other variations on the Orpheus legends in many of his plays and films.
<p>The set was by Norman Archambault. <strong>Ophée</strong> was performed in Boston, December 1965.</p></td>
