<br />
As a reminder: Almost every sketch in my sketchbook was non-commissioned and done on the floor of a con. These are not the best works of the artists. These are only representative of what the artist can do under pressure in a loud, crowded, and often stressed-out environment. Most of them were done as quickly as possible, to prevent huge lines from forming. Don't judge any of these artists negatively by the artwork you see, instead be as impressed as I am by what they accomplished in far-from-ideal conditions.<br />

<p>I was mildly surprised to see that my weight hadn't changed at all from last week's weigh-in. I've felt like I've been slipping over the last week. I've been hungry all the time and although I did plenty of exercise, it wasn't the same as all the walking I did at the pugless house. And yet, I somehow maintained instead of gained. That made me feel pretty good.<br />
<br />
The next step is to figure out how to tackle the craving problem. I think it's all in my head, since I'm not feeling any physical hunger. In fact, one of the worst ones hit right after I'd finished a decent-sized dinner. Having dessert usually takes care of it, but I'd like to reduce the number of desserts I have. The water trick doesn't seem to work on a full stomach (get out a bottle of water and tell myself that I can eat what I'm craving after I finish the water) or with the unspecific craving. Something is signalling my brain saying "I'm hungry!" when I'm not really. If I can figure out how to fool/suppress/bypass/endure that obnoxious signal, then staying on the diet will be much easier.<br />

<strong>Rapid Reviews - Dorothy of Oz</strong>
<p><span class="rss:item">Hubby-Eric's new comic book version of Oz has arrived, and I think it's worth reviewing...<br />
<br />
<img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/dorothyofoz01.jpg" data-align="right" data-border="0" width="100" height="150" alt="cover" /><strong><a href="http://www.dorothyofoz.net/">Dorothy</a></strong>: As I mentioned when I first brought this up, it's a dark version of "The Wizard of Oz". A very dark version. Dorothy is a sixteen year-old girl with a slightly goth look who hotwires a truck in the first few pages. This is not Baum's vision, but that's ok. It's a vision all its own, and I actually like it quite a bit. The comic is made up of studio photos enhanced and turned into artwork, which is jarring in some places at first. The model playing Dorothy fits the role nicely. Once you get used to the style, the art works very nicely and enhances the mood of the story better than traditional art might. It's got a very slick, very cool look to it. All of that wouldn't be worth a nickel if the story wasn't also strong... which isn't a problem. The story is a nice twist on the original tale, with the whole tornado thing happening and everything. But there's no good witch awaiting Dorothy's arrival, and things in Oz seem a bit amiss. I can't recommend this to hardcore Oz traditionalists, but any Oz fan with an open mind and just about any regular comic book fan ought to enjoy this book. If you're on the hunt for something different, <a href="http://www.dorothyofoz.net/store.html">try it</a>. Recommended. <a href="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/rapidreviews.html"><img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/4_0_starfish.jpg" data-border="0" width="75" height="15" alt="4 starfish" /></a><br />
<br />

<br />
I mentioned hardcore Oz fans would not like this. I mean it. I know of at least one hardcore Oz fan who would likely have a hissy fit if he saw this. That's his problem. I liked it. But don't get it for your favorite Oz fan unless you know ahead of time that they don't mind alternate versions of Oz.<br />
<br />
I <em>know</em> there's a word for the use of photos in a comic book form, but I can't for the life of me remember it. This isn't simply photos, though, the photos are enhanced with backgrounds added. I particularly like the enhancement of the color on Dorothy's eyes. Very nicely done.<br />
<br />
I absolutely love the next line after the good old I-don't-think-we're-in-Kansas type line. That and another scene got a good laugh out of me.<br />
<br />
If you want to see a good page on Oz, go visit <a href="http://www.eskimo.com/~tiktok/">The Wonderful Wizard of Oz Website</a>, aka hubby-Eric's passion.<br />

Some of the students in Marysville School District are walking out (<a href="http://www.king5.com/topstories/stories/NW_062104WABstudentstrikersSW.283c44cea.html">KING 5 News</a>) claiming that <em>"The teachers set the example so it's all right for them to be out here," said one mother.</em> As long as they understand that there are consequences for their actions, as there were for the teachers, I don't have a problem with the kids walking out. I mean, if their summer is so important that they are willing to repeat a grade, more power to them. I hope they enjoy failing all their classes due to non-attendance. And if the mother who thinks it's ok for the kids to "strike" complains when her precious little brats fail their classes, well maybe she ought to go back to school herself. State law requires 180 days of school between two dates, it never once says school days can't happen in the summer.<br />
<br />
Regarding Ray Bradbury's whining about Moore "using his title", <a href="http://www.boingboing.net/2004/06/04/bradbury_goes_nuts_o.html">Boing Boing</a> has the best response I've seen yet. It's pointed out that Bradbury took the title of <em>I Sing The Body Electric</em> direct from a Walt Whitman poem. Shame! He should "give the title back" to Whitman! But that's not all, no! <em>Something Wicked This Way Comes</em> was lifted straight from Shakespeare! My goodness, Bradbury is as bad as Moore! But that's not all! Many more examples of Bradbury's shameless title stealing can be found <a href="http://www.brianstorms.com/archives/000372.html">here</a>. Either Bradbury is pulling a "Moore" to get attention for upcoming reissues of <em>Fahrenheit 451</em>, or, if he's serious, he's a freaking hypocrite. I really hope it's the former, as it would be nice to see Moore hoist by his own petard.<br />
<br />
CNN's coverage of the launch of <a href="http://www.scaled.com/">SpaceShipOne</a> was utterly useless. They kept showing file footage, then were so busy talking about other things during the actual lift-off that they didn't indicate it was live. I finally got smart and turned over to MSNBC and got actual coverage. I went back to CNN for the touchdown, though, as they had Bert Rutan there on camera. Michael Melvill is the first non-government sponsored astronaut. Here's a little coverage from <a href="http://space.com/missionlaunches/SS1_touchdown_040621.html">space.com</a>.<br />

<strong>Rapid Reviews - AIT/Planet Lar</strong>
<p><span class="rss:item">Although I haven't finished off everything in the original <a href="http://www.ait-planetlar.com/">AIT/Planet Lar</a> box that was sent to me ages ago by Larry Young, I've read these two more recent offerings that Larry sent along so I'll share my thoughts on them now: <a href="http://www.ait-planetlar.com/hench.shtml">Hench</a> and <a href="http://www.ait-planetlar.com/tsstg100.shtml">True Story, Swear to God: 100 Stories</a>.<br />
<br />
<img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/shop_hench.jpg" data-align="right" data-border="0" width="100" height="150" alt="cover" /><strong><a href="http://www.amazon.com/exec/obidos/ASIN/1932051171/theaquamanwebsit">Hench</a></strong> by Adam Beechen: This is the story of a normal enough guy who gets into the sometimes lucrative but always thrilling job of being a henchman. The motives for getting into a job as a criminal seem understandable, and the results are pretty predictable. The artwork is servicable but uninspiring... but the story is so strong as to make up for a lot more faults than that. The story is all about the type of people who become heroes and villians, despite appearing to be about henchmen. We get to see them all through the eyes of a person who gets to experience both sides of the battle. And while the good guys might fight the bad guys, they aren't always good. Well, the artwork is the only thing wrong with the book, really. And that's not a bad problem... this one is definitely worth checking out. <a href="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/rapidreviews.html"><img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/4_0_starfish.jpg" data-border="0" width="75" height="15" alt="4 starfish" /></a><br />
<br />
<img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/shop_tsstg100.jpg" data-align="right" data-border="0" width="150" height="100" alt="cover" /><strong><a href="http://www.amazon.com/exec/obidos/ASIN/193205121X/theaquamanwebsit">True Story, Swear to God: 100 Stories</a></strong> by Tom Beland: This one... get this. I mean, go out and buy this RIGHT NOW. Don't hesitate. Just go get it. It's wonderful. The art style is simple and classic, and the stories are hilarious. Each page is a tale on its own, so it's a book you can read in easy chunks. While some of the stories are a wee bit adult... overall the quality is so high I loved it completely. Go get it. <a href="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/rapidreviews.html"><img src="http://www.eskimo.com/~tegan/blog/blogpics/4_5_starfish.jpg" data-border="0" width="75" height="15" alt="4 1/2 starfish" /></a><br />
