## Meeting Richard Morgan

\

Tuesday, March 23, 2004, 7 p.m., University Bookstore, Seattle, WA, <a href="http://www.richardkmorgan.com" target="blank">Richard K Morgan</a> read from his newest US release, *Broken Angels*, and his latest UK release, *Market Forces*. Any inaccuracies or bad quotes are strictly my fault.

\

Crammed between the Travel books and the miscellanea of Humor, Transportation and Comic Books, 20 people sit in rows of plastic chairs waiting on the voice of Richard K. Morgan. He's back there at the Travel shelves, leaning on the ranks of France and sipping from a capped latte cup. Nearly everyone has their backs to him and they pretend polite interest in what the Science Fiction Manager is saying about upcoming events and writers. Me, I'm interested in Morgan. He catches me looking at him and looks back and smiles. He almost seems about to laugh, as if we're sharing a joke. And maybe we are. He's a good-looking devil, the spit and image of his jacket photo from *Altered Carbon*, except for that grin, and he plainly doesn't take this "rising young star of Future Noir" stuff too far to heart.

Once the microphone is properly adjusted, Morgan dumps his jacket and admits he'd rather chat and answer questions than read aloud since, having been an ELT teacher for 14 years, he prefers to talk with people rather than at them. He reads a surprisingly long passage from *Broken Angels*, describing the potential team-members the protagonist, Takeshi Kovacs, is selecting.

The prose is fierce and clean, but his reading is conversational, voice clear and warm with his Norfolk accent. He punches his dry wit subtly, pauses for effect, makes an occasional mug. His eyes sparkle when he looks up at us. He's funny and understated and the grin reappears once he's done. Then he pulls up a chair, straddles it and says "All right, now for the questions!"

He is eager. His face lights up with every query, even though he's fielded them all before. He gives each reply thought before releasing it. Were you making a moral statement? "Yes and no...." Why did you single out the Catholic Church? "I needed a monolithic religion which could serve this position and the Catholic Church is very successful at being monolithic." What about the violence? "It's important that we see violence for what it is and be sickened, not dress it up in phoney images which make it OK... and of course, there is the adrenaline rush that comes from violence, the sick delight we take in it. So, perhaps I'm a bit sick and twisted, myself." More info demanded about the Cortical Stack and how people could stand to adjust to new bodies, further implications of resleeving, questions about the weapons, about religion and philosophy, about Quell, about Kovacs, about profanity.... Morgan breaks down his technology, his world and his character and shows the glittering shards in the light of conversation. This is like the best toy in the world, even better when shared. He is charming and profane, generous and blunt. Considering that the books are, as he says, a protest against oppressive systems, he should be. He speaks so easily, casually, and with such friendliness that it would be hard to be offended, even if he weren't in a crowd of instant friends.

I ask him about the influences which created this piece of [Tech Noir](TechNoir.html), as I call it, and he responds with my own reasons and a lot of my own reading list of Science Fiction and Noir Crime, though his emphasis is on the Science Fiction list more than mine. He is thrilled at the idea that I and others have been seeking this amalgam of hard-boiled pulp and Science Fiction. He promises more and hopes to see more from others because he writes the books he'd like to read and this is it. There's a new book in the UK, which is not in the series, but a third book in the Kovacs series is "marinading," he assures me. And it is me he seems to speak to. He has that ability to give attention to his interlocutor as if there is no one else, while including everyone around him. I wonder if he is as stellar facing a larger group, but it doesn't really matter, since he is here, now, and there doesn't seem to be any immediate press of other things. If the bookstore didn't close, we'd be here all night and be happy.

Finally, he reads the prologue page from *Market Forces*, the new UK book which will be released in the US in 2005. It's short, nasty and damned perfect.

Then we're all milling about, asking for his signature, exchanging a few words. Again the feeling of being his focus and a cause for delight. I would love to keep on chatting, as we seem to have tastes and goals in common and he is excited when I say I write a bit in the same field. But I'm not yet published, so I can't offer him a book in return and I have a bus to catch.

On the bus, I read the inscription on the title page of *Altered Carbon*: "Thanks for coming and grilling me." He prefers questions.
