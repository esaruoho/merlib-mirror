<a href="ney/">Ney Family History</a><br />
Final paper for a year-long genealogy certificate program at the University of Washington about my Luxembourg ancestors, John Peter Ney (1851-1920) and Margaret Mans (1854-1926), who settled in the town of Holy Cross in Ozaukee County, Wisconsin.
<a href="http://www.geocities.com/SiliconValley/Foothills/9637/ottelehp.htm">The Ottelé Genealogy</a>
<a href="http://home.att.net/~mattpenning/wsb/index.html">The Penning Family</a><br />
Descendents of Johannes Peter Penning born in Schoenfels in 1771. Click on "Links" for genealogy information.
<a href="http://www.pletschette.net/">Pletschette Family Tree</a>
<a href="http://possley.family-history.com/">The Possley/Poncelet Family</a>
<a href="http://www.genealogy.com/users/p/r/o/Tom-Prost/">The Thomas Prost, Jr. Family</a><br />
Tom Prost's great grandparents, Henry Prost and Helen Obladen, settled in Chicago, Illinois. Ancestral Luxembourg villages include Niederdonven, Oberdonven and Gostingen, as well as Nittel, Germany. Allied surnames include: Beckius, Bertrang, Boltz, Courte, Courth, Courthe, Demuth, Godar, Grosch, Hanzler, Hienkes, Kafs, Kass, Klein, Lentz, Medernach, Mesenburg, Molitor, Peters, Rech, Roob, Roos, Schartz, Schritz, Scholtes, Steil, Ulveling, Wagner, Waldgen, Wathier, Wattger, Weber, Weyer.
<a href="http://www.genealogy.com/users/p/u/l/William-G-Pull/index.html">The Poull (Pull) Home Page</a><br />
William Pull's descendants of Peter Poull (Pull) page.
<a href="http://members.aol.com/DaveZero/Index.html">Dave Schmit's Genealogy Home Page</a><br />
Researching Luxembourg surnames: Schmit, Marson, Schons, Goergen, DeVillet and Thill.
<a href="http://www.genealogy.com/users/s/c/h/Bill-Schuetter/">The Schuetter &amp; Schafer Family Home Page</a><br />
Names include: Biever/Biewer, Turpel, Grasser and Conzemius. Emigrated from Erpeldange and Ettelbruck in Luxembourg to Chicago, IL.
<a href="http://www.genealogy.com/users/s/c/h/Roland-Schumacher/">Shumacher Family Home Page</a><br />
Beginning in Senningen, Luxembourg about 1651.
<a href="http://www.genealogy.com/users/t/h/i/David-M-Thilges/">All Things Thilges</a>
<a href="/~lisanne/uselding/">Uselding Genealogy</a><br />
Genealogy and local history references to the Uselding surname.
<a href="http://homepages.rootsweb.com/~imrie/">Our Family Tree: Imrie-Wampach Genealogy</a><br />
Linda and Doug Wampach's web site.
<a href="http://members.iquest.net/~joemilr/england.htm">Wenner - Pommes</a><br />
Joe Miller's web site documents the experience of the S.S. England which was devasted by a cholera outbreak onboard in the spring of 1866 and was subsequently quarantined in Nova Scotia and New York. Among the passengers was his great-great grandmother Margaret Wenner and her brother Henry. Margaret later married Nicholas Pommes - a Luxembourg immigrant himself - in Worthington, Iowa. Margaret was orginally from Trintingen, Luxembourg, and other surnames include Simminger and Mousel.
<a href="http://wester.family-history.com/">The Wester Family</a>
Susanna Simmerl Youngblut<br />
<a href="http://www.intech.mnsu.edu/bunkers/">Suzanne L. Bunkers'</a> book <a href="http://www.english.mankato.msus.edu/publications/search.html">In Search of Susanna</a> details her search for an elusive great-great-grandmother and her own self-discoveries along the way.
<p><em>Susanna Simmerl was born on April 2, 1831, in Oberfeulen, Luxembourg. Her first child, a daughter, Barbara Simmerl, was born there on December 30, 1856. Susanna immigrated to the U.S. in the spring of 1857, along with her brother Peter Simmerl. In December 1857, Susanna married Frank Youngblut.</em></p>
