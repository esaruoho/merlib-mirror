<h2 id="find-other-musicians-to-jam-with">Find other musicians to jam with</h2>
<p>Are you looking for other musicians to play with and hang out with? Are you looking for help starting a jam? Check the messages on our <a href="http://www.guitarnoise.com/%7Eogre/cgi-bin/yabb/YaBB.cgi?board=jamming">jamming forum</a> or post your own.</p>
<p> </p>
<p><strong><a href="20001102.html">Build Your Own Band Buffet</a></strong><br />
<span class="smauthorname">By David Hodge</span><br />
I have become addicted to jams. Yeah, I know that between teaching and practice I play virtually every day. But there is something very special about playing with other people. My life can be divided into three phases: either I am playing in a jam, remembering and (over) analyzing the last jam or fully anticipating and/or planning the next one.</p>
<p><strong><a href="../../otherside/20001122.html">Music at Mary's</a></strong><br />
<span class="smauthorname">By Lee Budar-Danoff</span><br />
Somewhere in Maryland Mary has been hosting "pot luck" jams at her home. Everyone brings some food and their instruments and music, and then they all see what they can do! Mary provides name tags and beverages.</p>
<p><a href="../../articles/20011103.html"><strong>Getting Up On The Stage</strong></a><br />
<span class="smauthorname">By John Carrahar</span><br />
In the U.K.a musician who plays for spare change in the streets or in the subway is known as a busker. Various bars in my area hold jamming sessions known locally as “Busker’s Nights.” On these nights anyone can get up on stage, and sing a few songs. I’ve been asked to give tips to people who may be feeling nervous about poerforming for the first time.</p>
<p><a href="../../articles/20011026.html"><strong>Talent Showcases and Open Stage Nites</strong></a><br />
<span class="smauthorname">By Alan Horvath</span><br />
Many local clubs feature an "open mic" venue, where songwriters and musicians of all kinds can get their feet wet and see what it takes to pull off a show of your own.</p>
