<td rowspan="3" width="382" height="338" data-valign="TOP"><p><img src="images/posture.jpg" data-align="left" width="222" height="158" />Pedro, the dark male in the kush position (that is their lying down position with all four legs folded under, like a card table) in the center of the picture, is our oldest gelding, almost 7. Drake is trying to approach Pedro, and Pedro is letting him know to get away. Notice that Pedro has his ears back, head elevated and turned to an angle...that means "If you come any closer I am going to Spit on you!" Drake has his neck lowered, his tail raised over his back, and is looking away...his body language means "ok, ok, please don't spit on me, it's humiliating and smells bad".</p>

<td><p><img src="images/posture2.jpg" data-align="LEFT" width="283" height="154" />There are several people walking towards the fence and these boys know they are about to get a treat. They are preparing to try to intimidate each other to see who gets the food first. Notice how they all have their ears back. As they become more threatening to each other, they will raise their nose in the air and threaten to spit. If the threat doesn't get them the position they want, they are very likely to spit at the other llama. Charlie is about two in this picture and believe me he will be the "first" to get his treat!!!</p>
<p><img src="images/posture3.jpg" data-align="RIGHT" width="288" height="158" />These boys are alert and curious...notice the ears straight up, tail down and necks tall. They are standing in a pathway that runs along the side of our house and between the two pastures. They can see Jerry through the dinning room window and are curious about what he is doing. It isn't uncommon for all of them to come stand in this area to look for us especially if they want some food. Talk about making you feel guilty!!!</p></td>
