The events of September 11, 2001 made clear the most severe threat to American free enterprise in history.

The suicide missions using hijacked airliners targeted the twin towers of the World Trade Center as the most visible symbol of capitalism and the Pentagon as the center of U.S. military power. The hijacked plane in which passengers overwhelmed the terrorists and crashed in Pennsylvania may have been targeted at the White House or Capitol building.

The Islamist terrorists who carried out the September 11 attacks represent an ideology that seeks the destruction of capitalism and its greatest symbol, the United States, through *jihad*, or "religious war."

President Bush responded with America's War on Terrorism. Congress passed and the President signed the USA PATRIOT Act anti-terrorism bill. The Center strongly supports the Congress and the President in the War on Terrorism, recognizing the dangers to civil liberties in wartime legislation but supporting wartime law enforcement capabilities and scrutinizing the application of the law for abuses.

Islamist religious fanaticism poses a far more dangerous threat to free enterprise than communism during the Soviet era in Russia. It is imperative for an informed American citizenry to know this enemy. The term **Islamist** is used is the sense defined in the **<a href="islamism.htm" style="text-decoration: none">Encyclopeadia of the Orient</a>**. In brief: Islamism is opposed to secular democratic governments and institutions; is opposed to non-Muslim culture, values and art; is opposed to both capitalism and socialism in most forms; feels inferiority since the decline of the great Muslim empires that once ruled the known world; tends towards dreams and delusions of grandeur; is fascinated by modern technology; uses violence to achieve its goals.

**Islamic leaders caution that Islamist fanaticism is not representative of the views of most Muslims**.
