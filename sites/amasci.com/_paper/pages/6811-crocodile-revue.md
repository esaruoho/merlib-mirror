# Crocodile Revue

*Source page: `~redone/swrong/rev3.html`*

Sick and Wrong at the Crocodile Cafe (Seattle) 12/2/95

      I usually consider it unseemly to rave.  I try not to do it in person or
    in correspondence or in writing... but I'm going to make an exception-
    here in this I am going to rave.
      You shoulda been there.  This had to be one of the best shows I've seen
    this year.  Certainly as far as Seattle rock bands go nothing I've seen has
    surpassed it.  I took my friend Sylvia and even she was impressed.
      If you've read my previous reviews of Sick and Wrong you know what I
    think of The Crocodile Cafe - I like it.  The staff - particularly those
    watching the doors are not renown for their manners, but considering the
    frat-boy crowds they deal with it's understandable.  On this evening they
    had to deal with a police order to clear the front dining area because of a
    bomb threat across the street.  Whoever was in charge handled it admirably
    routing the public traffic through the kitchen and not closing down the
    show  They have my gratitude.
     This show was a benefit for Home Safe - a very worthy organization that
    provides free safe ways to get home for anybody.  The evening started with
    a performance of the play "A Very Bitchy Christmas."  I didn't attend this,
    waiting for the bands that were scheduled later in the evening.  Even
    though I didn't see the play or the actors I thank them and all the people
    involved for their efforts on behalf of Home Safe.  It is a people idea
    that can make a difference to those with real needs and it should be
    supported in any way possible.
     The lineup started off with Rouge Lipstick (AKA Lylith) doing passable
    Gothic Grunge rock.  They are good enough that I could be happy listening
    to them most nights.
      The lead is a better singer than dancer.  Her voice has a fine tone and
    can be quite lovely.  She could do a falsetto and a deep growl that made me
    check to be sure it was a woman singing - her burlesque costume was quite
    fetching and left no room for doubt.  In spite of the impressive range of
    her voice it seemed to me that there were gaps where she never ventured.
      The lead guitar had marvelous (mostly electronic) control over the sound
    of his instrument.  His control over his guitar strings was somewhat less,
    but still passable.  The drummer was the best of the lot.  I can't condemn
    any of it.  It was all entertaining even if they were just a warm-up band.
    I will listen to them again when the opportunity presents itself.
      Rouge Lipstick was followed by Girl With a Thousand Heads.  There are
    three good guitars here that have played together enough that they know
    each others music.  The harmonies between them are precious to hear.  The
    singer is loud and strong and talented though without the range of the
    previous lead.  The rocking guitars more than made up for the drumming
    which compared poorly with the other two drummers who set on the bandstand
    that night.  I could be amused listening these people.  I will also see
    this band again if the rock Gods be willing.
      Then it was Sick and Wrong's turn.  I would feel dishonest if I didn't
    mention that since I last wrote about this band I have become acquainted
    with some of them socially.  I think of them as friends so perhaps that
    colors my opinions... or perhaps not.
     I was somewhat apprehensive because I had built them up to Sylvia and I'd
    never seen them in close comparison to other talent.  Maybe following two
    good bands inspires them.  Maybe they've been practicing more.  Whatever it
    was I needn't have given a thought to their not living up to my billing.
    They played marvelously with all the energy I remember.
     If Young John Pimp had a bad night the last time I saw them then he had
    an exceptional night this time.  He drummed his ass off and it sounded very
    fine.  They all did.  Adam, Static and Muddy played as good as I've heard
    them play.  If there were glitches here I didn't see or hear them.  They
    sounded great.  Nobody forgot where they were.  *Everybody* was having a
    great time.  Rave RAve RAVE!  The guy on the mixer board even got the
    levels mostly right.  The crowd was appreciative and involved - at one
    point it was even necessary to douse a couple with water after they showed
    signs of getting a little too involved.
      When I first heard Sick and Wrong I thought they would loose their raw
    energy if they polished their act.  I was wrong.  They segued from setup
    into "Pink Paint" without the audience being the wiser till the band clued
    them in.  Only a band that has the background of this one could write a
    song like that... and only a person as twisted as Mr. Wendy would do it.  
    I like her for that.
     Adam Bratman's on-stage acrobatic jumps and dance were all appropriate
    and well executed.  He has spent time watching the masters of rock guitar
    and practiced their moves.  But that is not his strong point.  His lead
    guitar is and he's getting better - more together with the rhythm section.
    His duets with Muddy Checker were a marvel.
      Muddy's bass line on Uncle Buck had everybody moving - he and Adam even
    did a Chuck walk across the bandstand.  The rest of the songs were just as
    good.
      Static's rhythm playing filled in all the riffs and countered Adam's lead
    with a chord at every turn - he even did a backup vocal - and all of it
    done at an unholy punk pace.  I like "Pink Paint" - it's a good tune and
    makes a great intro, but this band comes alive when they forget the slow
    tunes and I don't mind at all.
      Maybe it's cause I've been listening to their tape or maybe they're
    finally getting the mix right but I didn't have any problem understanding
    the lyrics.  Little Miss Farrah and Mr. Wendy have a vocal resonance that
    is both raw and sweet at one time.  No one in this band is afraid to use
    dissonance but it doesn't happen by accident.  These two play the
    differences in their voices to fine advantage.
      I have to remark once again on the humor these people bring to their act.
    There is a cutting satire here that is rare in any music and exceptional
    to find in punk.  Mr. Wendy's and Farrah's costumes, Adam and Muddy's use
    of rock star moves - all are making a point as well as being highly
    entertaining.  Not only was I amused, I was grinning when I left.  None of
    the audience left early and most of them were grinning too.
      I will find a nit to complain about in the close of the set.  By
    comparison to the opening which verged on slick, it seemed to be lacking
    something.  The act is really together and everybody knows what to do
    through to the end and then there is just a slight feeling of being left
    hanging...  Perhaps a reprise of Pink Paint...?
     Maybe I'm nuts, but either they're getting better or I'm getting young
    and foolish.  Every time I hear them I get inspired to write one of these
    reviews.  They make me feel good.
      After the set I bought a T-shirt from Mr. Wendy and introduced Sylvia to
    her.  Muddy came up and said Hi when he saw me.  These are not only very
    talented people they are pleasant folk.  I intend to have fun listening to
    them play for as long as they can keep it together.  I'm pretty sure I'll
    enjoy every minute of it.
