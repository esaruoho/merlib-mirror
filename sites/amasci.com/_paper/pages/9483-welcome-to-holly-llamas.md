<tr>
<td></td>
<td><strong>Welcome to Holly Llamas. We're a small llama farm located about 20 miles north of Seattle. My husband and I have been raising llamas since 1987 when our two sons left home. Some have said it was empty nest syndrome. I think it was love at first sight!</strong></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td></td>
<td><strong>At the time our sons moved out, we had a couple of female pygmy goats, a male and two female Japanese sitka deer and a female burro. We have such limited space (only 1 3/4 acres) but had managed to raise a variety of animals while our sons were growing up. Our experiences with cows, a wonderful quarterhorse and other assorted livestock had given us some background in raising critters.</strong></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td></td>
<td><strong>We knew nothing about llamas, though, when an ad in a local newspaper for a llama for sale caught our eyes. We fell in love with Levi, a seven year old male llama, and brought him home. His gentle nature, intelligence and ease of care convinced us we needed to have more llamas. Within six months we bought two more and the deer, goats and burro were sold. Since then we've devoted our small farm to llamas.</strong></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td></td>
<td><strong>We limit our herd to around ten llamas. With some planning and a lot of crossfencing, this number is manageable for us and allows us to devote time to each llama. We breed for healthy, conformationally correct llamas with that silky fleece we admire. We have both North American and Bolivian crosses. Our young herd sire <a href="samba.htm">One Note Samba</a> is just getting old enough to breed. We're excited about seeing what he will produce. We will also share our experience with you in our search for a herd sire.</strong></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td></td>
<td><strong>We would also like to invite you to visit <a href="chena.htm">Chena and her new cria</a>. You can find out a little about birthing and newborn care here.</strong></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td></td>
<td><strong>You may also want to see another of our sweet females, <a href="jazz.htm">Jazz,</a> and learn about our training methods and some of our herd management procedures.</strong></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td></td>
<td><strong>Please stop by Wren's page. She's our <a href="wren.htm">"Christmas Llama"</a> and has information about what folks like to do with their llamas.</strong></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td></td>
<td><strong>From time to time we're able to offer some very special llamas to others. Our <a href="sale.htm">sale page</a> reflects what we currently have available and our <a href="adopt.htm">adopted llamas</a> page includes those who have recently found new homes.</strong></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td></td>
<td><strong>The brothers in the photo at the top of this page are Bailey and Scooter. Bailey is our priceless gelding. Calm, friendly and serious about protecting the young llamas he sometimes has to baby-sit, he is a favorite with all who visit our farm. (He actually thinks he manages the place!)</strong></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td></td>
<td><strong>Don't forget to check out our <a href="links.htm">friends</a> who have llamas, folks who sell llama products or publications and associations you may join to learn more about llamas.</strong> <img src="ananew4.gif" /> <strong>We now have links to other <a href="other.htm">interesting animals</a> we're sure you'll enjoy visiting.</strong></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td></td>
<td><strong>You can find out more about our first llama <a href="http://www.eskimo.com/~wallama/memorial/">Levi</a>, but be sure to bring a box of kleenex if you visit this site.</strong></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td></td>
<td><strong>Thank you for stopping by our website. Please also stop by our farm or give us a call, or<br />
<a href="mailto:jnhardman@webtv.com">e-mail.</a> We are always happy to talk with fellow llama llovers!</strong></td>
</tr>
