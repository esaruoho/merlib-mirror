**Transplanted Wildflower on California Housing Site\
Prompts Criminal Investigation**

 

Authorities in California have accused environmentalists of dirty tricks after discovering endangered wildflowers on a site scheduled for development. The state Department of Fish and Game has launched a criminal investigation into the sudden appearance of limnanthes vinculans, a low growing white wildflower commonly known as Sebastopol Meadowfoam, on the 21 acre Laguna Vista site in Sebastopol, near San Francisco.

 

Robert Evans, an opponent of the Laguna Vista housing development in Sebastopol reported finding specimens of the rare Sebastopol meadowfoam on the development site in April. Investigators determined that the 30 flowers did not grow on the site but had been transplanted.

 

"The location of the plants, the type of clusters that were found, the fact that they were on sloping areas that normally this type of plant is not found on, gave us the first indication," said Fish and Game's Troy Swauger. "To find this on a development site at this point indicates to us that they have been transplanted. We're considering this a criminal act."

 

The species, which grows less than a foot tall and has tiny, white flowers, has been found in less than thirty locations in seasonally wet meadows around the Sonoma County wetlands area known as the Laguna de Santa Rosa, according to a fish and game website.

 

Both federal and state authorities list the meadowfoam, as an endangered plant. Both possession of an endangered species and translocating an endangered plant species are illegal, said Swauger.

 

Environmentalists have opposed construction on the 21-acre patch of land because of its proximity to the wetlands area, which one city official said has already been greatly compromised by previous development in the county.

 

"Laguna de Santa Rosa is one of the most significant wetlands areas in Sonoma County," said Sebastopol city planning director Kenyon Webster, "a large watershed relatively diverse although severely compromised over the last 200 years through agricultural activity and development."

 

Webster said the state investigation sparked by the meadowfoam flowers has hampered the city's efforts to move forward with construction on the 145 units of housing.

 

"The additional environmental studies that we were starting to do and public review that we would have done will not occur," Webster said.

 

Swauger said whoever transplanted the meadowfoam could be slapped with a \$5,000 fine and face up to a year in county jail. Federal penalties for harming an endangered species could result in a \$50,000 fine for each incident and a year in federal prison.

 

"This isn't a standard poaching case, it's not a standard environmental violation case. This is something that's unusual," Swauger said.

 

Once its investigation is complete, Swogger said his department would turn its findings to the district attorney, who must then decide whether to press charges.
