<td rowspan="3" width="382" height="338" data-valign="TOP"><p>Our home offers us the best of both worlds, we are only 25 minutes outside of Seattle and nestled into a little valley that is like another world. Our ranch is about loving our dogs and llamas, being happy and feeling peace. We have enough llamas to enjoy them as pets yet without the work involved in having a large herd. Work is fun and healthy, making our life very comfortable to not only ourselves but to our many visitors.</p>
<p><em>(Click on Photo to enlarge)</em><a href="ranch1.html" onclick="window.open(&#39;ranch1.html&#39;,&#39;&#39;,&#39;width=360,height=290,scrollbars=NO,menubar=NO,toolbar=NO,location=NO&#39;);return false"><img src="images/ranch1sml.jpg" data-align="left" data-border="0" width="72" height="58" alt="Upper Property" /></a><br />
Between our house and street we have a large Cedar grove, this area makes for excellent protection on rainy days. At the end of each day the llamas return to this area to spend the night. You can depend on seeing them all moving towards the grove where they enjoy spending the early evening watching passing traffic. Many people stop and visit with the boys, some are regular visitors.</p>
<p><em>(Click on Photo to enlarge)</em><a href="ranch2.html" onclick="window.open(&#39;ranch2.html&#39;,&#39;&#39;,&#39;width=438,height=278,scrollbars=NO,menubar=NO,toolbar=NO,location=NO&#39;);return false"><img src="images/ranch2sml.jpg" data-align="left" data-border="0" width="88" height="56" alt="Upper Property" /></a><br />
Next is the area where we have our barn and lawn just above the house. The llamas have a walkway between our house and 2 pastures. You can see the side of the barn on the left and the corner of the garage in the lower right side of the photo.</p>
<p>The barn is only used for feeding and when it is necessary to contain a llama health care. The llamas choose never to sleep in the barn because the prefer the openness of the cedar grove. We also have never had to clean anything out of the barn except for occasional old hay. The boys have several communal dung piles around the pasture and don't want to leave messes in the barn:^)</p>

<td colspan="2"><p><em>(Click on Photo to enlarge)</em><a href="ranch3.html" onclick="window.open(&#39;ranch3.html&#39;,&#39;&#39;,&#39;width=448,height=298,scrollbars=NO,menubar=NO,toolbar=NO,location=NO&#39;);return false"><img src="images/ranch3sml.jpg" data-align="left" data-border="0" width="90" height="60" alt="backyard" /></a>From the barn you down past the garage and along the side of the main house. This is our patio which we share with the llamas on special occasions or when we just want to enjoy their company for a few hours. They love being in the back yard, kushing on the patio and taking a nap in the early afternoon. It's fun to have dinner on the patio on a nice warm evening surrounded by humming llamas.<br />
</p>
<p><em>(Click on Photo to enlarge)</em><a href="ranch4.html" onclick="window.open(&#39;ranch4.html&#39;,&#39;&#39;,&#39;width=430,height=280,scrollbars=NO,menubar=NO,toolbar=NO,location=NO&#39;);return false"><img src="images/ranch4sml.jpg" data-align="left" data-border="0" width="86" height="56" alt="backyard" /></a> Sitting on our patio we can look down to our favorite portion of the pasture. We have many barbecues and picnics under that big willow tree with a clean stream running through the pasture right behind the tree. Every year we have our 4th of July picnic in this area ... people love the setting and having the llamas walk up to them for snacks. Then there are the other llamas that are constant searching guests for hidden treats.<br />
</p>
<p><em>(Click on Photo to enlarge)</em><a href="ranch5.html" onclick="window.open(&#39;ranch5.html&#39;,&#39;&#39;,&#39;width=360,height=243,scrollbars=NO,menubar=NO,toolbar=NO,location=NO&#39;);return false"><img src="images/ranch5sml.jpg" data-align="left" data-border="0" width="108" height="73" alt="Creek" /></a><br />
Here is the far side of the pasture, the creek runs the entire distance of the pasture and the salmon run the creek year to spawn. The llamas always have fresh water to drink and a place to cool off when its to warm. Our llamas share their pasture with a wealth of wildlife. The Canadian Geese come in the spring and we usually have 2 or 3 family of baby geese each year. We also have the wild ducks, bald eagles, blue herron and the red tail hawks. Each year we get an occasional glimpse of the new baby beavers and when we are really lucky we get to see the mama beaver.</p></td>
