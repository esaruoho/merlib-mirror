<tr>
<td data-valign="top">1.</td>
<td data-valign="top">Correct!</td>
<td data-valign="top">Posters advertising Coke and the Bon Marche, homework assignments with Mission Macaroni logos, in-class TV commercials for M &amp;Ms, assemblies promoting the sale of Fundwraps wrapping paper, "Coyote Jr. High" salespeople making pitches during social studies classes, curriculum materials sponsored by Hershey's chocolate (showing the role of chocolate in the food pyramid), school newsletters with Campbell's logos, book covers advertising McDonald's, vending machines with Snapple signs, and reader boards with Pepsi logos have already crept into the Seattle Public Schools.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td data-valign="top">2.</td>
<td data-valign="top">Correct!</td>
<td data-valign="top">In 7 out of 10 middle schools and 4 out of 10 high schools, Seattle students are required to watch 2 minutes of TV commercials every day in class. These are ads for soft drinks, gum, candy, sneakers, etc., pitched to the early adolescent market. By contract, all teachers must show the program and may not turn the sound down. By law students must attend school. Every day...</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td data-valign="top">3.</td>
<td data-valign="top">Correct!</td>
<td data-valign="top">Any revenue generated from the sale of ads would go into the General Fund, which faces a $35 million shortfall over the next three years, just to pay its usual bills. Anything under $35 million will not affect class sizes.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td data-valign="top">4.</td>
<td data-valign="top">Correct!</td>
<td data-valign="top">In Seattle, each principal decides whether or not to sign a contract with Channel One and whether or not to renew it after each three year period. Channel One is a 12-minute, commercially-produced TV infotainment show with 2 minutes of ads geared to teens. Schools with Channel One cede control of twelve minutes of curriculum time a day to those with a narrow commercial interest. That adds up to 6 taxpayer-funded school days per student per year.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td data-valign="top">5.</td>
<td data-valign="top">Correct!</td>
<td data-valign="top">Whereas textbooks must be reviewed by a textbook committee composed of subject matter experts in the district, the sponsored classroom materials simply show up in the teachers' mailboxes and are used at the discretion of the individual teacher, who may or may not have analyzed the scientific basis of the claims made in the materials.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td data-valign="top">6.</td>
<td data-valign="top">Correct!</td>
<td data-valign="top">Any revenue generated form the sale of ads would go into the General Fund, where it could be used for anything. There is no guarantee that advertising revenues generated at a band concert will go exclusively to support the band, or even to support the band at all. Under the new advertising policy, extra-curricular groups will not be allowed to pursue sponsorship agreements on their own.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td data-valign="top">7.</td>
<td data-valign="top">Correct!</td>
<td data-valign="top">Any revenue generated form the sale of ads would go into the General Fund. The district does not have the funds to pay for existing programs, much less new programs. Further, there are already many after-school programs for Seattle youth funded by the Families and Education Levy and non-profit organizations such as Rotary and the Boys and Girls Clubs. Many are under-enrolled.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td data-valign="top">8.</td>
<td data-valign="top">Correct!</td>
<td data-valign="top">The only other major district in the U.S. which permits corporate ads is Colorado Springs. In its first year, district was able to raise only $73,150 and in its dsecond year, by expanding advertising into the elementary schools, raised only $85,265, even after plastering school buses and school walls with advertising. The Seattle School District estimates that it needs $35 million over the next 3 years.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td data-valign="top">9.</td>
<td data-valign="top">Correct!</td>
<td data-valign="top">Law makers are equally likely to conclude that districts with corporate sponsors will no longer need taxpayer funds.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td data-valign="top">10.</td>
<td data-valign="top">Correct!</td>
<td data-valign="top">Although most state funds for schools are earmarked for certain uses, a school district may petition to use the funds differently. Most districts do not make use of this waiver option.</td>
</tr>

<td data-valign="top">11.</td>
<td data-valign="top">Correct!</td>
<td data-valign="top">The new policy does not address this issue.</td>
</tr>

<td data-valign="top">Correct!</td>
