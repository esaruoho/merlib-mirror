<td colspan="2" data-valign="TOP" width="80%" data-bgcolor="#ffffff"><p><strong>And a Happy New Year to all of you, of course!</strong></p>
<p>We spent our eve over at a friend's house where we mostly watched movies (first <em>Dogma</em>, then <em>Galaxy Quest</em>) and played Balderdash. It was a nice, calm night, actually. I had champagne, of course - I love the stuff - but alcohol didn't interest me much. I wasn't in the mood for it, really. I enjoyed the asti, all the same, but didn't even come close to a buzz of any sort.</p>
<p>About around 11:45pm we turned the television to Dick Clark, then all waited for the ball to drop. I've done so for many years and every time it occurs to me that it's a bit silly, considering that it's not a live broadcast, and that the new year came to New York three hours earlier than our west coast celebration. Oh well. Tradition, and all that.</p>
<p>After midnight I went outside to hang with Odessa while she smoked and we chatted, and discovered there was ice on the ground. Considering how late it was and how many drunk drivers there would be on the road, Mike and I opted to crash on a couch rather than driving home. Gave us more time to play Balderdash, anyhow.</p>
<p>As for resolutions... I didn't make any. I haven't made a New Year resolution in a long time. When you're an overweight person, the standard resolution is often This year I'll really lose weight... And then the yearly failure to do so makes things all that much more difficult. Continual failure isn't fun, eh? Well, I'd decided a long time ago, many years past, that it was probably healthier to simply enjoy my life as I am, no matter what weight. So I stopped dieting. It was quite a booster to my emotional health, I'll tell you. Then when I discovered a few years later that I was overweight because I'm diabetic, and not the other way around, it just made dealing with it all a lot easier.</p>
<p>So. No resolutions for Julie, except perhaps to Live Life To The Fullest. Yeah. That'll do.</p>
<p><strong>Last night the question came up, once again...</strong></p>
<p><em>How old is <a href="http://www.dickclarkproductions.com/html/dccbios/DCbio.html">Dick Clark</a>, really?</em></p>
<p>This afternoon I did a little research, just for the heck of it.</p>
<p>At the <a href="http://www.fiftiesweb.com/bandstnd.htm">Fifties Web</a> American Bandstand site, it says... <em>American Bandstand began as a local program in Philadelphia in 1952. Then it was called Bob Horn's Bandstand. In July of 1956 the show got a new host, a clean-cut 26 year old named Dick Clark..."</em> and that his birthday is November 30, 1929.</p>
<p>Lessee...</p>
<p>2001 (in Nov.) - 1956 = 45...<br />
26 years old + 45 = 71.</p>
<p>Dick Clark is currently seventy-one years old. There you go.</p>
