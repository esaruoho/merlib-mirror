### The history behind some of the top choices

- [<img src="nap_sm.gif" data-align="right" data-border="2" alt="Full frontal cat napping (51K)" />](nap.jpg)*Howie.* His working title, it came to me during a furiously-paced game of "Hi, Bob" during which I realized that he operates at the same mental level (cat scale) as Bob & Emily's lovable neighbor.
- *Arnie.* He likes to play golf (when I practice putting, he sits by the ball returner & pushes stuck balls into the mechanism).
- *Luke.* He came from the shelter with this name, but I never liked it.
- *Webster.* I wrote a friend with the subject line "Webster" (referring to the fact that my page was newly on line). She wrote back, "I see you finally named that stupid cat." No, I hadn't. But I liked it. It works. Get it?
- *Woody.* I've been inducing a Pavlovian response by whistling the Woody Woodpecker theme at feeding time (refer to "Lunchtime" sound bite).
- *Rush.* He has many chins and likes nothing better than the sound of his own voice.
- *Lump.* He's just plain scared of people and has been known to hide for days when company is about. Recently I had a couple of guests for an extended visit, and for days a lump under my comforter was all they saw of him.
- *Dinner.* What other word in the English language do cats actually respond to?
- *Greebo.* My brother (who, I fear, reads too many Anne McCaffrey books) keeps suggesting this name. So here it is.
- *Bob.* Just because.

### Anecdotes

- When I first saw him in that cage in the shelter, he seemed bold and outgoing - talking to me & reaching through the bars, and calmly allowing me to hold him. While living with me he has proven a tireless and fearsome bug hunter; he has staked out every inch of my apartment as his own (including me), and won't hesitate to give me a nip if he's a little grumpy. However, he has in fact turned out to be a hopeless coward, deathly afraid of *anything* larger than his head. The presence of other people, my buddy's dog, or the neighbor's cat (at half his size) sends him scurrying in the deepest recesses of my closet. Some of my friends have seen nothing more of him than an orange streak hurtling towards the bedroom, or the internal reflections of two terror-stricken eyes from his burrow beneath the pile of low-priority laundry. Heck, an errant sneeze can cause him to bolt. The catnip mouse has defeated him in combat on more than one occasion. He's really quite a pathetic wimp.
- Although cats are generally not known for their humility, he does acknowledge and thank me regularly for saving his cat ass from that unmentionable fate, typically by chewing on the end of my nose at odd hours in the morning while I'm asleep.
- Nothing unusual about a cat that likes to nap, but noname is fond of sleeping *on his back.* If you've never seen a psycotic tabby dreaming God-knows-what, four legs twitching in the air, eyes open & demonstrating violent REM, you've never truly been creeped out.
- Although scarcely more than a year old, he is preparing himself for a future as a feline bag man; when crows perch outside my window his eyes narrow, and he grumbles and mutters to himself in a low growl: "F@#&ing birds . . . who the hell do they think they are? . . . I'd like to see them try landing on *this* porch . . . F%\$!ing birds . . ." He has also recently taken to singing to himself when in the litter box.

### Things I have learned from my cat

- The best place to store your valuables is underneath the sofa. When you need them, weep profoundly and someone will retrieve them for you.
- If it totally repulses, reviles and disgusts your roommate, it must be good to eat.
- Never hold a nervous cat in your arms during a thunder storm when you're naked.
- The worst thing about cats: They never put anything away.
- It's always nap time.
