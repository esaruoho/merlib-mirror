<p>They came at him in clicking, clattering swarms.</p>
<p>Prancing and scuttling on insectile legs.</p>
<p>He sheared through their chitinous-metallic carapaces, sparks flying, the light saber thrumming in his hands. Yet as fast as he cut them down, more enforcer droids surged forth to take the places of the fallen. Energy pulse-darts stung his body, numbing him, slowing his reactions.</p>
<p>Desperate now, he pushed out with the Force but barely budged the nearest droid. Another reared up behind him. He sensed it, spun, slashed. It collapsed in a jittering, smoking heap.</p>
<p>His surge of triumph was short-lived as sharp pincers fastened onto him, yanked him backward into a dark and confining shell. No amount of kicking and struggling could persuade it to open.</p>
<p>"I think thats enough for now," he heard his masters voice say.</p>
<p>The enforcer froze, then slowly opened. He tumbled out onto the bare earth and stood, brushing dust from his clothes.</p>
<p>"Vell, Master?"</p>
<p>"Disappointing," Darth Sidious said, turning away and tucking his hands into the wide sleeves of his flowing robe.</p>
<p>"Ohh, thees ees very wasteful," Nali Dhorr said. The green-skinned sub-viceroy of the now-defunct Trade Federation scowled as she surveyed the field of broken droids. "Very, very wasteful."</p>
<p>"What would you have me do with them?" the Sith Lord asked irritably. "Its this or construction work, and frankly, I dont trust them for construction work either."</p>
<p>"Disappointink, Master?" He hurried over to them, smoothing back the strands of wiry white hair that had escaped from his waist-length ponytail. "How might I improve?"</p>
<p>"They are <em>war</em> droids!" Nali protested. "Designed for battle!"</p>
<p>"At which they proved miserable. They failed me on Naboo, theyve failed me in every effort since then. Why, the entire Galaxy knows that to stop the droid army, all you must do is destroy their central command center. Until you devise a better way of controlling them, Dama Dhorr, they are useless to me."</p>
<p>"We can bettah conceal de command center --"</p>
<p>"That is what you said on Gethtooine," he snapped. "And still, the Jedi found it. No. I will not base my strategies on machines any longer. Living soldiers cannot be all put out of commission at once by a single well-placed laser burst."</p>
<p>"Master?"</p>
<p>"What <em>is</em> it?" He turned and peered at his apprentice from the shadows cast by his cowl.</p>
<p>"Please, I vish to be vorthy!"</p>
<p>Sidious sighed. "Dama Dhorr, if youll excuse us?"</p>
<p>She huffed haughtily and left, followed by her droid guards.</p>
<p>The apprentice knelt before his master. "How may I serve you better?"</p>
<p>He felt the touch of a hand on his bowed head. "Darth Tepes, I cannot find fault with your dedication and enthusiasm."</p>
<p>"Yet I fail you, Master ... vy? Vat can I do?"</p>
<p>"I fear the Force is not sufficiently strong in you. The training demands more of you than you can give. Unfortunately, you were the best candidate at the time."</p>
<p>"At the time?" he echoed in consternation. "You cannot mean to ... replace me?"</p>
<p>"If a suitable replacement can be found, I may have to. As a matter of fact, I do have my eye on one young fellow ... headstrong and stubborn, but I sense that he might be corruptible."</p>
<p>"But Master! I haff served you faithfully --"</p>
<p>"Yes, Tepes, I know. I do appreciate it. But you must understand my position. I am working to overthrow a galactic republic. The Jedi seek to undermine my every move. I cannot let myself be burdened with less-powerful allies out of sentimentality."</p>
<p>"Let me prove myself to you!"</p>
<p>"I have no doubts regarding the sincerity of your devotion. You are a diligent pupil, but the fact remains -- the Force is not strong enough in you to allow you to progress further in your training."</p>
<p>A crestfallen moan escaped him.</p>
<p>"Tepes, Tepes. I wouldnt dismiss you from my service entirely. You are dedicated and have a knack for torture that is unrivaled. Im certain I will not lack projects for you."</p>
<p>"Master, please! Another chance!"</p>
<p>Sidious shook his head sorrowfully. "My boy, you cannot change how strongly the Force flows in your veins."</p>
<p>"Vat if I could?" He looked up into that lined, weathered face. "Vat if I could change that? Vould I then be acceptable?"</p>
<p>"Possibly," Sidious said, eyes narrowing. "How would you propose to accomplish such a thing?"</p>
<p>"Give me leave to try!" he pleaded.</p>
<p>His mind-barriers, the product of many grueling hours of mental exercises, might as well have been non-existent as the Sith Lord probed his thoughts. He sensed his masters initial scornful skepticism, and then as Sidious delved deeper, reading for himself how it might be done, that skepticism turned to thoughtfulness.</p>
<p>"This could also be a rather novel solution to the Jedi problem," Sidious mused, stroking his chin. "Thus far, Ive had to hold off taking any direct action against them. A Jedi is often more troublesome dead than alive. But this ... this is very intriguing, Tepes. You have my permission to proceed."</p>
<p>"Thank you, Master!" He prostrated himself at Sidious feet and pressed the hem of the Sith Lords robe to his forehead. "I vill not fail you!"</p>
<p>"See that you dont." He tempered it with a smile, but the words were still chilling. "See that you dont."<br />

<p>* *<br />

<p>She had grown accustomed to the day, but the night would always be her time.</p>
<p>Beneath a sky as dark as the plait of hair hanging down her back, the woman in black approached the stone tower. She moved with the silence of a cloud, despite the carpet of dry autumnal leaves underfoot. Her golden eyes were keenly alert to the forest around her.</p>
<p>Small animals went about their nocturnal business. Find food and try not to become food, the law of the wild. None of them bothered with her; she was ten times the size of the largest hunter and quite a bit more dangerous.</p>
<p>The tower rose well above the treetops. It stood at the end of a twelve-foot wall that stretched for miles through the woods and across the countryside. It was the last remnant of an old defense, between lands that had known peace for the better part of a millennium.</p>
<p>Peace.</p>
<p>The word itself was still strange to her. As strange as words like home ... family ... or love.</p>
<p>Two lights shone from the towers oval windows. One was near the top, a warm and steady beacon shed followed from the village.</p>
<p>Shed made that journey on foot, for there were no suitable landing sites this far out. Sylvar was an agrarian/orchard planet, known galaxy-wide for its bounty of fruit, nuts, and grains. Something was always in season on Sylvar. The harvest never ended.</p>
<p>A thousand years ago, warring factions had threatened to destroy this world. Now it was a place of simple harmony, consisting mainly of widely-scattered villages and traveling bands of workers from a hundred different cultures.</p>
<p>The only other people who lived on Sylvar were a handful of artisans trying to avoid distractions, some retired military types seeking a quiet refuge from the bustle of Republic life, and those wishing privacy and isolation.</p>
<p>Such as the inhabitants of the tower.</p>
<p>The other light was on the ground floor. It flickered and flared inconstantly, irregular spurts of green-white accompanied by grumbling in many alien tongues.</p>
<p>Raven went up to the low window. Her reflexes warned her to pull her head to the side as a tool came flying out.</p>
<p>"Daz abak arr!"</p>
<p>She boosted herself lithely into the window and crouched on the curved sill. "You curse like a spice miner, Skywalker."</p>
<p>Anakin jumped, and whirled on her with a pair of metal shears in his hand. Then he recognized her, relaxing with a grin. "Raven! I didnt sense you at all!"</p>
<p>"Am I getting better or are you getting sloppy?" She gestured around the room, which was strewn with clothes, droid parts, and bits of unidentifiable electronics. "Sloppier," she amended.</p>
<p>"This isnt so bad," he said. "You should have seen the junkyard where I used to work!"</p>
<p>She hopped down from her perch. "What are you doing?"</p>
<p>He made a face. "Working on my lightsaber. Obi-Wan says I have to. But its tougher than I thought." Scorch marks on the worktable and blisters on his fingers proved his point.</p>
<p>"So the young genius has his limits, hmm?" she teased.</p>
<p>"Id like to see <em>you</em> try."</p>
<p>"Thats an art reserved for Jedi. Im lucky the Council let me keep the lightsaber I have. They comfort themselves with the thought that one day itll break and then Ill be out of luck."</p>
<p>"Well ... when it does, Ill fix it for you."</p>
<p>She picked up the chewed-looking, gutted handle of the one hed been working on. "Id better take good care of it until you figure out what youre doing."</p>
<p>"Im getting the hang of it. Hey, I built a protocol droid when I was only nine." He was thirteen now, tall and handsome in a daredevil boyish way that made it easy to see how readily he could charm even a queen.</p>
<p>"From a kit, wasnt it?"</p>
<p>"Yeah, from a kit, so?" He laughed. "It worked. And so does the lightsaber. Sort of. It just needs some fine-tuning."</p>
<p>Raven activated it. A five-foot beam of pale emerald shot from the end, punching a smoldering hole in the wall.</p>
<p>"Yow!" Anakin cried. "Careful!"</p>
<p>She switched it off and turned to him, arching one ebon brow. "Its rather ... extravagant, dont you think?"</p>
<p>"Uh ..." he stammered.</p>
<p>"Believe it or not, Ani, there <em>is</em> such a thing as too big to handle."</p>
<p>He scuffed one foot sheepishly. "I guess ... see, I just wanted ..."</p>
<p>Raven stifled a bemused chuckle. "An impressive one?"</p>
<p>"Okay, okay, I get the point!"</p>
<p>She relented, though he was so <em>fun</em> to tease ... shed never had a kid brother to torment ... and handed the silvery haft back to him.</p>
<p>"Im glad youre back," he said. "Its been awful quiet around here. Theres nothing to <em>do</em> on Sylvar."</p>
<p>"Which is why youre here," she said, her resolve to leave off teasing not lasting more than a few seconds. "To concentrate more on your training, and less on the queen of Naboo."</p>
<p>Anakin blushed and toyed with the long thin braid that lay over his shoulder. The rest of his sandy hair was too long and indifferently combed. "Miss <em>one</em> session ..."</p>
<p>"Didnt it also have something to do with Palpatine finding the two of you swimming with no --?"</p>
<p>"Um ... maybe," he cut in, blushing even more brightly. "So, Raven, how long are you staying? Are you home for good this time?"</p>
<p>Now she grew somber. "I cant, Ani. You know the Council doesnt approve."</p>
<p>"I also know you couldnt care less what they think."</p>
<p>"But <em>you</em> do." She cast her eyes at the door. "And <em>he</em> does."</p>
<p>"Itd be easier if you would agree to be trained," he said. "Then the Council wouldnt be afraid of you, because theyd know you were bound by the same rules as the rest of us."</p>
<p>"Until I broke those rules. I ... admire what the Jedi have accomplished. I admire many of their goals and ideals. But there is much of what they teach that I cannot accept. Thats why I left in the first place. Ive ... never been one to be able to keep my opinions to myself..." She shook her head. "Im not sure what Im trying to say."</p>
<p>"Youre <em>not</em> evil," he said with lower lip outthrust. "Maybe a little bit on the Dark Side, fine, but not evil. Not like the one who killed Qui-Gon."</p>
<p>"The Council doesnt see it that way."</p>
<p>"You dont care what they think, remember?"</p>
<p>"I dont care what they think of <em>me</em>. But what they think of Obi-Wan means a great deal to him."</p>
<p>"And hes already on shaky ground with them, and that <em>is</em> my fault," Anakin said glumly. "Master Yoda always looks at me like Im something slimy that oozed out of a rotten log on Degobah."</p>
<p>Raven smiled ruefully. "Likewise, except that he looks at me like Im something <em>poisonous</em> that oozed out of that rotten log."</p>
<p>"My futures clouded ... isnt everyones?" He looked up at her appealingly. "Theyre judging me for things I havent even done, things I might <em>never</em> do! Its not fair!"</p>
<p>"I know," she sighed, resting her hand on his shoulder. "And associating with me isnt doing you, or Obi-Wan, any good in their eyes."</p>
<p>"It does <em>him</em> good. Hes happiest when youre here, Raven. Even when you dont agree."</p>
<p>"Well, Im here now. I should go and say hello."</p>
<p>"I guess I should adjust the lightsaber." He winked. "Ill probably be another hour or two at least."<br />

<p>* *<br />
 <br />
 </p>
<p>Darth Tepes flung open the door with a resounding crash. "Eliry!"</p>
<p>"Down here, my sveet," her voice crooned.</p>
<p>His boots rang on the metal walkway, raised half a meter above the filthy floor. He kept carefully to the center. The stench and the screams had long ago ceased to bother him, but he could never get used to the bony, scabrous claws that shot from between the bars to clutch at him as he went by.</p>
<p>"Back!" Torek Roth, a burly man in the scarlet livery of Elirys personal guard, thrust a pronged stick at the sea of haggard prisoners. Energy arced from the end, eliciting a shriek and causing the rest to shrink back.</p>
<p>Their eyes glinted murderously as they watched Tepes pass. He drew his crimson-edged black cape closely around him as the passage narrowed and began its sloping descent.</p>
<p>He found Eliry at the bottom, a slender figure gowned in clinging red, with a tumbling mass of white hair that fell to the backs of her knees. Her skin was as winter-blue as his own. Her eyes, avidly observing the scene before her, were orbs of opaque blackness.</p>
<p>Two other guards had just finished securing a gaunt man to a metal frame in the shape of an X. The man struggled feebly against the bonds that held his wrists and ankles. He raised his terrified gaze to Eliry.</p>
<p>"Please, Mistress! Be merciful!"</p>
<p>"Oh, but this <em>is</em> merciful," Eliry replied, giving him a reassuring smile filled with tiny needle-teeth. "Youre already dyink. Those sores vill only spread, riddlink you vith infection. The flesh vill rot avay until your bones are exposed, and even then youd linger. This is much kvicker, believe me."</p>
<p>"I wish to live! I beg you! Let me live!"</p>
<p>"But there is no cure." She raised her hand, rings sparkling on three of the six elongated fingers, the sheen of the enamel that coated her curving nails catching rainbows in the dim light.</p>
<p>The guards responded to her signal and rotated the frame on the wheel to which it was affixed. The man wailed piteously as his legs went up and his head pointed down.</p>
<p>Tepes moved closer to inspect the prisoner. A Prhei, distant kin to the elite race that ruled this world. The only outward differences were in the skin -- pinkish, and in the case of the plague-ridden, blotched along the limbs with oozing crescent-shaped splits -- and the extremities, lacking the sixth finger and toe.</p>
<p>The chamber around them was circular, soaring nine levels high to a domed roof. The walls were lined with cages and catwalks, every cage filled beyond capacity.</p>
<p>Tepes stood alongside Eliry, bending to nip at her smooth shoulder. His teeth left a dozen pinpricks. "Good evenink, cousin-vife."</p>
<p>"How vent your day, my sveet?"</p>
<p>He scowled. "As I feared."</p>
<p>"He does not appreciate you."</p>
<p>She turned as one of her maidens brought her a flat bronze box, and picked daintily through the implements within until shed found the one she sought. It was a hollow crystal cylinder the width of her smallest finger, one end tapering to a curved blade.</p>
<p>The man on the frame writhed with renewed vigor as he saw it. He tried to lash his head side to side, but it was held firmly in place by clamps. He tried to raise his shoulders to his ears, also to no avail.</p>
<p>"There is vun ray of hope," Tepes said, stepping back to give her room to work. "Hes granted me leave to pursue our plan."</p>
<p>"Has he?" She affixed the blunt end of the crystal cylinder to a length of flexible tubing that fed into the top of a squat, accordion-sided droid.</p>
<p>The din in the dungeon increased tenfold as the prisoners began to shout. Pleas, threats, and savage glee mingled into a roar.</p>
<p>Eliry motioned to the guards. One of them threw a lever, and the frame rose until the mans head was suspended at chest-height. The other trundled the box closer.</p>
<p>"Ill need your help," Tepes continued. "Your expertise. And vell haff to re-fit a droid or two."</p>
<p>"Youll have vatever you need." She felt for the mans pulse. "Poundink vith fear. Perfect."</p>
<p>"No, Mistress, no, please, for the love of mercy, please!"</p>
<p>"Shh, now," she said kindly. "It vont hurt a bit."</p>
<p>With that, she placed the tip of the crystal cylinder against the artery in his neck. One swift push sliced deep, embedding the crystal. Not so much as a drop stained his skin. Rich crimson fluid gushed into the tubing.</p>
<p>The mans mouth gaped in a soundless scream as he felt his lifes blood pouring from him.</p>
<p>"Nicely done," Tepes said. "Your touch is so light, so skilled!"</p>
<p>"I learned from the best, my sveet," she said, favoring him with a loving smile. "Vould you care to cut the vein?"</p>
<p>"As you vish." He plucked a razor from the box the maiden held, and flicked it in one sure movement. The resultant flood ran along channels in the metal floor and vanished through a grate.</p>
<p>The sides of the droid began to bellows in and out, sucking the bright arterial blood with such speed that the mans vessels collapsed. Beneath the grime, his skin went pallid except for his face, which remained reddened.</p>
<p>The droid whirred and hummed. A glass bubble bulging from its top began to moisten with condensation.</p>
<p>"This is so much tidier," Eliry remarked, crouching to adjust the droids controls. "Ven I think of how our ancestors lived, it appalls me. Can you imagine havink to do it <em>ourselves</em>?"</p>
<p>"And havink to ingest so much," he agreed, wrinkling his nose. "Eight to tvelve hours comatose in digestion, and all to filter out the little bit ve needed."</p>
<p>"Yes, I much prefer this." She peered into the clouded glass bubble, where yellowish-clear droplets were forming.</p>
<p>"Vy do you extract them down here?" Tepes asked. "The lab is kvieter, and certainly cleaner!"</p>
<p>"Ah, but its not as much fun, is it?" Eliry waved to the rest of the prisoners, beaming as they cringed and stumbled over themselves in an effort to avoid meeting her eyes, lest she choose her next victim that way. "I find their fear nearly as invigoratink as the gift they give. Itll be interestink to see if ve can bestir similar fear in the Jedi."<br />

<p>* *<br />
 <br />
 </p>
<p>He opened the door as she neared the landing. Light streamed around him, shining on his brown-gold-chestnut hair, while she remained in the shadows of the curved stairwell.</p>
<p>Light and dark ... good and evil? Halves of the same whole, sides of the same coin?</p>
<p>Or just themselves? Two souls drawn together by Force or fate or nothing more than random chance?</p>
<p>Raven ascended another step. She couldnt see his face, needed desperately to read his expression because his thoughts were carefully closed.</p>
<p>So much that needed to be said, so many words hanging unspoken in the air between them. The months shed been away stretched out to seem like years.</p>
<p>Rather than try to voice them, she slowly extended her hand. As she did so, she dropped the mental barriers hed taught her to create. Hopes and fears, regrets and remembrances, all there for him to read if he wished.</p>
<p>Obi-Wan stepped down to meet her, reaching out. Their fingertips touched, their palms pressed together. The violet warmth and wispy smoke of the Soulfire gloved their enfolded fingers.</p>
<p>"Ive come back," she said softly. "I know we decided it was better if I left ... but I couldnt stay away."</p>
<p>"You chose to leave. I never wanted that."</p>
<p>"It had to be --"</p>
<p>"Raven."</p>
<p>She bowed her head.</p>
<p>"Do not change what you <em>are</em>," he went on. "You worry about the future, about what <em>may</em> be ..."</p>
<p>"At the expense of the moment?" she said, repeating the words of wisdom hed had from his master.</p>
<p>"Yes." He caressed the line of her jaw.</p>
<p>Her eyes drifted closed. "Yet your own Council cant do it; how can I?"</p>
<p>"What do you mean?"</p>
<p>"So worried about the threat Anakin may pose that they refuse to see the person he <em>is</em>. If it does happen, Obi-Wan, itll be because they drove him to it. Dont you think I know what it must be like for him? If, no matter what I do, theyll denounce me as evil ... why, what a temptation to have the game as well as the name!"</p>
<p>"Or prove them wrong."</p>
<p>"Would it work?" she asked, hearing her own bleak hopelessness.</p>
<p>"Even Jedi can be wrong. I was."</p>
<p>She opened her eyes and looked at him. "When?"</p>
<p>"When I let you leave."</p>
<p>Her inexpert mental foray searched his thoughts, finding no barriers and the truth of how much hed missed her ... nearly as much as shed missed him.</p>
<p>"What will we do?" she groaned. "Cant be together, cant bear to be apart."</p>
<p>"Trust our feelings," he said.</p>
<p>"The Council --"</p>
<p>"Theyre not here." He drew her into his arms.</p>
<p>She went with no resistance, giving in to the yearning passion she no longer wanted to control. His kiss was at once burning and tender, needing and giving.</p>
<p>Her senses seemed all to meld into one, so that she experienced each sensation in a multitude of ways. The feel of cloth beneath her hands as she gripped his shoulders came to her as the taste of fresh-baked bread. His scent was as the melody of winds secret whisper through the leaves. The sound of his low sigh sent cool blue flames flickering in the darkness of her mind.</p>
<p>"Take me to bed," she murmured against his mouth.</p>
<p>Somehow, they were in his room without seeming to climb the rest of the stairs or break from their embrace. He swept a hand at the door and missed, but it slammed gustily shut all the same.</p>
<p>The months of separation crashed heavily down on her. So many desolate lonely nights in the cold depths of space ... the hours seeming endless, the memories bittersweet torture impossible to resist.</p>
<p>With a breathy little growl, she fell back across the bed and pulled him with her. Old fears at being pinned beneath the weight of a man were long gone thanks to his gentle patience during their first weeks together.</p>
<p>Urgency blazed in their reunion. They both understood that there would be time later for languid and loving explorations; this time theyd been too long denied.</p>
<p>It was all Raven could do to wait until theyd both shed their clothing, a testament to her willpower that she didnt use what meager mastery of the Force she possessed to just shred them from his body the better to get at the smooth warmth of his skin. Cream-colored Jedi garb and her own basic black garments made a hasty pile on the floor.</p>
<p>"Oh, yes, now," she gasped. Her mind was already open to him; now the rest of her was as well, and it was never invasion but a welcome sharing, holding nothing back, taking what was freely given.</p>
<p>She felt as if she was formed of living crystal, dark glass with a heart of amethyst radiance that glowed brighter and brighter still, illuminating her from within.</p>
<p>He said her name again and again as they moved together, steady but quickening toward a conclusion just shy of frantic.</p>
<p>As that moment drew near, he lowered his head to hers, not for a kiss but to press their foreheads together, eyes tightly closed but each of them still seeing the other as clear as could be.</p>
<p>Their bodies shuddered in one shared exquisite release, souls seamlessly joined for that eternally brief instant, just as two candle flames brought to touch would flow into each other, impossible to tell where one began and the other ended.</p>
<p>The next thing Raven was aware of was the soft brush of his lips, and the realization that in the intensity of her response she had wept without knowing she did so. Obi-Wan gently kissed away those tears, stroked her hair and cheek.</p>
<p>She raised one hand to trace her fingertips along his face, marveling at what she saw not only with her eyes but with the sight of her mind.</p>
<p>There were still unspoken words between them, but it didnt matter anymore ... even if they were never quite able to say them, they both already knew.<br />

 <br />
 </p>
<p>Wooden sticks the length and weight of lightsabers clattered in a blur of swings and parries, until the taller of the two combatants broke from the exchange and signaled for a stop.</p>
<p>"No more," Raven said, mopping her brow. "Its too hot for this."</p>
<p>"Hot?" Anakin echoed, lowering his stick. "You call <em>this</em> hot?"</p>
<p>"Your world was baked by two suns, desert-boy. Mine had none."</p>
<p>"Tatooine would fry you to a cinder."</p>
<p>"It very nearly did."</p>
<p>Obi-Wan smiled at their banter, watching from a spot in the shade of the tower. A cool breeze riffled his robe and sent a swarm of crisp reddish leaves skirling across the raked earth of the practice yard.</p>
<p>Raven tipped her face into the breeze, closing her eyes in pleasure as the cool air blew loose strands of night-black hair back from her damp brow.</p>
<p>She had been back for seven days, and already it was as if shed never left. The time shed been gone seemed unimportant, a dreary dream from which hed finally awakened.</p>
<p>"You were on Tatooine?" Anakin perked up in interest.</p>
<p>"Once, briefly. And never again," she stated, dunking a dipper into a barrel of water and dumping it over her head.</p>
<p>"What were you doing there?" He hadnt even broken a sweat under Sylvars yellow and mild sun.</p>
<p>Raven wrung a stream from the hem of her tunic. "I owed your teacher some money."</p>
<p>"No, you didnt," Obi-Wan objected.</p>
<p>"You fed me, clothed me, paid my way around three systems," she said.</p>

<p>Coruscant was most beautiful by night.</p>

 </p>
<p>The hour was late and the night was growing old, yet Zadrek Kellnu still walked the ledges on her solitary vigil, watching the courier ships come and go on late errands.</p>
<p>Most fully-trained Jedi had no physical need for sleep, having honed their bodies to the point where they could get by with a few moments a day of quiet meditation.</p>
<p>But sleep was a mental necessity as well, she knew. A chance for the mind to sort through the cares of the day that couldnt be resolved by waking thought.</p>
<p>There would be no sleep for her tonight. Not with so much to worry about.</p>
<p>The boy, for instance. What was to become of the boy?</p>
<p>They could not turn him loose to go on his merry way; he would be far too vulnerable to their hidden enemy. For his own sake and all of theirs, he <em>had</em> to be properly trained and educated.</p>
<p>Some Knight would simply have to take over for Kenobi in that regard, she thought somberly, walking along the ledge.</p>
<p>Presentiment touched her like the brush of a moths wing. She pulled her robe more closely around herself as the night seemed to turn colder, and banished her other concerns to analyze the warning.</p>
<p>She shuddered, struck by the sudden knowledge that it was far more urgent, immediate, and personal. Quickening her pace, she turned a corner and stumbled over a body.</p>
<p>Vdrin stared up at her, his features agape in horror and agony. The side of his neck was torn away in a ragged wound. Judging by the discoloration of his skin and the stiffness of the body, hed been there for hours.</p>
<p>How could a Jedi Master have died without her knowing? How could he lay here undetected all that time?</p>
<p>Someone grabbed her from behind.</p>
<p>Mind and body reacted together, silently screaming a warning to the others and drawing a lightsaber that gleamed pearl-white.</p>
<p>No sooner was it lit than it was struck from her grasp, spinning off the edge and vanishing into the city below.</p>
<p>Six-fingered hands slammed onto her shoulders and spun her, sharp nails ripping the cloth of her robe to reveal her throat.</p>
<p>The Brams face was streaked with dried greenish-brown blood, and strings of gore dangled from his teeth as he snarled at her.</p>
<p>A massive wave of blackness swamped her mind, blotting out psychic sensation. Robbed of her powers, she could not save herself, could only stand as frozen as an ice sculpture as the Bram lowered his jaws toward her neck.</p>
<p>Brilliant amber flared, and Mace Windus voice boomed, "Kellnu!"</p>
<p>The Sith -- for so she now knew it to be -- shoved her so hard that she struck the waist-high rail and flipped over.</p>
<p>Zadrek Kellnu clutched the top and hung there, feet kicking in open space, unable to use the Force to boost herself back up, as helpless as a child. She saw at an odd angle how the Sith leaped to attack, agile as the best of the Knights.</p>
<p>Mace Windu was a staunch and solid warrior, holding his ground without any of the slick moves or fancy tricks that characterized the fighting style of so many other Jedi. He was a wall, unbreachable and resolute. The Siths best efforts met with an impenetrable defense.</p>
<p>They moved out of her line of sight, leaving her with only her ears to tell her how the battle was progressing.</p>
<p>Kellnu still sensed nothing. Did those in the temple not know what was happening out here? Her hands slipped and she fell, but caught a lip of stone and found purchase.</p>
<p>The battle ranged back into her view. She saw Windu stagger, not from a physical blow but from the same black wave that had drowned her powers. Now the Sith had more of an advantage and wasted no time taking it.</p>
<p>"I now haff the powers of seven Jedi!" he boasted, driving Windu back with a series of slashes and thrusts. "Soon I vill haff yours too!"</p>
<p>"You will have nothing!"</p>
<p>So saying, Obi-Wan Kenobi dropped from a higher ledge. A beam of violet sprang from his fist. The moment he landed, he bounced on one foot and kicked the startled Sith in the ribs.</p>
<p>"Psst!"</p>
<p>Kellnu looked up with a gasp, and saw Anakin Skywalker leaning over the rail, reaching.</p>
<p>"Let me pull you up!" the boy said.</p>
<p>She clasped his hands, sure that she would drag him over and down after her. He strained, then his eyes slitted in concentration and she felt herself drawn steadily upward by the unseen Force, and deposited safely on the ledge.</p>
<p>Nothing should have been able to stand against the combined assault of Kenobi and Windu, yet the Sith was doing so. Barely, but successfully.</p>
<p>"Im fine," she told Skywalker, catching her breath. "Help them."</p>
<p>He saluted smartly and dashed into the fray.</p>
<p>The Sith stunned Windu with a punch to the head, would have spitted him if Kenobi hadnt parried for Windu; a backhand swing scorched a line across Kenobis midsection and would have gutted him if the Jedi hadnt jumped back with such alacrity; he bent with a forearm pressed along the wound, not crying out but agony evident in his expression.</p>
<p>The boy attempted a leaping kick and was batted out of the air; the Sith somersaulted over Skywalker and stabbed downward; the boy rolled and arched into a bow and the yellow lightsaber sank into the stone, leaving a hole. It wedged and he growled, lashing out with a kick that landed between Skywalkers shoulderblades. The boy yelped and rolled.</p>
<p>As the Sith bent over to free his weapon, Kenobi shoved a hand at him and sent him sprawling at Windus feet. The Jedi Master stepped hard on the Siths wrist, pinning it, and breaking it by the sound. His lightsaber tumbled over the edge and was gone.</p>
<p>The Sith latched onto Windus leg with his teeth, shearing through flesh. Bellowing in pain, Windu recoiled and fell on his back, blood not running but <em>spouting</em>.</p>
<p>Springing upright, unarmed but still fast even by their standards, the Sith went after Kenobi with a shriek of purest fury. "You killed my voman!"</p>
<p>"No," a female voice said. "That was me."</p>
<p>His head whipped around, white ponytail flying.</p>
<p>A woman in black was leaning on the wall to support herself, her fair skin even paler, dark smudges beneath her eyes.</p>
<p>Kellnu knew her at once, this protégé of a Dark Jedi dismissed from their order. In that moment, in the shadows, she could have been Damon Blake come back to life.</p>
<p>"Raven, no!" Kenobi said through gritted teeth.</p>
<p>"I impaled her through the heart," Raven continued relentlessly, inching forward. "Watched her crumble away to dust."</p>
<p>"And I freed your prisoners!" Skywalker announced. "Smashed your towers!"</p>
<p>"I vill see you both die," the Sith vowed. He seized Raven and yanked her to him. Her head fell weakly back, leaving her neck exposed and vulnerable.</p>
<p>"Ani!" She thrust out an arm.</p>
<p>The boy threw his unlit lightsaber and it socked firmly into her waiting palm. As the Sith darted at her with the speed of a striking snake, she pushed the end beneath his chin and depressed the button.</p>
<p>Emerald light gushed from his open mouth, lit up his skull from the inside, and burst out the top of his head.</p>
<p>He struck out wildly, clawlike nails raking at Raven. His eyes were boiling in their sockets, his hair was aflame, and still he lived, still he fought. She lost her hold on the lightsaber and it slid out of his sundered head, leaving a gruesome blackened dripping trail.</p>
<p>Kenobi interposed himself as the Sith blindly reached to grapple Raven. The claws found him instead, tearing, drawing blood; the scent of it maddening the Sith further. His claws sank deep into Kenobis arm, the furnace cavern of his mouth yawed insanely wide.</p>
<p>Raven grasped Kenobis wrist, steadying it. Together, they brought the violet lightsaber up and out, plunging it into the Siths chest.</p>
<p>Whether he crumbled to dust or burned to ash, Kellnu was never fully sure. The night wind whisked the remains away, leaving just the empty shell of armor and clothing.</p>
<p>Raven and Kenobi sank to their knees together and clung to each other in the manner of grateful survivors. The boy flung his arms around them both. All three of them groaned from their various injuries but none pulled away.<br />
 <br />
 </p>
<p>* *<br />
 <br />
 </p>
<p>"Another one," Darth Sidious said to himself. "That Kenobi is becoming a serious problem."</p>
<p>Hed witnessed all, his senses easily able to penetrate the muffling psychic cloud that hed formed around the Jedi temple to confuse their powers.</p>
<p>Once hed realized his temperamental apprentice truly meant to go through with his attack, hed decided that lending a hand wasnt out of order. It had prevented the Jedi from divining the danger until it was too late.</p>
<p>Now one of the Council was dead, not much but a start. A shame Tepes hadnt been able to get rid of more of them. He would have, if Kenobi and his Padawan and his shades-of-grey woman hadnt turned up so inopportunely.</p>
<p>From his personal chambers balcony, located near the great spherical hall of the Senate, hed even had a visual view of the confrontation on the temples high ledges.</p>
<p>His powers had told him much more, picking up thoughts and feelings from all the involved parties. He took delight in Mace Windus stalwart struggles to avoid showing his pain, hands clamped to his leg in a futile attempt at stopping the bleeding.</p>
<p>He wrinkled his nose sourly as Zadrek Kellnu tore a strip from her robe and tied a quick tourniquet. Would have been nice if Windu bled to death, but his downturn of luck wouldnt even grant him that.</p>
<p>Tepes flamboyant death had loosed not only his own living Force but that of the drained and destroyed Jedi, which had been held in suspension until that moment.</p>
<p>The resultant disturbance in the Force made it difficult for Sidious to maintain his dampening cloud. As there was no further need for it, he let it go and formed a few lingering impressions to make the Jedi believe that it had all been Tepes doing.</p>
<p>Kenobi could never be turned, he admitted. For a time there, when hed been struggling to come to terms with the death of his mentor, there had been hate and anger in his soul. But he had turned it into a determination to live up to and exceed the example presented by the late troublesome Qui-Gon Jinn.</p>
<p>The woman? She lived cheek and jowl with darker emotions, thrived on revenge. As a bonus, she was already half-trained and adept with a lightsaber, and when her powers fully returned as they soon would, shed be a formidable prospect.</p>
<p>Sidious contemplated the fleeting touches hed had of her mind, and finally dismissed it with a frown. No, she was too willful by half, one that would never bow to a higher authority. Be it Jedi Council or Sith Lord, she would accept no Master.</p>
<p>The boy, on the other hand ... more and more, Sidious was certain the boy could be turned. The Council was already wary of him. With the further arrangement of events, hed be ripe for a change.</p>
<p>Such arranging of events would take time. Years, perhaps. He would want to tread very carefully this time.</p>
<p>In the meanwhile, he was once again left without an apprentice.</p>
<p>The temple was now bustling with activity, as the Jedi had awakened from a daze they hadnt even known possessed them to the realization that their very inner sanctum had been breached.</p>
<p>Sidious smirked bemusedly, letting their consternation be some bitter balm to his sour mood as he went back inside and closed the curtains.<br />
 <br />
 </p>
<p>* *<br />
 <br />
 </p>
<p>When three members of the Council came in, Raven and Anakin both rose from their spots on either side of Obi-Wans bed and moved in front of him protectively.</p>
<p>The only other remaining inhabitant of the infirmary was a Knight whod had the misfortune to run afoul of Darth Tepes on his way into the temple; that man had been spared the bite but it still remained to be seen whether hed recover from his wounds or follow two other unlucky Jedi into death.</p>
<p>He didnt stir as Mace Windu, Master Yoda, and Zadrek Kellnu entered the room.</p>
<p>Obi-Wan sat up, wincing as the movement pulled at the burn across his stomach. It was shallow but still painful. The gouges caused by Tepes claws had closed to reddish scars, the deep healing itch the worst of that.</p>
<p>"Its all right," he murmured.</p>
<p>Raven gave him a look that implied he didnt know what he was talking about, then returned her just-so-slightly defiant gaze to the Council members.</p>
<p>Anakin nervously eased back into his chair, a bit gingerly because Tepes kick had bruised his spine.</p>
<p>Everyone waited in apprehensive tableau as they all sized each other up. Only Master Yoda was unscathed. His wizened green face was pinched in heavy disapproval as he stumped on his walking stick over to the end of the bed.</p>
<p>Mace Windu walked with the aid of a crutch, his lower leg swaddled in bandages. The brutal bite had become badly infected, necessitating surgery.</p>
<p>Zadrek Kellnus arm was in a sling; shed gotten a serious sprain at some point in the proceedings and hadnt even been aware of it until later.</p>
<p>"I understand Ive put you in an awkward situation," Obi-Wan said, not contritely, not apologetically, simply stating the fact.</p>
<p>Raven said nothing, not even when Yoda harumphed and peered up at her. Her mind was controlled and serene, even with her mental barriers still barely a wisp.</p>
<p>Obi-Wan could sense that shed been right, the chemical that had been drained from her blood was gradually regenerating itself. The same might have happened to him and the other Jedi, had they been given enough time, and not brought to the point of death by the actual procedure.</p>
<p>"You did break the Councils edict," Zadrek Kellnu said gently, "but under the circumstances, we cannot fault you for it."</p>
<p>"Without that distraction at a crucial moment," Mace Windu said, "the Sith might have killed us all."</p>
<p>Yoda harumphed again. "Done well, have you," he admitted grudgingly. "Discussed this has the Council, and agreed that perhaps ... <em>perhaps</em> overcautious were we in that edict."</p>
<p>A faint quirk of a smile tugged at the corner of Ravens mouth.</p>
<p>"Does that mean Obi-Wans not in trouble?" Anakin asked.</p>
<p>Kellnu inclined her head. "Not at all. He has once again proved himself a most worthy Jedi."</p>
<p>"What about us?" he ventured, with a darting-quick glance at Yoda.</p>
<p>"You disobeyed your teachers wishes, disregarded several laws of the Republic ... "</p>
<p>Anakins face fell.</p>
<p>"But as I said," Kellnu continued, touching the boy on the shoulder and smiling down at him, "under the circumstances, we cannot fault you for it. If not for you, <em>both</em> of you," she added with a nod at Raven, "another Jedi would have been lost to us and we would still have been ignorant of the threat posed by Darth Tepes."</p>
<p>"Your willingness to put your life in danger to help a Jedi speaks well of you," Mace Windu said to Raven.</p>
<p>She regarded him evenly. "I only did what had to be done. For what I believe in, and for what Obi-Wan believes in."</p>
<p>"From now on," Windu said, "you are welcome on Coruscant, and in this temple."</p>
<p>"You mean she can be trained?" Anakin blurted excitedly. "She can be a Jedi?"</p>
<p>"No!" Yoda thumped his stick. "A Jedi she shall <em>not</em> be. Too old. Too easily ruled by emotion."</p>
<p>"Master Yoda --" Obi-Wan began.</p>
<p>"I will never be a Jedi, Anakin," Raven said. "Its not what I want, and we all know Id be very bad at it. And I would certainly not want to force the Councils hand, and take advantage of any gratitude they might feel toward me."</p>
<p>Yoda bristled, Windu frowned, and Kellnu looked stung.</p>
<p>"Its enough," Raven went on, "not to be shunned, and perhaps to be judged by what I do rather than <em>why</em> I do it or what I <em>might</em> do. Isnt that all any of us want?"</p>
<p>"Yeah," Anakin muttered.</p>
<p>"Difficult, she is," Yoda grumbled to Mace Windu. "Willful and disrespectful."</p>
<p>"Just like her father," Kellnu said, her voice soft yet pointed. "But if we put the burden of his past on her, were as guilty as anyone of being ruled by emotion over reason."</p>
<p>Obi-Wan couldnt believe that she would criticize other Council members in front of them. By the astounded looks they gave her, they couldnt either.</p>
<p>"My father?" Raven asked, brows knit in puzzlement.</p>
<p>"She didnt have one," Anakin said. "Like me. She just ... <em>happened</em>."</p>
<p>"When I see you ..." Zadrek Kellnu came forward and pressed her fingertips under Ravens chin, lifting her face and turning it, "Yes ... I see Damon Blake in your eyes, in your hair, in the set of your jaw. <em>He</em> was your father, Raven. And he was one of us."</p>
<p>"My mother was tei-gam, a sacred bride of Noct," Raven argued. "<em>Noct</em> sired me, not the Noctus, not Damon Blake."</p>
<p>"Question your heart for the truth," Kellnu advised.</p>
<p>"Blakes daughter." Mace Windu snorted and rolled his eyes at Yoda. "That does explain much."</p>
<p>"It is true," Obi-Wan said. "I know it to be so."</p>
<p>She sank onto the edge of the bed. "The Noctus ... my <em>father</em>?"</p>
<p>"Enough of this," Yoda said. "Permitted on Coruscant she is. The gratitude of the Council that far extends."</p>
<p>"Thank you," Raven said distractedly. Obi-Wan could sense the dawning realization and acceptance in her, followed closely by fresh pangs of grief for the man who had died to save her from Dol Bethra, cruel warden of the prison moon that had once been her home.</p>
<p>Yoda and Mace Windu left the infirmary. Kellnu remained, bending to give Anakin a hug that startled him.</p>
<p>"To you, young Padawan," she said, "my thanks for your help. The fear we felt when you first came before us is so much less ... and your future, I sense, is now so much more ... be well, Anakin Skywalker."</p>
<p>"Be well, Master Kellnu," he said, trying to wipe away a tear without letting on that the motherly embrace had affected him so strongly.</p>
<p>She turned to Obi-Wan and he felt the warm gossamer brush of her mind, composed and reassuring. He responded in kind.</p>
<p>Then she was gone, and they shared a relieved glance all around.</p>
<p>"What now?" Anakin finally asked.</p>
<p>"Home to Sylvar?" Obi-Wan suggested, watching Ravens reaction closely. "All three of us?" He held out his hand.</p>
<p>She took it. "Sylvar."</p>
<p>Anakin covered their hands with his own. "Like a family?"</p>
<p>"Yes." Obi-Wan didnt drop his eyes from Ravens. "Like a family."</p>
<p>She drew in a slow, tense breath and loosed it in a sigh. "Yes."</p>
<p>"Were going home!" Anakins whoop was nearly loud enough to rouse the other Jedi from his drugged state, but it ended abruptly when Raven added a few words.</p>
<p>"Where <em>you</em> can tell Vance Antilles what happened to his ship."</p>
<p>His horrified expression gave way to a grin, and the three of them laughed together as the lights of Coruscant began to shine against the darkening twilight sky.<br />
