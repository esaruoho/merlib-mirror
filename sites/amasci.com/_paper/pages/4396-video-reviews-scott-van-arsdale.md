<td style="text-align: center;" data-valign="top" width="475" height="668"><p><span style="mso-bidi-font-family:Arial">Two new instructional knapping videos have been added to <em>The Masters Series</em>, produced by D.C. Waldorf and distributed through The Flintknappers Corner.<span style="mso-spacerun: yes">  </span>One is <em>Flake Over Grinding, w</em>ith Dale Cannon, and the other is D.C. Waldorfs <em>Thebes Points and Their Variants</em>.<span style="mso-spacerun: yes">  </span>A good knapping video is the next best thing to getting live lessons from an accomplished knapper.<span style="mso-spacerun:
yes">  </span>Both videos deliver.</span></p>
<p><span style="mso-bidi-font-family:Arial">I wish Dales video was around when I started in flake over grinding!<span style="mso-spacerun: yes">  </span>Even though Ive been grinding for several years now, I still learned some useful things from the video.<span style="mso-spacerun: yes">  </span>The viewer gets to watch Dale as he turns an agate slab into a beautifully flaked knife blade, using grinding techniques.<span style="mso-spacerun: yes">  </span>Next he creates a matched Piccasso Marble handle and completes the knife.<span style="mso-spacerun: yes">  </span>Throughout the whole process, he explains the whys and hows of what he is doing.<span style="mso-spacerun: yes">  </span>The filming is excellent: the camera angles and the close-ups leave nothing to the imagination.<span style="mso-spacerun: yes">  </span>As an added treat, we get a taste of Dales musical talents.<span style="mso-spacerun:
yes">  </span>While Dale is grinding the handle, we listen to a dubbed recording of Dale on his guitar.<span style="mso-spacerun: yes">  </span>The video ends with an interview of Dale by Dane Martin. During this interview, Dale explains his views on flake over grindings place in the knapping world, which is insightful. This is followed by Dale playing a bluesy number he wrote, called <em>Downtown Osage Ooga-Booga Tonight</em>. Dont get the wrong impression though: this is a serious how-to video, in which the important material is delivered in a straightforward way.<span style="mso-spacerun: yes">  </span>You definitely want to take a look at this video if you are interested in flake over grinding.<span style="mso-spacerun: yes">  </span><em>Flake Over Grinding</em> does not replace Craig Ratzats <em>Lap Knapping</em> video, as Craigs tape covers the basics of lap knapping.<span style="mso-spacerun: yes">  </span>Dales video skips some of the basic stuff and jumps right in to flake-over details, and offers a good continuum.<span style="mso-spacerun:
yes">  </span>One last note: Dale hints that there will be another Dale Cannon video in the future.<span style="mso-spacerun: yes">  </span>Save a copy for me, please!</span></p>
<p><span style="mso-bidi-font-family:Arial">D.C. Waldorfs <em>Thebes Points and Their Variants</em> is another easy to watch and follow video.<span style="mso-spacerun: yes">  </span>As one might expect from a <em>Masters Series</em> video, it is not geared for the beginner, although beginners should be able to learn a good deal from it.<span style="mso-spacerun: yes">  </span>D.C. starts by spalling a difficult Indiana Hornstone nodule, and then selects one of the spalls to turn into a Thebes point.<span style="mso-spacerun: yes">  </span>As he produces a preform from the spall, he discusses such things as continuous and isolated platforms, and the different results obtained by using copper and antler pressure flakers (all of the percussion work is done with antler billets).<span style="mso-spacerun: yes">  </span>D.C. gives a detailed lesson in notching, which is outstanding.<span style="mso-spacerun: yes">  </span>He uses antler tines for direct pressure, antler, copper and steel nail punches for indirect percussion notching, as well as a copper tipped pressure flaker for direct pressure notching. The different approaches and results for each tool are noted. Then D.C. demonstrates how to make an e-notch and a dogleg notch. As with Dales video, the filming and editing are excellent. The camera angles and lighting ensure the viewer sees everything, and there are no dull periods.<span style="mso-spacerun: yes">  </span>The video begins and ends with some interesting synthesizer music by... Dale Cannon!</span></p>
<p><span style="mso-bidi-font-family:Arial"></span></p>
Both videos have a gallery segment.<span style="mso-spacerun: yes">  </span>In <em>Thebes Points and Their Variants,</em> we get a look at several of D.C.s fine Thebes points, made with various materials.<span style="mso-spacerun: yes">  </span>In <em>Flake Over Grinding</em>, we see several of Dales knives.<span style="mso-spacerun: yes">  </span>D.C. and Dale give little descriptions for each work shown, and some of them have histories, such as being featured on the cover of <em>CHIPS</em>.<span style="mso-spacerun: yes">  </span>A tip of the hat to the production crew of Val and D.C. Waldorf, and Dane and Mary Martin, of Mound Builder Video, for the quality of these tapes.<span style="mso-spacerun: yes">  Both videos will make worthy additions to your library.</span></td>
