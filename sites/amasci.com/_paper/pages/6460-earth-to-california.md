**Earth to California: thank environmentalism for your wildfires\
\**
The first spark of the California fire season ignited unseen, 'way back in 1988, when its kangaroo rat was declared an "endangered species." Since then, compliance with the federal Endangered Species Act (ESA) of 1973 has forced California communities, and countless more across the nation, to develop species and habitat <a href="http://theweeklystandard.com/Content/Public/Articles/000/000/003/310euctj.asp" style="text-decoration: underline; color: blue; font-family: Verdana, Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif">"conservation plans"</a> that severely limit human activities in "protected" areas.\
\
"Protected," that is, from everything except wildfires--especially on the vast tracts of government lands and forests in the West. "Species protections" and "wilderness" designations have erected formidable legal barriers to <a href="http://www.ncpa.org/ba/ba336/ba336.html" style="text-decoration: underline; color: blue; font-family: Verdana, Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif">common-sense forestry management measures</a>, such as cutting new roads through forests for fire-fighting equipment, bulldozing "firebreaks," and clearing away dead branches, underbrush, and sage scrub--fuels which sends fires leaping to the crowns of trees and raging out of control.\
\
The General Accounting Office warned in 1999 of the dangerous accumulation of fuel, but environmentalist pressures continued to prevent humans from *managing* environments that greens preferred to keep "pristine." In a recent <a href="http://online.wsj.com/article/0,,SB10678196322347900,00.html?mod=opinion" style="text-decoration: underline; color: blue; font-family: Verdana, Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif"><em>Wall Street Journal</em> article</a>, Robert H. Nelson surveyed the resulting damages and body counts. In 2000, 8.4 million acres went up in smoke. In 2002, 6.9 million more acres were reduced to ashes, as were 800 homes, in firestorms that also took the lives of 23 more firefighters, and cost \$1.7 billion. Now, three-quarters of a million acres of California are gone, and with them, over 3,500 homes, \$2 billion, and 22 more human lives. And all this doesn't count the <a href="http://www.enn.com/news/2003-11-06/s_10161.asp" style="text-decoration: underline; color: blue; font-family: Verdana, Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif">impact on critters</a>, either. These conflagrations destroyed many of the very "endangered species" and habitats that environments claimed they wish to protect.\
\
When 1994 wildfires destroyed 3 million acres and killed 14 firefighters, many called for timber-thinning in these overgrown areas; but the Natural Resources Defense Council (NRDC) opposed these plans as being a mere <a href="http://www.foxnews.com/story/0,2933,101730,00.html" style="text-decoration: underline; color: blue; font-family: Verdana, Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif">"pretext for accelerated logging."</a> Youd think all the deadly fires since would have changed their minds. But when Congress recently passed the Healthy Forests Initiative, which would fund tree-thinning measures, the NRDC *still* opposed the measure, calling it a "Trojan horse" that would sneak through logging.\
\
For environmentalists, then, it is far better that a state, its citizens, their homes, and even the environment itself go up in flames, rather than allow anyone to *profit* by preventing it.\
\
And the greens arent through playing with fire: they aim to make matters even worse. On October 13, the Western Environmental Law Center--representing something called the Forest Service Employees for Environmental Ethics--filed a <a href="http://www.pacificlegal.org/view_Commentaries.asp?iID=108&amp;sTitle=It%92s+Time+to+Snuff+out+the+%91Let+it+Burn%92+Idea" style="text-decoration: underline; color: blue; font-family: Verdana, Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif">federal lawsuit</a> to stop the U. S. Forest Service from using aerially applied chemical fire retardants, and to halt its use of bulldozers to create firebreaks in national forests.\
\
Anyone still want to claim that the main concern of environmentalists is "protecting nature"? Not me. And <a href="http://www.pacificlegal.org/view_PublicationDetail.asp?iID=188&amp;dt=July%202003&amp;sTitle=Anti%2Dpeople+Activists+Play+with+Fire&amp;iParentID=6" style="text-decoration: underline; color: blue; font-family: Verdana, Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif">the Pacific Legal Foundation</a>, which is fighting the greens lawsuit, doesnt buy it, either. "The fact that environmental activists are willing to sacrifice their favorite 'constitutents' (i. e., nonhumans) reveals the hypocrisy of their 'save the species' campaigns and unearths their true agenda--to discourage any human contact with wilderness areas, even if it means putting human life and private property at grave risk."\
\
As I put it in my recent "Death By Environmentalism" lecture:\
\
"But whenever the naked consequences of their actions are made clear...and when even *then*, they fail to recoil in horror and repudiate their agenda--such people may be called many things. But 'idealist' is not one of them.\
\
"And I, for one, mean for the world to know that."\
\
