  - [Personalized Yahoo](http://my.yahoo.com)
  - [Gopher Jewels](gopher://cwis.usc.edu:70/11/Other_Gophers_and_Information_Resources/Gophers_by_Subject/Gopher_Jewels): Over 2,000 pointers to information by category

Webrings link sites of similar content and you can move from one to the next without a lot of searching or passing through sites of no interest. My [JewishPage](jewish.html) is part of the Jewish Ring, for example.
- Lists of Search Engines:
  - [Netscape's Internet Search](http://www.netscape.com/escapes/internet_search.html): links to a dozen or so of the most popular search sites
  - ![NEW\>\>\> ](graphics/new8.gif)[Search Engine Listings](http://searchenginewatch.com/links/)
    - ![NEW\>\>\> ](graphics/new8.gif)[Search Engine Reviews Chart](http://searchenginewatch.com/reports/reviewchart.html)
  - [NlightN](http://www.nln.com): an interface to the most popular search engines
  - [Search the Web](http://www.w3.org/hypertext/DataSources/bySubject/Virtual_libraries/Overview.html) through varios Web browsers, arranged by subject
  - [All-in-One Search Page](http://www.albany.net/allinone/) for a variety of search engines for the Web; a bit confusing
- Search engines (Web indexes):
  - [Google!](http://www.google.com): "uses a complicated mathematical analysis, calculated on more than a billion hyperlinks on the web, to return high-quality search results... uses sophisticated text-matching techniques to find pages that are both important and relevant to your search...gives you an excerpt of the text that matches your query -- with your search terms in bold -- right in the search results...returns pages which match all of your search terms in the text of the page or the link anchors pointing to it...keeps a copy of many of the web pages it returns. If the server is down or otherwise not available, Google!'s copy of the page can be a life saver. Using our cached version can often be much faster than following the regular link, though the information you receive will be less up to date. But in many cases, no more frustrating 404 Not Found errors!"
  - [AltaVista](http://altavista.digital.com): full-text index; has the largest database: 22 million Web pages and 13,000 Usenet newsgroups; Boolean terms OK, as well as proximity "near"; relevancy rankings "not the most effective"; may restrict searches to certain parts of documents (titles, links, dates, etc.); does not support stemming; gives the most comprehensive results, "when you want to find as much as you can on a single topic" (Internet World survey, 5/96)
  - [Excite](http://www.excite.com): full-text index of the Web (1.5m pages using the most popular sites); searches by content rather than keyword; slower; 50,000 Websites, 10,000 newsgroups; relevancy ranking; no Boolean logic; does not display URLs in the search results; good for those interested in mainstream topics which are widely discussed
  - [InfoSeek](http://guide.infoseek.com): most complete and relevant results (Internet World survey, 5/96); full-text index (WWW, FTP, gopher); proximity rankings used; relevancy rankings; Similar Pages command; limited database at present; for when you want to find a specific piece of information
  - [Inktomi](http://inktomi.cs.berkeley.edu): one of the largest indexes of the Web; no proximity option
  - [Lycos](http://www.lycos.com): partial-text index (based on headers, titles, links, first few words) of WWW, gopher, FTP files; relevancy ranking, partly based on sites' popularity; search term marked in bold in results; Boolean search OK; minus sign (-) decreases relevancy of search term; casts a wide net
  - ![\>\>\>NEW \>](graphics/new8.gif)[Direct Hit](http://www.directhit.com): "you can instantly see the best sites that other people have selected as the most relevant sites for your search request. You benefit from the human intelligence of all previous searchers who were looking for similar information...Direct Hit tracks what sites previous Internet searchers have actually selected from the search results list to determine the relevancy of sites. Based on these previous selections and a number of other metrics, Direct Hit provides highly relevant search results for the next searcher who is looking for similar information."
  - <a href="http://www.multicrawl.com%3EMultiCrawl%3C/a%3E%0A%0A%3Cli%3E%3Ca%0Ahref=" http:="" data-www.opentext.com"="">OpenText</a>: Boolean operators OK; relevancy score; three types of searches: \*simple term; \*multiple terms and operators, with field filter (title, URL, etc.); \* your own weighted search; Similar Pages command; good if you care to expend some effort tailoring your search
  - [WebCrawler](http://webcrawler.com): does not display text summary for found items; sparse customization options; no Boolean operators or upper-case letters; relevancy ranking; uses well-traveled pages; 500,000 websites at present; good for finding the most popular pages, but not for complex searches
  - [Galaxy](http://www.einet.net/): "The Web's resources, compiled for professionals...": Business And Commerce; Engineering And Technology; Government; Humanities; Law; Medicine; Reference; Science; Social Sciences
- "Meta"-Search Engines (search search engines):
  - [Ask Jeeves!](http://www.askjeeves.com): searches the top several search engines and presents the results; when going to a result, leaves a frame behind so you can return to Ask Jeeves! easily
  - [All4One](http://all4one.com/): executes searches in Yahoo!, Lycos, AltaVista, and Webcrawler simultaneously
  - [Metacrawler](http://www.metacrawler.com/): queries nine search engines simultaneously; collates, ranks and displays the results; search by type of site; use + before essential words
  - [SavvySearch](http://www.cs.colostate.edu/~dreiling/smartform.html): queries 25 search engines; ranks results; search plan scans appropriate search engines first, others may be invoked later
  - A great search engine: [WebTaxi](http://www.webtaxi.com): you can narrow the number of engines by subject; WebTaxi can also remain as a frame on your screen, ready to use, while you continue to surf the Web
- Usenet searches:
  - [DejaNews](http://www.dejanews.com): searches Usenet newsgroups (except alt.\*)
  - [Search for a Newsgroup](http://alpha.acast.nova.edu/cgi-bin/news.pl) on a given subject
