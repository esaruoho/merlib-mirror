**What Is SPAM?**

- SPAM is a name given to e-mail that is sent in large volumes to people the originator doesn't know but whose addresses they have obtained through various means. It is the Internet equivalent of junk mail and is refered to in polite circles as Unsolicited Commercial E-Mail. It is usually unwanted by the recipient and is almost always nothing but advertising of one form or another (yes, religious propoganda is also advertising).

**Why Is SPAM Bad?**

- Unlike conventional junk mail where the sender pays for the material and the delivery charges (postage), SPAM is paid for, at least in part, by the recipient. If your Internet Service Provider (ISP) charges you for system storage space (or worse yet, per e-mail message), each message that you don't want but receive anyway costs you money. Time you spend downloading and reading the message also may cost you connect charges. It costs the sender very little (sometimes nothing) since they only store one copy of the message and the mailing list. Hundreds of thousands or even millions of copies of their message are sent around the world and someone else pays it.

  In short, unsolicited, broadcast e-mail is forced pay-per-view advertising.

  I used to receive 15-20 SPAM e-mail messages *every day*. I used to maintain a list of the people who sent these things so I could encourage people not to do business with them, but it became too much work! Now that I've changed my e-mail address, stopped posting to newsgroups, and no longer put my e-mail address anywhere on my web page, I don't get that many (but I still get a few!). But I shouldn't have to become a cyber-hermit just to solve the problem!

**The SPAMmers Will Argue...**

- The SPAMmers will claim that the First Amendment gives them the right to send you e-mail. The point they fail to grasp is that rights always come with responsibilities, they aren't something you get for free. The First Amendment does not guarantee anyone the right to say anything to anyone in any form they wish. You still can't yell "Fire" in a crowded theater, that's being irresponsible. Using up other people's computer resources is irresponsible and anyone who hides behind the First Amendment to justify this sort of behavior doesn't understand even the most basic concepts of the U. S. Constitution.

  However, since the Internet is a world-wide resource, the laws of the United States probably don't have a lot to do with it anyway. Anti-SPAMmers aren't asking that these people be stopped from advertising their message. That's what newsgroups, bulletin boards, and *targeted* mailing lists (i.e. mailing lists you sign up to be on) are for. We merely want them to stop doing it intrusively and arbitrarily via personal e-mail.

  One of the bigger problems is that a lot of "adult" products are marketed this way (since no legitimate advertising medium will take them, no doubt) but these people do *nothing* to ensure that the recipient is, indeed, an adult. Lots of children use the Internet and are bombarded with this garbage and then their parents are left to sort out resulting problems. No, we're not asking for the world to protect children, merely for the world to take some precaution and not rush to corrupt them too early! Try making *your* 6-year-old boy understand that he really doesn't want to send "Bambi" \$10 for pictures of her with her dog Spot!

  Some claim to be sending you a one-time-only message and say they're not keeping a list. There is no such thing as a one-time-only list. Even if they aren't maintaining a list, the people they bought their list from are. I have received multiple copies of "one-time only" messages. In SPAM, just as in junk mail, once you're on their list, it's difficult, if not impossible, to get off (more on mailing lists below).

  My favorite justification is "this is not a SPAM." Yeah, right. Look, buddy, do you know me personally? No? Then it's a SPAM. How stupid do you have to be to make that sort of argument? Just because you believe you are doing me a favor (which you're not, but for the moment I'm willing to entertain the minutest possiblity that you're stupid enough to actually believe you are) justifies your sending me e-mail I don't want? Sheesh, get a clue.

  Some messages are actually illegal. Some SPAM messages are the same old chain-letters and pyramid schemes that people used to pull (and still do) in regular mail. They believe that using e-mail gets them out from under Federal jurisdiction so that these schemes are no longer illegal but they're wrong. If it's illegal in the U. S. Mail, then it's also illegal (in the U. S.) by e-mail (check with the Feds-- we used to have links to news articles proving this, but they've expired).

  They say "it's just like telemarketers calling you" (at home, during dinner). Not quite (although I hate that, too). My CALLER-ID box fixes that problem. If I don't know the number calling me, I don't answer the phone. No problem. They can still leave a message, but of course, phone solicitors never do because they can't manipulate you in a message. I could put a filter on my e-mail that would only accept messages from addresses I know, but I may *want* to receive something from someone new sometimes. The SPAM-equivalent in the telephone world would be that I ignore the call but next time I go to make a call, their call is still there waiting for me and I still have to listen to it before I can make my phone call! No thank you.

**SPAMmers usually hide behind fake addresses**

- Almost all SPAMmers are far from what you would call legitimate businesses. Very few real businesses advertise this way because they know it annoys people. Check any publication about how to market on the Internet and the majority will advise staying away from bulk e-mail. Almost everything advertised via SPAM is, at best crap, and at worst, an actual ripoff.

  Legitimate advertising always gives you a way to respond to the advertiser. Any communication that has no authenticated source also has no responsibility for the information contained therein. Often you can't reply to these messages because the SPAMmers change the From: line (which gives the recipient a way to reply to the message). They do this so they don't receive the thousands of angry replies and "bounces" (notifications of non-delivery) from the invalid addresses. Since the beginnings of the Internet it has always been considered "bad form" to send a message that cannot be replied to. No one should put any creedence whatsoever in any message where the sender tries to remain anonymous (of course, this applies to more than just e-mail, this is just plain common sense advice-- why would you believe anything when you don't know the source of the information?).

  Their favorite trick is to use some other domain as an SMTP relay, making it look like their mail came from the other domain! It isn't sleazy enough that they're sending out junk to begin with, but they make what little tracing you can do point to somebody else! Fortunately, there is now anti-relay software to prevent this. But this is one of those things you just shouldn't have to deal with. It's like a bunch of children on a playground telling lies to each other. Grow up!

**Mailing lists**

- Just as mailing lists are bought and sold in the postal world, businesses are springing up to do the same with e-mail addresses. The one bright side is that many of them fake addresses to pad their totals by combining usernames and hostnames together from their list of valid ones into combinations that don't actually work. Ironically, the SPAMmers are cheating each other! At least the universe has a sense of humor.

  But thousands of machines still receive these messages, look up users, determine the address is invalid, and attempt to return the message to the sender (a courtesy). Then, of course, because there is usually no valid return address, they also have to process the return mail from the machine that had to process the fake return address. All this results in hundreds of thousands of machines constantly having to do pointless e-mail processing. This process costs the owners of the machines money and if they are ISPs, they will eventually pass the cost on to their subscribers.

**"But we're responsible, we give you a way to get off our list"**

- I've tried dozens of the "we'll remove you" web sites and e-mail addresses with no perceivable effect (or even acknowledgement of my requests) so far. I always wonder if they are really trying to provide a legitimate way of getting of their list or if all they're doing is *verifying* that my address is valid and then sell it to other SPAMmers! Some e-mail addresses are invalid but they put them there hoping that when you try to send mail there and you get the bounce, you'll just assume that *you* made the mistake-- they have no intention of removing you from anything. I have talked to postmasters at sites where these "remove" addresses point only to find out there never was any such address.

  The few that actually do seem to intend to respond to your request often don't work because they are sent such a huge volume of responses that they can't possibly process all of them or they never receive some because the incoming mail file goes over quota. This should be a clue to them, but of course, since money is involved, greed reigns supreme.

  The bottom line is, I shouldn't have to do something to get off a list that I never should have been put on in the first place. It should be the list maintainer's responsibility to filter good candidates initially! But there is no filtering, they just grab as many addresses as they can from as many places as they can.

**What can you do to stop it?**

- **DON'T DO BUSINESS WITH THE ADVERTISER.** They wouldn't do this unless it worked. People must be responding. If NOBODY responded to SPAM, it would die a natural death because it would not provide the SPAMmers with any leads. It's just that simple.

  Unfortunately, if a SPAMmer sends out one million messages and only *one* person buys whatever he's selling, then the whole process has probably been worth it to him (since it cost him so little) and he'll try it again. Until absolutely *everyone* ignores SPAM, it won't stop.

  COMPLAIN. Many people use procmail or other programs to help filter their mail and throw away e-mail that can be identified as junk mail before they ever see it (lowering even further the percentage of SPAM that actually gets delivered to a valid user). But this doesn't do anything to discourage the behavior.

  This used to be my number one suggestion, but I have to confess that over the years of receiving SPAM, complaining, even automatically, has become tiresome and seems to do no real good (for every SPAMmer that is stopped, two more seem to come along). Sometimes complaints to everyone in the chain merely annoys some well-intentioned system administrators who really are doing their best to help control the flow of SPAM. So I now make this suggestion with some restraint. Complain when you feel it is worth your effort and might do some good, but arbitrary complaining to the world is probably not helpful.

  If you can reply to the originator, do so. If they're foolish enough to include an 800 number, call them. Tell them you don't like what they're doing. Ask to be removed from their list. If there is no address or it doesn't work, complain to the postmaster at the hostname where the message originated. Sometimes this is difficult to find out since the senders may modify the headers to hide the origin of the message. But it is extremely difficult to completely hide headers that show the path a message took to reach you. Your mail reader may not show you all the headers, but they are there. If you save the message to a file, you'll find extra headers that help you see where the message has been.

  If enough people complain to enough service providers, they will do something. In my personal experience, most of these folks are happy to investigate instances of e-mail abuse. Some even have special addresses to use rather than \``postmaster`' to report cases of abuse because they have policies against this sort of activity and shut down accounts that violate their usage agreements (people sign agreements that say they won't SPAM and then do anyway... of course, they probably didn't even READ the agreement). If you get SPAM from within any of these domains, forward it (with FULL headers) to the appropriate address for investigation:

**When will it all end?**

- Yes, eventually SPAM will disappear. It would disappear overnight if we (collectively) would just stop doing business with SPAMmers. Unfortunately, the Internet is always growing so there is always a "newbie" coming along who isn't aware of all this and will respond and buy whatever crap they're selling. Of course, they learn, just like you learned not do business with phone solicitors (although I would wager that a higher percentage of phone solicitors are at least legitimate!). But each time one "victim" learns not to respond, two more "newbies" sign up for Internet access and the whole process starts again. To paraphrase P. T. Barnum: There's a sucker born every minute... and then he gets Internet access.

  The dream I hold onto is that one day all mail readers and mail gateways will require valid digital signatures and certificates to deliver or forward a message. This will truly authenticate the senders (which is only fair). This won't stop people from SPAMming, but it will stop them from doing so anonymously, which is 99% of the problem. When you send out 50000 messages and get back 49998 angry replies, your incentive to stop is greatly increased! Unfortunately I don't think this is in our near future, but it will happen eventually. When it does, the ride on the gravy train the SPAMmers have been taking will be over. Legitimate businesses will be able to use e-mail and recipients will be able to respond to the person who sends them mail, either to do business or ask them to not send them any more e-mail. A little accountability would sure be a good thing.
