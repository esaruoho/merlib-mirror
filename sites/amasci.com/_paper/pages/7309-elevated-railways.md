*Source page: `~robertf/transit/elevated.html`*

**Elevated Railways**\
Elevated railways are a good means of rapid transit and have many of the advantages of monorails, but cost more, are bulkier, and are noisier.\

<td data-valign="TOP" width="73%">Elevated Railways are a form of grade separated transportation so have many of the advantages of monorail — it is faster, safer, and causes less disruption to surface traffic.<br />
 <br />
Several cities use elevated railways, including Chicago (The El), Manila, Philippines (LRT), and Vancouver, B.C. (The Sky Train).<br />
 <br />
However, elevated railways have some of the same disadvantages as other railways, such as noise. Additionally, elevated light rail, usually with two parallel railroad tracks, requires a wide, bulky structure of steel and/or concrete to support the tracks and trains. The elevated structure of a monorail blocks less of the sky, casts a much smaller shadow, and because the two guideway beams are much lighter, the supporting structure can be much smaller.<br />
 <br />
The heavier construction required to support a railway is also more expensive than that for a monorail.<br />
