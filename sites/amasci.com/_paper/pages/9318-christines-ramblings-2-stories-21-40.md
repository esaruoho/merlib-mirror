<td><strong>21. Baby Makes Three</strong> <br />
<strong>     This one gave me a hell of a hard time getting started. I almost gave up on it. But I'm glad I didn't, because it turned out really fun once I got an angle on it.</strong> <br />
<strong>     I'd had a scene sitting around ever since I finished Ever After, in which Demona goes to the OB-GYN for a checkup, and I'd joked with some people about what labor would be like for MacBeth. But every time I tried to turn it into a story, I'd get stuck.</strong> <br />
<strong>     And then I decided to cross over with ER. At once, everything fell into place and from then on it was easy as could be. Poor Carter having to deal with an apparently injured-then-healed MacBeth, Doug Ross checking out the Weird Sisters ...</strong> <br />
<strong>     Needing a reason to get them sent to the ER in the first place, I introduced Bryce Canmore, and hot on his heels was Orpheus Bluestone. Until I wrote this one, I had no idea Matt and Eurydice were going to have a kid. Bryce's remark about his father killing Orph's father was one of those things that just happens and spawns its own storyline.</strong> <br />
<strong>     Title: It was almost "Here Comes Demona With a Baby Carriage" but that was too long, so I just chose something a little more manageable.</strong> <br />
<strong>     Memorable Line: Never before in history had a man known just what it was to give birth.</strong> <br />
 &#10;<p><strong>22. Mother's Day</strong> <br />
<strong>     Another of my favorites. I had not written much with Fox, but the idea of exploring more of the relationship between her and her mother was appealing (and partly triggered by a mailing-list discussion about the fates of New Yorkers during Oberon's sleep spell).</strong> <br />
<strong>     Then some things occurred to me -- Demona and Titania had quite a lot in common, and had probably met before back in the time of Shakespeare (I just know I am eventually going to have to write</strong> <strong>something set in Elizabethan England). Their conversation over brunch was one I greatly enjoyed.</strong> <br />
<strong>     It was time for Jericho to make his debut. Even then, I never expected he would turn out quite so bad. I knew Demona was going to corrupt him, turn him against humanity the way she failed to do with Goliath and Angela, but I had no idea he would get <em>that</em> bad! Honest!</strong> <br />
<strong>     In an RPG a few years back, I did a mean thing to the players and trapped them in the fairy realm for what they thought was a week. When they emerged, twenty years had passed in the outside world, and their own children had grown up. One of the NPCs, a woman of odd psyche named Raven (someday, there will be fantasy novels starring her), found herself falling in love with her own son, Shade.</strong> <br />
<strong>     So that probably set the groundwork for the direction the Demona and Jericho story developed. Given the gargoyle way of looking at things, they weren't inclined to see each other as mother and son --</strong> <strong>Demona's attitude on the whole subject seems more possessive than parental anyway, even in her dealings with Angela -- and were of the same apparent age. Once Demona noticed that Jericho, who was mateless and none too pleased about it, found her attractive, she certainly wouldn't be above using that to help her</strong> <strong>attain her goal of absolute loyalty.</strong> <br />
<strong>     Title: another case of the obvious.</strong> <br />
<strong>     Memorable line: "It is my intention to get ... what do they call it? ... shitfaced." "To the Dragon with Goliath and what he will or won't allow!"</strong> <br />
 </p>
<p><strong>23. Whirlwind</strong> <br />
<strong>     I am at heart a matchmaker, and nothing tickles me more than bringing characters together. It was time to mess with Beth Maza's life. And there was never a question in my mind who the perfect candidate was. It made for sort of the flip side of the Jericho thing -- Coyote <em>isn't</em> related to Beth, but looks just like a younger version of her father, which adds a forbidden spice.</strong> <br />
<strong>     Speaking of spices, I was planning to introduce the three identical humans with links to the Weird Sisters in this one, and I was going to call them Pepper, Ginger, and Saffron. But then I came to my senses, realizing they'd be the Spice Girls and I would probably barf all over my keyboard. I had prophesied the "Archmage's curse" in Mother's Day when I introduced Gabriel's three mates and it was on my mind nagging me because it was one of those things that wrote itself and I had no clue where it was going to lead.</strong> <br />
<strong>     Anyway, I thought better of it, thankfully, and just focused on Beth and Coyote. Wild, carefree outdoor sex with a hint of Native American mythos (of which, admittedly, I know next to nothing).</strong> <br />
<strong>     Title: was originally going to be "Mark of the Coyote," to go along with Mark of the Panther and to refer to Beth's cute little tattoo. But I didn't like it. So I wracked my poor overheated excuse for a brain</strong> <strong>until I came up with Whirlwind. A whirlwind romance, a guy who travels via dust devil ... it worked for me.</strong> <br />
<strong>     Memorable line: "My, my, Maza, look what you've got."</strong> <br />
 </p>
<p><strong>24. Lost and Found</strong> <br />
<strong>     I used to not like stories that didn't involve the main clan or characters. I avoided reading fanfic that was only <em>based</em> on Gargoyles but had whole other clans in whole other places with little or no connection to the actual show. Call me a snob, I know, I know.</strong> <br />
<strong>     So it kinda troubled me when I would get requests from readers for "more about Aiden," for instance, or other of my own characters.</strong> <br />
<strong>     But I finally decided I would take a stab at writing a story set mostly completely apart from the clan yet still connected to it. I watched the Avalon episodes and, as I'd already had Mary and Finella wash up near Castle Wyvern in the 1970's, got to wondering what would happen if they'd had a stowaway in the</strong> <br />
<strong>form of a misplaced egg. An egg which would, thanks to the peculiarities of Avalon time, only be of an age equivalent to a human pre-teen.</strong> <br />
<strong>     It</strong> <strong>was right around here that Christi Smith-Hayden introduced her Little Blessings, and got me thinking about</strong> <strong>hatchlings and what it would be like trying to raise a gargoyle on your own.</strong> <br />
<strong>     I loved writing about Angus, the way he wailed with a piece of shell still pasted to his nose, the</strong> <strong>way he'd lay on the floor to drink milk from an old hot water bottle. And, like every new parent can attest,</strong> <strong>the idea of a kid that sleeps through, solid as a rock, for hours on end, is a veritable dream!</strong> <br />
<strong>     This one is a sad story but also weirdly uplifting, at least it is for me. Here's this woman, Eihblin</strong> <strong>Driscoll, who has lost everything. Her husband, her son, her brother. She's alone in the world with no one to</strong> <strong>love, no one to care for. And then she finds an egg, which fills that emptiness.</strong> <br />
<strong>     Title: again fairly obvious; his egg had been lost and now was found, but also Eibhlin's purpose</strong> <strong>and lease on life.</strong> <br />
<strong>     Memorable line: She could see the tiny wings sticking up over the edge of the nestlike shark fins as the creature moved and struggled.</strong> <br />
 </p>
<p><strong>25. Love Machine</strong> <br />
<strong>     This is the second-tackiest thing I've ever written, but</strong> <br />
<strong><em>this</em> one, I'm also insanely proud of.</strong> <br />
<strong>     In my circle of acquaintances ... and this is gonna come</strong> <br />
<strong>as a huge shock to you all, I'm sure ... I am the dirty-minded</strong> <br />
<strong>wicked one. On one occasion, the gang decided to buy an</strong> <br />
<strong>inflatable sheep as a gag gift for a friend, and I was the only</strong> <br />
<strong>one who dared go into the shop (Lover's Package; and as it</strong> <br />
<strong>happened, it wasn't a matter of me having to <em>dare</em> to go ...</strong> <br />
<strong>hell, I had a punch card!).</strong> <br />
<strong>     I was also at one time going to become a home-lingerie-</strong> <br />
<strong>party saleswoman. Like Tupperware, only lots more fun, going</strong> <br />
<strong>around to parties selling naughty nighties, potions &amp; lotions, sex</strong> <br />
<strong>toys, etc. But then I got pregnant (not as a direct result, might I</strong> <br />
<strong>add) and since I had <em>evening</em> sickness instead of morning</strong> <br />
<strong>sickness, it kind of took the fun out of going to such parties. Not</strong> <br />
<strong>to mention that it would send a weird message to the</strong> <br />
<strong>customers ...</strong> <br />
<strong>     Anyway, therefore, when it came time to write the story</strong> <br />
<strong>of Goliath and Elisa's bachelor/ette parties, it was no contest at</strong> <br />
<strong>all. In fact, much of Love Machine is autobiographical -- Club</strong> <br />
<strong>Victoria, with drag queens followed by strippers, is based on a</strong> <br />
<strong>place in Portland where my friends took me for my</strong> <br />
<strong>bachelorette.</strong> <br />
<strong>     Yes, all of the merchandise Fox shows to Xanatos really</strong> <br />
<strong>does exist. No, I do not own all of it. No, I won't tell which ones.</strong> <br />
<strong>    Yes, there <em>are</em> places that make cakes like that: the one here</strong> <br />
<strong>in Seattle is called "The Erotic Bakery" and the things they can</strong> <br />
<strong>do with marzipan would curl your hair (in my defense, I could</strong> <br />
<strong>say that I only go there because it's the only place in town to</strong> <br />
<strong>buy those "Darwin fish," but I doubt such a statement would be</strong> <br />
<strong>believed ...)</strong> <br />
<strong>     I am sometimes asked if I have ever gone too far in</strong> <br />
<strong>fanfic. And I have to say yes. The end of this story is too much.</strong> <br />
<strong>When Jackal's extendo-thingie wraps around Fox's neck ... too</strong> <br />
<strong>far.</strong> <br />
<strong>     I really disliked Jackal and Hyena after Upgrade. I</strong> <br />
<strong>thought they became way too comic-booky, too far removed</strong> <br />
<strong>from realistic (I thought about writing a naughty Inspector</strong> <br />
<strong>Gadget crossover but since I've never watched that one, I</strong> <br />
<strong>doubted I could manage). But anyway, that's why I presented</strong> <br />
<strong>Jackal in the most ludicrous possible light.</strong> <br />
<strong>     This gave me the chance to introduce Godiva (inspired</strong> <br />
<strong>in a roundabout way by Galatea from my third-ever fanfic, the</strong> <br />
<strong>ST:TNG one called "Fully Functional"), and to do fun things such</strong> <br />
<strong>as get Owen plastered and basically show everybody kicking</strong> <br />
<strong>off their shoes and having a good time. It was written</strong> <br />
<strong>extremely fast. Like Kittens, this one just wrote itself, and was a</strong> <br />
<strong>smooth, delightful ride all the way through.</strong> <br />
<strong>     Some brave souls have asked me what the rest of the</strong> <br />
<strong>joke is, the one that we only hear the punchline, "all I got was</strong> <br />
<strong>hand lotion." It is a real joke, so feel free to e-mail me if you</strong> <br />
<strong>want it in its full gross glory ; )</strong> <br />
<strong>     Title: this one was a pain in the butt! I couldn't come up</strong> <br />
<strong>with anything I liked. I was going to just call it "His and Hers,"</strong> <br />
<strong>because it was about his party and hers. But I hated it. The</strong> <br />
<strong>story was almost done and still had no title. Then I was saved</strong> <br />
<strong>by the radio.  Saturday Night Fever, Star 101.5. I walked into</strong> <br />
<strong>work that night, got shift report and did my paperwork, and sat</strong> <br />
<strong>down to try and write while in the lobby, the residents were</strong> <br />
<strong>gettin' down to the disco music. And then ... the song began, that</strong> <br />
<strong>five-beat followed by "I'm just a love machine, and I don't work</strong> <br />
<strong>for nobody but you ..." and I leaped up and screamed in joy!</strong> <br />
<strong>Perfect!</strong> <br />
<strong>     Memorable line: All of them ... </strong> <br />
 </p>
<p><strong>26. The Wedding</strong> <br />
<strong>     Despite horrible temptation, I decided to be nice to</strong> <br />
<strong>Goliath and Elisa, and let them have a perfect wedding. No</strong> <br />
<strong>interruptions, no laser fire, no attacks, just a sweet and</strong> <br />
<strong>wonderful ceremony.</strong> <br />
<strong>     And I got to use what I think is one of my best opening</strong> <br />
<strong>lines ever: "Aunt Agnes, this is Goliath." That line is like</strong> <br />
<strong>dropping a cherry bomb in an outhouse ... you just hold your</strong> <br />
<strong>breath and wait for the explosion. But then, to have further fun,</strong> <br />
<strong>I didn't bring on the explosion the way everyone expected.</strong> <br />
<strong>Rather than go nuclear, Aunt Agnes starts getting on Goliath's</strong> <br />
<strong>case about not being gainfully employed. I love it!</strong> <br />
<strong>     Having been so nice to Goliath and Elisa all through the</strong> <br />
<strong>wedding, then I got mean and gave them their first almost-spat,</strong> <br />
<strong>their guilt-and-longing disagreement about children. This was to</strong> <br />
<strong>make it have even more impact when they meet Elektra.</strong> <br />
<strong>Instead of seeing her as an abomination, she represents their</strong> <br />
<strong>hope made real.</strong> <br />
<strong>     Goliath's speech took a long time and choked me up.</strong> <br />
<strong>He's normally so undemonstrative that to have him do that,</strong> <br />
<strong>express his feelings in front of everyone, was a big step.</strong> <br />
<strong>     The goblet they use -- we have one, a gift from Tim's</strong> <br />
<strong>parents, two that fit together to make one. It was so neat that I</strong> <br />
<strong>had to include it.</strong> <br />
<strong>     Title: again, there was never a question of what to call it.</strong> <br />
<strong>     Memorable line: "Does changing diapers cross your eyes, mister Goliath?" and His tuxedo <em>exploded</em> off of him in shreds ...</strong> <br />
 </p>
<p><strong>27. Fallen Angels</strong> <br />
<strong>     I am not at all in touch with popular music. I listen to</strong> <br />
<strong>classical, with occasional forays into the mid to late 1980's.</strong> <br />
<strong>Billy Joel, Hall &amp; Oates, Stray Cats, Duran Duran ... those are my</strong> <br />
<strong>bands. I'm officially an old fart because I hear music nowadays</strong> <br />
<strong>and don't get it, don't see what "kids today" see in it.</strong> <br />
<strong>     So, when I made Scarlet Angel, I knew they were</strong> <br />
<strong>destined to be exceedingly minor players in my universe. Yet,</strong> <br />
<strong>much to my surprise, although they'd only appeared in one</strong> <br />
<strong>story and been mentioned in a few others, they had gotten</strong> <br />
<strong>something of a following. People were sending me lyrics.</strong> <br />
<strong>Asking me if they sounded more like (some band I'd never</strong> <br />
<strong>heard of) or (some other band I'd never heard of).</strong> <br />
<strong>     It was weird. And it made me feel bad, because here's</strong> <br />
<strong>all these people liking them, when all along I'd planned to kill</strong> <br />
<strong>them off.</strong> <br />
<strong>     Did I let that stop me? Nope.</strong> <br />
<strong>     I am, as I've said before, a wuss about killing characters.</strong> <br />
<strong>I didn't want to give Julianna, Aquarius, and the others the "Vito</strong> <br />
<strong>treatment." Not just quick-boom-over. I wanted to make Ebon</strong> <br />
<strong>suffer, and I wanted to show just how much Jericho had been</strong> <br />
<strong>twisted by Demona's expert psychological manipulation.</strong> <br />
<strong>     This story mostly, though, came about because I'd gotten</strong> <br />
<strong>this idea in my head about Demona seducing a Quarryman,</strong> <br />
<strong>then changing halfway through the act. Like many ideas, it</strong> <br />
<strong>wasn't going to die out on its own. I had to write it. But how to</strong> <br />
<strong>make it fit? That's how I came up with the memory-drug angle,</strong> <br />
<strong>and Gustav Sevarius (who is one of my all-time favorite</strong> <br />
<strong>characters).</strong> <br />
<strong>     Now, it stands to reason that the clones were given</strong> <br />
<strong>those L.A. area names was because that's where Disney is</strong> <br />
<strong>located. But why would <em>Demona</em> choose those names?</strong> <br />
<strong>Unless she'd spent some time there. Sigh -- there's another story</strong> <br />
<strong>I'll have to write someday. At the time, I was reading "They</strong> <br />
<strong>Thirst," by Robert McCammon (one of the best vampire novels</strong> <br />
<strong><em>ever</em>) and it is set in L.A. so I borrowed its brooding horror-</strong> <br />
<strong>film castle.</strong> <br />
<strong>     By this point, I was realizing that Jericho was much</strong> <br />
<strong>more than I'd bargained for. His ... "solo scene," shall we say ...</strong> <br />
<strong>proved that. And proved how Demona was getting caught in</strong> <br />
<strong>her own game.</strong> <br />
<strong>     He had totally taken on a life of his own, and between</strong> <br />
<strong>him and Gustav, I had more villains than I knew what to do</strong> <br />
<strong>with (and here I was planning to have Thailog go bad again ... I</strong> <br />
<strong>wound up having him focus his revenge solely on Demona).</strong> <br />
<strong>     Title: multiple meaning. It chronicles Jericho's fall from</strong> <br />
<strong>grace (his first deliberate murders and other assorted sins and</strong> <br />
<strong>evils). It is also about Demona, how far will she fall to achieve</strong> <br />
<strong>her ends? And of course Scarlet Angel, who now number</strong> <br />
<strong>among the fallen ...</strong> <br />
<strong>     Memorable line: What would he do, she wondered, if he could see her like this? Would he flee from her in shame, or fall upon her with a frenzied lust?</strong> <br />
<strong>     Additional silliness: Elaine Kristen is back! I used to play</strong> <br />
<strong>the occasional superhero RPG, and she was the annoying nosy</strong> <br />
<strong>reporter.</strong> <br />
 </p>
<p><strong>28. Lead Me Not ...</strong> <br />
<strong>     The Thanksgiving waterfight is one of my favorite</strong> <br />
<strong>scenes (and even includes a none-too-subtle plug for my own</strong> <br />
<strong>book &lt;g&gt;).</strong> <br />
<strong>     I had been accused of having all my female characters</strong> <br />
<strong>be sexually assertive and multi-orgasmic. To which I replied</strong> <br />
<strong>with great blinking innocent eyes, "Are you telling me that's</strong> <br />
<strong><em>not</em> the way it is for everyone?"</strong> <br />
<strong>     But it wasn't an inaccurate point, and certainly one that I</strong> <br />
<strong>had to take into consideration when it came time for Aiden and</strong> <br />
<strong>Lex to go all the way. Because if there is any character who is</strong> <br />
<strong>the antithesis of sexually assertive and multi-orgasmic, it's gotta</strong> <br />
<strong>be Aiden. This time, I had to craft a scene that was fraught with</strong> <br />
<strong>adolescent awkwardness, haste, guilt, and fear of being caught.</strong> <br />
<strong>     This story took a lot more planning than I usually do,</strong> <br />
<strong>because instead of only involving a few characters, I had</strong> <br />
<strong>everybody running around doing stuff. For as long as it is, it</strong> <br />
<strong>wound up surprisingly fast-paced and I'm pleased with my</strong> <br />
<strong>action scenes (something that usually gives me trouble).</strong> <br />
<strong>     I got to have a blast with Birdie, from her brazen visit to</strong> <br />
<strong>the men's room to her theft of Elisa's car to her Speedy</strong> <br />
<strong>Gonzales impression -- in fact, it was here that Birdie's talent for</strong> <br />
<strong>mimicry first showed up. This also marked the first appearance</strong> <br />
<strong>of 23rd Precinct studmuffin Rick Alvarez (who is based on a</strong> <br />
<strong>character Tim played in my last game, Rick Avery; I have</strong> <br />
<strong>written quite a few stories about Rick and his previous</strong> <br />
<strong>incarnation, Sir Richard). <em>And</em> I introduced Broadway to</strong> <br />
<strong>Elektra. Whew. I was busy.</strong> <br />
<strong>     All that and killer unicorns too.</strong> <br />
<strong>     Title: originally, this was to be called "Dark Side," and</strong> <br />
<strong>Demona was to try and recruit Aiden with promises of power,</strong> <br />
<strong>ya know, "come over to the dark side, Aiden." But I just couldn't</strong> <br />
<strong>do it. Aiden is too damn <em>nice</em>! She didn't <em>have</em> a dark side!</strong> <br />
<strong>The more I wrote, the less the title fit. Then I got to the part</strong> <br />
<strong>where Demona supposedly rescues Aiden from her</strong> <br />
<strong>kidnappers, and it reminded me of how she tried the same</strong> <br />
<strong>thing on Brooklyn. So I recycled the same dialogue from that</strong> <br />
<strong>episode, and used the title "Lead Me Not ..." because the rest of</strong> <br />
<strong>the quote is " ... into temptation," which was the name of the</strong> <br />
<strong>episode ; )</strong> <br />
<strong>     Memorable line: "What bees these mortals fool." and "Uh ... what's this part about a 'chaste vessel'?"</strong> <br />
 </p>
<p><strong>29. Romances</strong> <br />
<strong>     A bunch of little ideas were cluttering my head, so I</strong> <br />
<strong>found a common thread, tied them all together, and released</strong> <br />
<strong>them as a story in three sections.</strong> <br />
<strong>     The first, Girl Talk, came about because I'd gotten some</strong> <br />
<strong>pleas from readers for more Brooklyn. When's Brooklyn going</strong> <br />
<strong>to get lucky? Which is something I'd been meaning to do, but</strong> <br />
<strong>hadn't come up with any good ideas (though there was one</strong> <br />
<strong>about Angela seducing him while he's in the driving simulator; I</strong> <br />
<strong>don't know if that one will ever happen, maybe, who knows?</strong> <br />
<strong>&lt;g&gt;). I get tired of writing the same kind of sex over and over, so</strong> <br />
<strong>I am always trying to experiment with different positions and</strong> <br />
<strong>acts. And I've always felt that those little talons up on top of</strong> <br />
<strong>their wings can be used for something ...</strong> <br />
<strong>     The second, Fairy Favours, continued this theme of</strong> <br />
<strong>experimentation -- the question had been raised as to whether</strong> <br />
<strong>or not the Third Race viewed/had sex the same way as</strong> <br />
<strong>mortals. I wanted to get Puck's point of view, especially as until</strong> <br />
<strong>Aiden cast her spell, both Puck and Owen were inexperienced.</strong> <br />
<strong>It was also a chance to take a look into Owen and Cordelia's</strong> <br />
<strong>private life.</strong> <br />
<strong>     The last section, First Date , had to be done because I'd</strong> <br />
<strong>let Preston Vogel and Robyn Canmore meet at Goliath and</strong> <br />
<strong>Elisa's wedding and figured they better go out before too long. I</strong> <br />
<strong>didn't have a very good feel for either character, though, and</strong> <br />
<strong>don't really like my finished product. Though I did like the</strong> <br />
<strong>exchange between Robyn and her brother John, and the</strong> <br />
<strong>appearance of Bryce's mother Brianna.</strong> <br />

<strong>     Title: that came right from the dream, what can I say?</strong> <br />
<strong>     Memorable lines: When his head came off, whoever had done it gave a mighty pull, and that [spine] must have just slithered out of him like a strand of spaghetti; also "Luck, my furry grey ass."</strong> <br />
 </p>
<p><strong>37. The Horror of Innsbrook</strong> <br />
<strong>     Tim was big on Lovecraft in college. I'd never read any,</strong> <br />
<strong>nor played Call of Cthulhu with his group. Even now, though he</strong> <br />
<strong>hasn't played in years, he has all the books, a T-shirt with an</strong> <br />
<strong>airbrushed Cthulhu on it, a Cthulhu-fish bumper sticker (like the</strong> <br />
<strong>Darwin fish, only with tentacles), another that reads "Cthulhu for</strong> <br />
<strong>Prime Minister: this time vote for the Greater of two Evils", a</strong> <br />
<strong>wood Cthulhu sign that I had one of those carve-it-while-you-</strong> <br />
<strong>wait guys do at the mall (and did he look at me funny? you be</strong> <br />
<strong>the judge), and last but not least, a Rancor (is that what it's</strong> <br />
<strong>called? the big ugly monster that Luke fights in the pit in Return</strong> <br />
<strong>of the Jedi) with wings and gross rubbery fake worms bristle-</strong> <br />
<strong>drooping out of its face.</strong> <br />
<strong>     So, eventually, I gave some of the Lovecraft books a try.</strong> <br />
<strong>Not bad. Some great word use and word creation. I never did</strong> <br />
<strong>buy the whole business about his monsters being symbolic</strong> <br />
<strong>representations of the female genitals, or whatever the hell that</strong> <br />
<strong>was all about. Pretty good reading.</strong> <br />
<strong>     Much later, thinking about where I could send Broadway</strong> <br />
<strong>and Elektra, it occurred to me that introducing some hideous</strong> <br />
<strong>shambling monsters might be fun. A Cthulhu crossover, why</strong> <br />
<strong>not? I steeped myself in Lovecraft, reading nothing else for a</strong> <br />
<strong>week straight. And then, when my dreams were getting <em>way</em></strong> <br />
<strong>too weird and even my waking thoughts were affected, I sat</strong> <br />
<strong>down to write.</strong> <br />
<strong>    Another challenge. Could I capture the Lovecraftian</strong> <br />
<strong>style? He is one of the most imitated authors ever, but often</strong> <br />
<strong>one of the <em>least-successfully</em> imitated authors. Now I know</strong> <br />
<strong>why. That is a lot harder than it looks!</strong> <br />
<strong>     Then I had to decide <em>why</em> I was sending the travelers</strong> <br />
<strong>there. It was primarily Elektra's quest, so it had to have</strong> <br />
<strong>something to do with her. Thanks to Lovecraft's own stories, I</strong> <br />
<strong>knew his yucky oceanic fish-beasts often liked to mate with</strong> <br />
<strong>humans, but it was the bad side of such crossbreeding.</strong> <br />
<strong>     A warning for Elektra ... that was kind of grim, was there</strong> <br />
<strong>nothing hopeful? Then I came up with the idea of denying the</strong> <br />
<strong>day. Gargoyles turned to stone, humans did not, how about a</strong> <br />
<strong>middle ground? <em>That</em> was the lesson Elektra could learn, so</strong> <br />
<strong>that when she had her inevitable confrontation with Demona,</strong> <br />
<strong>she'd be able to deliver one whopper of a surprise.</strong> <br />
<strong>     Title: I wanted something that sounded suitably</strong> <br />
<strong>Lovecraftian, and that was. Anyone reading that title would</strong> <br />
<strong>have a pretty good idea what they were in for.</strong> <br />
<strong>     Memorable line: Now I remembered! Now I remembered the truth of my father's death, how he had raged and threatened my mother until she buried in his head the bloodied cleaver that she had just used to sever the <em>malformed and vestigial wings from my back!</em></strong><em> </em> <br />
 </p>
<p><strong>38. Xantasia</strong> <br />
<strong>     After the last two, I really needed something light and</strong> <br />
<strong>frivolous.</strong> <br />
<strong>     At the time, I was running an RPG for Tim in which he</strong> <br />
<strong>was playing a young nobleman with a secret life as a pirate</strong> <br />
<strong>and occasional highwayman (this character of his, Benedict</strong> <br />
<strong>Tate, is mentioned again in The Scottish Rogue). I was also</strong> <br />
<strong>reading an anthology called "Highwaymen!", in which several</strong> <br />
<strong>authors had taken their inspiration from a beautiful and</strong> <br />
<strong>haunting poem by Alfred Noyes.</strong> <br />
<strong>     And it seemed to me that Xanatos would make a</strong> <br />
<strong><em>great</em> highwayman! I toyed with having it be a past-life kind</strong> <br />
<strong>of thing, rejected that idea, and then thought it was time to do a</strong> <br />
<strong>"holodeck episode." Then it became something of an in-joke,</strong> <br />
<strong>because Riker once fell in love with a woman on the holodeck,</strong> <br />
<strong>so I decided to put a little stress on the Xanatos marriage by</strong> <br />
<strong>introducing Jacqueline.</strong> <br />
<strong>     In the second part, I returned once again to Disneyland</strong> <br />
<strong>simply because I love it so and wanted the gargs to have a</strong> <br />
<strong>chance to enjoy it. I sent Broadway and Elektra there so they</strong> <br />
<strong>could have a nice date, a fun evening out with some friends,</strong> <br />
<strong>and they could also get a pointed hint when they witnessed</strong> <br />
<strong>Lex pop the question.</strong> <br />
<strong>     Title: Xantasia itself just seemed like one of those things</strong> <br />
<strong>that <em>someone</em> would have gotten around to saying</strong> <br />
<strong>eventually, like Xanadu and Xanatopia -- the man has such a</strong> <br />
<strong>colossal ego! The first section, "Though Hell Should Bar the</strong> <br />
<strong>Way," is a line from that aforementioned poem; the second, "Another Double Date," is a reference to the last time Lex and Broadway went out, though with Aiden and Birdie.</strong> <br />
<strong>     Memorable lines: He was used to non-projectile weapons, and the recoil very nearly knocked him off his horse; also "That's Aiden! And she's with some <em>guy</em>!"</strong> <br />
 </p>
<p><strong>39. FoxFire</strong> <br />
<strong>     Inspiration comes from the weirdest places ...</strong> <br />
<strong>     I'd known for a long time that Fox had a very</strong> <br />
<strong>complicated past. A teenage runaway, headed to L.A. to try and</strong> <br />
<strong>become a star -- a lot of young girls get eaten up that way.</strong> <br />
<strong>Rebelling against her father, she wouldn't realize she was</strong> <br />
<strong>mostly destroying her own life with parties, drugs, promiscuity,</strong> <br />
<strong>and so forth. She had overcome it, put it behind her. What if it</strong> <br />
<strong>came back to haunt her?</strong> <br />
<strong>     What if Fox had borne an illegitimate child and given</strong> <br />
<strong>him up for adoption, never to think about him again? And what</strong> <br />
<strong>if he somehow tracked her down? (the issue of rights, child vs.</strong> <br />
<strong>parent, is something that interests me; I have pet peeves about</strong> <br />
<strong>courts deciding abused children should go back with their</strong> <br />
<strong>parents solely because of the biological bond; that case with</strong> <br />
<strong>the little girls switched at birth, etc., so I also follow stories</strong> <br />
<strong>about adoptees and whether their right to know outweighs their</strong> <br />
<strong>mother's right to privacy).</strong> <br />
<strong>     But Fox might be hard to track down. She would have</strong> <br />
<strong>been under an alias anyway. I was mulling all of this over for</strong> <br />
<strong>several weeks, wanting to upset the Aerie Building applecart</strong> <br />
<strong>with the introduction of a twenty-something guy who'd grown</strong> <br />
<strong>up under drastically different circumstances.</strong> <br />
<strong>     So I'm watching Pop-Up Video on VH-1, thinking about</strong> <br />
<strong>what sort of circumstances I wanted for this kid and how I was</strong> <br />
<strong>going to get him in touch with his mom. Then the video for</strong> <br />
<strong>"Gimme All Your Lovin'" comes on ...</strong> <br />
<strong>     Here's a kid, growing up in a dying desert town (that</strong> <br />
<strong>video was filmed in Lancaster, my home town &lt;g&gt;), and along</strong> <br />
<strong>comes this amazing car and out hop three hot babes.</strong> <br />
<strong>     It was like getting whacked between the eyes. The</strong> <br />
<strong>Weird Sisters as the ZZ Topp Eliminator girls! Because, of</strong> <br />
<strong>course, Fox's son would be just as much fey as Alexander, so</strong> <br />
<strong>Titania might have an interest.</strong> <br />
<strong>     Then things got even more bizarre. Is it just me, or does</strong> <br />
<strong><em>everyone</em> portray Oberon as a buffoon and Titania as the</strong> <br />
<strong>true power behind the throne? I wanted to make him <em>do</em></strong> <br />
<strong>something for a change. So he concocted his whole elaborate</strong> <br />
<strong>scheme just so he could give Titania her grandson.</strong> <br />
<strong>     T.J. turned out to be a neat character. His electro-kinetic</strong> <br />
<strong>powers, which work only on metal and electronics, have</strong> <br />
<strong>proven to be quite useful. And his attitude, about striving so</strong> <br />
<strong>hard to be a normal person despite all the evidence to the</strong> <br />
<strong>contrary, combined with his bitter-yet-touching reactions to Fox</strong> <br />
<strong>and Alex, make him appealing.</strong> <br />
<strong>     One thing I like best about this story was Xanatos'</strong> <br />
<strong>discovery of Lydia's true identity of Titania. It put him in a</strong> <br />
<strong>situation where there was no one good choice, which is</strong> <br />
<strong>something I love to do in my RPGs because I believe that most</strong> <br />
<strong>of the time, that's how it is. Eventually, though, Fox is liable to</strong> <br />
<strong>find out, and then it's going to hit the fan big time.</strong> <br />
<strong>     Title: Fox's design company is FoxFire Fashions, so</strong> <br />
<strong>when I wanted a story to focus on her, it seemed like the right</strong> <br />
<strong>choice.</strong> <br />
<strong>     Memorable lines: "Calling a woman plump might have been flattering a few hundred years ago, but ..." and "Next time I ask you to do a background check, would you please be <em>thorough</em>?"</strong> <br />
 </p>
<p><strong>40. Lyre, Lyre</strong> <br />
<strong>     It took me so long to write this one that I was beginning</strong> <br />
<strong>to think I'd never get around to it. I'd first come up with the title</strong> <br />
<strong><em>way</em> back when Orpheus Bluestone debuted in Baby Makes</strong> <br />
<strong>Three, but I'd been putting it off because I didn't want to write</strong> <br />
<strong>myself into any corners.</strong> <br />
<strong>     That's the thing about setting stories far ahead in a</strong> <br />
<strong>timeline. If I later come up with a brilliant idea, but have already</strong> <br />
<strong>made it impossible, I want to pull my hair out in clumps. Future</strong> <br />
<strong>Imperfect was only a <em>possible</em> future, so that was okay, but</strong> <br />
<strong>this was a lot more risky. I didn't want to give too much away,</strong> <br />
<strong>and I didn't want to state something as history that I might then</strong> <br />
<strong>need to change.</strong> <br />
<strong>     Remember at the Gathering when Greg was talking</strong> <br />
<strong>about the Archmage? What a great character he turned out to</strong> <br />
<strong>be, what a nifty idea Greg had for him later ... but he was dead?</strong> <br />
<strong>Getting out of that required some fancy footwork on Greg's part,</strong> <br />
<strong>involving the Phoenix Gate (and sometimes I think I'm the only</strong> <br />
<strong>one in the world who grasps the concept of that sort of closed-</strong> <br />
<strong>loop time travel without having a brain meltdown).</strong> <br />
<strong>     I wanted to avoid, if possible, setting myself up for that</strong> <br />
<strong>kind of trouble. Yet I also wanted to drop some tantalizing hints</strong> <br />
<strong>-- Amber, for instance, who appears in daylight and is Orph's ex-</strong> <br />
<strong>girlfriend, and yet it isn't for several more stories that I reveal</strong> <br />
<strong>that Amber is Goliath and Elisa's daughter.</strong> <br />
<strong>     I also held back because I knew I had to kill Matt</strong> <br />
<strong>Bluestone, and it has been my experience that killing <em>my</em></strong> <br />
<strong>characters is one thing; killing <em>Greg's</em> can get me neck-deep</strong> <br />
<strong>in all sorts of hot water.</strong> <br />
<strong>     But I did it, and then as I was doing it, I realized that</strong> <br />
<strong>Eurydice was going to go with him. I really like that scene.</strong> <br />
<strong>     Then something else occurred to me as I was doing</strong> <br />
<strong>some math. Jon Canmore's kid was due now too, which let me</strong> <br />
<strong>make a lifelong link between Bryce and Orpheus.</strong> <br />
<strong>     Orph's power of suggestion also let me do something</strong> <br />
<strong>else that needed doing. I'd been really getting worried about the</strong> <br />
<strong>Demona situation. Before 2031 when she and MacBeth were</strong> <br />
<strong>fated to get together, she needed to have a change of heart. But</strong> <br />
<strong>lately in my stories, she'd become really twisted. I had to have</strong> <br />
<strong>some catalyst for her change. Orph saved my bacon, lemme tell</strong> <br />
<strong>you!</strong> <br />
<strong>     Title: I never even saw the Jim Carrey movie; I don't</strong> <br />
<strong>much like anything he's done since Once Bitten. But it was just</strong> <br />
<strong>right for a story about the guy who inherits the Lyre of</strong> <br />
<strong>Orpheus.</strong> <br />
<strong>     Memorable lines: "There will always be a Guardian."</strong> <br />
 </p>
