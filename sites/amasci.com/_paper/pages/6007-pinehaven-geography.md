We've had great fun exploring and naming features.

The <u>fried eggs</u> (Roger's name, from the topographic contours) are neat little hills overlooking [Homestead Way](images/homestead-way.jpg) (the old jeep track that connects the access road to an old homestead site from the early part of this century. <u>Ball Point</u> overlooks Homestead Way from the the other side.

[The Troika](images/troika.jpg) is a great example of glacial erratics in the middle of the <u>Parklands</u>. Closeby is the [<u>Lost World Plateau</u>](images/lost-world.jpg), an area where the bedrock reaches the surface, and nothing much grows.

[Eeyore's Corner](images/eeyore.jpg) is indeed a dark and gloomy place, at the extreme northwest of Pinehaven, right underneath <u>Antoine Point</u>, which has expansive views out to the Pasayten wilderness and Loomis Forest to the <u>West</u>, Hailey Mountain to the North, Mt Bonaparte, Mt Phoebe and the Okanogan Forest to the <u>East</u>, and Tunk Mountain to the South.

The <u>Western Meadow</u> is at the edge, bordering State land, containing the seasonal outflow from the <u>wetland,</u>which descends further to the <u>Springbox</u>, a deliciously cool place in the August heat.

[<u>The Wetlands</u>](images/wetlands.jpg) contain a veritable cornucopia of frogs and other acquatic life. In the spring, you'd never get any sleep around here. Directly above the wetlands is [<u>Lake Arvia</u>](images/boating-pc.jpg) - one of our favourite places, refreshing in Summer, not least of which because the hoards of Dragonflies eat all the bugs!

[<u>Broken Foot Gluch</u>](images/broken-foot.jpg) is a small ravine named in honour of our friend Alina, who bravely scrambled down its contours on our first work party, and leads to <u>the Glade</u> might easily have been the place Robin Hood met his merry men to plot the Sheriff's downfall.

The <u>Ravine</u> is the south-eastern portion, leading down to <u>Antoine Creek</u> and contains the lowest elevation of Pinehaven.
