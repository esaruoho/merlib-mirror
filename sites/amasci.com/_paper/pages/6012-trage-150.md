**MESSAGE FROM THE CHAIRMAN**

At about midnight, while on this past May's McClellan Butte mission to extricate an injured climber and his deceased partner, the cool night air and light mist falling made adding a warm layer seem like a good idea. Fortunately, I said to myself, I have my very thick REI Bi-Polar Fleece Jacket right here in my pack. Not there? Hmm. Well, maybe I stuffed it in *here* . No? Yikes. Well, I must have put it *here* . Or *here* . Or ... well, hell. Just where IS that damn thing?!?

You get the picture. It wasn't in my pack; it was hanging on my clothes rack in the garage. Fortu nately, it didn't get all that cold during the night, and I did have a heavy Patagonia underwear top in my pack, which ensured I didn't have to plead with Brian Williams to hold me tight and keep me warm. Brr-rrr, indeed.

Still, that incident underscores a point about which I feel very strongly: We must ensure that our personal field equipment is up to the task at hand. Of course, personal gear is just that - personal. Everyone will have their own interpretation on what they need in a particular situation. But four things, I believe, are givens.

Personal gear: My seven years in SMR has taught me that you have to prepare for the worst. Even an innocuous-sounding carry-out can become an all-night ordeal, or an overnight bivouac. So we gotta take more than we think we need, with food and clothing adequate for 24 hours. That doesn't mean we have to pack like we're on a week-long trip, as those poor ESAR kids do. But be sure you've got PLENTY of extra clothing, extra food in the form of energy bars or other long-lasting snacks, and niceties such as a small foam pad or stove. Keep in mind that *we may be asked to provide for others*. Often the victims of an accident need more clothing, or are hungry, or need padding in a litter, or need to have an IV drip from Geoff Ferguson warmed over a stove. So pack accordingly. Don't throw on a light pack because "It has what I need. Think of what someone else might need, too.

Helmets: Gotta have, period. OK, OK, there might be the one-mile Mount Si carry-out that doesn't require it. But helicopters are apt to show at any time nowadays, so we MUST assume one will be involved in virtually every mission. No, a Petzl helmet will not do much to stop a Blackhawk blade. But wearing one creates a more serious atmosphere, which I believe is beneficial. And a helmet will surely protect you from flying rocks, limbs and swinging jungle penetrators. With that helmet, pack eye protection. Blackhawks toss up prodigious amounts of dirt and debris. It's impossible to work under one without full, ski-goggle type eyewear.

Footwear: Think about it for a minute. What's our most common mission? Sprained ankle on Si or Granite or McClellan. And in a lot that I have seen, inadequate footwear may well have been a contributing factor. I believe above-the-ankle, all-leather boots with Vibram soles should be a minimum requirement for field use. Forget fabric-and-leather trail runners. They're useless.

Mount Rainier: A special case. We aren't that active down there, but two missions in five days recently shows that it's still on our radar screen. On one of those missions we had problems when a field team at Schurmann was not provided with the food and shelter it had been led to believe was available. I wasn't surprised to hear this; I've run into the same problems on Rainier on several occasions. Bottom line: We cannot count on the park for anything. If a field team leaves the trailhead, it MUST be prepared to operate as a fully self-contained unit. Tents, stoves, ropes - the whole nine yards. I know, that adds 10 pounds per pack. So be it.

Keep in mind, this is just the Chair making noise. So do with this as you see fit. But think as well about our role in the field and our function as a role *model* for other rescue units as well as everyday hikers and climbers. Then, stick that extra headlamp and jacket in the pack.

Thanks, Doug Gantenbein, SMR Chairman

**MRA 40-th Anniversary Meeting, Mt. Hood**

The 40th anniversary meeting of MRA was held at Timberline Lodge at Mt. Hood on June 19, 20, and 21. SMR members in attendance included Brian Williams, Paul Williams, Jim Baker, Doug Hutton, Chris Madden, Bill Davis, and Bill Lahr. Paul Williams received an award as one of the "Founding Fathers" of MRA. The program included ski mountaineering training, crevasse rescue and glacier travel, and organized climbs of Mt. Hood.

The 40th anniversary meeting, hosted by Portland Mountain Rescue, convened at Mt. Hood's Timberline Lodge, the site of the originalorganizational meeting of MRA. There were 200 plus folks in attendance with the following people from SMR: Paul and Pat Williams, Brian and Brita and Conner and Trevor Williams, Doug and Sue Hutton, Mike Norman and wife, Chris Rommel, Bill and Dorothy Lahr, Bill Davis, Chris Madden and Jim and Char Baker. Registration began on Thursday for those folks arriving early, but activities really got underway on Friday with ski mountaineering, glacier training, and GPS classes and people scattered all over the south side of Mt. Hood. That evening, following a buffet dinner, Dr. Cameron Bangs, a self professed expert on hypothermia and frostbite (and deservedly so) did a very informative talk on those subjects.

Saturday featured climbs of the mountain with the effort being shortened by snow cat rides to the top of the Palmer lift (8,500 feet) for those folks wanting to start at 3,4,5, or 6 AM. Brian Williams was one of the very early risers, taking the 3 AM cat. For the saner folks who wanted to start at a sensible hour (a group that included Chris, Doug and Jim) the lifts began operating at 7 AM. All SMR folks summited except Jim who "wimped out" at the hogback. At 2 that afternoon Dr. Bangs conducted a review of the tragic O.E.S. accident (Oregon Episcopal School) of particu lar interest since three of the SMR members in attendance participated in that mission. That.night featured a banquet, introductions, awards, roasts and speeches. The highlight of the awards was the recognition of past MRA presidents present which included SMR's Paul Williams, eighth president of MRA, who incidently, had participated in the formation of MRA 40 years ago. Dick Pooley, Crag Rats member who was the MRA's first president, provided a history of its begin nings including view foils of some of the documentation signed for SMR by Ome Daiber. Follow ing was the key note speaker, Clair Israelson, a ranger from Banff with 26 years experience in SAR. He gave a spell binding slide show which featured a dramatic multi-day rescue on Mt. Robson, and he also touched on the human side of our activity particularly concerning relations between SAR people and subjects and their relatives.

On Sunday the business meeting was conducted by outgoing president, Tim Cochrane. Newly elected officers include: President - Tim Kovacs, Maricopa County - Central Arizona MRA Vice President - Rocky Henderson, Portland Mountain Rescue Member at Large - Rod Knopp, Idaho Mountain Search and Rescue.

Chris Madden, SMR's voting representative, was accepted for the position of "MRA Clearing house Chair", a big job involving the organization of research papers and other mountain rescue information and publications for access by MRA and other SAR units. (He will inherit 3 file cabinets - is that why you bought a bigger house Chris?)

Beautiful lodge, great meeting, good food, nice folks, perfect weather!

**GEORGE SAINSBURY RECEIVES AAC ANGELO HEILPRIN AWARD**

SMR's Honorary Member George Sainsbury received the American Alpine Club's *Angelo Heilprin* *Citation* at the AAC Annual Meeting held in Bellevue, Washington on December 6, 1997. The Citation for the award is as follows:

 

For his pioneering work in starting the Mountain Rescue Association in the United States; his efforts to coordinate mountain safety education and rescue under the auspices of the American Alpine Club; his decades of personal commitment to engaging - at much cost to himself - in numerous mountain rescues his hardened but wonderful macabre and well earned sense of humor; and his untiring and relentless pursuit of information and data for inclusion in the annual report "Accidents in North American Mountaineering;'' this year's citation is presented in the name of our founder, Angelo Heilprin, to **George Ross Sainsbury** December 6, 1997 Bellevue, Washington

 

Editor's Note: the following quote from the AAC Membership gives some background for the Angelo Heilprin Citation:

"It was through the dedicated efforts of Angelo Heilprin that The American Alpine Club was founded. In May 1901, he and 12 others met in Philadelphia to draft the objects of the proposed association. In March 1902, the AAC was formally organized; Angelo Heilprin was elected the first Vice President of the Club.

The Angelo Heilprin Citation was established by The American Alpine Club in 1976. It is awarded annually to that person who has, in the opinion of the citation committee, shown exem plary service to the club. It is specifically understood that service to the club itself can be differen tiated from outstanding mountaineering accomplishment or service through the club to the climb ing community. The purpose of this citation is to recognize those who have worked to maintain and strengthen the organization and thus further its ability to serve its fundamental purposes.''

Shortly after beginning this process all field personnel were paged and instructed to return to the search base. At search base they were informed that the subject has been located by construction worders at a work site located at the Orillia Road underpass beneath the I-5 freeway.

**97-33 - Mt. Rainier, Cowlitz Glacier, Search-Recovery, October 25-26, 1997**

SMR was paged by King County for a possible mission for a missing climber on Mt. Rainier at 1600 on October 25. The originally asked for 8 to 10 climbers to search for a missing climber somewhere above Gibraltar Rock. This was later amended to 4 climbers to fly and observe as the avalanche hazard was judged too high to put people in the field.

The SMR team met at Longmire and was briefed. The subject, Nicholas Giromini, and his partner, Mark Moyer, were descending and had a route disagreement at about 12,500' at 1530 above Gibraltar Rock. The partner went back over to the Disappointment Cleaver route and descended to Camp Muir and when he did not see his partner, he used the cell phone of another party to call 911. The reporting parting party returned to Paradise on Sunday morning, October 27.

The park had two rangers climb to Camp Muir on Sunday as well as a team from Tacoma. The Tacoma team was turned back by weather at about 7500'. The rangers made Muir in horrible conditions. at 0815 on October 28 the Seattle team was driven to Gray field to fly in a Chinook and search the mountain. At 1200, the SMR team was dropped at Camp Muir to search while the Chinook returned to Gray Field. They started to search above Camp Muir toward the beehive. At 1300 the rangers in the filed found a harness below Camp Comfort, and followed below thid down the fall line to the subject who was located about 600' below. The subject was deceased and found face down in the snow at 1400. The subject and all team members were transported by the 206th to Kautz Creek, and they then returned to Longmire by Park vehicles by 1645. The subject had no pack, no parka, and fingerless gloves. He apparently made it through the Gibraltar ledges and probably fell after he exited the ledges. He was not wearing crampons.

Seattle MR participants: Mike Maude, In-Town OL; Chris Madden, Base OL and Truck Driver, John Petroske, Field OL; Al Errington, Gordon Smith, Brent Hostetler, Field. Seattle MR personnel, 6; Man-hours, 85.25; Miles, 472.

**98-01 - Table Mountain, Search/Recovery, January 18, 1998**

A snowmobiler, Craig Rodgers, 38, Lake Stevens, was high marking a 30-35 degree east -facing slope at about 6000 feet on Sunday afternoon when his machine got stuck at the high mark. While trying to pull his machine around, the slope about 100 feet above him fractured to the ground, subsequently carrying him and his machine about 400-500 feet down and burying him under 2-3 feet of snow. Nearby snowmobilers used trail marker wands to probe for the victim, but failed to locate him. Avalanche control of adjacent slopes to protect rescuers Monday morning produced further large climax slides. Organized rescue efforts Monday afternoon found the victim through coarse probing. The victim was located about 60 feet below and 20 feet across the slope from his machine.

The victim was not wearing a transceiver, and snowmobilers who responded to the acci dent did not have probes or shovels. The weather at the time of the accident appears to have been overcast with some light snow.

Seattle MR participants: Bill Davis, In-Town OL; Geoff Ferguson, Brent Hostetler, Field. Seattle MR personnel, 2; Man-hours, ??; Miles, ??.

**98-6 - McClellan's Butte, Rescue/Recovery, May 2-3, 1998**

On the afternoon of May 2 at about 1530, the two subjects, Subbarao Vedula, 29, and Bill Nagle, 28, were descending with their partner (Andrew Pickering, 24), after having summitted on McClellan's Butte. This climb was reportedly a training outing preparatory to climbing Mt. Rainier. They opted to descend their ascent route directly east down the mountain's south ridge into what is normally called the "second gully", a very steep snow chute with poor run outs. This general area has been the site of several accidents in the past.

When the party was still well above the point where the gully intersects the trail, Subba slipped on the steep snow, lost control, and proceeded to bounce over exposed rocks sustaining significant injuries to his head and torso. In the attempt to reach Subba, Bill also slipped, fell about 200 feet downslope, and came to rest after hitting brush and trees. Both subjects came to rest at about the 4300 foot elevation only a few yards apart. Andrew reached his injured partners, reportedly administered what first aid he could, and went for help.

SMR was paged by KCP Saturday afternoon at 1710. There was a good turnout with a total of 22 SMR personnel involved. Weather was not good, with fog up high and light drizzle and rain during most of the mission. Snow level was a bit above 3,000 feet. When the truck arrived at base at 1845, Greg and Fergie (SMR Team \#1) were already flying in a MAST Blackhawk with the hope of being inserted as close as possible to the subjects. Shortly after 1900 they were off-loaded by jungle penetrator at approximately 2600 feeet elevation somewhere above Alice Creek. This location required a hard bushwhacking, climbing traverse to reach the subjects.

As other SMR personnel arrived, they were quickly dispatched into the field. A ride by 4x4 shortened the hike in by about a mile and 500 vertical feet. They were all dropped off where the trail crosses the old logging road on the east side of the mountain at 2220 feet.

Team 2 (Caley, Gantenbein, Phillips, Olcott, and Farash) left base at 1905 accompanied by Andrew, the reporting party. Team 3 ( Madden, Wick, Ricker, and Ellsworth) left at 2000.

Rusho and Stolnack (Team 4) left at 2120. Team 5 (Williams, Garth and Kecia Bruce) were dispatched at 2205. With help from ESAR and SPART the following gear was carried into the field: 2 litters, 2 low stretch ropes, 1 climbing rope, and 2 rigging kits.

The teams experienced a fair amount of difficulty approaching the subjects because of the dangerous terrain. This problem was not helped by the rain and darkness. First on the scene was Team \#1, arriving at the subjects at 2100. They began administering first aid and at 2130 the KCP were advised that Subba had died as a result of his injuries. Bill Nagle sustained a ruptured left quadriceps, a right radius fracture, and multiple deep lacerations and contusions.

The next 2.5 hours were occupied by getting the personnel and gear to the subject's site and setting up to transport Bill. The real work of transporting Bill began at 0100; this involved a series of 5 lowers and 2 raises with multiple knot passes plus a tracking line traverse. At 0550, word came that the Balckhawk might attempt to fly. Because of the poor weather the teams kept moving the litter toward base. The MAST Blackhawk flew over base at 0700 in marginal weather just barely above the tree tops. It worked its way upslope, arriving above the teams just as they reached an open area in the 1st gully at about 3700' elevation. A crewman and MAST litter were lowered. The patient did not have to be repackaged because their litter would accomodate the SMR litter. Bill was hoisted on board and on his way to Harborview Medical Center at 0740.

Just before 0600, nine Tacoma Mountain Rescue personnel arrived with the aim of either helping transport Bill or, if MAST could evacuate him, they would go on up and recover Subba. Since MAST succeeded, Tacoma proceeded on up SMR Team 6 (Eades, Nolz, and SPART member Cynthia Hickey) left base at 0700 to join Tacoma. Deb Wolf arrived just after 0700 to take over as truck driver and base OL. Tacoma was able to quickly lower Subba. Team 6 assisted in completing the evacuation with help from ESAR teams.

The overnight teams were out of the field and left base by 1000; Team 6 was back at base at 1500.

Seattle MR participants: Mike Maude and Al Errington, In-Town OLs; Jim Baker and Deb Wolf, Truck and Base OL's; Greg Prothman, Field OL; Garth Bruce, Kecia Bruce, Doug Caley, Glenn Eades, Al Ellsworth, Art Farash, Geoff Ferguson, Doug Gantenbein, Chris Madden, Ron Nolz, Gayle Olcott, Tom Phillips, Bob Ricker, Dave Rusho, Scott Stolnack, John Wick, Brian Williams, Field. Seattle MR personnel, 22; Man-hours, 308; Miles, 1510.

 

**98-07 - Mill Creek, Stevens Pass, Search/Rescue, May 27, 1998**

SMR was paged on May 26 to assist Chelan County in a search in the Union Gap area north of Mill Creek for three men, ages 19-21, who were missing after a hike on Saturday and Sunday, May 23-24. The subjects, all from Ephrata, had a tent, sleeping bags, stoves; two of them had hiked in on Saturday, and were joined by a third on Sunday. They were due to return on Monday. A limited search by family and friends was carried out on Tuesday with new snow and bad weather. A full search began on Wednesday by Chelan SAR units augmented by Snohomish SAR and SMR.

The SMR teams and truck arrived at the Search Base on Highway 2 at the Smith Brook Road between 0600 and 0800. SMR entered the field at 0840 with a large group which was directed to search towards Union Gap, then from Union Gap along the Pacific Crest Trail to lake Valhalla, and from there down to the Yodelin road. The SMR team carried out this search plan, arriving at Highway 2 at 1500 without any sighting of the subjects. At 1505, Chelan County Mountain Rescue, apparently directed from the air by the Snohomish County SAR helicopter which had managed to fly over the pass and into the search area in spite of the poor weather and limited visibility, reported finding tracks of 3 persons, and a dog descending into the Rapid River

drainage west of Union Gap. At 1630, the campsite occupied by the missing subjects was found in the Rapid River drainage near Little Pete Lake Creek at 3200', and all subjects were OK. The subjects were walked up the drainage to Union Gap, and from there out to Smith Brook Road to search base, arriving there at 2030. All SMR personnel, except for Ferguson who had joined a SPART team with the subjects, returned home around 1630 with the truck remaining at search base until 2130.

Conditions all day were wet and overcast, with wet soft snow on the trails. Subjects stated that they became disoriented on Monday due to fog and snow. They were unable to find their track due to new snow and wandered around getting wet and hungry before deciding to make camp where they were found Wednesday. The subjects seemed to know the area, but appar ently had no maps or compasses.

Seattle MR participants: Tracy Petroske, In-Town OL; Art Farash and Deb Riehl, Base OL's; Art Farash, Truck; Brian Williams, Field OL; Jim Baker, Glenn Eades, Geoff Ferguson, Doug Gantenbein, Allen Gerber, John Petroske, Dave Rusho, Field. Seattle MR personnel, 11; Man-hours, 247; Miles, 2460.

 

**98-08 - Dingford Creek, Rescue, May 30, 1998**

At 1509 KCP paged SMR to assist in the rescue of a hiker with a broken leg "on the Snow Lake Trail". SMR was instructed to proceed to Alpental. After a few phone calls, it was deter mined that the location was actually up the Middle Fork of the Snoqulamie River heading for an infrequently used trail to Snow Lake. Quick pager action by In-Town OL Doug Gantenbein in concert with Art Farash and Tom Morgan (Forest Service) diverted the SMR responders to the correct location.

John Wick and Art Farash arrived with the SMR truck at the trailhead/basecamp at 1720. Wick would be Truck and Base OL. Officer Z-Big' arrived shortly after that and took charge of the rescue. At 1745 SMR Team 1, Art Farash, Geoff Ferguson, Dave Rusho, and Brian Williams, left the trailhead accompanied by an ESAR team carrying an ESAR litter and SMR technical gear. Alan Gerber left the search base at 1811 with another ESAR team.

The evening is warm with heavy cloud cover and very light breezes. The subject, a male in his 60's, is reported to be approximately 2 miles up the trail. SMR Team 1 reached the subject at 1818. The subject has a compound fracture of the lower left leg. He had been crossing a fast running stream and slipped when exiting the stream. Ferguson treated the subject while the other SMR team members set up a highline.

The subject was packaged in the litter, the highline was tensioned with a 3 to 1, and the litter raised on the highline to clear the stream. The subject was easily (and dryly) transported across the stream and then, when the highline was slackened, was lowered to the trail at 1907. All in all, an excellent and appropriate use of a technical system in a non-vertical problem. The subject was then transported via wheeled litter to the trailhead/basecamp, arriving there at 2104. ESAR provided lots of personnel for this. The subject was then transferrred to 4 X 4 for the 6 miles of rough road to a waiting ambulance.

During the debriefing (2135) by the incident command, Randy Houser, it was discovered that a SPART member was missing. It was decided that the missing member had probably walked past the turn off to the trailhead/basecamp and was heading down the Middle Fork Trail. An ESAR team was sent by 4 X 4 to the next trail junction (about 6 miles down stream), with the plan to hike back upstream and intercept the missing SPART member. Another team of SPART members was sent from the trailhead/basecamp to search the trail going down stream from the junction at 2155. Wick took over SPART Base. (All SPART were then in the field.) At 2202 the SPART team leaving the trailhead/basecamp met the missing SPART member who was back tracking. The mission was concluded at 2210.

Seattle MR participants: Doug Gantenbein, In-Town OL; John Wick, Truck and Base; Brian Williams, Field OL; Art Farash, Geoff Ferguson, Alan Gerber, Dave Rusho, Field. Man-hours : 49. Miles : 700.

**Robert K. Byhre**

**1926 - 1998**

Bob Byhre became a climber when he was still in his teens. He enlisted in the Navy in World War II and served as a radio operator and a teacher of survival classes.

After World War II he spent some time in Alaska adventuring and climbing, and later en rolled in mechanical engineering at the University of Washington. He became a member of MRC in 1952 and served as Vice Chair and Secretary respectively. With his engineering background he was mostly interested in safety and equipment. He developed power for the cable winch by using a chain-saw motor, as well as a ladder assembled from packboards.

Bob was involved in almost all rescues that took place between 1952 and 1963. One commendation came from the Air Force in 1962 when an Air Force airplane crashed on Mt. Constance and Bob and other MRC members climbed to the accident site to take pictures and to identify the airplane parts. He mentioned this as one of the more difficult climbs because of very poor weather and very rotten rock. Other rescues involved saving climbers as well as removing bodies on Mt. Rainier.

In 1963 Bob suffered a bad fall on Big Four, but despite serious injuries he was back in the mountains a year later. In March of 1966 he was leader of a party to climb Mt. Rainier in winter. (Prior to that date climbing Rainier between September and May was forbidden), and in 1966 he also became an instructor of the University of Washington climbing course, both summer and winter sessions.

The "highest mountain rescue ever attempted" took place in May 1960 on Mt. McKinley. The Alaska Rescue Group had a cooperative agreement with MRC. As the call for help came to Seattle, Bob was the in-town coordinator, in charge of finding equipment for a total of 30 MRC members who flew to Alaska just a few hours after the call was received.

Bob became the subject of an advertising campaign for Camel cigarettes, and gave all the proceeds to MRC, which helped MRC to acquire their first truck.

After the late 60's Bob was no longer able to physically be involved with rescues, but his love for the oudoors remained. In the 1970's he took up kayaking and diving until a bad back forced him to give up these activities. He passed away on May 5, 1998 after a long illness.

Bob is survived by his wife, Helga. Contributions in his memory can be made to Seattle Mountain Rescue.
