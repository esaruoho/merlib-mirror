<td colspan="2" data-valign="TOP" width="80%" data-bgcolor="#ffffff"><p><strong>Busy day.</strong></p>
<p>I've actually been working today. Calling around looking for somewhere to do some testing.</p>
<p><em>Hello my name is Julie and I'm with Blah-blah-blah. We do psychological and educational assessments and we're currently doing a neuropsychological assessment study in the area. We're looking for a site where test subjects can meet with researchers...</em></p>
<p>Over and over.</p>
<p>I also had to make a run to the grocery store for yet more cat food. Woody goes through a huge amount of food daily, and - of course - he's picky about what he'll eat. If there's not enough gravy, get ready for the yowling. He reminds me of a skinny, bony, little old man... rattling his silverware on the table and screwing his face up into a yell until he gets his way.</p>
<p>A friend online pointed me toward Stephen King's website today, and I went browsing over there. I found the page where the details of the <a href="http://www.stephenking.com/OnWriting.html"><em>On Writing</em> exercise</a> are... there are links to "winning" entries, as well. Interesting reading.</p>
<p>I finished up editing Chuck's story <em>Daedalus</em> last night - it's always a pleasure to read his work! It's even more fun to listen to him reading his own stories, his voice full of humor and so perfect portraying the characters. He's even adept at using semi-colons outside of dialogue in a way that doesn't bug me too much. ;)</p>
<p>Now I just need to make a copy of the edits so that both he and Gene will get one...</p>
<p>And THAT makes me want to go back to OfficeMax and get that printer/fax/scanner/copier I was looking at. There are a few things I miss about working in the office (the friends I'd had there, mostly), but the one I notice the most often, as shallow as it might seem, is the lack of access to a copier. Now that I'm working again, even on a part-time basis, not having a copier nearby keeps tripping me up during paperwork.</p>
<p>Completely unrelated, but the above brought it to mind... before I was laid off last year, some people I counted amongst my friends were folks from work. I considered them my friends. Then, after the layoff, they all... well, just disappeared. I know - kind of - where most of them are and what they're doing, but none of them have made any effort to keep in touch. (Excepting Gene, of course, but since we were well acquainted long before I started working there, his case is different.) Not a one has called me up to say hi. Not even the co-worker who I drove home the night he and his wife got smashed at the Pinkslip Party; the same guy who tossed his cookies in my car. One who, over three plus years at the company, I considered myself close to.</p>
<p>It'll give me a different perspective, should I ever work in an office again. It'll be easier to remember that those people and I are brought together through artificial circumstances... they're not really that interested in my life. Any conversations that indicate otherwise are probably just a way to pass the time, and shouldn't be taken very seriously. *shrug* I can't guarantee I won't be interested in their lives - that's just the way I am.</p>
<p>(And, if by remote chance should one of those past co-workers read this... well, hello there!  Drop me a line, eh?)</p>
