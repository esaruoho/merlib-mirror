&#10;<h1 id="story-problems">Story Problems:</h1>
<h2 id="be-sure-to-show-all-your-work">Be sure to show all your work!</h2>
<p>1. In the <em>Channel One</em> contract, students must watch twelve minutes a day.<br />
1a. How many school days/year does this add up to equal?</p>
<p><strong>6 days/year</strong></p>
<p>1b. If students are required to watch 2 minutes of commercials during this twelve minutes/ day, how many school days is devoted to commercials?</p>
<p><strong>1 day/year</strong>. "The total cost to the taxpayer, for the advertising time alone, exceeds the total value of the equipment Whittle leases the school (when figured over the course of a three-year contract.)" --Gloria Blackwell, President of the California State PTA (p23)</p>
<p>2. Over the next three years, the Seattle School District faces a $35 million budget reduction. The chief financial officer of the Seattle School District estimates that the policy will bring in $1 million a year (we are giving him the benefit of the doubt. Colorado Springs has a similar policy and only brought in $73,150 the first two years and $85,000 their third year.). How much money, in units of millions, would the district still be lacking?</p>
<p><strong>$32,000,000 over the next three years.</strong></p>
<p>3. School district 11 in Colorado Springs, Colorado has 33,000 students. Under their advertising policy adopted in 1994, they will bring in $121,808 for the 1996/1997 school year. After paying the new advertising agency they hired to bring in more money, they will have $85,265. How much money is left per student?</p>
<strong>$2.60</strong> "It's a drop in the bucket...It allows us--this doesn't look big--to buy one more violin or something for a school." --Tracy Cooper, Spokeswoman for District 11 and manager of the ad program.<br />
