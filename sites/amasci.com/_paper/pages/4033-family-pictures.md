<td data-valign="top"><p><span lang="en-us">Hey Mom!  <img src="images/SM_15.GIF" data-border="0" width="39" height="39" /></span></p>
<p><span lang="en-us">You wanted to see what the house looks like, so I've put together this outdoor tour.  Let's begin with <a href="1-Approaching.html">driving up the driveway</a> where you will see our fence on the right, including the section Justin and I repaired this summer, a bit of our house over the fence, and the neighbor's car at the end.  When we look out our front window we see the bamboo that is off to the left.</span></p>
<p><span lang="en-us">From the long, common, drive you can walk up our <a href="2-FrontWalk.html">front walk</a>.  In the window you will see our 14 year old cat, <a href="3-Front%20window%20-%20Alice.html">Alice</a>, asleep on her blanket.  The red spot is a leaf from the plum tree, not to worry.</span></p>
<p><span lang="en-us">Back to the driveway, if we continue down and look <a href="4-Just%20past%20house-looking%20back.htm">back</a>, we see some of the juniper, pine and here-to-stay ivy.  Wait, is that Alice in the window?  WHAT a surprise!</span></p>
<p><span lang="en-us">"BUT," you say, "these are all fall pictures, what does it look like in the winter?"  Aha!  This one time I am ready for your question!   Looking <a href="4-Just%20past%20house-looking%20back1.htm">back</a>, last winter.</span></p>
<p><span lang="en-us">Whoops, couldn't stop turning, here's the <a href="5-Looking%20back%20at%20driveway.htm">driveway</a>, the neighbor's fence, and their leafy bamboo, too.  Oops, now I'm back on track, <a href="6-Neighbors%20driveway-looking%20back.htm">here's</a> our actual driveway as seen from our neighbor's driveway, the Odyssey, a remarkably clean garage (thanks to my graphics editing program!), and the front of the house, again.</span></p>
<p><span lang="en-us">Stepping past the Odyssey, here's a shot of the <a href="7-Front%20yard%20from%20side.htm">front yard</a>.  Moving to the walkway and looking off to my right you see the Japanese Maple centering the <a href="8-Front%20yard%20from%20front%20door.htm">landscaping</a>.  That's pretty much it for the front yard.  </span></p>
<p><span lang="en-us">As I go through the gate I am confronted by the <a href="9-ThroughGateIntoBackYard.htm">side yard</a>.  Covered in gravel, it serves as a staging area for fence building, yard waste collection, and Christmas tree storage from January through March.  Now what is that in the distance?  A <a href="A-LavaterraInTheFall.htm">Lavaterra</a> which has shed half its leaves!  Yet it holds forth one, solitary <a href="B-FlowerCloseUp.htm">flower</a>.</span></p>
<p><span lang="en-us">As we take our gaze off that one lonely flower and look to the right we see the expanse of the <a href="C-LookingDownTheLengthOfTheBackyard.htm">back yard</a>. What you don't see in the fall are the many bulbs planted along the length of the fence.  Well, were planted.  The squirrels come along and eat them then climb atop the picnic table for a refreshing <a href="squirrel.htm">drink</a>.</span></p>
<p><span lang="en-us">Here is a little better view of the <a href="D-BackOfHouse.htm">back of the house</a>.  When stepping out of the patio door we see the fledgling wall of <a href="E-SmallBambooCurtain.htm">thin-leaf bamboo</a>.  Notice how we <em>don't</em> see the neighbor's open garage with piles and piles of cardboard boxes, rusted storage containers, and assorted stacks of papers and magazines.  All topped off with a lovely rusted bird cage.  To gain a more complete understanding of our neighbor's decorative taste, page back to the <a href="A-LavaterraInTheFall.htm">Lavaterra</a> picture, look in the background and see the lovely blue tarp.  This is their attempt at sprucing up the place.  And, believe it or not, it is an improvement.  You see, they put in a hot tub, around which they began to frame in 4 sliding glass patio doors to protect them from the wind, yet afford them a full 360 view.  Unfortunately, it also afforded US a full view.  THANK YOU, thank you for the blue tarp.  The lovely blue tarp.  Which leads back to the Lavaterra, fast growing, up to 7 feet tall.  Why, oh why, does it have to lose its leaves in the winter?</span></p>
<p><span lang="en-us">Well, there's a whole other side yard, but I seem to have failed to take any pictures of it.  If I get home in time this week, I'll add them.</span></p>
<p><span lang="en-us">Meanwhile, how about looking at some <a href="trees.htm">trees</a>?</span></p>
<p><span lang="en-us">That pretty much wraps up a five cent tour.  Hope you enjoyed it.  While you were reading this, I was cleaning out your cupboard!</span></p>
