<td width="600" data-valign="TOP"><h1 id="luxembourgers-and-catholicism">Luxembourgers and Catholicism</h1>
<img src="images/luxline.gif" />
<p>Luxembourg was largely untouched by the Protestant Reformation of Europe - which began with Martin Luther in 1520 - and native-born Luxembourgers are predominantly Roman Catholic to this day. Catholicism plays an important part in the culture of Luxembourg and the festivals and traditions celebrated. Emigrant Luxembourgers brought their Catholic faith to America. Establishing churches in their new communities was a priority and served as one way of preserving their cultural identity.</p>
<p><img src="images/luxline.gif" /></p>
<h3 id="religious-beliefs-and-practices"><img src="images/lux.gif" data-border="0" /> Religious Beliefs and Practices</h3>
Sign of the Cross in Lëtzebuergesch<br />
Am Numm vum Papp, am Sohn, am Hellege Geescht. Amen.
<a href="http://www.igd-leo.lu/igd-leo/emigration/relig.html">Religious Life</a><br />
The <em>Institut Grand-Ducal's Section de Linguistique, de folklore et de toponymie</em> site contains a lot of wonderful information about the role religion played in the lives of emigrant Luxembourgers.
<a href="http://www.igd-leo.lu/igd-leo/emigration/priests.html">Priests and Bishops</a><br />
Many Luxembourgers became priests in America. Nicholas Gonner's 1889 book <a href="gonner.htm">Luxembourgers in the New World</a> also includes a list of American priests of Luxembourg descent. From the <em>Institut Grand-Ducal.</em>
Women Religious<br />
Many women with Luxembourg heritage also became nuns, primarily joining teaching orders. Because women took vocational names when they entered into religious life, researching nuns can be difficult if you do not know the order they belonged to. Women often had their photograph taken when they took their finals vows, for the families they were essentially leaving behind.
<p>Possible clues for identifying the order a nun belonged to can come from these photographs and the habits they are wearing or from the <a href="http://www.geocities.com/Athens/Forum/3975/order.htm">abbreviations</a> they may have used after their name, for example <em>Sister Mary Salome Ney, O.P.</em> (Order of Preachers - Dominican Sisters). A very complete list of orders and their corresponding abbreviations can be found in the indispensable <em>Official Catholic Directory</em> published by P.J. Kenedy &amp; Sons, available in many public libraries.</p>
Research Tip: In the U.S. Federal Census for the years of 1880-1920, nuns were indexed according to the Soundex code <strong>S236</strong>. Or in other words, as if their last name was "Sister". Because most, if not all, nuns had Mary as part of their religious name is it important to look for both Sister Mary Salome and Sister Salome. By 1920, many nuns were also soundexed to their surnames.
<a href="http://www.igd-leo.lu/igd-leo/emigration/kirmes.html">Kiirmes</a><br />
Originally a celebration for the feast day of a parish's patron saint. From the <em>Institut Grand-Ducal.</em>
Bretzelsonndeg <img src="images/pretzel.gif" /> Pretzel Sunday<br />
On the third Sunday of Lent, called Pretzel Sunday in Luxembourg, a boy gives a girl he likes an ornately decorated cake shaped like a pretzel. If his feelings are returned, the girl gives him a chocolate egg on Easter. In leap years, the custom is reversed.<br />

<h3 id="patron-saints"><img src="images/lux.gif" /> Patron Saints</h3>
<img src="images/ourlady.gif" /><br />
From a Luxembourg postcard, postmarked 1932.
<a href="http://www.luxcentral.com/stamps/LuxStamps1944-1949.html#Notre.Dame">Our Lady of Luxembourg</a><br />
Image of Our Lady of Luxembourg, Consoler of the Afflicted and patron saint of Luxembourg City, from a 1945 Luxembourg stamp.
<a href="http://www.igd-leo.lu/igd-leo/emigration/lady.html">Cult of Our Lady</a><br />
Octave, a pilgrimage to honor Our Lady of Luxembourg, is an important religious ceremony in Luxembourg, held each year from the third to the fifth Sunday after Easter.
<a href="http://www.igd-leo.lu/igd-leo/emigration/patron.html">Patron Saints of Luxembourg-American Communities</a><br />
The patron saints of churches established by emigrants in America reflect their Luxembourg heritage.
<a href="http://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15645a.htm">St. Willibrord</a><br />
Entry from the Catholic Encyclopedia for St. Willibrord, the patron saint of Luxembourg. A <a href="http://www.luxembourg.co.uk/dancproc.html">dancing procession</a> is held each year at <a href="http://www.ltam.lu/Echternach/">Echternach</a> in his honor. St. Patrick's Parish in Washington, DC, also has a biography of <a href="http://users.erols.com/saintpat/ss/1107.htm#will">St. Willibrord</a>.
