<h4 id="places-to-start-learning-about-website-planning-and-html">Places to Start Learning About Website Planning and HTML:</h4>
<ul>
<li><a href="http://www.december.com/present/wdsem.html">John December's Web Development Seminar</a></li>
<li><a href="http://www.ncsa.uiuc.edu/General/Internet/WWW/HTMLPrimer.html">NCSA's Beginner's Guide to HTML</a></li>
<li><a href="http://info.med.yale.edu/caim/manual/contents.html">HTML Style Guide</a></li>
<li><a href="crash_co.html">The 1994 Version of the Crash Course in HTML</a> by Eamonn Sullivan. The place it all started for me and my Notepad. ::chuckle::</li>
<li><a href="http://www.zdnet.com/pcweek/eamonn/crash_course.html">The 1996 Version of the Crash Course in HTML</a> by Eamonn Sullivan. Old information can be helpful in understanding the evolution of our art. Plus, this page is a nice starter's guide. You can throw a simple page together in minutes with no fancy software, just an ASCII text editor like Notepad, SimpleText, or PICO.</li>
<li><a href="http://www.w3-tech.com/crash/CrashCourse.html">Another Version of the Crash Course</a> at the W3-Tech School of Web Design. It has Crash Courses in HTML, Java and JavaScript.</li>
<li><a href="http://www.december.com/works/wwwu/ch33.html">The World Wide Web Unleashed -- Chapter 33: Basic Hypertext Markup Language (HTML)</a></li>
<li><a href="http://www.pan.ci.seattle.wa.us/pan/content.htm">A Practical Guide to Developing World Wide Web Content</a> Published by <a href="http://www.ci.seattle.wa.us/">Seattle's Public Access Network</a> (PAN). A very nice guide, written for content providers who wish to publish materials on the City of Seattle's PAN WWW Site. It's also a very handy overview for anyone who is thinking of developing WWW content elsewhere.</li>
</ul>
