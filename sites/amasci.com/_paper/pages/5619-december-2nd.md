The frame continues...

**February 8th, Tuesday 2003:** Finally updated the web site with all the pics including a set of pics that Fred Meyer lost for a month and a half...yes, now I am just missing one picture and need to contact the guy that has it and see if he can send it.  Nite!

**February 14th, Friday 2003:** For Valentines day, I ordered myself most of the front end suspension bits from Kanter Automotive (<http://www.kanter.com>) ...new ball joints, all new bushings, coil springs, man, lots of stuff...I need to measure the sway bar to order the new rubber for it.  I guess...that will be yet another day...cheers!

**February 24th, Monday 2003:** Long ago before I bought my restoration project it was stored by the previous owner Mike and he in turn bought that from a man in Canada who had pulled the car from a wrecking yard after a couple of years sitting there and getting rained on.  I need to confirm this history but all I had was a first and last name...so, with about 25 years behind in searching, I set out to look for a Gary, somewhere in Canada...yeah, easy you might say...uh, nope.  A few clues aside from the name, the original data-plate was missing and a British Columbia plate was rivited in place...stating a date of December 16th, 65...didn't help much but at least I know the car spent most of its time in Canada.  Enter the internet, a nice tool, especially for searching...okay, guess the odds?  I did a search on Gary and Canada and turned up about 30,000 records...okay not good.  I went through the first few, mainly because the last name was fairly unique.  I found a reference to a guy that lived in Alberta (Close to BC) and was working with Bison Preservation and Raffles...there was a phone number...would 25+ years have steered me to this?  What they heck, only cell phone minutes...I called and a gal answered, "Hello?", I said hi and asked if Gary was there (I was expecting some company for the preservation of tundra animals)...she said he'd be back tomorrow, I asked it Gary had ever owned an Edsel...she said, "Why yes?" ... my heart skipped a beat... "Was it a convertible light blue..?", No she replied, but that was a long time ago she added.  I mentioned I was restoring an old car and was trying to find its history... "Who did you buy it from?" she asked, Mike Hinsch I replied, she said, "Goodness me, they I guess that was us...Gary will be home tomorrow if you want to talk to him about it...Great I said, very very cool and what a stroke of luck, to call a number of a guy organizing raffle tickets to save Bison in Canada...sheesh!  I will call him tomorrow.

**February 25th, Tuesday 2003:** Checked with Kanter today and the front end suspension parts should be here Thursday so I can begin some of the assembly.  I still need to get a case of the rubber Rhino-Hide like paint...gotta look up that web site again and post it here, good stuff for frames (especially in salty locations of the US) and great for underbody sound insulation and protection.  Got home late from a friends house so I could not call Gary and chat on the Edsel, I will call him tomorrow...the suspense is killing me...it would be great to track the car back farther.  Stay tuned!

**February 27th, Thursday 2003:** Spoke to Gary back in Edmonton Alberta, nice chap, when I called I told him I was John from Seattle in Washington and he responded with a chuckle, "Should I be apologizing for something I've done", great sense of humor and a good older guy, turns out he did have an Edsel, about 30 of them over his lifetime.  He caught site of the Edsel in 1966 in a wrecking yard in Edmonton, just across the street.  Owned by Black Pete, the wrecking yard like most, keeps a car around as long as there is more to sell off of it.  Gary could see the old boy as it aged over the years, seasons reducing the fine metals to moon crater textures, people coming by and stripping more precious chrome and other vital parts off the convertible...at that time there was still a social sting (bad marketing) to the Edsel so noone was taking the entire wreck.  He said as he watched the car deteriorate he thought of picking it up for restoration but never really committed to it...ten years later he did.  Around 1975 he picked up the car and held onto it for a short time, not doing anything to the car other than keeping her from being picked over further then crushed.  He sold the aging hulk to a man in Vancouver BC who began some restoration and even drove the car once (to a Drive In, hence the movie tickets I found).  Gary lost touch with him for a few months then bought it back from the Vancouver guy when he fell out of the restoring bug...it had some body work done, most of the paint was stripped and blasted to metal and primered with browns and grays.  Gary kept the car, still as it was without restoring it, for about another ten years, roughly until 1986.  In 1986, Mike bought the car and huffed him down to Oregon in the US, where he was able to collect nearly everything necessary to bring the car back to life.  He like most of us car buffs, had a few projects underway (and still does) and the state of this car was pathetic so she sat in the back of his resto queue.  Then about 15 years later, Frosty as we call him, found his way to Bonney Lake Washington, near Mt. Rainer (Or Mt. Tacoma as it was originally named by the local Native Americans).  Whew good info, but as for who turned the seven year old car into the wrecking yard after a small rear fender crunch, that remains a mystery.  I will need to see if Gary remembers the name of the yard and if they have records that go back the nearly 30 years it took to find it's way into my family.

**March 4th, Tuesday 2003:** The bushings arrived from the rear leaf springs so I will have to get the new rear springs ordered this payday...also called and got info on replacing the bushings, just have to send back the wrong ones. Also got a call from Nostalgia Sounds <http://nostalgiasounds.com>. They do wonderful radio restoration work, so my 1954 Chevy 6-volt radio is ready. I'll have to get them my Edsel radio next...I'll get a few pics of the restored radio and speaker, yes, they do the original speaker too...for that authentic sound and due to the sensitivity of the old speakers, you would not want to use a modern replacement to get good sound.

**March 8th, Saturday 2003:** The rain has been terrible, I don't arrive home until after dark so working on the frame is impossible...and of course today is stormy, nearly every bit of free time I have had has been during downpours and storms...sigh...maybe someday I will get a little weather cooperation.  Got the rest of the correctly sized bushings but will of course wait until good weather.

**March 11th, Tuesday 2003:** The coil spring arrived today and of course it is raining and dark now that I am home so I will have to just look longingly at the tarped frame in the driveway.  The news says rain for the next five days.  joy.

**March 15th, Saturday 2003:** For once in the last two weeks the sun was peeking out most of the morning and afternoon, got the final coat of paint on the frame, a special auto paint called POR15 "Paint over rust" (<http://www.por15.com>, these guys are great and helpful), about 80 bucks a gallon but you can literally hit the finish with a hammer and it wont chip, it is flexible too...go figure, nice stuff.  The frame looks great.  I was also able to put the spindles on, coil springs in and even a set of nice new yellow shocks.  The frame is starting to come together...of course I had to cut my day short, it began to pour about dinner time but I was getting ready to stop for the day anyway.  My new compressor works great, the hand-me-down died a couple of weeks ago so McClendon hardware was able to get me a floor model contractor grade one for a great price, thanks Deb!

|  |  |

| <img src="edselpics/05_steering.jpg" data-border="0" width="460" height="310" /> | **March 16th, Sunday 2003:** Worked on the passenger side steering arm and the drivers steering box today a bit, new bushings, and man, they are hard to replace...had to torch out the old inners, fetch out the burnt goo with a screw driver and then hammer out the sleeve, not the most fun or easiest of jobs.  The frame pieces for the steering turned out great...am waiting for the new sway bar rubber along with new bumpers for the control arms, those should be here later this coming week.  Night! |

**March 21st, Friday 2003:** Been out with the flu from work for the last couple of days and have just been sitting around getting steadily worse then better today.  The new control arm bumpers lower and upper arrived today as well as the new sway-bar rubber (polyurethane). These will be handsome additions if I ever get good weather and over my flu...I will be ordering the disc brake system shortly (a month or so)...maybe a bit longer...not sure, once I get the disc brake kit I can move the car over to sitting on it's tires instead of suspended on blocks in the driveway.  I don't plan on showing the car but what the heck, I am cleaning and working on every bolt, I may...probably not, I am not following the original color scheme for the drab undercarriage and components so I know I would be faulted there...This is my restore and I am going to enjoy every moment of it.  On to suspension...
