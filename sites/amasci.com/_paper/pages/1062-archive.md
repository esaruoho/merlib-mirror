<span id="April 2001">April 2001</span>

Hey everyone! Spring is springing all over the place, and making me wish I wasn't so busy, so I could go enjoy the sunshine more. I've been pretty happy with what I've been busy with, though. I've been working my tail off for SLGC these days, since I got shanghai'd into being membership chair several months ago. I've been throwing parties, coordinating retreat, and dealing with auditions and I'm exhausted! I've also been keeping up with the writing. I've got a couple of recent reviews up on Gamezilla, and have done a few articles for the Seattle Gay Standard, where my friend Matthew is the culture editor. I was writing a column for a while on Themestream.com, but they folded last month (sigh.. the dot-coms keep dying.)

The biggest thing I've been doing the last couple of months is rehearsing like crazy. I was thrilled to be cast as Buttercup in SLGC's production of HMS Pinafore. It's the biggest role I've ever had and I'm happy as a clam. I'm also just about to start rehearsals for Sing for the Cure, a huge, multi-movement work about breast cancer that Seattle Men's Chorus is doing. They've assembled an 80-voice women's chorus from other choruses in the area, and we'll be performing the show with them in June.

We also took a great vacation to Vegas in March. We brought Charles and Jerome along with us and had a blast. I wanna go back soon, because there was so much I missed! Vegas (especially the Strip) is just HUGE and there's more there than anyone can see in a month. I also managed to get pretty lucky and broke even on my gambling budget :)

Healthwise, I'm doing somewhat better than I've been. I was a wreck back in December and January, when I got laid up by bleeding uncontrollably for about 10 weeks. My doctor finally found a hormone therapy that stopped it, (after doing an Endometrial Biopsy to make sure I didn't have cancer) and also sent me to a Hematologist, who discovered that I'm critically anemic. So, I'm now on a very intense iron therapy, and that seems to be making a difference. My cholesterol is also down these days, too, so I'm pretty happy about that. All that's left is to figure out what's going on with my insulin levels.

Mike is finally getting out from under the Evil Test Manager, and is currently in the process of switching over to working under Sherri again, this time in Office. I think the new position is going to do him really well, and I'm going to be very glad to have my Perky Husband back again, since I was getting kind of tired of the Cranky Husband I got to see all the time when he had to deal with the ETM. 

Chris and Diana succumbed to homesickness and moved back down to Southern Oregon last month. I miss them and Zoe terribly already, but I understand that they wanted to be near their families again. We'll probably go down for a visit sometime this summer. I can't stay away from my baby Bug that long!

Crisis struck my Dad's side of the family again a few weeks ago. My cousin James, who's only 39, had a fairly massive stroke. He's currently being treated at the Mayo Clinic, and is steadily improving, though he will probably never walk again. We're all holding together well, though, and hoping he'll get through without too much permanent damage.

More news later when I get more than 20 seconds of free time!

**Oct 99**

Happy Fall Everyone!

Summer's come and gone once again, and Mike and I have been through a lot of changes since the last update in March. We're still living in the rental house on Burke, waiting to get some financial p's and q's dealt with before we buy a house, but we're almost there, and if I keep to my update schedule (or lack thereof..) by the next news, we should be in the new house. We're still trying to decide exactly where to move to, as we have so many people and places scattered all over the Seattle area that finding a central location is proving impossible. We kind of have been considering Bothell or Juanita, Kenmore, etc. Somewhere in the Northshore area. Since Dave and Steven bought a house out in godforsaken Monroe, it would be nice to be closer to them instead of having to drive 45 minutes just to see them. But then again, we also have friends in West Seattle, and we spend a lot of time on Cap Hill...hmph...Oh well, the one thing we do definetly know is that we have to kill Mike's bridge commute. 520 is starting to eat away at his brain and he needs more sleep and less driving time.

Various things caused a shift in Mike's work situation recently as well. In August, he switched from the IE team and is now working as a test lead in a semi-related department whose name and product we're not allowed to talk about. What I will say is that it's a revolutionary product and it's one of Uncle Bill's pet projects, so they're getting a lot of time and attention. There's still some minor chaos even there, but then again, working at MS is chaotic by definition.

As for me, I did end up getting to do the Carmina Burana recording project with Seattle Choral Company this summer. 2 days of solid singing and recording in a stuffy old cathedral..but it was worth it. The final result should be out in stores by Christmas, and I'll be sending a few copies to people as well. The fall rehearsal season has started again and we're hard at work on our fall show- Christmas at Benaroya, which is a wonderful show of non-stop festive music- some familiar favorites, some new classics, and some glorious holiday masterworks, including pieces by Rutter, Lauridsen and Vaughan Willams. If you want more info on this show, see the SCC website at [www.seattlechoralcompany.org](http://www.seattlechoralcompany.org). We're also doing the New Year's Carmina show again, and this time we'll be including the Ode to Joy from Beethoven's 9th- 2 bombastic choral works for 2000- should be quite the evening. In February, we'll be doing Composers of the Cinema: The Sequel, a followup to our wildly popular show last winter of movie music. This show will feature pieces from Saving Private Ryan, Star Wars and Amistad, as well as Prokofiev's Aleksander Nevsky.

As for the SLGC, we're well underway again too. Our big show in Feburary will be An American Quilt, a program full of American folk songs, and songs about Puget Sound. We'll also be doing a humorous mini-oratorio kind of thing called The Return of Mrs. Olsen. You'll never look at coffee the same way again :) Something Else will also, of course, be doing a lot of shows here and there for various functions- and we're available for rent! Contact the SLGC for details.

Oh! And we're now the proud (godparents? Aunt and Uncle? something like that) of Zoe Raelynn Godfrey, who was born on August 6th. See pics of her on our [friends](private/friends.htm) page. And Dave and Steven became puppy parents recently and bought a minature Sheltie puppy named "Myra Ellen's Dew Drop Inn" or Tori, for short. Pics coming soon.

More news when I get the time to write it!!

-Shawna

<span id="March 99">March 99</span>

Well, I obviously don't get enough time to update this stuff!

Here's what's happened since last Summer:

Christmas went pretty well, and I got to have my first two performances with my choirs. The SLGC Christmas Eve show at the Egyptian went fairly well, except for the fact that the space is acoustically dead and I felt like I was singing into a sponge. The Choral Company New Year's show was really great, too, except that I started getting dizzy from an ear infection and had to sit out the second half- I was really bummed since I had worked so hard on the Carmina Burana. Oh well, I'll get a chance to do it next year. The Choral Company also announced that we'll be actually doing a recording of Carmina this summer.   I probably won't be able to do it, since I've been with the group for such a short time, but it's a great effort nonetheless.

January came and went rather quickly, and in February I had 2 more shows. The big one was the SLGC's performance of "Is Nothing Sacred" on the 13th. We had a pretty good audience, and I loved doing my role as Mary Theresa. The show got a standing ovation at the end (right after my big solo! Yay!) and it was one of the best experiences of my life. Two weeks later was the SCC's debut in Benaroya Hall with the movie music and the Philip Glass piece I'd been working my ass off on. What a show! We almost filled the house, and everything they say about Benaroya is absolutely true. The place is acoustically perfect and I wish I could sing there for the rest of my life. It's designed for orchestra, of course, but the incredible sounds we were producing as a choir in that place were nothing short of heaven. Fortunately, we get to go back there, for our first annual Christmas program on Dec. 5th, and for "Composers of the Cinema: The Sequel" next Februrary. Wow! I almost feel like a professional musician now! If only I was getting paid... :) Oh! And I also auditioned for, and got into the SLGC's small offshoot group Something Else! I don't have a lot of small group experience, so this should be really good for me. My next performances are April 16th and 17th, which are the SLGC's annual fundraising Cabaret. We're doing a Rodgers and Hart revue, and I have a few solos and a few duets and group pieces that should be a kick. If you can at all make it, I encourage you to come. I then have a few shows in May and June, including the SLGC's debut at the Northwest Folklife Festival. I'll have more date details soon, or you can check out the websites for the [SLGC](http://www.eskimo.com/~roise/slgc) or [SCC](http://www.seattlechoralcompany.org) for more info.

As for Mike, he \*finally\* finished up with IE 5, which is now live on the web for [download](http://www.microsoft.com/ie)! The poor boy barely got any sleep in January and February, but it's finally over with, for now. We don't have all the details yet, but things look \*very\* good for him getting a new position as a lead very soon. His boss, Sherri recently accepted a promotion to a position left vacant by her boss, and so now she needs to put together leads for all the teams he used to cover, and Mike is a prime candidate to take over the Behaviors team. He's a little nervous about the change, but I have faith in him, and I know he'll be an excellent lead.

We're also slowly building up our cash reserves to buy a house. We were going to do it this summer, but we decided to wait until review season is over for Mike in August, and wait for the after- summer drop in mortgage rates. Besides, moving will be nicer in cool, clear September than Hot, muggy July or August. The housing market here is truly hideous, still, but we're tired of paying rent, and Mike is tired of the bridge commute, so it's time to go. We're looking at Bothell, Kirkland or Kenmore, but even a smallish 4 bedroom house is going to be ex\$pen\$ive!! Oh well, at least we didn't completely lose our minds like our friends Dave and Steven, who made the mistake of moving all the way out to Monroe! Ack! It now takes just as long to go visit them as it would for us to drive to Tacoma! Oh well, at least Monroe smells better- if you don't count the aroma from the boots of the shit-kicking rednecks that live there :)

Speaking of friends, [Jay and Mel](http://www.teleport.com/~ammonia) finally moved up here in December, when Jay got a contract job in Mike's department. They stayed with us for a few weeks til they could afford an apartment, and they're now living just down the street, which is nice, since it means I can now go bug Mel in the middle of the day, instead of sitting around the house ignoring the laundry and dishes like I usually do. :) We're hoping to slowly convince the rest of our friends to move up here, too, but we have less faith in that :)

As for our other friends, Chris and Diana found out in December that they're going to be parents. We visited them in early March, and they found out then that they're having a girl, and planning to name her Zoe Raylynn. Diana sure looks different these days, but she looks like a happy mother-to-be.

That's all the news I can think of for now! Keep yourself well, and [Keep in touch!!](mailto:allegro@eskimo.com)

The summer was basically just hot and lazy for us. Aside from work, we didn't really do a lot. We did take a couple of trips, tho. We took one trip to Portland in June for my graduation-that was an interesting experience. Considering what has been happening lately, it's a little weird to realize that my memories of my graduation will probably be tainted somewhat by realizing that my keynote speaker may be kicked out of office. Oh well, I liked his speech anyway :)

We also took another trip to Portland in August, for [Jay and Mel's](http://www.teleport.com/~ammonia) handfasting. It was a wonderful ceremony, held at McIver park, and Mike and I got to play some roles in the ritual. It was the first time I'd taken part in something like that and it really felt great to be a part of it. I need to spend more time involving myself in my spiritual side again, and the way that made me feel really helped.

Over Labor Day weekend, we took another trip, this time to Ashland, where we spent some time with Chris and Diana and Andy, etc. Mike really needed a vacation, so we didn't do all that much-just bummed around and hung out. We both feel much better for having zoned for a few days.

The best news I have right now is that I'm \*finally\* getting a chance to pursue my real career! In late August, I auditioned for the [Seattle Choral Company](http://www.seattlechoralcompany.org), the premier large-ensemble choral group in Seattle. I wasn't expecting to get in, as it's been some time since I did any serious singing, but the audition went really well and I passed! I'm really happy to finally be singing again, and I'm looking forward to starting rehearsals soon. Our first concert is New Year's Eve (details are on the SCC website.)

I also auditioned for, and got into, the [Seattle Lesbian and Gay Chorus](http://www.geocities.com/WestHollywood/Village/2289/). We had our first rehearsal of the season recently, and it looks like it's going to be a lot of fun. Where the SCC is doing more serious "stand there and sing" kind of music, the SLGC is more of a show choir, with a lot of fun material. This all is going to mean working pretty hard, but consdering that I've finally realized that my heart belongs with performing, I know this is what I was meant to do, so I enjoy the hard work.

In news about other people- Mike's dad recently got a contract position at Microsoft, as a tester for SMS. He's working up here during the week and spending the weekends back home in Portland with the family. I don't envy him the stress of doing so, but he's hoping that they'll hire him full-time eventually and the whole family can move up here.

Chris and Diana are doing fairly well, too. Chris has been promoted a couple of times, and is now the Circulation Director for the Ashland Daily Tidings. He gets to wear a tie and everything :). We're quite proud of him though. Diana also has a new job. She's still working in the maternity ward at Rogue Valley Medical Center, but she's also doing some office work for Ashland Hospital and she likes that a lot better, since she doesn't have to be on her feet for it. Andy is living with them, too, and he's happily working in the press room at the Tidings.

That's about all the news for now. Check back later to see if there's anything new, otherwise, please [stay in touch!](mailto:allegro@eskimo.com)
