<td colspan="2" data-valign="TOP" width="80%" data-bgcolor="#ffffff"><p><strong>Writing stuff.</strong></p>
<p>Well, there's about an inch of snow out there now. Wet snow, good for making snowmen and throwing at loved ones. :) We don't have the kind of yard where we can go out and play, but it does look very attractive on our decks and little bridges.</p>
<p>Today I plan to work on the book. Write, write, write, edit, edit.</p>
<p>I've been trying to get past the feeling that my success with NaNoWriMo was a fluke. I have a lot of work done on the novel. I just need to get past the stuff I've done so far, delete the uneccessary and nasty bits, and keep going.</p>
<p>Tonight I finally made a step that helps get me off in the right direction: a title, at last! It's probably a draft title, of course, but all the same it's something I can use instead of referring to it as "my NaNoWriMo thing" or the "Oh My God What Am I Going To Write About Novel" (the former draft-draft title).</p>
<p><strong><em>Beyond the Call</em></strong></p>
<p>For those of you who've read it, I pretty sure you'll get it.</p>
<p>I had it online until just a few days ago, when I ran out of room on my web account and had to start deleting things. So it'll be a while before it's available again, sorry.</p>
<p>Anyhow, I'm pleased.</p>
<p><strong>I have a history with semicolons.</strong></p>
<p>I don't like them much, you see. As an editor, when I see them in someone's writing, I tend to look for an alternative which wouldn't use a semicolon. They break up the flow, call attention to the act of writing, when the reader shouldn't be aware of anything other than the literary world they've been transported to. I see a semicolon and - bam! - I'm slammed back into my bones and out of the writer's world. It's especially bad in dialogue. Nobody speaks with a semicolon. It's a writing thing.</p>
<p>Folks in the monthly writing group have heard me say it many, many times. I'm sure they're tired of hearing it by now. "When you're reading," Julie says, "you shouldn't be aware that you're reading. Things like semicolons distract the reader and remind them that they're looking at printed words on a page." And my friends smile indulgently and nod... which is all right, really. Everybody has their own peeves, and mine being semicolons is easily indulged.</p>
<p>(The lesson Julie badly needs to learn: how not to write in passive voice. I know.)</p>
<p>So last night I continued my read of Stephen King's <a href="http://www.amazon.com/exec/obidos/ASIN/0671024256/cerejulislist"><em>On Writing</em></a> and I came across:</p>
<p><em>...Language does not always have to wear a tie and lace-up shoes. The object of fiction isn't grammatical correctness but to make the reader welcome and then tell a story... to make him/her forget, whenever possible, that he/she is reading a story at all.</em></p>
<p>I'm really beginning to like this King guy more and more.</p>
