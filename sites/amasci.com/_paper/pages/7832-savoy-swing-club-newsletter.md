## <img src="images/dog1.gif" data-align="bottom" data-hspace="10" width="32" height="42" alt="Lindy Dog" />How Did It All Get Started?

Why the interest in Lindy Hop? For most it began with triple time swing dance classes with Walter and Nancyanna Dill. Walter and Nancyanna are icons of the Seattle social dance scene and founders/directors of [Living Traditions](http://www.adhost.com/ltdance), an organization dedicated to non-threatening, non-competitive partner dancing. Wa and Na are masters at getting ANYone dancing and feeling good at doing it. They are also masters at mentoring new and exciting dance forms for their students. They were instrumental in introducing Seattle to Lindy Hop, as well as providing the catalyst for the growth of Argentine Tango, Cajun/Zydeco, and Salsa through special weekend workshops and their annual dance camp, Wild Week.

It was at Wild Week 1991 that a handful of Seattle Dancers were first introduced to Lindy Hop by Steven Mitchell and Erin Stevens, a dynamic duo teaching Savoy-style Lindy Hop culled from sessions with Frankie Manning. Frankie, the now 81-year old inventor of the first air step and an original member of Whiteys Lindy Hoppers, was brought out of retirement by Erin and Steven and is the star of the international Lindy Hop Scene. The Dills brought Frankie to Seattle in April, 1992 where he charmed dancers young and old with his spirit and smooth style. At the next Wild Week, Steven Mitchell was back and this dance found a permanent home in Seattle. A group started meeting to practice the new moves and choreography learned at the feet of these masters.

At first practices were held sporadically, a couple of times a month following the dance workshops. Dancers asked other dancers and the group grew by word of mouth. In the early days, we struggled to find others willing to do this dance the Savoy way, vacillating between growing a dance community and providing a place where accomplished dancers could practice. Growing pains forced us to get organized and in 1993 we selected not only a name, but spelled out our mission, elected a 7-member board and officially sanctioned both a performance group and regular instruction. Practice became a weekly event where we continued to review and teach each other new moves. From those early days, our [Friday night practices](practice.htm) at Dance On Capital Hill, remain the heart and soul of our Lindy Hop breeding ground.

On June 8, 1993 our first session of classes was held at Loyal Heights Community Center. At first we offered beginning Lindy Hop classes every other week. After one session of 6 classes, the demand and need for an intermediate level of class was apparent. To accommodate this, we had beginning and intermediate level classes taught at the same time with an hour of free dancing afterwards.

This format was acceptable until early 1995 when students complained of the 2-week gap between classes. Students also wanted to be able to take both classes. Loyal Heights Community Center was not available on a weekly basis and the increase in class attendance was pushing the capacity of the rooms. So in the spring of 1995, we moved classes to our current home at the beautiful and spacious Mt. Baker Community Center. We now provide [classes every Tuesday night](classes.htm) to more than 100 students of Savoy style Lindy Hop.

Instruction increased the number of new dancers, but how do we bring Lindy Hop to the unenlightened? Our mission directed us to preserve and promote Lindy Hop through instruction, special events, and performance. [Performance](performa.htm) remains an important part of our Savoy Swing Club activities. It is an opportunity to share our love of this dance and excite others with its exuberance.

Lindy Hop continues to grow in the greater Seattle area. In fact, Lindy Hop master and supreme Lindy Dude Steven Mitchell rates Seattle as the fastest growing Lindy community in the U.S. The dance somehow works its way into your soul (I think a lot of it is the music). Lindy Hop is now a permanent part of the lively social dance scene in Seattle and here to stay.

*-- Christine Nelson, President*
