I have been receiving SMALLnet for several years but have never contributed before. I have just gotten back into model aircraft after an absence of 35 years.

I plan on converting it to use modern mini R/C gear. The plane has an 8-foot wingspan and ready to fly weighs 30 ounces (original FF form).

The best thing I've done in years is get back into model flying, and the second best thing is finding the existence of SMALL.

Chuck Swiger \< 

 \> adds:

The last SMALLnet Posting discussed fuel tanks. I've just finished a tank project and am really pleased with how it turned out, because it's a "clank" tank, complete with rotating pickup for those upside-down flights.

Basically, if you can't find one for sale, make your own! I had looked at the Acme web site and asked about one, but they said it would take two weeks to make one. So I decided to try rolling my own.

If you can work a solder gun, you can learn a fun skill by soldering together bits of all those soup tins you toss in the dust bin. The heart of my 'clank' tank is two sizes of brass tubing from the hobby shop that fit together snugly but freely. The OD of one just mates with the ID of the larger.

I cut a piece of the large tube at a 45 degree angle and solder back together to make a 90 degree angle. Then, as luck would have it, a lead sinker from the fish shop has a hole in it that fits over the tube with just a little reaming, and tack- solders onto the tubing easily.

This rotor fits over a bit of the smaller tube soldered into the tank. I used tin-plated steel sheet from a coffee can rescued from the garbage at work. The plastic label peels off and careful cutting with tin snips (watch those sharp edges!) yields a stock of easily-solderable metal to work with.

With a little practice you can make any shape you need. Smooth edges with a file and/or Dremel tool. Test for leaks and give a good cleaning with flux remover and waste fuel.

Some pictures are posted at: \< <http://mail.schillernet.us/gastank/gastank.html> \>

<div class="Joe">

...Chuck, I've built my own metal tanks off and on for many years, and the most time-saving trick I know in doing that sort of work is to first make a solid wooden form block the size and shape of the desired finished tank.

That makes precise forming quick and easy -- and supports the sheet metal firmly during the major soldering too. \[JW\]

</div>

John Hodnette \< 

 \> submitted:

I want to add my comments to Dereck Woodward's about document laminating film (Doculam).

I purchased a Herr Starlite and covered it completely (except for the underside of the fuselage and a patch on the right wing) with 1.5 mil clear document film from the end of a roll here at work. (They were going to throw it away because the roll was too short to set up on the machine.)

It was very easy to work with, once you got used to it trying to curl up on itself.

<span id="315"></span>

<div class="green">

Source: Doculam\

</div>

Search on the web for "Doculam" and you can find several suppliers. Check out \< <a href="http://" data-www.laminatorwarehouse.com="">http:// www.laminatorwarehouse.com</a> \>. They sell minimum two rolls of Doculam 1.5 mil, 27" wide, 500' (1125 sq ft) for \$24.15 a roll (\$.0215/sq ft.). If you get together with several buddies, you can share the two rolls and never run out of film. Just for comparison, Tower sells Coverite in a roll of regular covering 27" x 15' (33.75 sq ft) white for \$15.99 (\$0.474 sq ft).

When I am flying my Starlite, the sun shines through the clear covering and changes colors.

I got my motor and speed control from Todd's Models, and a lithium ion two-cell pack and charger from a seller named Brad (beelitehobbies) on E-bay. I am regularly getting 30+ minute flights. After hand launch, I set the throttle at just over 50% and she climbs until you can barely see her. I fly from a lawn chair to keep my neck from breaking. Now you can see why I had to use the red covering on the fuselage and part of the right wing.

This is as much fun as any of the models I have flown in over 30 years in modeling. John Hodnette
