# <span id="SECTION00200000000000000000">The Framework of Doctrinal Decision Making</span>

One of the biggest problems with theology is that there is no set of rules to guide theological insight. In science, truth is determined by matching the best theory with the known data. In creative writing, the strength of the writing is based on how well the reader is affected in the way the author desired. However, theology has no such rule set to judge the truth of any claim. Therefore, I will begin by stating my set of rules that I use to judge the truth of theological claims.

# <span id="SECTION00210000000000000000">The Source of Revelation</span>

Before I determined what I believed, I first decided where I thought authoritative data came from. Therefore I started at the most fundamental part of theology, which is God's work.

## <span id="SECTION00211000000000000000">God's Work as the Revelation</span>

God's first work that I knew about was the change He made in my life. When I accepted Jesus into my life in seventh grade, I was totally changed. I stopped focusing on myself and started reaching out to others. My personality was transformed, and I felt renewed. Although I had heard the gospel story many times (and nominally believed it), that day God revealed to me what He had done for me. He revealed Himself to my heart, and I believed. He continues to work in my life each and every day. It is to Him that I owe my entire life, and this is the basis of all my belief.

The second work of God that I knew about was God's work in Jesus. I learned this when I accepted Jesus, and continued to learn more about this as I grew in my faith. Jesus Christ was the ultimate revelation of God on Earth, because He was God on Earth. This is the fullest part of God's revelation. It is also most important, because if Jesus had not come and died, there would be no savior to believe in. God's other work includes His work among the Hebrews before Christ, His creation, and His work through the Holy Spirit after Jesus's ascension. Anytime God acts upon nature, He reveals more about Himself. God's work is His revelation, and it is how we know whom we serve.

## <span id="SECTION00212000000000000000">The Bible as Witness to the Revelation</span>

The Bible was the first spark of God's revelation to me. Although the change happened in my heart, it was first started by reading His word. The subject of my revelation is also the subject of the Bible, and that is Jesus. The Bible has been recognized as the most accurate and faithful account of the works of Jesus and his disciples, and throughout history those who have had experiences similar to mine have based their belief on the Bible. Therefore, I know that I can at least trust the Bible to be an adequate source for instruction regarding my faith.<a href="#foot15" id="tex2html1"><sup>1.1</sup></a> Also, those who wrote it were the closest to the event, closest to Jesus, and therefore should have better insight into Christianity than anyone else. Also, the Old Testament is a faithful account of God's work in the Hebrew people. We know God by His works, and we know many of God's works by the Bible.

# <span id="SECTION00220000000000000000">Interpreting the Witness</span>

Although I'm not sure if the Bible is one hundred percent accurate (although it might be), it is the best that I've got. I know for sure that I'm not more accurate than it is. Therefore, I read the Bible as if it was completely accurate, because I don't have a better way. I don't see how people today could have a better understanding of the significance of the Gospel than those who had the same Spirit that we do, although lived with Jesus himself.<a href="#foot17" id="tex2html2"><sup>1.2</sup></a> Therefore, I have nothing left to do but to depend on the Bible for the best understanding I can have.

## <span id="SECTION00221000000000000000">Exegetical Reading</span>

Because the Bible contains the best understanding of Christianity, it would better instruct me if I read it as it was read in the first century instead of imposing my beliefs on it. If I impose my own beliefs on the Bible, then I imply that I know more than the Bible, which is not true. Therefore, Bible reading should be done exegetically. I should attempt to read the Bible the same way the original readers did.

Many modern theologians, especially those in Liberation Theology, Feminist Theology, and the like, want to read a given set of values into the Bible. I am not necessarily saying that their doctrines are wrong, as much as their whole approach to Christianity is wrong. Christianity is not a tool to promote a person's own social views. Instead, a life with Christ should transform a person's social views. Christians should read the Bible for what is says it means, instead of what we want it to mean.<a href="#foot19" id="tex2html3"><sup>1.3</sup></a>

## <span id="SECTION00222000000000000000">Consistent Interpretation</span>

Because our society is significantly different from first century society, it is necessary to apply the teachings of the Bible for situations which did not exist at that time. Therefore, after reading the Bible exegetically, the person must apply what was written to the first century society to today's society without adding to or subtracting from any of the original meaning. This is a daunting situation to say the least. When a person applies Biblical teachings to the problems of today, he needs a consistent set of rules or principles on which to do that. Otherwise, the interpreter is simply reading his own opinion into the Bible. I have not established a consistent set of principles yet. Hopefully, as I grow in faith, I will learn some of the principles to make a proper application of the Bible to most situations.

# <span id="SECTION00230000000000000000">A Pragmatic Theory of Belief</span>

After making a Biblical interpretation, how does one determine whether they believe correctly? In many instances the only differences between two opinions are grammatical, and yet the two parties cannot agree. Other times, the two sides simply differ in what they emphasize. Language itself is a barrier because words can be vague. Therefore I will use Pragmatism for determining whether a belief is correct or not.

## <span id="SECTION00231000000000000000">Belief as a Rule for Action</span>

Pierce's pragmatism simply says that the way for a person to make his ideas clear is to show concrete actions that the idea or belief requires a person to take. If you are not quite sure what my belief means, simply ask me what actions I will take because of my belief, and you will have a clearer understanding of what I'm saying. For example, if you asked me what I meant by the idea of \`\`the fire is hot,'' I would say that \`\`the fire is hot'' means that I shouldn't put my hand in the fire, but I should put my marshmallows there. \`\`The fire is hot'' also entails certain consequences of certain actions. It means that if I do decide to put my hand in the fire, it will hurt really bad. Later pragmatists went so far as to say that beliefs were only rules for actions. Therefore, not only could beliefs be clarified by stating the resulting actions, but that those actions themselves were the belief. The problem, though, is that this leaves out the predictive content of statements (i.e. - putting my hand in the fire will make it hurt). However, the purpose of belief is purely practical. We have beliefs because they help us live. We are taught in elementary school that gravity pulls things to the Earth. However, scientists know that this isn't true (gravity is actually a curving of space). However, the truth itself is so much more complicated that elementary students would have trouble applying the idea of gravity to their everyday situations. Therefore, the belief, although it isn't technically true, is useful for the students, because it helps them to make sense out of the world and perform appropriate actions based on their theory of gravity. So, the belief is worth holding because it helps them to live. A scientist, however, would need the more modern theory, because he is dealing with a different set of situations. But, in everyday life, both theories match up adequately enough with reality in order to produce appropriate actions in both the elementary student and the scientist.

## <span id="SECTION00232000000000000000">Belief is the Action</span>

Using pragmatic theory enables the Christian to avoid many debates. This is because many stated beliefs all end up producing the same action in people, and are thus, at least according to pragmatism, the same belief expressed differently. For example, take predestination. Those who believe in predestination believe that God has predestined them to go and preach the gospel to all the nations, so they do. Those who do not believe in predestination still go and preach the gospel to all the nations because they want to be sure that as many people as possible get saved. Although the statements of belief are different, the resulting action is the same.<a href="#foot24" id="tex2html4"><sup>1.4</sup></a> Therefore, purely theoretical arguments can be avoided, and the Christian life can be the measure of Christian faith.

I once saw a quote that said that doctrine is important because abstract thought always led to a concrete action. However, this quote seems to acknowledge that the resulting action is the most important. Therefore, if the purpose of doctrine is action, shouldn't doctrines that produce the same actions be deemed the same? Also, shouldn't two people who claim that they believe the same thing yet act differently be distinguished? Therefore, the distinction should be based on the actions instead of the beliefs. In this paper, I will discuss many beliefs that have few, if any, resulting actions. Although I usually have an opinion on such subjects, I feel that their impact on the Christian faith is minimal. The truly important subjects are ones that have a direct impact on living the Christian life.

## <span id="SECTION00233000000000000000">The Christian Life as Determining Heresy</span>

In the past, a person was deemed a heretic based on what beliefs they affirmed. However, with a pragmatic perspective of belief, it is more natural to base heresy on a person's actions. Therefore, when any person is accused of heresy, their life will show whether or not the accusations are true. Orthodoxy can be based on orthopraxy. If a person worships God, feeds the hungry, and shares the Gospel, then he is orthodox. If he lacks these, he may be a heretic.<a href="#foot26" id="tex2html5"><sup>1.5</sup></a>

# <span id="SECTION00300000000000000000">Who God Is</span>

After understanding where God's revelation comes from, it is then possible to figure out to some extend the nature of God. However, we must keep in mind that God's revelation to us must be within what we can understand. There is a probability that most things about God are beyond our understanding. Therefore, knowledge of God should only be considered as a close parallel to what actually is.

# <span id="SECTION00310000000000000000">God's Attributes</span>

God's work is our only way of knowing Him. Therefore, it seems appropriate to describe God in terms of what He has done. There is very little chance that anyone will ever really know a whole lot about how God operates, but we can know what He has done in the past. In fact, this is one of the main ways God is known in the Old Testament. He is the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob; He is the one who delivered Israel from Egypt. Thus instead of trying to describe God, we admit that we can't, and simply affirm His history with us. However, there are several traits that the Bible does ascribe to God.

The following is a list of traditional attributes of God and what I think of them:

- Omniscient - I agree that God knows more than anyone else, possibly even everything that has happened or is happening right now. However, I do not believe that God knows the future.<a href="#17" id="tex2html6"><sup>2.1</sup></a>
- Omnipresent - It seems by most biblical passages that God is everywhere. However, some passages show God as not looking at certain events. Currently, I believe that God is omnipresent, and that the conflicting verses are simply being metaphorical.
- Immutable - I really do not like this attribute. I prefer to think of God as consistent rather than immutable. His personality doesn't change. However, my relationship with Him can change, and He can do new things. Each of those require some amount of change on the part of God. Therefore, it is better to leave God as consistent rather than immutable
- Loving - This is one of the most basic parts of God's nature. He has loved us, and that is why we were given a second chance through Jesus.
- Holy - God is holy, and therefore we must be holy, too. God is blameless, and we can only marvel at His righteousness
- All-Powerful - Because I believe that God formed the world, I also believe that within the world He is able to do anything He wants.

Although these are interesting descriptions of God, our main knowledge of who God is is based on what He has done. He is a very present help in trouble. He is our fortress. He is our provider and our victor. This type of description is the most important. A close look at the names of God in the Old Testament reveals that our primary knowledge of God isn't theoretical or theological, it's practical. We know God by what He has done for us. He has done good things for us in the past, and we trust Him to continue to do good things for us in the future.

# <span id="SECTION00320000000000000000">The Ways God Works</span>

My view of the Trinity is that each person in it is simply a function which God performs. There is some Biblical dispute, mainly about God turning away from Jesus while He was on the cross, which is awfully strange if Jesus and God were exactly the same person in two different roles.

## <span id="SECTION00321000000000000000">Father</span>

The Father is usually seen as the mastermind behind the trinity. The Father is generally recognized as the creator - the one who creates and sustains all types of life. This role is the one involving planning the future, providing for His people, and the saving acts which He did for Israel.

## <span id="SECTION00322000000000000000">Son</span>

Jesus is the embodiment of God. Since Jesus is both God and man, it has been debated throughout the centuries how this interplay works. One view is Adoptionism. This view holds that Jesus was just a man, but He became divine when God adopted Him (usually attributed to His baptism). The orthodox view is that Jesus is fully divine and fully human, without any sort of conflict or confusion between the two natures. Modalism holds that Jesus was so much the same as God that there is no real distinction between the Father and the son except in how they function. The son is simply God on Earth. This is my view. Jesus's name means God with us, and the description of His coming in John 1 sounds a lot like God Himself coming down to Earth to move in among His people. However, even though it was God Himself, we should remember that Jesus was also a human, and expressed many of the human limitations during His stay on Earth.

Jesus's work on this Earth was His death. His death closed the door on the old way of serving God through the law, and opened up the new way of serving God through living with God's Spirit. We remain obedient to God, but instead of having to rely on a written law, we can rely on the commands that God has put on our hearts. Jesus freed us from the condemnation of the old way, and gave us a new start.

## <span id="SECTION00323000000000000000">Holy Spirit</span>

The Holy Spirit's function is to equip the believer. The Holy Spirit writes God's commands on the hearts of the believers, demonstrates the power of God, and gives the believers the sense of the presence of God. The Holy Spirit also gives the spiritual gifts which are listed throughout the Bible. The Spirit is also referred to in James as the Wisdom of God. This makes sense because the Spirit is our instructor and counsellor. The Spirit can also gives the believer amazing experiences in worship. The Spirit has come to take the place of the law. The Spirit is our seal, the way that God knows us. We are marked for all the world to see with God's Holy Spirit in us.

# <span id="SECTION00400000000000000000">A Brief History of Time - A Theological History of the Earth</span>

First of all, please note that this is a theological history of the Earth, and not of the rest of the universe. In the Bible, it is not very clear if the events described within relate only to the Earth or to the universe as a whole. Therefore, I will only talk about the Earth, since we know that it was involved.

# <span id="SECTION00410000000000000000">Creation</span>

First of all, God created. That is the primary theme of Genesis. It is God's creation - He is in control. He initiates relationships with mankind, He brings both happiness and sorrow, and He holds the destiny of the world in His hands. These theological points will have the most impact on a person's behavior, and therefore are most important for the Christian. There are several other points in the creation story which are being debated. Although most of these have very little impact in a relationship with Christ, they are awfully fun to argue about. The three most important are when did God create the Earth, how long did it take, and how does evolution fit in this picture. Here are the most common answers:

- Direct Creationism - this says that the Earth was created in seven twenty-four hour periods. The Earth is only thousands or tens of thousands of years old. Evolution only accounts for variation within a species, and not for new species.<a href="#17" id="tex2html7"><sup>3.1</sup></a>Contradictory scientific theories are based on flawed methodology. Some believe that the Earth is really old, but the Earth as we know it is only a few thousand years old.
- Theistic Evolution - this theory holds that the Bible tells *what* happened, while modern science tells us *how* it happened. Human beings are part of the evolutionary chain, although they were, at some point, made into special beings, separate from the rest of creation.

Personally, I hold to a very liberal Direct Creationism viewpoint. I think that the world was created billions of years ago, but that human existence began around six thousand years ago.<a href="#foot45" id="tex2html8"><sup>3.2</sup></a> The seven days in Genesis are obviously metaphorical because the sun doesn't enter the picture until Genesis 1:14-19. Without the sun, there can be no day-based time, because time operates at different speeds based on what measure is being used. If the sun is not there, then neither can a literal day be there. However, the story of Adam and Eve seems to have a less poetic and more historical tone. Therefore, I interpret that part literally.

# <span id="SECTION00420000000000000000">Fall</span>

According to Romans, all have sinned and fallen short of the glory of God. The question is, why? Most Christians believe that Eve committed the first sin, and Adam committed the second. Also, since then, all people have sinned. The question is, have subsequent generations sinned because they are born sinful, or is it because they are raised sinful? I have two reasons for thinking that original sin is a by-birth situation.

- Soul is generated from the father (explained in section [4.2](pragmatic-confession.html#receivingthesoul))
- Sin includes failing to worship God, which I think all babies are guilty of

Therefore, we all inherit guilty souls. Even if that didn't condemn us, our lack of worship would. However, all Biblical views of sin lead to the same conclusion - sin is a universal problem which needs a solution. Some have said that we did not really need a savior, just someone to rally to. However, this overlooks the heinousness of sin. If that is overlooked, then our Christian lives will turn to sensuality. If we forget the seriousness of sin, it will lead us into it all the more.

# <span id="SECTION00430000000000000000">Redemption</span>

All Christians must look to the cross. The entire story of Christianity centers on Jesus's work on the cross. Through that act, the Christian is freed from the chains of sin and the judgment of the law. Our hope and our joy rest in this one act.

# <span id="SECTION00440000000000000000">The End</span>

Christians must believe in a final victory for Jesus over the forces of this world. That is the hope which Paul talks about so often. As Christians, we hope for Jesus's return and His establishment of a righteous kingdom. However, as Christians living in a godless world, we must expect trouble. The apostle John tells us in Revelation that it will get much, much worse, and eventually climax as God brings His judgment upon the world, as the world goes into a warring chaos.

Several questions have been asked about this time period. These include:

- Will Jesus take His people with Him before, after, or during the great tribulation?
- Where will the new kingdom be?
- What will life be like in the new kingdom?
- What happens between death and resurrection?

One of the more interesting arguments is that of intermediate state - what happens to a person between death and resurrection? Although this is hard to discuss without a further understanding of body, soul, and spirit, I will make an attempt. The Roman Catholic view is that Christians go to purgatory to \`\`work off'' their sins. I think this is totally against the forgiveness of sin which Christ achieved on the cross. Another view is \`\`paradise''. Paradise is mentioned in the parables of Jesus, as well as by Jesus on the cross. Therefore, it seems likely that paradise is a real place.

As for when Jesus will take His people, I think it is obvious from scripture that Jesus will take His people after the great tribulation. First of all, even though there will be rough times during the tribulation, Christianity was never meant as an escape from trouble. Also, the passage in 1 Thessolonians is very unclear about when the rising will occur. Those words, as Paul said are to be for comfort, not instruction.

# <span id="SECTION00500000000000000000">Nature of Man</span>

# <span id="SECTION00510000000000000000">What is Man?</span>

The nature of man is a very important issue for the Christian, because it explains both how he is to act toward the world and to God. In order to find out what man is, we must see what the Bible says about man's origin. Genesis clearly states that man is created in the image of God. However, this is a tricky phrase, because it does not say in what way man is in the image of God. That could be understood as saying that we look similar, we both have reason, we both are moral beings, or we both have a trinitarian makeup. I usually go with the morality issue, because that seems to be the most distinctive human trait.

Man can also be understood in terms of His purpose. From the beginning, man has served one primary purpose - to bring glory to God. That has made some secondary purposes as well - we are to fill the earth and subdue it, and we are to make disciples of all nations. However, all this is done so that God will be glorified in His creation. The only problem is that man has sinned, but God brought that into an even greater glory by sacrificing His son Jesus for us.

<span id="SECTION00520000000000000000"></span><span id="receivingthesoul"></span>\
What is a Soul?
==================================================================================

A very controversial issue these days is the makeup of the human. Do humans have more than simply a physical nature? Is there more to us than simply deterministic physical chemistry? The Bible seems to think that there is more to people than scientific determinism. The Bible speaks of making right choices. If life were entirely deterministic, then this would not be possible. Some say that although life is governed entirely by physical laws, there is some element of randomness that is observed. However, randomness is not the same thing as choice. Randomness, although it is not deterministic, has nothing to do with the choices that a person can make. Therefore, the Bible, with its emphasis on personal choices (Jeremiah 29:11) and consequences for those choices seems to be espousing a view that people are not bound entirely to deterministic laws.<a href="#foot57" id="tex2html9"><sup>4.1</sup></a>

If the physical world is governed by deterministic factors, yet I am not, I can conclude that there is more to me than a physical body. I must have something more. I call that something more \`\`soul''. Personally, I refuse to give it any more specifics other than it is not deterministic. Soul might be a non-deterministic property of all matter, or it might be quite distinct from matter. I think that the soul is distinct from the body, and can even exist without the body. However, when we are resurrected, we will gain new bodies to go with our souls.

If we believe that each person has a soul, then the question is, how does a person get a soul? There are two major viewpoints on this - creationism and traducianism. Creationism states that God creates each soul individually and then implants that soul into the person sometime between conception and birth. Traducianism says that the \`\`soul stuff'' is in the father's reproductive organs, and is replicated in the same processes that the body is replicated in. This seems more logical and down-to-earth to me. Instead of God having to churn souls out all the time, and then plan exactly the time when the soul enters the body, I think it is easier to just say that the soul is continuous with the male sex organs.

# <span id="SECTION00530000000000000000">Death</span>

Death is a natural part of life. These days, it seems that whenever someone dies, someone else is to blame. We are scared of dying, and view it as an abnormality. We forget that we live in a world that is full of death, dying, and living. Everything that lives dies. If someone has a terminal illness, they are still not at any more risk of dying than the rest of us. They are just going sooner.

# <span id="SECTION00600000000000000000">The Community of God</span>

The community of God is also known as the Church. Within, I will use the word Church to refer to the event of God's community coming together (whenever that happens to be), and will use the word community to refer to all Christians within a geographical area, whether or not they have become part of the actual organizational structure. This is my favorite theological topic because it is the most practical and the one that I disagree with most Christians on.

# <span id="SECTION00610000000000000000">The Members of the Community</span>

The community is made up of people who have faith in Christ for salvation. It is an exclusive community. If members of the community found out that someone associating with the community as a member did not rely on Christ for salvation, then the community should disassociate with that individual, at least in a Christian context.

Church should be attended only by members of the community. Although non-members should be allowed to attend Church, Church should not be geared to meeting their needs. Church should be only for meeting the needs of the community. Sermons, if there are any, should be directed at the believer, not the non-believer. Calls for salvation should not be given in Church. If the Church is busy catering to non-believers, then the believers will not be able to mature as Christians. Christians should be equipped at Church to live the Christian life and to minister to those who don't know Christ. Then, when they are in the world, they should witness to non-believers. Then, when the non-believer becomes a Christian, he should be welcomed with open arms.

# <span id="SECTION00620000000000000000">Relationship of God and the Community</span>

In God's relationship with the community, the most important thing is God's Spirit at work. It is this Spirit that guides, protects, and nurtures the community. God, through His Spirit, is continually reminding us of His love towards us. The focus of the community's relationship to God is that of savior - God alone has saved us. This is essentially the same attitude that the Jews had of God - they were His people because of His saving influence on them. So it is with us now, except that we have Jesus, God's ultimate act of salvation.

Because of our salvation orientation, the community should actively worship God for all He has done for us. He should be worshiped both within the context of Church and within the daily lives of the people of the community. Also, the individual Christian should keep up communication with God throughout the day in prayer and thanks. If the community keeps its focus on God, then the Spirit can minister to the people in amazing ways.

One of the ways that God has given us to receive His grace is through ordinances. The ordinances are those things which Jesus ordained during His earthly ministry. There are three ordinances - baptism, the Lord's supper, and foot-washing. Each of these, while not having any metaphysical importance, do help the believer remember His relationship with God, and the salvation that God has given.<a href="#foot62" id="tex2html10"><sup>5.1</sup></a>

# <span id="SECTION00630000000000000000">The Behavior of the Community</span>

The Christian community should be characterized by unity. All Christians are unified in that they are saved by God's amazing grace. Being that we have been given so much by such a wonderful God, we have no real reason to have any sort of quarrels among ourselves.<a href="#foot64" id="tex2html11"><sup>5.2</sup></a> Now that we have Jesus, we have no reason not to be at peace with each other. If we recognize the amazing amount that we have been given, then we should have nothing left to do but to love and forgive fellow Christians.

# <span id="SECTION00640000000000000000">The Organization of the Community</span>

In Acts, every leader of the community had been involved with the community prior to the assignment to the leadership position. Timothy was only sent to lead communities after he had worked there a considerable amount of time. Paul never sent anyone to a community to hold any position unless that person had worked among them previously. Also, many of Paul's own claims to authority came from him working among the people. Although Acts is not necessarily written to establish the normative behavior of Christian communities, this unwritten rule seems to have worked effectively. Personally, I think the best leaders come from within the community itself, not from without. Having an authority structure where the leaders come from the community, or at least have to have spent time with the community first, establishes a leadership that the membership can trust. In addition, I think that all Churches should be able to survive and maybe even thrive without a pastor. Any minister should work to make the community as self-reliant as possible, so that if something does happen to the pastor, the community does not wither away.

As for other Church positions, The Bible explicitly names several. The question for the Christian is, are these positions good for every Church, or only the Churches which had them? Should there be more positions than outlined in the Bible? How much freedom does a Church have in creating and filling positions? Going by a basic principles approach, I have these beliefs:

- The ultimate human authority of the Church, if there is one, should be chosen for moral character, active faith in Christ, and good leadership skills. In addition, he should be a man.<a href="#24" id="tex2html12"><sup>5.3</sup></a>
- All leaders should demonstrate moral character and an active faith in Christ. These are more important than any skills a person may have.
- Positions should be created in order to keep the main leadership of the Church from being bogged down in activities not directly related to ministering the gospel to believers and unbelievers.

Another topic of great interest is ordaining. Historically, Church clergy were ordained by the Church for life. After sufficient study and training, the Church said that a person was ready for lifelong ministry. Although the New Testament apostles were lifelong ministers, that does not mean that every minister needs to be a lifelong one. In fact, the only ordaining service in the Bible is only for a specific ministry. Ordaining, if done at all, is a position-by-position ordainment. It is a commissioning to a specific task. Those who are ordained have no better merits or qualifications than those who are not. The difference is simply whether they have been commissioned to a task. Then, once that task is finished, the ordainment should be removed or renewed. If ordainment is understood in this light, it seems that both men and women should be able to partake, because all have been assigned tasks.

I believe that the community should be unified. Division between Churches and denominations only makes Christians the laughing stock of the world. Until Churches start teaming up between denominations, God's house will be divided. For this reason, I am also in favor of large Churches. When there are cities with a large number of small Churches, each Church tends to fight the other Churches, have disagreements within themselves, and spend so much money on paying a minister and having a building that they are never able to meet the community's needs. If Church members would simply swallow their pride and combine Churches, then some of the money wasted on paying for empty Church buildings could be used for evangelism and feeding the hungry instead\!<a href="#foot70" id="tex2html13"><sup>5.4</sup></a>

# <span id="SECTION00650000000000000000">The Relationship Between the Community and the World</span>

## <span id="SECTION00651000000000000000">Christians and Non-Christians</span>

Christians have a mission to fulfill - to preach the gospel to all the world. In order to do that, Christians must live in the world and know what the world's ways are. However, they should make sure that they do not live as the world does. A Christians life should be lived in response to God, not in response to the world. However, a Christian's communication with the world should relate to its current situation. However, Christians should also not become too involved with making the Gospel relevant and scientifically pure that its power is missed. Paul said that he came not using good arguments but with the power of God. If I argue you into the kingdom, then an argument can pull you right back out. But if God's power compels you to come in, there is nothing that could ever make you leave. These are the balances that the Christian must walk - be relevant, but not ignore God's simple power, and be in the world, but be untouched by the world.

## <span id="SECTION00652000000000000000">Christians and the Political World</span>

I have tried to find for many years the relationship between Christianity and politics. Which rules should apply to both Christians and non-Christians. Which rules are specific to the community of faith? It is hard to know. Therefore, at the risk of sounding apathetic I will say this - matters of politics are not matters of faith. By this, I do not mean that Christians should not be politically active. Rather, when Christians disagree over political matters, they should not let that impact their spiritual relationship with each other. This is because the New Testament focuses on how the Christian community behaves, and never deals with whether or not the community should impose its rules on the secular world. The New Testament never speaks of how government should behave. It speaks of governing officials and their behavior, but never of the laws themselves. Therefore, since the Bible does not define the relationship between Christian ethics and governmental policy, neither should the Christian. Christians should follow their political views, but never mistake political views for religious views. For example, I believe that abortion is wrong. I also believe that abortion should be illegal. I have friends that believe that abortion is wrong, but believe that abortion should be legal. My friends speak out against abortion at every moment, but they do not think that the laws should enforce that. So, we have no faith disagreements, only political ones. Even though I am planning on voting against my friends in the next election, that decision has nothing to do with their relationship with Christ.<a href="#foot74" id="tex2html14"><sup>5.5</sup></a> The law is for the unrighteous - if we succeed in winning the world for Christ and everyone is in the kingdom of God, our political outlook will be a moot point.
