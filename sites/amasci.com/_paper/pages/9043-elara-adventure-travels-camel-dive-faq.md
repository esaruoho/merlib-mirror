<td height="327" data-valign="top" width="500"><p><br />
<strong><br />
</strong>Q: What's included in the cost of my tour package?<br />
A:  The price includes:</p>
<ul>
<li>Round-tip airfare between New York and Cairo</li>
<li>Inter-Egypt airfares</li>
<li>Group-based ground transprotation</li>
<li>Five-star/first-class hotel accommodations</li>
<li>Most meals and gratuities (drinks not included)</li>
<li>Camel rental</li>
<li>Guide, dive leader and support crew services</li>
<li>Scuba equipment rentals</li>
<li>Private entrance into the Great Pyramid</li>
<li>A guided tour of the Egyptian Museum</li>
<li>Walking tours of Old Cairo</li>
<li>A traditional Egyptian folklore show</li>
<li>A shopping excursion to the Khan El Khalili Bazaar</li>
<li>A Nile River dinner cruise</li>
</ul>
<p>Q:  What's not included?<br />
A:   The following:</p>
<ul>
<li>Transportation to and from our New York departure point</li>
<li>Gratuities and transportation for services provided on a personal basis</li>
<li>Tipping for crew and/or guide</li>
<li>Items of a personal nature such as telephone calls, faxes, laundry, room service, meals and/or snacks not eaten with the group, viewer-paid TV programs, etc.</li>
<li>Beer or wine with meals</li>
<li>Optional training courses and dive certificate programs</li>
</ul>
<p>Q:  What is the camel riding portion of the package like?<br />
A:  Contrary to popular opinion, camels are quite agreeable and even-tempered. As a result, riding on the back of one of these sturdy, tireless animals doesn't require a great amount of skill. After receiving a few basic instructions and practicing for a short period, most people find traveling by camel to be a great novelty and a great deal of fun! Your itinerary includes a total of three site transfers by camel.<br />
<em><br />
</em>Q:  How much time we will spend on the camel?<br />
A:  The time you'll spend traveling by camel will vary. Your minimum ride will be one hour; your miximum ride, three hours.</p>
<p>Q:  Is this trip dangerous?<br />
A:  There are always risk involved  in using animals as a mode of transportation. Although camels are 'mild mannered,' animal behavior, in general, is never completely predictable. In addition, there are inherent risks taken when scuba diving--even under the best of all possible conditions. Your tour leaders and guides make safety a first priority, but cannot guarantee that accidents won't happen. If you have specific safety concerns, please contact us.</p>
<p>Q:  What are the Egyptian people like?<br />
A:  In general, you will find them to be unusually kind, generous and fun-loving people. As a result, they are one of Egypt's greatest treasures.</p>
<p>Q: What will the weather be like?<br />
A:  The weather along the coast of the Red Sea from October through April is mild with temperatures ranging from the lower 80's during the day to the lower 50's at night. The majority of days are bright and sunny. Evening temperatures during the winter can reach into the mid 40's. Occasional strong winds can blow, especially in the spring. In the event of severe winds, support vehicles will be available to transport divers to safety.</p>
<p>Q: What will we eat?<br />
A: You will eat traditional Egyptian meals which are typically simple, natural and delicious. Staples of your diet will include breads, rice, cooked beans, rice, eggs, cheese, hommus, falafel, and beef or chicken kabobs. You should remember, however, not to eat any uncooked vegetables or fruits which have not been or cannot be peeled.</p>
<p>Q: Will we be safe?<br />
A: While in Egypt, your personal security is assured by members of the Egyptian Tourist Police. For additional information on personal safety issues, please contact the U.S. Department of State Overseas Citizen Services in Wahington, D.C. at #202.467.5525<br />
</p></td>
