# North American Conference of Daylight Lodges

*Source page: `~daylight/htmls/nacdl/nacdlhist.html`*

### A brief history of the North American Conference of Daylight Lodges

During the summer of 1986, the **EXPO 86** World's Fair was held in Vancouver, British Columbia, and several Masonic Lodges in the city were asked by the Grand Lodge of British Columbia to hold emergent meetings so that Brethren visiting from around the world could attend a meeting.\

This included Meridian Lodge No. 108, the province's only Daylight Lodge. However the Master of the Lodge, Ben Robinson, insisted that Meridian was a daylight Lodge and therefore the meeting should be during the daytime. Facing much opposition to the idea, during a visit to Daylight Lodge No. 232 in Seattle, Washington he mentioned this and that he felt like just saying, "forget it." Daylight having shared forty years of daytime Masonic fellowship exchanging visits with Meridian Lodge, through its secretary urged him to plan a daytime meeting and offered to send announcements about this meeting to a list of some thirty daytime Lodges he knew about.\

Still faced with many questioning Brethren even in his own Lodge, Robinson went ahead and planned a daytime meeting for the summer. Letters were sent out by Daylight Lodge and the replies slowly cam in and the worrying began about the success of the venture and perhaps that it would be a failure.\

On the day of the planned meeting, at 10:00 in the morning the Grand Master of British Columbia and his suite of officers, visiting Brethren which included the Senior Grand Warden, Ernest Hazelwood, and Grand Secretary, Ron Brinkman, of New Mexico, both charter members of Zia Daylight Lodge No. 77 in Albuquerque, Past District Deputy of the Grand Master Coe Tug Morgan from Daylight Lodge No. 232 in Seattle and nearly 200 other Master Masons from as far away as South Africa, helped Worshipful Master Robinson open Lodge.\

During the meeting, the most successful of all the emergent meetings during **EXPO 86**, Robinson expressed the dream that there could be a meeting of daylight Masons on a regular basis but no more thought was given to his comment as this was a special event tied to a World's Fair.\

In 1989 Ernest Hazelwood became Grand Master of New Mexico and Zia Daylight Lodge No. 77 seeking to honor him, caused Robinson's dream to come true. Zia Daylight invited all the daylight Lodges they could contact, to come to Albuquerque and join in honoring their Grand Master. During this two day event it was decided to form an informal organization of daylight masons and the name selected, for lack of a better one, was **North American Conference of Daylight Lodges**.\

Officers were then elected, or in the somewhat usual Masonic fashion were "railroaded," with M:. W:. Ernie Hazelwood as president, W:. Ben Robinson from Meridian Lodge in Vancouver as vice president and V:. W:. Coe Tug Morgan from Daylight Lodge in Seattle as General Secretary.\

High Noon Lodge No. 38 in Delaware hosted the second official meeting of the Conference in fall of 1992. That Lodge's Past Master David Marinelli had served as Grand Master of Deleware in 1989 and it was intended to honor him. Sadly he died of cancer shortly before the meeting held in Newport, Delaware with many distinguished members of the Craft in attendance including the Grand Master of Delaware and the District of Columbia. At this meeting M:. W:. Brother Hazelwood established a tradition of opening a Lodge meeting by placing Brothers from different Grand Lodges in the several stations and places and seeing how things were different yet the same from Grand Lodge to Grand Lodge.\

The second meeting proved a great success and prompted Daylight Lodge No. 542 in Tulsa, Oklahoma to invite the Conference to meet in that city in October 1995. Daylight Lodge's invitation was accepted with great pleasure and by accepting this invitation, the Conference members established a custom of moving as much as possible, from East to West and trying to meet every third year.\

Daylight Lodge No. 542 Oklahoma host of a highly successful Conference meeting, is the home of the famous Oklahoma Indian Degree Team. At this meeting more formal bylaws were adopted incorporating many of the customs already in practice and setting out officers to consist of a President, 1st and 2nd Vice President and a General Secretary. The newly created position of 2nd Vice President would be the General Chairman of the next Conference meeting.\

The Tulsa meeting also saw the first attendance of a different contingent of Canadian Daylight Masons. These Brothers were from the Province of Ontario, home of about half a dozen daytime Lodges. And an invitation from Merrimac Valley Daylight Lodge in North Andover, Massachusetts to meet in New England, October 8, 9, and 10, 1998 was accepted.\

The present officers of the North American Conference of Daylight Lodges are M:. W:. Ernest Hazelwood, New Mexico as President, W:. Ben Robinson, British Columbia as 1st Vice President, W:. Jack Chapman, Massachusetts as 2nd Vice President and V:. W:. Coe Tug Morgan, Washington as General Secretary.\

In the Pacific Northwest of the U.S. and West Coast of Canada there are at least seven daytime Lodges. The oldest of these is Daylight Lodge No. 232 in Seattle, chartered in 1920, was and continues to be a Lodge largely associated with the arts. Started by entertainers, over its seventy-five years of successful existence the fields of theatre and music have provided nearly 60% of the presiding officers. In Vancouver, British Columbia, Meridian Lodge No. 108 was chartered in 1922 and served many of the same community of Masons, who working in the field of entertainment, could not attend Masonic meetings at night and inspired by St. Cecile Lodge in New York City and Daylight in Seattle, organized the Lodge.\

In Portland, Oregon a Lodge called Midday No. 188 was started at the same period and operated successfully for over fifty years before consolidating with Sherwood Lodge No. 164 in the community of Sherwood south of Portland. Sherwood was one of those Lodges which finding itself falling with evening meetings, took the bold step of changing to a mid-morning meeting and seeking as new members, retired Masons for surrounding communities. its great success prompted Midday Lodge which had found that it could not maintain its existence in downtown Portland location, to unite with Sherwood to form what is now known as Sherwood-Midday Lodge.\

Just a few miles north of Portland in Vancouver Washington, in the early 1990's, Ridgefield Lodge No. 237 after a number of years trying to maintain a Lodge with declining membership because of few new members and advancing age of the current members, made the same change to become Ridgefield Daylight Lodge and since then has grown from meetings with attendance that was barely sufficient to hold a legal Masonic meeting to one of the most successful Lodges in the area.\

In the City of Renton, Washington on the southern edge of Seattle and home of the Boeing 737 and 727, Tyee Lodge No. 115 also changed to a daytime Lodge.\

The Most Worshipful Grand Lodge of Washington, Prince hall has at least two daytime Lodges. True Resolution Lodge No. 16 one of the oldest in the Grand Lodge meets in Vancouver, British Columbia, and Olympus No. 45 serves the needs of Prince Hall Daylight Masons in Seattle, Washington.\

The respective Grand Lodges have recognized the success of their daylight Lodges and the leadership the members have shown. In Washington, for example, Daylight Lodge No. 232 has produced three Grand Chaplains, three Grand Organists, four Deputies of the Grand Master, a Grand Historian and Grand Orator and a number of Committeemen, an affiliated Past Grand Master and Prince Hall's Olympus Lodge No. 45 claim the current Grand Secretary and Grand Treasurer. In the Grand Lodge of Colorado, the small Centurion Daylight Lodge No. 195 in the small community of Monument has given that Grand Lodge two Grand Masters in the short 17 years it has existed.\

The success of Daylight Lodges can bee seen in the Grand Lodge of New Mexico where the last five new Lodges chartered have been daytime Lodges and Grand Lodge of Canada (in the Province of Ontario) where not less than seven new daytime Lodges have been started in the last five years.\

Daytime Masonic Lodges not only retain the tradition of operating at the time real Masons worked, but seem to be providing a much needed place for older retired Masons who have no desire to spend nights driving to meetings in the dark and many younger Masons to be mentioned in Masonic ways by older and experienced Brothers who by showing their daring inclined to somewhat progressive and forward looking ideas. It is these Masonic Brethren from around the United States and Canada who formed the North American Conference of Daylight Lodges to pass along the message of the success of daytime Lodges.\

Those interested in additional information of daylight Masonic Lodges around the country and who cannot find on in their immediate area, can contact the General Secretary of the North American Conference of Daylight Lodges, Coe Tug Morgan, 3952 Evanston Ave. North, Seattle, Washington 98103-7709, telephone 206-632-2970 or via Daylight Lodge's e-mail at daylight@eskimo.com.\
