# Frequently Asked Questions

*Source page: `~robertf/monorail/faq.html`*

> **FAQ\
> (Frequently Asked Questions)**\
> \
>
> **Q. Why monorail instead of light rail?**\
> **A.** There are a number of reasons why monorail is a better solution. Briefly, monorail is faster, is safer, gives a smoother ride, is quieter, is less expensive, doesn't split neighborhoods like a railway does, doesn't require nearly as much right-of-way, results in far less loss of homes and businesses along the route, and should have better results in reducing traffic congestion. For more information see the rest of this site, especially the [table comparing monorail and light rail](compare.html).\
> \
> **Q. Why monorail instead of a subway?**\
> **A.** Actually I think a subway is better than monorail. It has most of the advantages of monorail plus it is hidden underground. The view from a monorail is certainly better than tunnel walls, but that is not an important argument. The subway killer is the extremely high cost!\
> \
> **Q. Why monorail instead of express busses?**\
> **A.** Actually express busses are an excellent alternative, and I think there should be many express bus routes to supplement monorail, along with local bus service. So why monorail at all? Because busses can still get delayed in traffic and monorail has a higher capacity. I think monorail should be used for main trunk routes with regional express busses serving medium-traffic routes.\
> \
> **Q. Why will the Green Line go only to 15th Ave. N.W. and N.W. 85th St.? Wouldn't it make more sense to continue on to Northgate?**\
> **A.** Yes! It would make a lot more sense to continue on to Northgate. In fact early planning showed it going to Northgate. But my understanding is that as the planning progressed, ETC decided to end the line at 85th in order to avoid too high of an initial cost, and they now plan a future extension to Northgate and Lake City.\
> \
> **Q. Why would the extension to Northgate make sense?**\
> **A.** It is only about three miles further to extend the monorail to Northgate, and less than two more miles to Lake City Way. I would like to see the extension follow Holman Road, N. 105th St., Northgate Way to about Ashworth Ave. N., then down the hill to the north end of North Seattle Community College, then across the Interstate 5 freeway to the Northgate Transit Center, then north on 5th Ave. N.E., then along Northgate Way to Lake City Way N.E. There would be a station at Aurora to connect with busses on Aurora and hopefully a future monorail along Aurora, a station at North Seattle Community College, a major station at the Northgate Transit Center to connect with many Metro and Sound Transit busses, a station for Northgate Mall and other businesses in the area, and a station near Lake City Way and N.E. 115th St. to connect with a proposed monorail that would go up Lake City Way after coming from Downtown Seattle by way of the University District. The extension would greatly increase the usefulness of the Green Line. It would add important destinations at the north end of the line, increasing overall ridership. The parking at the Northgate Transit Center would be the only real significant park-and-ride lot along the line, and should attract many drivers from the north that currently drive Downtown. It should better demonstrate the capabilites and advantages of a larger monorail system.\
> \
> **Q. How much longer before the Northgate/Lake City extension could be built?**\
> **A.** I think it could be finished along with the initial part of the Green Line! The voters have approved a 14-mile first phase of the Green Line from West Seattle to Crown Hill, ending at 15th Ave. N.W. and N.E. 85th St. It is estimated that it will take four or five years to finish that line. But the construction of the Seattle Center Monorail took only about nine months. I figure that most of the Green Line can be built in less than two years. What will take a long time is the two bridges — an alteration to the West Seattle Freeway bridge across the Duwamish Waterway and a brand new high-level crossing of the Ship Canal between Interbay and Ballard. The Northgate/Lake City extension has no significant bridges. If the Green Line is approved this November, and if an effort is made to get the Northgate/Lake City extension on the ballot in the next general election, and if it passes, then construction of the extension should take no more than two years and it would probably be completed around the same time as the two bridges on the initial Green Line project.\
> \
> **Q. What changes or additions would you like to see?**\
> **A.** Very briefly, these are some things I would like:
>
> 1.  Change the routing in Lower Queen Anne and Belltown to avoid the loop through or around Seattle Center, and go directly from Key Arena south on 1st Ave. N. and 2nd Ave. One of the most important purposes of the monorail is high speed transit from north Seattle, and the extra curves and length of the proposed route will add several minutes to the run time. My proposal also eliminates controversies over aesthetics in the Seattle Center and preservation of the existing Seattle Center Monorail.
> 2.  Use the Alweg design used for the Seattle Center Monorail, so the two lines are compatible and because a wider beam increases stability and smooth riding.
> 3.  Use the same fare structure as Metro Transit, and have transfers interchangeable between systems, so that bus riders can transfer to the monorail without extra payment, and vice versa. This will lower per-passenger revenues for the monorail, but should greatly increase ridership. One of the prime purposes of the monorail is to get people out of cars and into public transit. Inconvenient fare structures will discourage people from riding, especially those who have to transfer.
> 4.  Extend the line to Northgate and Lake City.
>
> **Q. The Seattle Monorail Project is only for Seattle. Don't we really need a regional system to get more people off the roads?**\
> **A.** Yes, we really do need a regional system. The people of Seattle, fed up with failure of state and regional politicians to build a suitable regional high-speed mass transit system, took things into their own hands and have voted for the start of a city-wide system. I believe that in the long run, Sound Transit, the regional transit agency, should give up on their light rail plans and build a regional monorail system. If they decide to do so, then I think the Seattle system should be incorporated into Sound Transit&;rsquo;s operations. In the meantime Seattle will go ahead with their own system.\
> \
> **Q. Will it be easy to transfer from buses to monorail and vice versa?**\
> **A.** The current system on Metro buses is that when somebody pays their fare, they can receive a transfer that permits the rider to transfer to another bus and pay no additional fare. There is some controversy about whether bus riders will have to pay a higher fare on the monorail.\
> \
> I believe that one of the main goals of the monorail should be to encourage people to use public transit. I believe it important to integrate the monorail into the bus system so that people can easily and smoothly transfer between modes of transportation as easily and for the same fare as if tranferring from one bus to another. Furthermore, I think the monorail fare should be the same as the bus fare.\
> \
> It will be a few years before the monorail is operational, and I hope that these details can be worked out before then.\
