<td width="100%"><h1 id="guitar-columns">GUITAR COLUMNS</h1>
<p>On the Guitar Column page, we try to cover a lot of ground for guitarists of all levels. Topics, most suggested by emails from our readers, range from theory (basic and intermediate) and technique to soloing to song writing and arrangement to alternate tunings to coping with the emtional ups and downs of guitar playing itself.</p>

<h2 id="where-to-start">Where To Start</h2>
<p>If you're a beginner who wants to know more about theory, we suggest you start with latest theory series which begins with <a href="../columns/2000/20000801.html">The Musical Genome Project</a>. If you're an intermediate player who wants to learn about alternate tuning and what you can do with it, check out <a href="../columns/2000/20000404.html">On The Tuning Awry</a>. And of course there's also the wonderful series which teaches you how you can learn songs right from the recordings instead of relying on TAB. <a href="../columns/2000/20000103.html">Happy New Ear</a> is the first piece of that set.</p>
<p><a href="beginner1.html" class="featureTitleLink">Absolute Beginner Part 1: Chords</a><br />
So...you've just got your first guitar. Now what do you do? Well, if you haven't got a clue at all, perhaps we can help you. GuitarNoise welcomes you to the first of our Absolute Beginner articles. It is our hope to get you started playing the guitar as quickly and as painlessly as possible.</p>
<p><a href="2001/20011018.html" class="featureTitleLink">Your Very Own Rosetta Stone: A Guide To Reading Musical Notation - Part One</a><br />
A quick guide to reading music notation. This is something that many of you have asked for. It is also something that any and every serious musician needs.</p>
<p><a href="2003/20030215.html" class="featureTitleLink">Timing Is Everything: A Guide To Reading Musical Notation - Part Two</a><br />
You know what notes the note is, now it's time to learn what else music notation can teach you. Turns out it's pretty vital information! This is the long awaited sequel to "Your Very Own Rosetta Stone."</p>
<p><a href="../beginner/index.html" class="featureTitleLink">Easy Songs For Beginners</a><br />
If you already have a grasp of guitar basics (holding your guitar, tuning it) then the next step is to start building a repetoire of songs. This series of lessons will teach you a bit of music theory and technique while you learn to play some pretty easy and well known songs. For best results you really should begin with the first lesson and work your way up.</p>
<p><a href="../intermediate/index.html" class="featureTitleLink">Songs For Intermediates</a><br />
What we’re going to do with these lessons is take the next logical step in moving from being a strict beginner to being a well-rounded guitarist. This will cover a lot of different topics. If you’ve read any of the <em>Easy Songs For Beginners</em> pieces, you already know that I prefer to teach lessons by using specific songs as examples.</p>
