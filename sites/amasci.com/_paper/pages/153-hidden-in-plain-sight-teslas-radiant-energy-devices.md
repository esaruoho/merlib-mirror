> > <h4 id="electrostatic-hovercraft-force-limits-for-a-giant-lifter">ELECTROSTATIC HOVERCRAFT: force limits for a giant "lifter"</h4>
> > <blockquote>
> > Assume 1-atm, max surface e-field of 3MV/M<br />
> > C = farads/cc = 8.9e-14<br />
> > E = energy stored per cc at max field = 4.01e-4 joule<br />
> > F = mech. force to store E with work over 1cm = 4.01e-3 newton<br />
> > F = surface lifting force on one M^2 = 40.1 newton = 9.0lbs<br />
> > F = lift produced by 50ft disk = 1640 lbs<br />
> > &#10;</blockquote>
> > The above value is for ideal conditions: a permeable wire grid fuselage with charged-exhaust reaction force only, and no aerodynamic lift. If instead we used a solid fuselage shaped like a Frisbee or like an icecream cone, perhaps Coanda effect would give us far higher thrust, much like replacing a 1950s-era narrow-aperture turbojet engine with a modern turbofan. But that ignores a fascinating possibility: active suppression of normal gas-breakdown.<br />
