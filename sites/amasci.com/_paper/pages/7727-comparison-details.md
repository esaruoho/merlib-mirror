# Road Atlas Routing Programs

## A Comparison

##### *Last update this page 03/13/96 23:34*

This page describes the [comparison matrix](compare.htm). You might find it useful to display the matrix in a separate window while going through this page. Also, the contents of the matrix are on this page in case you cannot view the matrix (tables are not supported by all browsers).

The matrix has the following columns:

- Feature: an item or property of the product.
- Atlas: Microsoft's Road Atlas route planning program.
- Sts: Street atlases for each product.
- Camp: Microsoft's Destination Campground product.
- Golf: Microsoft's Destination Golf product.
- Europe: Microsoft's Destination Europe product.
- Ski: Microsoft's Destination Ski product.
- Pro: Microsoft's Automap Pro product.
- MapNGo: DeLorme's route planning program.
- Phone: DeLorme's phone number addition to their product line.
- Trip: Rand McNally's TripMaker route planning program. (no input)
- AAA: their products (no input)

The individual features are broken out in sections below:\

## Work w/o CD

Do you need the CD to run the product, or just for certain features?

**<u>Microsoft</u>**

**Automap Road Atlas** can be setup in one of two ways:

Minimal hard disk space then you must always run with the CD (not positive, but seem to recall this mode from setup).

Normal where all routing and places information is on the hard disk. If you want to see the relief map or pictures of places or expanded information on places, the CD is needed.

**Automap Streets** can be setup with the CD needed all the time in order to minimize hard disk space, or selected cities (regions) can be loaded to the hard disk for faster response. If you go outside these regions, the CD is then needed. This feature is useful if you want to take some cities with you on your laptop that does not have a CD.

**<u>Delorme</u>**

The CD is needed all the time for **Map-N-Go**. Probably true for **Street Atlas** and **Phone Search** as well.

## Work with Road Atlas

Will the Street product (or other) work with the main route planning program; or are they separate.

**<u>Microsoft</u>**

**Automap Streets** and **Automap Road Atlas** merge into the same program if you buy both products. You switch between one or the other, but you cannot display both at the same time. You cannot route within **Streets**.

**Destination Campground** is integrated into Road Atlas. It provides extra places you can see and route to.

## Work with Streets

Will the Road Atlas product (or other) work with the city streets program; or are they separate.

**<u>Microsoft</u>**

**Automap Streets** and **Automap Road Atlas** merge into the same program if you buy both products. You switch between one or the other, but you cannot display both at the same time. You cannot route within Streets.

**Destination Campground** does not have any functionally within **Streets**. But, you can get an address and look it up in **Streets**.

**<u>DeLorme</u>**

**Phone Search** and **Street Atlas** appear to be integrated. Apparently you can look up an individual and have the address pinpointed on the the **Street Atlas**.

## Route Planning

This is aimed at the City Street and other accessory programs. Can you plan a route through city streets? My guess at this time is none of them. However, if you want to give someone a map to your house, you can probably copy the map to **Paint** then draw a route.

**<u>Microsoft</u>**

**Destination Campground** is indicated as having routing as it is just another table within **Automap Road Atlas**, and you can route to the new places. I would guess that **Golf** and **Ski** have similar capabilities.

## Itinerary

This is tabular directions of how to get there, what cities you pass through, what time you will be at each point, how many miles, etc. Some products may just show this on screen, but not let you print it. A yes in this field means you can print it.

For an example of **Automap's** itinerary, you will find one about half way through the [sample route](Sample_Trip.htm).

**<u>Microsoft</u>**

**Automap Road Atlas** will let you print a detailed itinerary and you can include points of interest within a selected distance from your route. **Destination Campgrounds** you have routed to will have the directions on how to get to the campground.

**<u>Delorme</u>**

An itinerary is displayed on screen, but you cannot print it.

## Strip Maps

If you have ever ordered AAA's TripTik, this is what is implied: show your route as a series of short maps each covering 20 to 200 miles. They have expanded detail of smaller cities, points of interest, travel times and distances.

**<u>Microsoft</u>**

**No strip maps. But, you may expand any desired section of your trip and print it.**

**<u>DeLorme</u>**

They print out a very detailed strip map and it can contain lodging information and points of interest. However, there are two problems:

A trip of 1500 to 2000 miles could produce 30+ pages of maps. You can select what pages you want to print, but you do not know what a page contains until you print it.

The orientation of each strip map is not intuitive. Knowing which way is north may not be obvious.

## Optimization

Will the product optimize a route for you? If you say you want to go from NYC to LA, Boston and San Francisco; can you get it to suggest the route should be NYC to Boston to San Francisco to LA? And, if it had optimization, can you disable it?

I think most all products route you as entered and do not optimize. **Automap Pro** does have an optimize option, as it was designed for the traveling salesman.

## Stop Over

Between your start point and end point, how many intermediate places can be on your route and how may you specify the time to stay at the intermediate point? This will be specified as a number (or ? mark) for the number of stop over points; then a series of letters to indicate options:

- **T** - time of stop over
- **N** - spend the night
- **S** - start time to resume the trip

**<u>Microsoft</u>**

**Automap Road Atlas** offers 25 stop-overs and all you can specify is the length of time to stay (23 hr. 59 min., max). If your standard day is 8 to 5, and if you want to go through Dallas, spend the night, then leave at 10 A.M. you have to play tricks:

1.  Do the route and find out what time you arrive in Dallas;
2.  Re-specify the route with lay over time in Dallas as the time needed to reach 5PM. It will then have you stop there for the night.
3.  For the next morning, specify another stopover in Dallas of two hours. This will keep you there until 10 A.M.

**Automap Pro** offers a wide variety of options.

**<u>DeLorme</u>**

**Map-N-Go** offers 50 stopovers (I think they term way points). I think it is like Automap where you can only specify a stopping time.

## Daytime Preferences

What hours of the day would you like to travel? How often would you like rest stops? What is the maximum distance you would like to travel in a day?

**<u>Microsoft</u>**

**Automap Road Atlas** lets you specify the desired start of day and end of day. It will terminate a day's travel near the ending hour in a city/town nearby. The trip will then resume at the desired start time the next day. If I want to go long or short one day, I have to play games.

To go long one day, I have to specify preferences to cover the earliest and latest day. Then I have to play games with the stopover. Arriving early for a night stopover requires killing time at the stopover. Leaving late on a morning requires traveling to the stopover <u>again</u> and staying a while.

**Automap Pro** has a variety of options.

**<u>DeLorme</u>**

No time preferences.

## Rest Breaks

This is another variation of Daytime preferences.

If I am going to travel 650 miles on an Interstate with a speed limit of 65 MPH (and I don't speed), will I get there in 10 hours. Of course not! I am going to stop for gas, rest, lunch. Maybe my average speed is 55 MPH. Does the product let me set the real speed I will travel on an interstate then compensate for stops to get the real arrival time? Or, do have to guess and set average speeds with stops for differing roads?

**<u>Microsoft</u>**

**Automap** does not account for rest/lunch stops or gas stops. For me, averaging. 55 on interstates gives a good average for an all day trips. **Automap Pro** may have provisions for rest breaks.

**<u>Others</u>**

As far as I know, no other vendors offer this feature.

## Exits

Do the products list the exit numbers off of the interstates?

Neither **Automap Road Atlas** or **Streets**; nor **Map-N-Go** or **Street Atlas** give exit numbers. **Destination Campground** has exit numbers in it's directions to get to the campground. These appear in **Automap's** itinerary when routing to a campground.

No information on any other products.

## Avoids

What happens if the chosen route sends you over some roads you do not want to travel (too difficult, road work, out of the way, not a good choice)? Can you avoid the route?

Codes are:

- **R** - Roads or road segments
- **A** - Areas

**<u>Microsoft</u>**

**Automap Road Atlas** allows both roads and areas. If the LA basin area is indicated as an avoid, a route from Palm Springs to Santa Barbara will divert you to the upper desert.

**<u>DeLorme</u>**

Has no avoids. Sometimes have to play games with stopovers (which may take you out of your way).

## Preferences

Can you specify routing preferences, e.g., shortest, most scenic, quickest.

**<u>Microsoft</u>**

**Automap** allows calculating routes for Quickest, Shortest, Preferred, and Alternates.

Quickest is determined by the average speed you assign to various road types (interstate, country, etc.) It then assumes you travel this speed on this type of road. It does not know about congestion or rush hours.

Preferred is determined by a weighting bar you set for various road types and ferries. It will try to keep you or the types of roads you prefer. I did one route from coast to coast where we wanted only small roads and ferries. It did what we wanted and took us miles out of our way to avoid interstates and satisfy our desires.

Alternates: for any route, you can ask for alternates. It will find routes of similar time/mileage that might be acceptable.

**<u>DeLorme</u>**

They offer features similar to **Automap**.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Speeds

Can you specify the speeds you will travel on differing types of roads.

**<u>Microsoft</u>**

**Automap** lets you set your estimated speeds for regular, slow, urban and slow urban for the following road types: Interstates, Freeways+tollways, divided highways, US and state highways, County roads, and others.

It does not know about rush hour or areas of congestion; just the types listed in the above paragraph.

**<u>DeLorme</u>**

They offer features similar to **Automap**.

## Mileage

Can you state the gas mileage of your vehicle? Can you state the cost of Gasoline?

**<u>Microsoft</u>**

**Automap** allows the fuel cost, tank capacity, how much is in the tank at journey start, mileage for city and highway, and at how low the tank can get before a refill is indicated. Plus, you can work in gallons or liters and in miles or kilometers. I think **Automap Pro** has similar features.

**<u>DeLorme</u>**

I don't think they offer such features.

## Gas Ups

Does it tell you where you should gas-up? What flexibility do you have?

**<u>Microsoft</u>**

Given your mileage, tank size, and how low to go; tells you where to gas up. But, if you decide to gas up at the city you spent the night, the whole program is blown. You cannot reset, unless you re-compute from the new gas up point.

**<u>DeLorme</u>**

Has no such offering.

## Rest Stops

Does it show roadside rests? Can you route to them?

Codes used in the matrix:

- ov - just an overlay. Can see them but not route to them.

**<u>Microsoft</u>**

**Automap** just shows the rest stops as an overlay; and there is no information on the rest stop's facilities. You cannot route to a rest stop. I have done trips where I planned to spend the night in a rest stop and I had to specify the nearest town for the stopover.

**<u>DeLorme</u>**

I think they are the same as Automap.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Hotels and Motels

Do you have information on them? Can you route to them? How about costs?

**<u>Microsoft</u>**

Double clicking on the state symbol will give menus for information on demographics, lodging, geography, traffic laws, etc. This requires the CD.

Lodging is just the 800 numbers for hotel/motel reservation.

**<u>DeLorme</u>**

I think they give lodging information for each city requested.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Sites of Interest

Does the product point out sights you might want to see, and how do you specify same? And, there are different ways to indicate sites of interest: a) icons on the map; b) pictures; c) audio descriptions.

**<u>Microsoft</u>**

In **Automap**, sites can be mountain peaks, amusement parks, recreation areas, campgrounds, etc. And, you can specify the distance from your route in which to accept the sites. In addition, you can globally specify the types of sites you wish to see on the entire map. Plus, you can turn on little camera icons that produce images of selected sites when double clicked.

**<u>DeLorme</u>**

**Map-N-Go** is more into the multimedia. Some of their pictures also have sound, and I would assume they have more pictures. Not sure how they compare on the non-multimedia points of interest.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Relief Map

Can it show an elevation map?

**Automap** and **Map-N-Go** have a relief map feature. **Automap**'s has a better feeling of elevation differences. See the [sample map](samples.htm) of the Yosemite region to see an **Automap** sample. The **Automap** resolution is only a few miles. When you blow up a small region, you start seeing pixels. The CD is required to see relief features.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Save Routes

Can you save a route, then come back later to refine it?

Yes on all so far. The older Automap did not have this provision.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Vehicles

How many vehicles can be specified? Can I plan a route and compare the cost if I took a 4 cylinder subaru verses an 8 cylinder pickup pulling a 30' trailer?

Don't know about other vendors. **Automap** only lets you specify one vehicle and it cannot be affixed to a journey. If I change it and do a route, I have to change it back when returning to a journey with a different vehicle. I think **Automap Pro** allows multiple vehicles.
