&#10;<p>In the past I've spent time as an embedded designer and software engineer, consulted on textbooks, lectured about electricity education, designed science projects for kids, and built physics exhibits while running the electronics dept. at the Museum of Science in Boston.<br />
</p>
<p>Today I spend my free time running SCIENCE HOBBYIST, a large website for amateur science and science education. You'll also find me hanging out on the <a href="http://lists.nau.edu/archives/phys-l.html" rel="nofollow">PHYS-L</a> physics education <a href="http://physicsed.buffalostate.edu/PHYS-L/index.html" rel="nofollow">forum</a>, holding forth about various ways to <a href="http://amasci.com/elect/elefaq.html" rel="nofollow">explain</a> all things <a href="http://amasci.com/miscon/elect.html" rel="nofollow">electrical.</a><br />
</p>
<p>I was born in the US but I was raised on the <a href="http://goo.gl/maps/hLZPG" rel="nofollow">island of Guam</a>, which gave me a bit of an "outsider" viewpoint which I've carefully tried to preserve. And when you're in <a href="http://amasci.com/miscon/k6.txt" rel="nofollow">3rd grade</a> while both your parents are school teachers, it gives you a critical eye regarding the <a href="http://amasci.com/miscon/miscon.html" rel="nofollow">foibles of educators</a>. I was bitten by the science bug at an early age and had fun with a chemistry set, but I soon discovered where the REAL magic was hidden: <a href="http://amasci.com/amateur/elehob.html" rel="nofollow">hobbyist electronics.</a> Even more magical is the Black Art of <a href="http://amasci.com/emotor/mestat.html" rel="nofollow">electrostatics.</a> And to search for traces of true magic in the sciences, nothing beats actual <a href="http://amasci.com/freenrg/wbelief1.html" rel="nofollow">magic.</a></p>
