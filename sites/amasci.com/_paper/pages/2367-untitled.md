Freemasonry A Way of Life

##### Re-printed from: <u>Freemasonry A Way of Life</u>, Tacoma Scottish Rite, 817 S. Vassault St. Tacoma, WA 98465.\

**The Fraternity of Free and Accepted Masons** is the oldest, largest and most widely known fraternal organization in the world.

Volumes have been written about it. Yet, to many, Freemasonry remains a mystery.

This folder is an attempt to relate a few facts that will be informative to all and correct a few misconceptions.\

**History**

Freemasonry can trace its ancestry to the operative craft. Operative craftsmen were the cathedral builders of the middle ages. These men, because of their special knowledge and skills were permitted special travel privileges and traveled from country to country employing the secrets and skills of their craft. These Master Craftsmen developed means of recognition and identification of their work.

In the late 17<sup>th</sup> and early 18<sup>th</sup> century as Cathedral building came to an end, numbers of these operative Stone Mason Guilds or Lodges began to accept as members good men who were not Stone Masons. Thus the Lodges came to be composed almost entirely of these Accepted or Speculative Masons. From these groups Freemasonry of today had its beginning.\

**Grand Lodges**

In 1717, four Lodges of Freemasons, meeting in London, England, formed the first Grand Lodge in the world.

This Grand Lodge chartered Symbolic Lodges and Provincial Grand Lodges in many countries, including the United States.

Today, there are more than 150 Grand Lodges in free countries of the world with a membership of more than 6,000,000.

The Grand Lodge is the administrative authority in its territory, known as a jurisdiction. The Grand Lodge of Washington is the Supreme Masonic Power and Authority in this Jurisdiction.

In the United States there are more than 50 Grand Lodges. The include the 50 States and the District of Columbia. There are approximately 3,500,000 Freemasons in the Jurisdictions of the United states.\

**Masonic Lodges**

The Basic Unit of all Grand Lodges is the Masonic Lodge, often knows as the Symbolic Lodge, Blue Lodge or Craft Lodge. It is the Masonic Lodge that receives and acts upon petitions for the three degrees know as the Entered Apprentice, Fellow Craft, and the Master Mason Degrees.

There are 252 such Lodges in the Grand Jurisdiction of Washington, with a total membership of approximately 34,000.\

**What is Freemasonry**

Freemasonry is not an insurance or beneficial society. It is not organized for profit. It is not a charitable institution, although it does assist its members by means through times of hardship.

It teaches Monotheism. It teaches the "Golden Rule." It seeks to make good men better through its firm belief in the Fatherhood of God, the Brotherhood of man and the immortality of the soul.

It uses builders tools as symbols to teach the basic moral truths, thereby impressing on the minds of the members the Cardinal virtues of Brother Love, Relief and Truth, which should apply to everyday activities.\

**High ideals**

From time immemorial, Masonry has stood for the highest ideals of civilization; and wherever Masonry has flourished, these ideals have come nearest to realization and highest standards of living have been obtained. Wherever Masonry has been cast out, the standards of living have immediately gone down, civilization has deteriorated, and the freedoms of the people abolished.\

**Tenets of Freemasonry**

The tenets of Freemasonry are ethical principles that are acceptable to all good men. It teaches tolerance toward all mankind.

It is known throughout the world even behind the Iron Curtain where Masonry cannot exist.

Freemasonry proudly proclaims that it consists of men bound together by bonds of Brotherly Love and Affection.

It dictates to no man as to his belief either religious or secular. It seeks no advantage for its members through business or politics.

Freemasonry is not a forum for discussion on partisan affairs.\

**A Way of Life**

Freemasonry is kindness in the home, honestly in business, courtesy in society, fairness in work, pity and concern for the unfortunate, resistance toward evil, help for the weak, forgiveness for the penitent, love for one another, and, above all, reverence and love for God.

Freemasonry is many things, but most of all:

Freemasonry is a Way of Life!\

**Freemasonry and Religion**

Masonry is neither a religion nor a substitute for one. Some people and some churches oppose Freemasonry in the belief that Masonry is a religion and may conflict with some of their teachings. Masonry has no creed, no priesthood and no plan for salvation. Neither is it an off-spring of any church, ancient or modern. It espouses none of them nor is subservient to any. While Masonry does require a belief in a Supreme Being, each Mason worships in his own fashion according to his religious faith, whether he be Christian, Jew, Moslem, Protestant, Roman Catholic, Buddhist or Hindu.\

**Is Masonry a Secret Society?**

Contrary to what many believe, Freemasonry does not hid its existence or its membership.

There has been no attempt to conceal the purpose, aims and principles of Freemasonry. It is an organization formed and existing on the broad basis of Brotherly Love, Relief and Truth.

Its constitutions are published for the world to behold. Its rules and regulations are open for inspection.

It is true that we have modes of recognition, rites and ceremonies with which the world is not acquainted. In this regard, all human groups and institutions have private affairs. For instance, families have discussions which do not and should not concern their neighbors.\

**Are Masons Active in Politics?**

Masons do not discuss religion or political matters in their Lodge nor does any Lodge endorse candidates of any political persuasion.

As an individual however, he is urged to take an active part in civic affairs.\

**Who Can Become a Mason?**

Any adult male of good repute, of high moral character, of sound mind, who believes in God and has the usual senses of a man, especially those of hearing, seeing feeling and is capable of reading and writing English.\

**What Does Freemasonry Do for a Member?**

It does far more than can be summarized here. In short, through its teachings, it encourages a Mason to apply to his daily living the principles of Brotherly Love, Relief, Truth, Temperance, Fortitude, Prudence and Justice.

It does not interfere with the duties he owes to God, his neighbor, his family or himself, but rather by learning to understand, to live and practice these fundamental precepts, he has an opportunity for self improvement. It helps a good man become a better man, a better father, husband, brother or son. Many men prominent in local, national and world affairs are Masons.\

**Masonic Charities**

From the earliest records individual and collective charity has been an essential characteristic of the Fraternity. In addition to the many charities of the individual member and Lodges, every Grand Lodge has large scale charity establishments. In Washington, through their Grand Lodge, Masons support the Masonic Retirement Center.

Members of the Masonic "family" which are all known for their charitable work, contribute annually more than \$550,000,000.00 to charity, or more than \$1,500,000.00 per day. Masonic charity is more than giving money for some needy or worthwhile project. It is the giving of your time, your talent, your fellowship, your understanding, your praise for anyone whose needs comes to our attention in our daily lives.\

**What are the "Higher" Degrees?**

There is no higher degree in Masonry than that of Master Mason. There is considerable confusion in the minds of many due to the fact that the York Rite and Scottish Rite have additional Degrees. A Master Mason may petition either or both for membership, after which he is eligible to petition the Shrine. The degrees given in these Rites elaborate the Masonic teachings.\
