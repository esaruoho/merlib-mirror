<td width="800" data-summary="best science archives"><h2 id="explore-these-massive-time-sucking-indexes-of-coolsci">Explore these Massive, time-sucking indexes of CoolSci:</h2>
<blockquote>
<dl>
<dt><a href="http://www.boingboing.net/tag/science/" rel="nofollow">BoingBoing: Science</a></dt>
<dd>
You can read a tagged BB to remove all those distracting nonScience articles! :)
</dd>
<dt><a href="http://sprott.physics.wisc.edu/pickover/pc/realitycarnival.html" rel="nofollow">Reality Carnival</a></dt>
<dd>
Cliff Pickover's mathPhysics-besotted fracture leading to the Imaginal Realm. Wipe the wormhole powder off your knees. And don't forget/remember (choose one,) to stay away from the brown DMT.
</dd>
<dt><a href="http://science.videosift.com/" rel="nofollow">VideoSift: Science</a></dt>
<dd>
Waste your work hours watching all these upvoted-ly best videos
</dd>
<dt><a href="http://blog.makezine.com/category/science/" rel="nofollow">MAKE Magazine: science</a></dt>
<dd>
Don't limit yourself to their science projects, also take a look at the <a href="http://blog.makezine.com/" rel="nofollow">main page</a> of Makezine blog
<div style="width:320px;height:260px;margin-left:10px;margin-top:1px;float:right;">
&#10;</div>
</dd>
<dt><a href="http://www.metafilter.com/tags/science" rel="nofollow">Metafilter: Science</a></dt>
<dd>
Check out the Scientifical version of The Blue
</dd>
<dt><a href="http://www.reddit.com/r/science/" rel="nofollow">Reddit: science</a></dt>
<dd>
Redditors scientific
</dd>
<dt><a href="http://www.thenakedscientists.com/HTML/articles/" rel="nofollow">The Naked Scientists</a> from UK Cambridge U.</dt>
<dd>
Articles by guest columnists, also check out their archive of <a href="http://www.thenakedscientists.com/HTML/Shows/Archive.htm" rel="nofollow">podcasts</a>, and their <a href="http://www.thenakedscientists.com/forum/">forum</a>
</dd>
<dt><a href="http://www.twis.org/" rel="nofollow">This Week In Science</a></dt>
<dd>
Audio archives of the weekly radio show, from U. of Cal. Davis
</dd>
<dt><a href="http://hubblesite.org/newscenter/archive/" rel="nofollow">Hubble Space Telescope's Greatest Hits</a></dt>
<dd>
Lots and lots of pictures, great for desktop wallpaper. Also try <a href="http://www.nasa.gov/multimedia/imagegallery/iotd.html" rel="nofollow">NASA picture of the day</a> and <a href="http://apod.nasa.gov/apod/astropix.html">Astronomy picture of the day</a>
</dd>
<dt><a href="http://apps.exploratorium.edu/10cool/index.php" rel="nofollow">Exploratorium's</a> "Cool Sites" archive</dt>
<dd>
The Exploratorium's Learning Studio maintains a monthly "Top Ten" page for science sites. They've ferreted out the cream of webscience cool sites. Don't miss their past months' archive.
</dd>
<dt><a href="http://apod.nasa.gov/apod/astropix.html" rel="nofollow">AstronomyPicture of the Day</a> NASA archive</dt>
<dd>
Ten years of collected photos and links from the famous APOD site.

</dd>
<dt><a href="http://www.last-word.com/" rel="nofollow">The Last Word</a></dt>
<dd>
Science questions answered, from <a href="http://www.newscientist.com%0A" rel="nofollow">New Scientist</a> magazine.
</dd>
<dt><a href="http://science.slashdot.org/" rel="nofollow">SLASHDOT: Science</a></dt>
<dd>
One of the best science-blogs. Updated by thousands of compulsive science geeks!
</dd>
<dt><a href="http://www.scientificamerican.com/section.cfm?id=ask-the-experts" rel="nofollow">The Editors Selections</a></dt>
<dd>
Science hotlist from <a href="http://www.sciam.com" rel="nofollow">Scientific American</a> Magazine.
</dd>
<dt><a href="http://alife.ccp14.ac.uk/zooland/zooland/" rel="nofollow">ZOOLAND Artificial Life Resource</a></dt>
<dd>
The famous/infamous (delete one) Santa Fe Institute brings us this brainmeltingly-extensive collection of links to sites involving Artificial Life; a-life. Zerosum breakage and titfortat dealings. Simulations, animations and applet-based growings. Fractals and fishtanks and emergentboid wings ...these are a few of my favorite things!
</dd>
<dt><a href="http://www.falstad.com/mathphysics.html" rel="nofollow">Awesome Physics Applets</a> by P. Falstad</dt>
<dd>
Get lost in acoustic resonance and Electromagnetic hallucinatons. And... and... HE GETS ELECTRICITY RIGHT! Slow charge flows through always-filled pipes. Also check out <a href="http://physicsweb.org/resources/Education/Electronic_textbooks/" rel="nofollow">online textbooks</a>
</dd>
<dt><a href="http://www.cs.cmu.edu/~mwm/sci.html" rel="nofollow">Hands on Science Centers Worldwide</a></dt>
<dd>
Science Museums have the coolest websites. Here they all are, all at once. Take it slow, don't get brainburn.
</dd>
<dt><a href="http://dir.yahoo.com/Computers_and_Internet/Internet/Devices_Connected_to_the_Internet/" rel="nofollow">Interesting Devices</a> on the Net</dt>
<dd>
From Yahoo, this is an immense and venerable collection of sites with live stillcams, interactive robots, and all manner of instrumentation connected to the net. Spy on people's offices all over the world. See the coffee pot that started it all. Watch 24hr timelapses at the UW. Talk to the cat. See if the pop machine at MIT has any warm Jolt cola left.
</dd>
<dt><a href="http://sln.fi.edu/tfi/jump.html" rel="nofollow">Franklin Institute's hotlist of online exhibits</a></dt>
<dd>
A list of highly interactive Cool Science sites. Also see their <a href="http://www.fi.edu/tfi/hotlist/" rel="nofollow">monthly hotlist</a>.
</dd>
<dt><a href="http://www.yahoo.com/Science/" rel="nofollow">Yahoo's SCIENCE index</a></dt>
<dd>
Hours of surfing pleasure. If you find something in Yahoo that should be linked in Cool Science, drop me a note!
</dd>
<dt><a href="%0Ahttp://robotics.nasa.gov/archive/crw_archive.php" rel="nofollow">Cool Robot of the Week</a></dt>
<dd>
A large collection maintained by <a href="http://robotics.nasa.gov/index.php" rel="nofollow">NASA RAP</a>. With archives going <a href="http://robotics.nasa.gov/archive/robot_news.php" rel="nofollow">back to 2001</a>.
</dd>
<dt><a href="http://www.yahoo.com/Science/Museums_and_Exhibits/" rel="nofollow">SCIENCE MUSEUMS</a>, Yahoo</dt>
<dd>
More and more museum sites.
</dd>
<dt><a href="http://www.nap.edu/csarc.html" rel="nofollow">Cool Science Archive</a> from the <a href="http://www.nap.edu" rel="nofollow">Nat. Acadamy Press</a></dt>
<dd>
Also check out the NAP <a href="http://books.nap.edu/info/browse.htm" rel="nofollow">reading room</a> for all sorts of science books online. Yes, complete actual illustrated books on the internet for free. Also try some non-science at <a href="http://www.litrix.com/titles.htm" rel="nofollow">Litrix reading room</a>.
</dd>
<dt><a href="http://www.sciencefriday.com/pages/misc/hotlinks.html" rel="nofollow">Science Friday: HOTLINKS</a></dt>
<dd>
NPR Radio's TALK OF THE NATION: <a href="http://www.sciencefriday.com" rel="nofollow">SCIENCE FRIDAY</a> is not to be missed. They've also built a nice collection of eclectic science links.
</dd>
