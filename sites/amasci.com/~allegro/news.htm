Aloha! July 01

I think I finally have gotten myself back on Seattle time, though Ive been back from Hawaii for a few days. Its not just about the three-hour time difference, theres a concept called Hawaii time where everything moves slower and more laid-back. Being as laid back as I am to begin with, I almost fell over during my 6 days in Maui.  

A recent stretch of incredible stress and chaos inspired us to pack our bathing suits, sunglasses and pasty Northwest thighs and hop a plane to lazier latitudes. Wed considered Oahu, with the well-established tourist traps and big-city life of Honolulu, but since we were hoping for some R&R, we decided on Maui, instead, with its smaller, but still modern resort area of Kaanapali Beach. 

Having never been to Hawaii before, I probably should have prepared better for the flight, at least. 2 hours to San Francisco wasnt bad, but the SF-Maui flight was pretty icky. Im not a small person in the first place, so airplane seats are not my favorite place to be stuck for 5 hours. Mike also unfortunately got stuck sitting next to a man who was even more obnoxious than the Aloha shirt he was wearing. Once we finally touched down, though, it was all worth it. 

The air whooshing up through the jetway was thick and warm, and smelled like the ocean, which was about half a mile from the airport. Kahului-Wailuku, where the airport is, is the largest metropolitan area on Maui, but theres only about 50,000 people there, and it feels mostly like a small central California suburb- only surrounded by dozens of acres of sugar cane and pineapple fields. Its useful if you need a dose of civilization or have to find a mall for some reason. 

After getting our rental car (an absolute necessity unless you plan on staying in your hotel the entire time) we took the relatively quick 35- minute drive to Kaanapali. This drive gives you clear evidence that you just landed on a volcano. Through the mountains to the South, theres a stretch of road that goes through an area which is almost desert-like, and surrounded by red cliffs. It then spits you out into more vegetated area, onto a highway bordered by the water, with several surfing beaches and a great lookout point where you can see the tiny island of Molokini (actually the rim of a volcano crater sticking out of the water.) 

The closest actual town to the resort area is the cute little tourist trap of Lahaina. Formerly the seat of the Hawaiian kingdom, Lahaina now has several little historic sites, a bunch of missions, a defunct sugar mill, and- of course- a Hard Rock caf. Along the main drag of Front street, theres probably several dozen shops selling hats, t-shirts and the ubiquitous boxes of macadamia nut chocolates, but theres also a few nice galleries and cafes as well.  

We made the great choice one morning to have breakfast at a little place called Lahaina Coolers, which is also a crowded late-night hangout. The waiter was cute and flirty, and the food was great, with fresh juices and meals served on plates shaped like aloha shirts. If you can get into town early enough to find parking nearby, its a great stop. 

The hotel we chose was the Westin Maui resort. Its pricey, but we wanted the whole resort experience. The room was niceeven with just a mountain instead of ocean view. However, silly me- Id forgotten that we were doing this in the summer. Summer, techincally is the off-season for Hawaii, because few people are escaping icky winter weather at home. Rates are cheaper and places are generally less crowded. Unfortunately, its also when kids are out of school.  

Our dream of sitting quietly by one of the five free-form pools with a drink in hand, getting baked by the moist Maui sun was kind of shattered by the nine million kids running around (yes, I counted them all.) I dont mind kids when Im going to Disneyland or something, but it was a bit disconcerting to see so many of them in a place I had hoped to relax. 

Fortunately, we found a way to avoid most of them by going out when the pools had just opened at 8 am, and by hanging around the adults-only pool at the top of the complex. While we were still occasionally disturbed by a gaggle of children coming up to use the water slide, we usually had 2 or 3 hours each morning more or less alone.  

I would highly recommend not eating at any of the hotels restaurants unless youre too lazy (like we were) to go anywhere else. The food they serve is actually quite good (I had a mushroom Napoleon from room service that was amazing) but isnt really worth the price. Everything in Maui is rather more expensive in the first place, but the restaurant prices there are absolutely obnoxious. Ill never again be crazy enough to spend $9.50 on a quesadilla.  

Instead, hit some of the places in Lahaina which are somewhat cheaper, and also hit the Foodland or Safeway there and stock up on nibbles which you can stash in your rooms minibar fridge. Its also a good idea to put together a picnic lunch one day for a driving tour of the rest of the island, which we did on our next to last day there. 

One of the most hyped activities (and there are dozens) on Maui is the road to Hana. Hana is a tiny town on the southeastern tip of the island which is at the end of one of the narrowest, twistiest roads in the country, through some of the most incredible, lush scenery around. This is the area where a lot of movie filming is done, including the opening scenes of Jurassic Park. Most tour books recommend taking an official van or limousine tour, to let someone familiar with the road drive while you take in the sights. If you really dont think youd be comfortable driving a road like that, take one of the tours, but theyre expensive, and if you think you can handle one-lane bridges in an unfamiliar rental car, go for it. 

We drove about halfway to Hana ourselves, stopping at the midway point where theres an arboretum called (quite appropriately) the Garden of Eden. Part of the park is drive-through, with a few trails. The garden features all sorts of tropical plants, not just those found in Hawaii or the Pacific islands. Its a little like being on a different planet. Its just as densely foliaged as a Northwest forest, but everything looks different. They even have pine trees, but they look a little like an aged artificial Christmas tree. 

The other hyped attraction we did was the Maui Ocean Center, a small but very well put-together aquarium featuring local ocean life. The highlight of the center is a walk-though plexiglass tube surrounded by water filled with sharks, stingrays and all sorts of colorful fish. Its even better than the dome at the Seattle Aquarium, and is well worth the admission price.   

Being lazy and hanging out in our room far too much, we didnt hit any luaus or spend much time on the beach, but overall Id say Maui was worth it,  now-peeling sunburns notwithstanding. We plan to go back at some point (probably during the school year) and hit some of the places and attractions we missed this time around (I might even get talked into snorkeling.) If you really want relaxation, stay someplace besides Kaanapali, but if only a resort will do, the Westins not bad, nor is the Sheraton or the Hyatt (which has a great breakfast buffet.) If you go, say Aloha for me!
