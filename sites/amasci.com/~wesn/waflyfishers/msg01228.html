I just returned from a fun outing as guest, fly instructor, guide for US
Ink Corp.  We flew into Clowhom Lodge which is at the end of Salmon
Inlet about 30 min by plane north of Vancouver, BC.  First evening was
fly instruction on the lawn and then next morning we loaded up in 10
boats equipped with 5-9 horse motors and travelled up lake and fished
the mouths of feeder streams.  Initially, more than 1/2 the group of 8
used spinners and worms not confident that flies and their talents could
produce.  By the end of the trip all had caught rainbows and cutthroat
on the fly with several very much committed to flyfishing.  None had
every flyfished before.
I returned to find the Snoqualmie with lots of steelhead but few
receptive to anything.  Leland is surely right, we need rain bad.  I had
a chat with Kim Chandler, the local game warden today, and they lowered
the Power House pool and knocked a hole in it so that steelhead can
escape if the Power plant shuts down for some reason.  Four years ago it
shut down and over 100 steelhead died from lack of O2.  They released,
today, 93 steelhead and 2 chinook from the cement pool.  Prey for rain;
steelheading will really pick up if it does.  We should also get a
number of cutthroat on the next rain.  Jere
