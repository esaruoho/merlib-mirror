Yes, you are right, let's re-negotiate before we blow a ton of more money.  Buy
them out like we did the commercial netters on the East Coast and we saw a
quick, initially anyway, recovery of the Atlantic Salmon.  I'm not to sure the
do gooders didn't get us into the mess in the first place with dual sovernty
etc.  Many people, fishermen included, do not realize the size and impact of the
Indian commercial fishery.  This is not a few small set nets on the river.  They
supply the Japanese in a big way and there buyers are waiting to buy at our
westcoast sites.  When there is no market and the price of fish is down,  they
dump the males out to rot and slit the females and sell the eggs to "bait
buyers" and for caviar.  A local fishermen in Fall City watched as the "bait
buyers" lined up to buy eggs on the Olympic Penninsula (mouth of the Quinnault
community..I can never remember the name)  There's big bucks in it.  Eggs go for
$6 a lb.  A good size king can have 6-8 lbs of eggs in her.  Much of it is
unregulated!  Our gov't does not want to hazzle them because of public sentiment
towards the plight of the American Indian.  Someone responded that they have to
regulate their netting according to the projected runs of fish determined by the
govt.  NOT SO.  Several years ago, we were informed that they had been given a
50% share of the decision making regarding "harvestable" fish.  Now, I am told,
by game agents, that they do as they want.  They are their own agency and make
their own decisions.  I have very negative feelings about the recovery of our
anadromous fish runs.  Yesterday, I learned, there will be 56,000 more people
moving onto the Eastside of Lake Washington in the next five years!!!   That's
ugly, for us and the salmon.  Jere
