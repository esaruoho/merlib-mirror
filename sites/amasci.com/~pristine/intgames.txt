Pure Mac: Internet Games 

 Internet Games 

Up until a year or so ago, internet gaming on the Mac was more of an annoyance than a pleasant diversion. But within the past couple years, some excellent services
have been developed for the Mac. GameRanger leads the way with a whole horde of top class internet games. GameRanger is very easy to use
so even novice Mac users can join in on the fun. Bungie.net and Battle.net offer their top commercial games with full support for the Mac.
And the long heralded Mac shareware company, Freeverse, now offers their own game server, HMS Freeverse , for a number of excellent card games.
Older services such as Kali and utilities such as IPRemote are still available for gaming, but the hassles involved no longer seem worth it with
the excellent offerings above now available. To top things off the ancient chess and backgammon servers
are currently well supported through the programs Fixation and MacFIBS 
and Java based board and card games you play through your browser are available at Excite and Yahoo . Overall, internet gaming on the Mac is looking good. 
Make sure to check the Game Utilities page at Pure Mac to insure your gaming experience is all it can be.

QuakeWorld is an Internet multi-player specific version of Quake. While the original version of Quake can be played over the
Internet, many modem users - the majority of players, had less than satisfactory play. Symptoms like excessive lag - actions
actually happening much later than you did them; packet loss - the game would freeze and resume several seconds later; and various
other difficulties plagued users.

After realizing how many people played Quake on the internet, and how many wanted to, but couldn't due to the play being
unsatisfactory, John Carmack of id Software decided to create a version of Quake that was optimized for the average modem Internet
player. This Internet specific version does only 1 thing, play deathmatch games over a TCP/IP network such as the Internet. It has no
