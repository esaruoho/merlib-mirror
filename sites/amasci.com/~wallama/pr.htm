Charlie


Not all llamas are cut out for PR work, Charlie has a very calm and curious disposition. He likes new people, new places and new experiences. We have been working with him the last year, and he has only had one real outting as a PR llama. We hope by this summer he will be able to start visiting some Rest Homes and some community events.
Our approach was having Charlie first become comfortable inside our own home. That meant being able to walk on cement, climb stairs, walk on linoleum, carpet or wooden floors. Next we started walking down narrow halls, around corners, through different doorways. Even visited the bathroom where he had to learn how to back out with out feeling any panic. In addition he has been exposed to many friends inside our house, which has helped him feel comfortable with different situations. Charlie is now so comfortable with being in any part of the house that if he finds a door accidently left open...he just walks right in and snoops around.


Fred is our Brother-In-Law who has gone through an amputation of one leg this past year. This was the first time Fred had been to our house since he had started using his wheel chair. As you can see , Charlie doesn't seem to be the least bit concerned about the wheel chair. He is happily eating grain out of Freds hand, and Fred is delighted to have Charlie be so accepting of his new disability.


This was the first time these children had seen a llama, as you can see Charlie is very clam and giving the little boy a sniff on the cheek. For what ever reason, he seems to love doing this with anybody that is new. The kids were delighted and enjoyed it even more because Charlie was in the house. Lots of pictures were taken and they could hardly wait to share their experience at school.


This is a delightful friend of Jerry's who had also never seen a llama. As you can see he needs a cane for support, and is still a little shaky. He had no fear of holding Charlies lead rope because of the calmness he felt from Charlie. He looks forward to returning often to visit us and Charlie. He is retired and pretty much alone in this community, with family and friends being far away. Charlie made him feel very happy and gave him something new to look forward to.
