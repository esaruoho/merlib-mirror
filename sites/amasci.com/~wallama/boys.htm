Tip: When first exposing your llama to a pack on his back, add a small amount of weight ...using equal weight on both sides of the back. This will give the llama the feeling of something being on his back and as he becomes used to the feeling you can start adding additional weight.

Our first two llamas were Paco & Pedro, this Pedro, he is now a 7 year old gelding and that look on his face expresses his ATTITUDE! He is not mean nor threatening to us but he is the only one that I can not halter. He was a year old when we first got him and was desentisized or trained in any way shape or form. He has been sick only once and that was one of the few times he willingly accepted human contact. He was so ill that he layed on the ground with his head in my lap. Once he recovered that was the end of that!!!

Pedro is also a strong communicator letting you know what he wants. When we have treats he will come running up to us like the rest of the boys but he will stop a few feet away and refuse anything you offer out of your hand. Instead he looks at you, looks at the treat in your hand and then points his nose towards the ground. You lay it on the ground and he immediately eats.

Beau Bridges was my third addition, he was only four months old when he arrived at our farm. Because he was my first cria I always called him Baby...still do:^) Baby is now an intact male 6 years old. He is my wise old owl and nobody pushes him around. He is very calm and seldom fights but he makes his presence and wishes known to the other llamas.

When he first arrived it was Pedro that decided to become his serogate mother. Baby was the only llama that we got as a single llama, out of the other 6 boys all came with a buddy. I think it made their adjustment earsier having another llama that they already know.

Also what was interesting about Pedro is that every llama that came after baby he wanted to beat the crap out of...go figure?
