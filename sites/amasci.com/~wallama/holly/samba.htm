ONE NOTE
SAMBA
One Note Samba, our young herd sire, is 1/2 Bolivian. His sire is the marvelous full Bolivian stud, FW Lambada, owned by Bill and Joan Martin of Far West Llamas. His mother, Tootle Lu, is North American. We are very pleased with the result of this cross. Samba is strong and athletic with wonderful silky fleece. His first babies should be on the ground in June and July of 1998. Samba's one note is quality!

Herd Sire Search
Our effort to find our perfect herd sire has been quite an adventure. Our first attempt was to purchase a youngster only six months old as a prospective herdsire. We were sadly disappointed when he neared two years with knock knees and buck teeth! Not exactly what we had in mind for a stud!
A year later we purchased a beautiful, flashy youngster at an auction and anxiously awaited the day when he would be able to breed our females. This male at two years was everything we hoped for with straight legs, wonderful conformation and color. Unfortunately, he didn't seem to have a clue as to what to do with a female llama! After much discussion, we decided he might just be too young and waited another year for those hormones to kick in.
Sadly, he didn't improve with age and at three years old still did not understand how to get the job done. He would bump into a female, she'd react and he quickly go the other direction! The people we bought him from took him to their farm so he could learn from the other studs. Our friends thought this was hilarious, of course, and kidded us about our llama in "stud school". He did breed at their farm and we brought him home.
A couple of months later, we were so excited to have him breed our best female who had recently had a cria. Well, same old story and this time we decided to leave them together for a few days to see what might develop. Since nothing seemed to be happening we decided it was time to give up on this guy. We wanted a stud we could depend on so this guy was returned to the folks we bought him from who then gelded and sold him.
You may have guessed what happened next! The following spring this female was taken to another farm to be bred and vigorously rejected the male. After a blood test, it was determined she was pregnant and four months later had a handsome appaloosa son. This male evidently performed in the dark by sneaking up on a kushed female before she had a chance to reject him!
After all this, we were so pleased when Samba came into our lives. He is so beautiful and gets better every day! He has now bred his first female and knew exactly what to do. He is an aggressive yet gentle breeder and was certainly worth waiting for! We've learned so much since we bought that first young male and are so anxious to see Samba's babies in 1999. Samba's one note is quality! And the best part of having our own stud is that we can finally keep our females and crias home with us!
