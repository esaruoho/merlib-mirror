The Past

Way back in 1960, the residents of the Lake Goodwin area decided they needed their own fire department. Waiting for an engine from Marysville would usually result in heavy fire damage. Not that the Marysville Fire Department was incapable of preventing such, but rather because the fire had between a 20 and 30 minute head-start. Long distances and narrow roads resulted in precious time lost to the ravages of fire.

No strangers to establishing community rooted organizations, the citizens of the Seven Lakes Resort area had previously created the local community club and constructed a large building to hold its meetings in. This new project they were ready for, and proceeded accordingly.

The members of the Lake Goodwin Community Club contacted local and state officials regarding the procedure for forming a Fire District. After many meetings and discussions, the boundaries for the proposed Fire District were determined and the stage was set for the final act. All that was needed now was a positive vote by the citizens in the affected area, and at the same time, election of the first Fire Commissioners.

On February 27, 1960, a special election was held to allow the voters their opportunity to speak. In one of the most amazing results ever, the fire district formation was given the go ahead with a unanimous "yes" vote. All 83 of them. The first 3 Fire Commissioners were elected as well. They were Lloyd Clark, Lyle Jewett, and Harry Sanderson. The easy part was done.

The first years required the department commissioners to either meet at a local residence, or the local real estate office. Even after firefighters were recruited and trained, equipment purchased, and an engine acquired, they still had to meet at the real estate office. During this period, one of the resorts elected to get out of the business and sell of the land once utilized by tourist and locals alike for week long getaways, and evening parties. The owners of Schuh's Resort divided up and sold the land they once called home. Having a very strong community spirit, the Schuhs donated one parcel to the newly formed fire district, a lot measuring 60' X 60'. Over the next few years, an additional 2 bordering lots were purchased by the fire district, resulting in a lot measuring 60' X 180', (the current size and location of the Headquarters Station). Now the land was had, but where the station?

In 1963 a small fire station was under construction. Consisting of 2 bays and a meeting room, volunteers were called upon to build the entire structure. A small block building, it quickly was outgrown. 1963 was also the time when the district ordered its first new fire engine. At one point, this and two other units were parked inside, requiring an exact procedure for pulling out of or backing into the bays. Eventually, something had to be done.

During this early period the district had annexed a few more square miles, with and end result of 18+ square miles. In the early 1970's, the district was approached by citizens and firefighters about building another station. The commissioners had also been approached about acquiring two used fire engines from Seattle, 1949 Kenworths. The search was underway for more property. With the additional responsibility of coverage in the south-east of the district, the new station's location was determined to be in that area. Finally, a location was found near Lake Loma. A lot owner agreed to sell part of his land to the district for a mere$2,000.00, $200.00 a year for 10 years. Kind of hard to beat that deal, especially for a not so rich department.

Around the mid 1970's, the district was once again approached about additional space. The firefighters requested that the fire department build another 2 bays, a meeting hall, kitchen, and hose tower. To accomplish this task, an employee was hired under a federal program which paid part of his wages for 2 years. During this period, he helped build the two story addition, with help from volunteers. After the initial two years was complete, the district elected to keep this individual on as a firefighter/maintenance person. To do so required the passing of a special levy every two years. The voting public was agreeable, and for many years. In 1979 the district purchased, through donations, a new "type 3" aid unit. In 1982, the district was granted the funds to purchase two new fire engines and the related equipment. Another vote of confidence from the public. These two new engines were the latest pride and joy of the department. Each identical to the other, the units were scheduled to arrive about 3 months apart in 1983. Manufactured by W.S. Darley, these units packed a thousand gallons of water combined with a 1000 GPM 3 stage pump, (2 stage with a high pressure booster line pump). And, with the addition of "jump seats", no more tail board riding.

In the early 1980's, the district went through some political turmoil. In 1983, the entire board was besieged by the public and some of the firefighters for a change, a reorganization. Resigning, one by one, each board member position was vacated. The remaining board members would appoint a replacement, then resign at the next meeting. In a 2 month period, the board was awash in new blood. Shortly there after, a new chief was appointed. The employee was relieved of duties and replaced. Things were going to be different. They were!

Quite a few of the members were not in favor of the actions of the board, and resigned in protest. This outcome left the fire department in an immediate need of new volunteers. The district was also tied up in litigation for approximately the next 7 years. The terminated employee filed various suits, generally contending that the district was culpable of wrongful discharge. It would be another 10 years before he would step foot in the station he once was paid to inhabit. Another 10 years before he would inspect the fire engines he developed the specifications for.

The district hired a temporary "caretaker" who was also a volunteer firefighter. This lasted for about 3 months. The district then found and hired another firefighter/maintenance person. He lasted approximately 1 1/2 years. Another firefighter was hired in 1985. In 1986, the district hired it's second full time employee, a firefighter/mechanic. In 1987, the fire chief, (3rd since the termination) elected to retire. Things had settled down and the chief felt comfortable enough to leave, and seek a less stressful life. So the board went on a search for another chief, but not a volunteer chief. This time they would hire a career chief. In August of 1987, the selection of a new Fire Chief was announced. The existing career firefighter would be promoted to the rank of Fire Chief. His first task was to appoint all new officers, as the board had asked all existing officers to resign, so that the new chief would be free to appoint his staff.

Eventually, the district received the news that after a protracted court case, and 2 appeals, the terminated employee had lost his suit(s) against the district. The dark financial cloud hanging over the department was now dissipated.

In 1990, the department took possession of a new aid unit. The district had recently passed an EMS Levy, and the funds were partially directed to this purchase. Another vote of confidence from the public.

1991 brought the promotion of the firefighter/mechanic to a rank of Assistant Chief. Another large bay was added to the south side of the Headquarters Station in 1993. There were 4 units parked outside for many years, resulting in rust, water damage, and occasional theft/damage. Now they would be parked undercover, though not heated nor insulated. Never the less, dry and secure. 1994 resulted in the hiring of another full time employee. This person was charged with taking command of the station near Lake Loma, and performing various secretarial duties, house cleaning, and ground maintenance, as well as firefighting functions. He would be given the tittle of Captain. Shortly there after, the latest pride and joy of the department arrived, a 1994 fire engine. The old 1963 engine could now be retired. It was sold to a fire department in eastern Washington, one which was desperately in need.

1995 saw the first part time firefighter program for the fire district. It required 8 firefighters to perform 12 hour shifts on the weekends, 6 AM to 6 PM, two firefighters to a shift. Considered a great success, it started and ended with the summer season, as planned. It has since been expanded, beginning with the 1996 summer season. Shifts are 12 hours each weekday and 24 hours each weekend/holiday. This has resulted in nearly 24 hour a day, 7 day a week coverage.
