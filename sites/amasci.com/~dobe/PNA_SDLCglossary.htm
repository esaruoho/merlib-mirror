Dobe Doinat's Systems Development Life Cycle Glossary

Introduction to Systems Analysis

Term
Definition
System A group of related procedures
Data Consists of basic facts, the raw material.
Information Information is the finished product after data has been processed.
Processing Transformation of data input into accurate, meaningful information that is required.
Input Data is input by workers or machines to an Information System.
Output Information is output by the Information System.
Information Systems Components Software, hardware, data, people and procedures.
Application An Information System can also be called an Application. Application software produces information needed by users.
Hardware Computer platforms and peripherals used for input, processing and output.
Software System and Application software, or programs control the IS environment, local or networked, OTS or legacy, includes the Operating System.
Legacy System How the system worked before proposed or implemented changes through Systems Analysis.
Derived Information All information is either produced directly, or derived, which would imply some function performed upon the data to produce a new synthesis.
End Users Includes employees, customers, vendors or others who directly interact with the Information System.
Corporate Culture A set of beliefs, rules, traditions, values, and attitudes that define a company's personality and influence its way of doing business.
BIS A Business Information System could be production-oriented, service-oriented, or a combination of both.
Subsystems A larger IS may be broken into a series of smaller subsystems.
EDI Electronic Data Exchange is one standard interface between different Information Systems.
Interdependent IS Output from one may be Input to another.
Boundary A not always clear-cut interface between two systems or processes. This will effect the Scope of a project.
Vertical Market IS Application software is said to be vertical when it is for a highly specialized need or requirement.
Transaction processing Another term for operational systems.
MIS Management Information Systems generate timely and accurate information for all management levels.
EDP Electronic Data Processing were one form of early operational systems.
DSS Decision Support Systems help make business decisions by analysing internal or external data. A typical example is a What-If Analysis.
What-If Analysis Using a model, determine the best course of action based upon hypothetical data and presumptions. The better the model, the more precise the prediction, of course.
EIS Executive Information Systems combines the features and capabilities of both MIS and DSS, but with more flexibility and support for unstructured decision-making.
ESS Executive Support Systems support the information requirements of top-level managers and their need to make unstructured decisions.
Structured Decisions Occur regularly, have predefined information requirements and result in predetermined actions.
Unstructured Decisions Do not occur regularly, cannot be predicted, and whose information requirements cannot be predefined.
Semistructured Decisions A combination of structured and unstructured decisions. A set of predefined inputs and outputs do not exist.
Expert System Computers capable of simulating human reasoning and decision-making by combining a knowledge base with inference rules. Cannot actually use common sense or intuition.
Knowledge Base A stored database of known and reliable information.
Inference Rules Determine how the Knowledge Base is used to reach decisions, operating within preprogrammed limits
Fuzzy Logic Not actually whatever drove me to think this was a good idea, but rather one which allows logical inferences to be drawn from imprecise relationships.
AI Artificial Intelligence is beyond the scope of this page to define, but a Fuzzy Logic System may, depending on your definition, be an example.
OAS Office Automation System is a now archaic term.
Empowerment Enabling people to handle tasks and make decisions that were assigned previously to higher organizational levels.
Organizational Levels Operational personnel, Lower Management, Middle Management and Top Management pyramid scheme.
Operational Personnel Operational personnel perform repetitive functions with well-defined procedures.
Lower Management Direct day-to-day operations, make appropriate decisions and take correction actions where applicable. Highly structured.
Middle Management Focus on longer range tactical planning, usually at least a month to a year.
Top Management Focus on even longer range strategic planning, hopefully ensuring the company will survive and grow in the future.
Tactical Planning Plans to allocate company resources to achieve organizational objectives.
Strategic Planning Establish the overall company mission, policies, goals and future product lines.
Exception Reports Identify variances from set targets which might require action.
SDLC Systems Development Life Cycle is a series of steps used to build an information system. These include Planning, Analysis, Design, Implementation, Operation and Support.
Systems Request Identifies the IS and describes the desired changes or improvements.
Preliminary Investigation Critical step in the planning phase of SDLC, begun with a Feasibility Study which determines if it is appropriate to continue.
Preliminary Investigation Report The final report to management on a Preliminary Investigation of a Systems Request, including recommendations.
Feasibility Study The Feasibility Study is a part of the Preliminary Investigation.
End Product The output or a deliverable.
Prototype Early working version of an information system. This can speed up SDLC process objectives.
Systems Analyst Investigates, analyzes, designs, develops, installs, evaluates and maintains a company's IS. May plans projects, develop schedules, or estimate costs. Must conduct meetings. deliver presentations, write memos, emails and documentation.

Term
Definition
Mission Statement Describes a company's overall purpose and direction.

These may be triaged by setting priorities and assessing the feasibility of successful completion and/or a cost benefit analysis.
Source (of System Projects) Users Requests, Top-Management Directives, existing systems, IS Department or other external factors.

Feasibility Evaluation by operational, technical and economic testing procedures to decide if any Service Request should proceed to Preliminary Investigation.
Feasibility Report The final report to the Systems Review Committee on the merits of the Service Request.
Operational Feasibility Is the request a practical approach that will solve a problem or take advantage of an opportunity to achieve company goals?
Technical Feasibility Is the request technically achievable, including the necessary technical resources and people and expertise available?
Economic Feasibility What are the tangible and intangible benefits and is the problem worth solving? Will it result in a sound business investment?
Tangible Benefits Those benefits which will produce dollar savings or reduce existing expenses.
Intangible Benefits Cannot be measured specifically, but are important. These may include enhancing the company image or improving customer service.
Determination of Feasibility Feasibility analysis is an ongoing process that must be performed during all phases of the SDLC, and a determination of feasibility can change over time.
Criteria Tangible vs. Intangible, Discretionary vs. Non-Discretionary, Cost vs. Benefit
Discretionary A project which may or may not proceed because a choice exists.
Non-Discretionary Projects where no choice exists, for instance by complying with Federal Law.
Cost Benefit Analysis A determination of whether a given business makes sense economically.
Preliminary Investigation Critical step in the planning phase of SDLC, begun with a Feasibility Study which determines if it is appropriate to continue.
Preliminary Investigation Report The final report to management on a Preliminary Investigation of a Systems Request, including recommendations.
Objective of the Preliminary Investigation 1) Understand the true nature of the problem.
2) Define the Scope and Constraints of the proposed project.
3) Identify the benefits that are likely when the project is completed.
4) Specify time and money estimates for systems analysis and subsequent developmental phases.
5) Present a report to Management.
Scope Defining the range or extent of the project to establish the boundaries of the Systems Request. "Not for oral use!"
Constraints A condition, restriction or requirement that must be satisfied.
Evaluating Constraints Present vs. future, Internal vs. External and Mandatory vs. Desirable

Analysing Requirements

Term
Definition
Structured Analysis Examines a system in terms of its inputs, outputs and processes.
Process-Centered Technique using structured analysis using:
1) Data Flow Diagrams 2) Data Dictionary 3) Process Descriptions
DFD Data Flow Diagrams do not show program logic or processing steps but rather show how data moves through an IS with symbols for identification of components. This is a logical model of the IS.
Symbols include:
1) Processes
2) Data Flows
3) Data Stores
4) External Entities
Process Descriptions 1) In naming, use a Verb Noun format.
2) Use standard symbol.
3) Example: "Create Invoice" or "Verify Order".
Data Flow Descriptions 1) In naming, use an optional Adjective with a singular Noun format.
2) Use standard symbol.
3) Example: "Daily Payment" or "Paycheck".
Data Store Descriptions 1) In naming, use a Plural Noun (optional Adjective) format.
2) Use standard symbol.
3) Example: "Patients" or "Accounts Receivable".
External Entity Descriptions 1) In naming, use a singular Noun. in ALL-CAPS format.
2) Use standard symbol.
3) Example: "EMPLOYEE" or "CUSTOMER".
Source Terminator External Entity which supplies input.
Sink Terminator External Entity which receives output.
Context Diagrams A Top-Level Data Flow Diagram showing the Scope of the system.
Diagram 0 A Data Flow Diagram just below the level of the Context Diagram, showing more details of the system.
Conventions for DFD 1) Each Context Diagram must fit on a single page.
2) The Process name should be the same as the name of the IS.
3) Use unique names within each set of symbols.
4) Do not cross lines.
5) Use abbreviated identifications.
6) Use unique reference number for each process symbol.
Exploded Diagram Also said to be "partitioned" or "decomposed", it shows the Process in greater detail.
Leveling Process of drawing a series of increasingly detailed diagrams until the desired degree of detail is reached.
Balancing Maintains consistancy among an entire set of diagrams, including I/O data flows, data definitions and process descriptions. In other words, all input and output data flows of the parent are preserved on the child DFD.
Data Dictionary A central storehouse of information about the system's data used to collect, document and organize specific facts about the system, including:
1) Data Flows
2) Data Stores
3) External Entities
4) Processes
5) All Data Elements
6) Other meaningful combinations and relationships
Data Element The smallest piece of data meaningful in an IS.
Record A database data structure which includes all related data elements (fields) in a data flow or retained in a data store.
Data Structure How the related data elements are combined and organized.
Primary Key Data Element in a record which uniquely identifies that record.
Structured English A subset of standard English describing logical processes clearly and accurately conforming to these rules:
1) Only use Sequence, Selection and Iteration.
2) Use indentation for readability
3) Use a limited vocabulary found in the Data Dictionary.
Decision Tables Shows a logical structure with all possible combinations of conditions and resulting actions.
Decision Trees Graphical representation of the conditions, actions and rules found in a decision table.
Physical Model Shows how the system's requirements are implemented.
Logical Model Shows what the system's requirements are, regardless of how it must be accomplished physically. May also be referred to as a Business Model.
Modular Design Based on 3 logical building blocks, Sequence, Selection and Iteration.
Sequence The completion of one process step in sequential order, one after another.
Selection The completion of one of two or more process steps based on the results of a test or condition.
Iteration The completion of a process step which is repeated until the results of a predefined condition end the repetition, also known as looping.
Gane and Sarson Popular diagram symbol methodology.
Yourdon Popular diagram symbol methodology.

Evaluating Alternatives and Strategies

Term
Definition
Make or Buy Decision  Choice between in-house development or purchase of software.
In-house Software Developed within the IS department of the Company. This might be done to:
  • Satisfy unique requirements
  • Minimize changes in business procedures and policies
  • Meet constraints of existing systems
  • Meet constraints of existing technology
  • Develop internal resources and capabilities
Purchased Software (OTS) Horizontal or vertical application commercially available. Some reasons for this are:
  • Lower costs

  • Proven reliability and performance benchmarks
  • Implemented by others for observation and research

  • Temporary or permanent contract personnel
  • End-User systems

Outsourcing Use of outside companies to handle a portion of a company's workload by contract.
End-User systems Applications which can be operated and managed by end users on their own using software suites.
Software Suites Integrated applications which can easily exchange data.
Information Center (IC) Responsible for supporting end-user computing with hotline assistance and training.

Term
Definition
Logical Design Also known as the Essential Model, it defines the logical functions and features of the system and relationships among its components. It defines WHAT must take place, not HOW to do it. It therefore does not address the actual methods of implementation. For instance:
  • Data required to be entered
  • Specificity of key formats and sort order
  • Defined requirements and constraints
Physical Design Plan for the actual Implementation of a logical system. It is concerned with HOW the system will meet the requirements of implementation. For instance:
  • Describes the actual process of entering, verifying and storing data
  • Physical layout of data sources and sinks
  • How the reports are generated given the requirements
Purpose of System Design Phase Create a Physical Design that satisfies the logical system requirements. Major activities:
  • Review System Requirements Document - To become thoroughly familiar with it, not to redo the activity
  • Design system components - Input, Output, Files, Databases and System Architecture
  • Present completed design - Design Specification Document
If an analyst has unforeseen design issues or problems emerge, they may have to return to requirements analysis, however this may indicate the earlier work was incomplete and is used only in very limited situations.
Output Requirements Drive the design process because they describe what the system must produce to meet business needs.
Effective System One which satisfies the defined requirements and constraints, widely accepted by users who use it to support Company objectives.
Reliable System Adequately handling errors, detecting them as early as possible, allowing for correction without damage to the system itself.
Maintainable System Flexible, designed with future modification in mind.
Design Considerations
  • User needs
  • Data entry
  • Data flow and storage
  • Use modular, structured processing design
  • Use standard processing guidelines
  • Be aware of many design tradeoffs.
Hard-Coded Not easily manipulated through programming.
Design Tradeoffs Conflicting design goals force the analyst to consider many alternatives constantly.
Code Set of letters, numbers or symbols which represent an item of data used in input and output to a system. Types include:
  • Sequence codes
  • Block sequence codes
  • Classification codes
  • Alphabetic codes
  • Mnemonic codes
  • Significant digit codes
  • Derivation codes
  • Cipher codes
  • Action codes
  • Self-checking codes
When developing a code:
  • Keep codes concise
  • Allow for expansion
  • Keep codes stable
  • Make codes unique
  • Use sortable codes
  • Avoid confusing codes
  • Make codes meaningful
  • Use one code for one purpose
  • Keep codes consistent
Checklist for Output Design Before considering the output details of a report or screen form the analyst will ask:
  • What is the purpose of the output?
  • Who or what needs this output?
  • Why is this output needed?
  • How will it be used?
  • What specific information is to be included?
  • What format should be used?
  • When will the information be provided and how often?
  • How often must the data be updated?
  • Will simultaneous user access be required for screen forms?
  • Are security or confidentiality issues involved?
Information Delivery Types of Output
  • Printed output
  • Screen output
  • Audio output
  • Fax and Faxback systems
  • Email
  • Internet or Intranet hyperlinks
  • Automated Teller Machines (ATM)
  • Specialized or industry-specific
Turnaround Documents Output documents from a system later re-entered into the same or another IS
Point of Sale (POS) Retail IS terminal.
Reports Classified by Content
  • Detail reports - One or more lines of output is produced for each record processed
  • Control break reports - This breaks out important data onto it's own line in a report
  • Mock-up reports - Used in a Design Phase to iterate toward greater usability and user acceptance.
  • Exception reports - Shows records meeting specific criteria
  • Summary reports - This one usually goes to Management
Reports Classified by Distribution
  • Internal reports
  • External reports
Control Break When the value of a control field changes, a control break usually causes specific actions. On a Control Break Report, for example, this may be a subtotal.
Screen Design Considerations
  • Character output
  • Graphical
  • Special effects
Special video or audio effects are sometimes added to screen displays. Blinking, marquee movement, exploding, fading, reverse video, animation of text or graphics are all easily accomplished but should not be over-used. First, develop a design that is logical and user friendly, then use special effects sparingly. Use these to draw the User's attention to the most important data.
Output Control Ensuring information output is correct, complete and secure.
  • Integrity
  • Security
Exception Report Shows only those records that meet a specific condition or criteria, such as those requiring action.
Summary Report Generally, reports used by individuals at higher levels in the organization include less detail than reports used by lower-level employees, thus a Summary Report leads to a Summary Execution.
Paper Report Considerations
  • Page heading lines
  • Column heading lines
  • Column heading alignment
  • Spacing between columns
  • Order of data items on detail lines
  • Detail lines grouping
  • Group headers and footers
  • Report footers
  • Improvement of report design
  • Documenting a report design
  • Design consistency
  • Special form reports
  • Volumes and Time Impacts
  • Report approval

Main Objectives of Input Design
  • Select most suitable media and methods
  • Efficient input procedures
  • Reduce manual input volume

GIGO Garbage In, Garbage Out (An old computer mantra.)
Batch input Batching data means to do a lot of processing simultaneously, often on off-peak-load hours to reduce impact on users and other computing efficiencies.
Data Capture Identification and recording of source data.
Data Entry Process of converting source data to computer-readable format.
Data Input The process whereby the data actually enters the IS.

Control Total A hash total or some other scheme for data authentication.
Online data entry Web or net data entry considered synonymous with direct data entry.

Swipe scanners Using optic or magnetic data strips
Magnetic data strips For rapid input of data
Efficient Input Procedures
  • Reduce Input Volume
  • Input necessary data only
  • Do not input data that can be retrieved from system files or calculated
  • Do not input constant data
  • Use codes
Single Source of Data SSD is known by different names at different businesses. It means don't input the same data twice, which would be redundant and leads to errors.
Reduce Input Errors
  • Sequence checks
  • Existence checks
  • Data type checks
  • Range and Limit checks
  • Reasonableness checks
  • Validity checks
  • Combination checks
  • Batch Control (volume vs time issues)
Hash totals Hash techniques involve the use of summing or other processes which, taken by themselves, might be meaningless information, but is rather used as a reliability check.
Key Tasks in Input Design
  • Design Data entry and input procedures
  • Design Source documents for data capture (or other data capture)
  • Design Input data records
  • Design Data entry screens
  • Design User interface screens
  • Design Audit trails and system security measures
Source Document Effective Source Document Design implements Input Control, thereby ensuring that input data is correct, complete and secure, which promotes the accuracy and quality of data.
Form layout
  • Heading zone
  • Control zone
  • Instruction zone
  • Body zone
  • Totals zone
  • Authorization zone

External source documents Representing the business to the world, the interface must be attractive and professional.
Internal source documents Form appearances are usually less critical than documents or screens seen by the world at large.

Repeating fields Instancing related data records for new input.
Constant or non-repeating fields Data only input once per record
Screen displays
  • Attractive and uncluttered
  • Meaningful, logical order
  • Consistent
  • Specific, understandable, professional
  • Display error messages readably for a long enough time.
  • Special effects
  • Feedback
  • Screen display layout documentation
Screen Design Presenting Information (Output)
  • Attractive and uncluttered
  • Meaningful and logical
  • Consistent with titles, messages and instructions
  • Consistent with locations and terminology
  • Specific, understandable and professional
Gathering Information (Input)
  • Restrict user access and require data validation
  • Provide descriptions of all fields
  • Show sample formats required

  • Should match the layout of the source document.

ERD Entity-Relationship Diagram is a graphical model of the IS that show the relationships among system entities.

  • 1:M   One-to-Many Relationship
  • M:N   Many-to-Many Relationship
The relationship between two entities is referred to as Cardinality. Unlike DFDs and flowcharts, ERDs do not depict data or information flows, only their relationships dut does not imply a superior/inferior relationship.
One-to-One Relationship (1:1) Exists when exactly one of a second entity occurs for each instance of the first entity.
One-to-Many Relationship (1:M) Exists when one occurrence of the first entity can be related to many occurrences of the second entity, but each occurrence of the second of the second entity can be associated with only one occurrence of the first entity. Many may mean any number, including zero! (Also see ERD)
Many-to-Many Relationship (M:N) Exists when one instance of the first entity can be related to many instances of the second entity, and one instance of the second entity can be related to many instances of the first entity. Many may mean any number, including zero! (Also see ERD)
Cardinality How Instances of one Entity relate to Instances of another Entity. For instance, they may be either Mandatory or Optional. (Also see ERD)

Record Design Specifies the fields and identifies the Primary Key, if any, for all records in a particular file.
Normalization Process by which inherent problems and complexities in a record design are identified and corrected.
First Normal Form (1NF) A record is in the First Normal Form if it does not contain a repeating group.
Second Normal Form (2NF) A record is in the Second Normal Form if it is in 1NF and if all fields that are not part of the Primary Key are dependent on the entire Primary Key. If any field in a 1NF record depends on only one of the fields in a combination Primary Key, then the record is not in 2NF. A 1NF record with a Primary Key that is a single field is automatically in 2NF.
Third Normal Form (3NF) A record is in the Third Normal Form if it is in 2NF and if no Non-Key Field is dependent on another Non-Key Field.
Conversion of Normalization Forms The three normalization forms constitute a progression in which a record in 1NF is better than one that is unnormalized. but a record in 2NF is better yet, and a record in 3NF represents the best design.
To convert from unnormalized to 1NF:
  • Expand the Primary Key to include the Key of the repeating group
  • Therefore, the new Primary Key may quite likely be a combination of other key fields
To convert from 1NF to 2NF:
  • Create a new record design for each field in the Primary Key
  • Then, create new record designs for all combinations of Primary Key fields taken two at a time, three at a time and so on until there exists a record with the entire original Primary Key
  • Next, for each new record, designate a field (or combination of fields, as a Primary Key
  • Finally, place each of the remaining fields with its appropriate primary key, which is the minimal key upon which it depends, and remove any record that has had no additional field placed into it
To convert from 2NF to 3NF:
  • Remove all fields from the 2NF record that depends on another nonkey field
  • Place these into a new record that has the other field as a Primary Key
Normal forms beyond 3NF exist, however are rarely used in most business-oriented systems.
Design to reduce redundancy. This is what normalization is all about.

Hierarchical Database Data organized like a family tree, like Explorer in Windows. The disadvantage might be that each Child may have one and only one Parent. Also, the changes to the structure of a record require reorganizing the database and modifying the application programs accessing it. In addition, they are complex and may be difficult to maintain.
Network Database A relationship between two records is defined as a Set and are further defined as Owner and Member Records. It has much of the same disadvantages as the hierarchical model.
Relational Database Data is organized in 2D tables or Relations. Each row is called a Tuple and each column is called an Attribute. Think of a relation as a file, tuples as records, and attibutes as fields.
An important advantage would be simplicity. Modify or add new entities, attributes or tables at any time without restructuring the entire DB. Yet, because it is powerful and flexible, it is the predominant design approach in today's business world.
Object-Oriented Database An object is a unit of data along with the actions that can affect taht data. It uses an Object Library and is by it's very nature extensible. Methods are the actions defined, and messages execute a method. Each object may inherit methods and attrubutes or properties from other objects in the Object Library. It focuses on data rather than processes, and provides for a high degree of flexibility, ease of maintenance and reduced operating costs through reusability of code.

System Changeover The process of putting the new IS online and retiring the old system. There are four main changeover approaches:
  • Direct cutover
  • Parallel operation
  • Pilot operation
  • Phased
Direct Cutover Changeover occurs immediately when the new system becomes operational. Usually the least expensive method, but involves more risk.
Parallel Operation Both the old and the new systems operate fully for a specified period. The output from both systems, having identical input, should reveal any problems with the new system.
Parallel Changeover is not practical if the old and new systems are incompatible technically, or if the operating environment cannot support both systems.
It is also the most expensive but the least risky.
Pilot Operation Implement the complete new system at a selected location of the company as a test platform before implementing on a wider scale.
Phased Cutover Implement the new system in stages or modules, not all at once

  • Maintenance history documentation shows steadily increasing adaptive and corrective maintenance
  • Operational costs or execution times are rapidly increasing and routine perfective maintenance does not reverse or slow the trend
  • A new off-the-shelf (OTS) application becomes available that provides the same or additional services more efficiently, faster, or less expensively than the current system
  • Technology becomes available which offers the same or additional functions more efficiently
  • Maintenance changes or additions are difficult and expensive to perform
  • Users request significant new features to support business requirements
  • Bill Gates needs a new space station! (Just kidding, Bill!)
