 		The current plan is to route the Green Line monorail through or around the Seattle Center, then south along Fifth Avenue through Belltown to Stewart Street, then west to Second Avenue, then south along Second Avenue the rest of the way through Downtown Seattle.
 
But I think that there are several important reasons why it would be better to have one station just south of Key Arena then route the line south along Second Avenue through Belltown and continue along Second through Downtown. For more about why I prefer a Second Avenue route through Belltown, click here.
 
But it now looks very doubtful there will be any real effort to change the route to Second Avenue, which means the Green Line will go down Fifth Avenue through Belltown. It also means that most likely the old monorail will be demolished.
 
Under these circumstances, I feel the Green Line should use a Thomas Street routing across the Seattle Center. This route would permit the new monorail to closely follow the same route as the old one without as much impact on travel time as other suggested routes.
 
Under my plan, the Green Line would follow W. Harrison St. from Elliott Ave. W. to Queen Anne Ave. N., as in the current preferred alternative. But east of Queen Anne Ave. N., the guideway would curve somewhat to the south so as to pass over a parking lot on the southwest corner of Harrison St. and 1st Ave. N. and then cross over 1st Ave. N. to a point just south of Key Arena. Someplace around 1st Ave. N. there would be a station, probably either on the west side of the street where the parking lot is, or on the east side just southwest of Key Arena.
 
After passing the south side of Key Arena, the guideway would curve to the east to align roughly with Thomas St., passing just south of the Center House, and then curving to the north and then east enough to follow the old monorail route through the Experience Music Project and thence south along Fifth Avenue North and Fifth Avenue to the proposed curve onto Stewart Street.
 
It would be necessary to demolish the existing monorail station that is the Seattle Center terminus of the existing monorail. This would provide the space needed for the alignment of the new monorail, which would need to curve south a bit west of the Experience Music Project in order to clear the structure of the Center House. I have no suggestion regarding whether or not the old station should be replaced, or where a new station should be located.
 
I am very much in favor of building the Key Arena station oriented more-or-less northwest and southeast, and quite close to First Avenue North, to allow room for a switch in the guideway south of Key Arena. So now you ask, “Why build a switch there?rdquo;
 
Why? Because I anticipate that after the Green Line has been up and running for a while, people are going to start realizing that the Fifth Avenue routing is wasting too much travel time, and I think it is only a matter of time before a decision is made to run a more direct route down Second Avenue through Belltown. With the proper orientation of the Key Arena station, a switch can be built southeast of the station so that express trains can use the new guideway south onto Second Avenue. Of course there would also be a switch at Second and Stewart. Most trains would use the Second Avenue route, but some trains would still use the Fifth Avenue route.
