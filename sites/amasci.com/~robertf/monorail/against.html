 		Monorail is just an expensive amusement ride. It is true that the Disney resort parks such as Disneyland have monorails. However most of these are scaled-down versions made for the park and are obviously designed primarily for amusement purposes. However the proposed system for the Seattle region is a full-size version designed for the purpose of rapid mass transit. No doubt some people will ride them for fun, but most of the riders will be using the monorail for the usual transit purposes, such as going to work, going to school, going shopping, or visiting friends. So the monorail will be no more of an amusement ride than busses, light rail, commuter rail, and subway trains.

Light rail trains can be switched to different tracks, but monorail trains can only go back and forth on one guideway. There really are such a thing as switches for monorails! There are methods for moving beams in a way to line up with different beamways, so monorail trains can be switched. The Monorail Society has a good explanation, with animated photos of switches in operation.

I don't want a monorail on my street because it will be too noisy! Are there trucks and diesel busses on your street? How much do they bother you? Monorail trains will be quieter!

The monorail structure will be ugly and block views. This statement mostly reflects a fear of the unknown. Modern design results in a smaller, sleeker structure. We have many skilled architects and designers that are quite creative and can make the structure more attractive. Features can be added to the basic structure to make it more attractive. Trees planted under the guideway help hide it. People in each neighborhood can work with designers to come up with attractive designs. For example, one architects' drawing for a proposed monorail in San Antonio, Texas, resembles a Roman aquaduct, with fake stone columns and arches.

The Monorail Society's web site has a page debunking arguments against monorail, with photos comparing subways, light rail, and monorail.
