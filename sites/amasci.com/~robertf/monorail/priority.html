 		My opinions regarding the sequence of operations in building a regional monorail system are as follows:
 
1.   Stop light rail:   Sound Transit needs to stop wasting money on an exorbitantly expensive, unsafe, slow, noisy, destructive, and inadequate light rail system.
 
2.   Build the Green Line:    Voters have now approved this line from West Seattle to Ballard and Crown Hill by way of Downtown Seattle. However, I think the route should be altered to eliminate the loop around or through the Seattle Center, instead going south from Key Arena and following 2nd Ave. through Belltown. This would eliminate extra curves and longer track, decreasing travel time from the north and also eliminating the conflict over the future of the existing Seattle Center Monorail.
 
3.   Get Sound Transit to build a monorail instead of the Link light rail project:   This route will connect with the Green Line near the International District and follow Dearborn Ave. S., Rainier Ave. S., Martin Luther King Way S., Boeing Access Road, East Marginal Way S., Interurban Ave. S., parallel Interstate 405 from 68th Ave. S. to Southcenter Mall, continue west to Pacific Highway S., follow Pacific Highway S. to Seatac Airport, where there would be a station in the airport passenger terminal. If funds permit, the monorail would continue south on International Blvd. S./Pacific Highway S. as far as possible (eventually to Tacoma).
 
4.    Extend the Green Line from Crown Hill to Lake City:    The route would be via Holman Road, N. 105th St., and Northgate Way, but then deviate south to North Seattle Community College, the Northgate Transit Center, and then serve the Northgate Mall and go easterly to Lake City Way, and follow Lake City Way north to N.E. 145th St.
 
5.   Build a monorail line north along Aurora:    This route would extend north from downtown Seattle along Aurora Ave. N. and would be the primary high-speed access to the city from the north. This route corresponds to the Blue Line proposed in the long-term plans by the Seattle Monorail Project. Initially it would serve north Seattle and Shoreline, but should be extended as quickly as possible to southeast Edmonds, Lynnwood, and Everett. A station near 105th and Aurora would provide a transfer point with the Green Line. Large park-and-ride lots further north would encourage many drivers to drive to a park-and-ride lot and take the monorail downtown instead of driving downtown.
 
If enough people in Seattle and Shoreline want this route, then it could be speeded up using my Jump Start Blue Line Proposal, which would build a branch line of the Green Line up Greenwood and Westminster Way to Aurora to permit operation of the northern part of the Blue Line before completion of the high-level bridge across the ship canal.
 
6.   Build a monorail from downtown Seattle to Lake City:    It would be via Capitol Hill, the University District, and Lake City Way. Near 110th and Lake City Way this route would interconnect with the extended Green Line. The Lake City route would later be extended to Lake Forest Park, Bothell, and Woodinville.
