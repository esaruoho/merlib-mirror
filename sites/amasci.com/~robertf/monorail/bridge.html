The Controversy
 
An important part of the Seattle Monorail Project is a bridge to carry the Green Line of the monorail across Salmon Bay from Interbay to Ballard. A design for the bridge has not been selected yet, nor has the location of the bridge. There seems to be some support for a cable stayed bridge, and the location would probably be a short distance west of the Ballard Bridge.
 
My Opinions
 
I AM OPPOSED TO A CABLE STAYED BRIDGE.
A cable stayed bridge consists of tall towers from which a number of cables fan out from the tower to various positions along the bridge deck. (This is different from a suspension bridge, in which there are tall towers, but with large cables going from tower to tower with smaller cables hanging down from the large cables to support the bridge deck.)
 
Sunshine Bridge in Tampa, Florida
A typical modern cable stayed bridge
 
The photo above shows a cable stayed bridge in Florida, and I think the design is beautiful. But notice how high the towers extend above the bridge deck. I think that the tall towers would be out of place in the low-lying terrain around the Ballard Bridge. The same argument is true for a suspension bridge.
 
Looking again at the picture, imagine what the bridge deck would look like without the towers — a long graceful rise from ground level to a height that permits ships to pass underneath, then dropping gradually back to ground level. We need a different design to support the monorail guideway but without the high towers.
 
I LIKE THE BOX GIRDER ALTERNATIVE.
Recently the Seattle Monorail Project has presented artist’s conceptions of three basic bridge designs — cable stayed bridge, arch bridge, and box girder bridge. Their arch bridge has the central arch rising above the bridge deck. I would like an arch bridge if the top of the arch would meet the bottom of the bridge deck. The box girder design (shown at right) is simpler and has a cleaner, less obtrusive look, and is low profile. I feel the box girder alternative is the best choice and I am going to support this alternative.
