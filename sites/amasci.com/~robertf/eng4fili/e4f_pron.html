English for Filipinos
Pronounciation

There are some sounds in the English language that are not in Filipino languages. The sounds that cause the most difficulty for Filipinos are the sounds of the English letters "F" and "V", and the letter combination "Th". Filipinos that have not learned how to say these sounds can have a problem, for example, if they talk about the fork (ang tenador) but what they say is "the pork" (ang karne ng baboy).

P
The letter "P" in both Tagalog and English is made by closing the lips and then opening them suddenly and letting some air come out. In English there is more of a puff of air than in Tagalog, but still the sound is almost the same.

Do not worry about your "P" sound, it probably sounds fine already. I only explained how to make the "P" sound to help you understand how the "F" sound is different from the "P" sound.

F
In Tagalog, an "F" is pronounced the same as "P". This is because in Tagalog there is no sound like the letter "F" in English. But in English it is a much different sound.

In order to make the sound of the English "F", first close your lips together like to make a "P". But now move you upper lip forward until your upper teeth are resting gently on top of you lower lip, and now your upper lip will be out in front of you lower lip, with some space between the lips. Now blow air out through your teeth. That should make the sound of the English "F".

When you are speaking, and you want to say the "F" sound, you don't really first put your lips together like to make a "P". I only said that above to help you feel the difference between the "P" and the "F". When you are speaking and want to make the "F" sound, you actually directly place your upper teeth on top of your lower lip and blow air out through your teeth.

B
The "B" sound is the same in Tagalog and English. You make the "B" sound the same as the "P" sound, except at the same time you make the "P" sound you make a sound in your throat. "P" is what linguists call an unvoiced sound and "B" is the same except that it is voiced. Say a "P" and then a "B" and notice how your lips go together the same way, and the only difference is that with the "B" you make a sound in your throat. It is like humming when you make the sound of the letter.

V
In Tagalog, there is no sound like the English "V", so in Tagalog the "V" is pronounced as if it is a "B", but it is not the same in English. The English "V" is the same as "F", except that the "F" is unvoiced and the "V" is voiced. Just like with the English "F", put your upper teeth gently on top of your lower lips with your upper lip forward of your lower lip, but with some space in between, then blow air out through your teeth but at the same time also make a sound in your throat (like you do with a "B").

Th
There is no sound in Tagalog like the English "Th" sound. In fact there are two different "Th" sounds in English. Filipinos tend to pronounce one of these sounds like the letter "S" or "T", and the other sound like "D". But that is not the correct way in English.

Both of the English "Th" sounds are made by placing the end of your tongue between your teeth, as if to bite your tongue, but just touch both your upper teeth and your lower teeth gently against your tongue. Don't bite your tongue off! Bite your tongue very, very gently. Leave your lips open part way and blow air out through your teeth.

The difference between the two "Th" sounds is that one is unvoiced and the other is voiced. The sound of "th" in "teeth" and "bath" is unvoiced, which means you don't make that humming sound in your throat. But the sound of "th" in "the" and "this" is voiced and you make a hum in your throat at the same time you blow air out through your teeth.
