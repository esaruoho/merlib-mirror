Sick and Wrong at the Lake Union Pub 8/2/95

  People who play at BD/SM are seeking intense experiences.  They want
something that will effect their (perhaps jaded) perception.  Sick and
Wrong can do that.  This band has the energy to invigorate and the skill to
make it feel good.

  They played all original (to this listener) tunes with a power that is
based in more than their amps.  They are loud, and they play fast, but
unlike some Seattle area grunge/metal musicians there is an intensity to
their performance that is derived from their involvement with the charged
atmosphere of the local scene.  It is raw and emotional - not refined, but
to refine energy this intense without losing its hard edge would take
nothing short of genius.  This sort of power can only be derived from a
knowledge of and an empathy with their audience.  Perhaps because of their
choice to limit their focus they have an easier task in this, but the
results speak volumes for that decision.  They do very well at handling and
directing the energy in the music to their ends.

  Technically there were flaws.  The mixing was poor.  I could hardly hear
the lyrics over the instruments.  In particular Farrah's voice seemed to
disappear in spite of her obvious efforts.  Perhaps the worst fault I can
find is that the songs and the set were rather briefer than I would have
preferred.

  There was humor in the performance, something that is rare and valuable
in this sort of music.  That they are aware of it's value says much about
their sophistication as performers.  Mr. Wendy's stage presence (complete
with latex mini-dress and 14" rubber prosthesis) is something to behold.

  I arrived in time to catch the rope demo between sets.  There was a very
fetching lady who served as a model for a net tie as well as sundry others
modeling cuffs, a rope/hair-braid/wrist tie and assorted other wrist ties
by both the group and the assembled players.  The atmosphere was friendly
and I, as an unknown lurker, was accepted without comment.  I did note that
despite the arrival of a carrier task force in port today, there was not a
sailor to be seen.  All things considered, a peak musical experience.
Catch these people if you get the chance.
