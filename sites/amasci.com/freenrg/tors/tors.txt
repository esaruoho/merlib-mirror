Mr. Vogt performed time-flow experiments at a couple of these spots by
comparing the frequency of widely-spaced crystal oscillators (at around
30MHz if I recall.)  He found a small effect at the 'Oregon Vortex', like
a few tens of PPM I think.  But it was unclear whether it could have been
caused by temperature drift.  If not, then a crystal oscillator "probe" on
the end of a long cable can be used to map the vortex effect.  Vogt is
local to Seattle and spoke at one of our 'Weird Science' meetings a couple
of years ago. 

His website:

   http://www.wolfe.net/~archive/
   http://www.wolfe.net/~archive/gravity.html


I visited the Oregon Vortex last year, and the height-change effect is as
Fred says:  clearly visible even when not facing the distorted building. 

In addition my wife and I noticed a powerful subjective effect.  While
driving to the site (on a narrow paved farm road) we saw a sign saying
"Vortex 1/4 mile".  But then after many minutes there was nothing.  I was
certain we had traveled a couple of miles and had missed the site.  But
then we came to a crossroads with a sign saying (if I recall correctly)
"vortex 1/8" mile!  After another LONG time driving, we arrived.  I
thought that the distances must have been mismarked on the signs. 

But then while driving out again, we immediately crossed some railroad
tracks. I thought that was strange, because we had just left, yet there
were railroad tracks near the START of the long road, out near the main
highway.  Were there two sets of tracks?  No, because there was the
highway!  Our drive OUT from the vortex seemed to last a couple of
minutes, while the drive IN towards the vortex seemed to last about 20
minutes. 

If the center of the vortex was truely operating with many minutes of
time-slip compared to the outside world, wouldn't this be easily measured?
If the time "phase" is genuinely altered, the speed of light would alter,
and we would see a huge bubble of optical distortion covering the region
like a lens made of aerogel.  Radio waves would be deflected as well,
leading to easily detected "dead zones."  I think that such a huge,
obvious effect is surely subjective.

I tried to detect any deflection in the beam from a laser pointer in the
region of the Seattle vortex Fred Epps told me about, but the wooden boom
that I used was too flexible to detect changes less than 1cm over about
two meters.  And I wasn't certain about the exact location (in Gasworks
Park north of the city.)

The "height change"  effect seen at the Oregon Vortex is supposed to be
objective, and detectable by cameras.  It must be based on the deflection
of light, as if the vortex region was like a distorting lens.  Hey, maybe
somebody could rig up a pair of stronger lasers to project parallel beams
over thousands of meters, then take the device to the region of a vortex
effect and see if the beams remain parallel. Position the lasers a couple
of meters apart, so the beams remain spaced at 2M over a large distance,
then send the beams through a suspected vortex region and see if the 2M
spacing changes at all.  As I found before, the main challenge is to
construct a solid base for the lasers which can be carried from place to
place, but which does not bend significantly or otherwise change the
laser alignment.
