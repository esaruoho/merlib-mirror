Some exciting changes to the Bay Cup and its website


The Bay Cup has three new Yahoo! Groups for people to use. They are:

  • Bay Cup People
    A group for all members of the greater Bay Cup family. This group's primary
    mission is to help facilitate communication among Bay Cup members. For
    example, rather than wondering if someone is going to the next Bay Cup
    event, or even an NAC, check this group, see if someone is offering a ride
    and take him or her up on it. Or, suppose you lost your foil with a green
    grip. Put that information here and perhaps someone who found it can drop
    it off with a friend who will email you that she'll bring it next week.
    That's basically the gist of this group.
  • Bay Cup Coaches
    This group is restricted to invited members, who are primarily the decision
    makers and key personnel at the various clubs in the Bay Cup. The point of
    this group is for these members to notify the administrator (me, Eric) of
    any needed tasks to complete. For example, Joe Schmoe's name is misspelled
    as "Joe Smoe" and so his points are split among two entries in the standings
    page. No problem. Put that to-do item in the group, and I'll get around to
    it. (Sending me email will just get it buried among the other emails I'm
    receiving daily.) On the flip side, I can send a request to club X to get
    the results in to me ASAP because we want to know who won and such. This
    group will be able to handle this communication transaction.
  • Bay Cup Group
    This group of invited only members is the advisory group for dealing with
    the nitty-gritty details of the Bay Cup. As you know, we're no longer a
    loose, informal fencing program. It is time to create some structure to
    the Bay Cup, if only because we have seen discrepancies and weaknesses with
    the informal process. This group will provide guidance to the administrator
    on matters concerning budget, referee standards, allowable expenses, awards
    and other core issues about the Bay Cup and its near future direction.
Join a group or email me to be included in the invited-only groups.

Another new development, courtesy of Jeff Powell at XO Communications, and
a member of the Salle DeCesare club, is a new (hopefully more permanent)
location for the Bay Cup website. We will be purchasing a new URL for the
Bay Cup. It will probably be www.thebaycup.org or something similar to that.
Look on this website for information on the URL change when that comes around.
The Bay Cup pages will also soon get a facelift with a new look and feel.
Expect to see a new look by mid to late March, but definitely before the
next 2003-2004 season.

We hope that these new features will make the Bay Cup fencing experience for
all fencers and parents and coaches a more pleasant one. These changes are
both to help enhance the experience and requirements as we see the Bay Cup's
growth in the near future. We need to be nimble and ready to adapt to new
challenges and opportunities.



What is the Regional Youth Circuit and how do they
relate to the Bay Cup series?


The Regional Youth Circuit events (RYCs) are regional youth events (duh!)
instituted in no small part by the USFA national office, as a way to help
increase youth fencing, regionalize the sport (to help defray travel costs),
and move more "national" quality events to local levels. The genesis for
the RYCs came from Wendell Kubik and his group in Texas, as they tried
(successfully) to grow youth fencing by getting the clubs together to hold
mega regional events.

At around the same time, the Bay Cup grew in the Bay Area, out of the minds
of several club owners, Dan Tibbetts, George Platt, Michael Pacheco, and
Connie Yu. At first, it consisted of several key events, two being the
venerable JCC FallFest and SpringFest. After a while, it grew with more
youth and junior fencers competing. The organizing of the Bay Cup was
made to administrate the competitions as it became more complex.

During the 2000-2001 season, the national office asked the regionalization
committee to look into creating regional events, eventually to take over
for national (youth) events, and perhaps have these regional events be
qualifying paths to the Summer National Championships. The first test was
in the 2001-2002 season with the first Bay Area Regional Youth Circuit held
at Stanford University. The event itself was a valuable test of the concept
and showed that such an event can be organized and conducted at a local
(or regional) level.

At the end of the 2001-2002 season, the regionalization task force committee
met during the Division I Nationals in Louisville KY, to explain some more
(to others) about the RYC concept. It also set some policies in terms of
the numbers of events and such. The discussions there revealed to many
Bay Area attendees and observers that the RYC concept is nothing more than
what the Bay Cup is, except that they envisioned running all weapons and
age categories over a weekend. The Bay Cup series has events spread out
over the course of the season.

So, during the Bay Cup scheduling process, the Bay Cup administration
chose to set three weekends as "mega" youth events, and designate them as
the Pacific Coast's Regional Youth Circuits. (There had been discussions
with other fencing concentrations along the Pacific Coast, most notably
Southern California and Portland/Seattle area, about them hosting RYCs.
Because of the brief time-frame -- four months from the meeting to the
start of the season -- no RYCs were scheduled for other areas in the Pacific
Coast outside of the Bay Area. There are plans for next season, though.)

The decision to designate three weekends as RYCs was made because of several
factors:

  • It was a quick and simple way to comply with the RYC concept as envisioned
    by the national office and the Regionalization Task Force
  • Putting many events into these weekends helped free up other weekends for
    either rest days or other competition days: as the Bay Cup competition
    numbers have grown, most clubs cannot hold more than two events per day,
    thus requiring more days, more facilities, and more events run in parallel
    in different locations.
  • These RYCs offered opportunities for the host to showcase the events and
    put on a more spectacular show than the usual Bay Cup tournament.
For those who are wondering what the Regional Youth Circuit events are, and how
they relate to the Bay Cup, the upshot is that the Bay Area's Regional Youth
Circuit events, are nothing more than mega youth Bay Cup events. They are
Bay Cup tournaments, through and through. There are operational differences,
like requiring fencers to submit entry forms (as opposed to walking in to
sign up) and a bit more formal atmoshpere. Other than that, the events are
just one (or three) of your six Bay Cup events.

Know the rules...


Recent incidents at Bay Cup tournaments have brought about a request that
fencers and Bout Committee personel and referees know and properly apply
the rules concerning the operations of the competition. Most fencers and
(hopefully!) referees are aware of most infractions rules. But other rules
have been misapplied or ignored.

Specifically, all foil and epee events MUST check the tip weights at
each bout. The host organizer is responsible for supplying the test
weights. We do not have armorers available to perform weapons check (in
most cases), so other weapons check steps will be excluded.

Another aspect is withdrawal from competition before being eliminated.
Fencers cannot withdraw except for injury and other emergencies that the
bout committee accepts as a valid reason. Withdrawing without a valid
reason earns a black card for the fencer and exclusion from the results.

Ignorance of the rules is no excuse.



Junior Sabre program key to taking lead in
Junior Bay Cup Club standings


As in past Bay Cup seasons, the junior sabre events will prove to be the
key factor in determining success in the Junior Bay Cup Club points totals.
In prior years, sabre powerhouse Sacramento Fencing Club took consecutive
years honors just through the strength of their sabre squad, consistently
supplying ten to fifteen competitors to each junior sabre event, the
junior men's sabre, the junior women's sabre, and the youth-12 mixed sabre.
This year, with the return of junior sabre counting towards Bay Cup points,
CFA pulls out with a strategic lead by providing competitors, albeit not as
large in numbers compared to SacFC, to the junior sabre program.

The Bay Cup program hopes this move will inspire other clubs to step up with
their own sabre programs and bring competition and equality to the junior
sabre program in the Bay Area. With a stronger sabre program, everyone
benefits, and sabre fencing in the Bay Area can expect to see a renaissance
since its last heyday in the mid-70s and early-80s (that's 1970s and 1980s
for some of you folks).



Some successes, more work to do, from Orlando FL NAC


The first North American Cup, held at Disneyworld's Wide World of Sports
in Orlando, Florida, showed surprising strengths in Bay Area fencers, as
well as weak areas where fencers can now concentrate on improving.

Top of the list goes to Pilar Alicea of HFC, who won the Div II Women's
Foil competition, earning her B02. Natalie Wang, LFF, was third in that
event.

Nascent cadet epeeists, Nathaniel Sulat from GGFC and Michael Pearce of HFC
finished 5th and 6th respectively in the tough cadet Men's Epee competition.

Sam Perkins from SFA earned his C02 with a second-place finish in the
Division III Men's Foil event. Colin Lindsay from TFC took sixth in that
same event, as well as in the Division II Men's Foil. Florence Lee of CFA
earned her C02 with a 3rd-place finish in the Division III Women's Foil.

Joel Kaufman from EBFG and Philip Hedges, TFC, both made top-8 (3rd and 8th
respectively) in Division II Men's Epee, on the last day of competition.
Ali Glasser Stanf/HFC, seeded first coming out of the pools, finished 5th in
Cadet Women's Foil. Lastly, Clinton Kershaw from Sier fenced admirably,
losing to eventual winner Parker Miner, to take the bronze in the very tough
Cadet Men's Foil.

That said, there was a big gap between the results of the top finishers and
the others who attended. Many favored fencers did not do as well as hoped
or expected. Part of the reason may be the first-event nerves hitting some
fencers at this time of the year. Part may be the long jet-lagging distance
West Coast fencers had to face getting to Orlando. And from some observers,
part of the reason is the lack of competition against a tougher group of
opponents. Many of the younger fencers coming out of the youth-level
competition have not experienced fencing with the "big boys" and need more
experience dealing with hard-hitting, faster moving, more battle-tested
veterans.

The main competitive difference this observer noted was the "will to win"
drive present in the top fencers from around the country versus those
who showed up to "test the waters" (and perhaps take in a day or two in
Disney World, which is a perfectly good reason to attend this competition).

As safe and pretty as fencing is, the bottom line is the visceral will to
defeat an opponent. The difference between the champions of the day and
the silver medalists isn't technique or speed or strength. It's the heart
and the will to win. The winners were those who screamed after each touch.
They are those who know they will score the next touch even before the
referee gives the command, "fence!" It's that absolute confidence, that
focus, that sense of invincibility that Bay Area fencers need to develop.
The winning fencer is, as Olympian Peter Westbrook describes it, "harnessing
the anger." The winning fencer draw all the power from within to control
and influence the whole bout. Then, every touch will go his or her way.
The psychological influence aspect of the sport is usually the deciding
factor when equals are matched. Our Bay Area fencers, especially the
younger crowd, have not developed this killer instinct. It will benefit
some Bay Area fencers with more "combat" experience, especially those
events available locally in the Bay Cup.



Long Beach Invitational a first test for Bay Cup fencers


The first large tournament of the season was the Long Beach Invitational,
held at Cal State Long Beach on October 5-6, 2002. Bay Cup fencers did
exceptionally well, especially in men's epee and women's foil. Men's
foil had a lower than usual turn-out from Bay Cup fencers, but the other
events had ample representation from the Bay Cup fencers.

Winning the gold in mixed epee is Keith Lichten of East Bay Fencers' Gym.
Taking second is Nicolas Sallembien from Stanford Fencing Club. Also in
the top-8 are three fencers from Golden Gate Fencing Center: Gyorgy
Ordody, Charles Cox, and Dylan Bethel, who earned his A2002. So five of
the top-8 were Bay Cup fencers.

In mixed foil, Dmitri Kirk-Gordon finished second, losing to Cedric Anen.
Clinton Kershaw, a frequent Bay Cup top finisher took fifth, also losing
to Anen in the quarter-finals. Eric Dew finished ninth, losing a tough
bout to Jeffrey Chang with many bad calls by the referee.

Bay Cup fencers did not fare well in mixed sabre, as Haj Matsukata doing
the best, finishing 13th. David Lee from Sacramento Fencing Club took
fifteenth, and Mario "Antonio Banderas Jr/Bruce Capin Jr" Martinez left
the DEs at eighteenth place.

Women epeeists from the Bay Cup did not have a large presence at the Long
Beach Invitational. Still, Janel Obenchain finished fifth and Kimberly
Gerriets finished seventh. Heath Cabot, the winner of the first Open
Women's Epee in Concord two weekends ago, finished eighteenth.

Bay Cup women foilists Tammy Lee (GGFC) and Jade Falcon (HFC) made it into
the top-8, but lost in the first rounds to Puerto Rican fencers. Still,
it was a good showing, with Simone LaPay (PFC) being the only other from
the Bay Cup area to attend. She finished 24th out of 37.

Jade Falcon did better in women's sabre, as she finished second, losing to
Leslie Taft from San Bernardino. Along with Jade were Elizabeth Earls in
a suprising third place, Jeanette Strumillo in fifth, and Rita Comes just
missing out from the top-8 with a ninth place finish. Liz Enochs from
HFC picked up the tail end with an eleventh place result.

The Long Beach Invitational is the first major event in the Pacific Coast
Section, and is always a good warm-up for most fencers in the section, as
well as neighboring states like Colorado, Arizona, New Mexico, Oregon, and
Washington. The mixed epee had 103 competitors, the mixed foil had 97.
These are large events, and Craig Bansmer, Bonnie Ahern, and Jo Redmon
all did a fantastic job running the tournament. This year's prizes were
substantially nicer and bigger (and heavier) than last year's crystal
displays.


Halberstadt takes early lead in the Senior Bay Cup


After the completion of almost all senior events (just missing the
Category-2 Women's Foil), Halberstadt takes a surmountable, but commanding
lead in the Senior Bay Cup Club Standings. The Halberstadt team was
fueled by the strength of its women fencers during the last weekend of
September. HFC women fencers came in mass to Concord, CA, for the Golden
State Three-Weapon Women's Fencing Competition.

Pilar Alicea led the HFC women's squad with a silver-medal finish in the
foil event, losing to two-time winner, Doris Willette. Lucia Mattox from
HFC earned her D02 taking third (losing to Pilar), tying with Simone LaPay
from PFC. Simone re-earned her D02 the hard way, defeating the number
four and five seeded fencers in the Direct Elimination table, before
eventually meeting up with Willette.

Sue Vellone, the third HFC foilist rounded out the finish with a top-16
result. Not her best, but good for an early season competition.

An almost identical result occurred in women's epee as Alexandra Mummery
silvered, losing to GGFC's Heath Cabot. Tied for third were Janel
Obenchain from GGFC and Victoria Bowen from HFC. Laura Ochoa-Frongia
was the third HFC epeeist and she finished thirteenth.

And in yet another almost identical finish, Pilar Alicea took home her
second silver in sabre, losing to Stanford's Rita Comes, while
teammate Liz Enochs bronzed along with Jody Ambalong from LFF.

Lucky for the rest of the clubs in the Bay Area that HFC neglected to
send in their top women fencers, including some top foilists Karen
Martos, Manel Kappagodda, and Jade Falcon. Had they done so, HFC's
lead over the second-place team, Stanford FC, might have grown by another
2000 points.

The women's epee achieved critical mass with three B's, one A, a C,
and fifteen competitors, matching the largest showing from last season.
On another positive note, the women's sabre's attendance broke into
double-digits as ten competitors joined in the fray. That number also
matched the largest turn-out for women's sabre from last season. Given
that most Sacramento women sabre fencers are no longer participating in
the Bay Cup, this number is a positive sign of improving women's sabre
participation in the Bay Area. Indeed, this OWS1 size is greater than
the seven men who attended the OMS1 at Stanford last weekend.

It is also heartening to see some "new blood" in women's epee, with two
new fencers from CFA: Heather Stephenson, earning her E02 in her very
first tournament, and Julia Roshkovsky. Valeria Morrison from Swordplay
Fencing Academy is another newcomer to the sport. Let's give them all a
big Bay Cup welcome.

Results from the Junior Epee on Saturday, September 28, and Junior Foil/
Youth-14 Epee on Sunday will be announced soon. The Junior Bay Cup's
Club Standings are currently led by Sierra Fencing Center. However, only
Women's Foil and Epee have been conducted, and it's too soon to tell
which club will make a definite pull ahead.



First weekends' results in the Senior Bay Cup
competition heating up with large attendance
and what appears to be a tight race.


The 2002-2003 Senior Bay Cup kicked off the season with three simultaneous
competitions, foil in San Francisco, sabre in Stanford, and epee in San
Jose. The foil events had a total of 59 entrants in two events. It is
the largest ever Category-2 Men's foil Bay Cup event, and bodes well for
continued growth of foil in the Bay Area.

The foil event started off with the Category-2 competition with Jim
Fruchterman from Palo Alto (TFC) winning a close bout, 9-8 in overtime.
John Zelkowski from San Francisco (HFC) won the open men's, defeating
Los Altoan Kevin Urbain with very little problems (sorry, Kev, but that's
how I saw it).

The sabre event at Stanford was not as well attended as can be. However,
we are expecting continued mediocre turn-out for sabre until the Bay Area
can pull itself up by its bootstraps in regards to sabre. Scott Stimson,
back from an ankle injury last season, defeated Matt Carauddo for the
gold in the Cat-2 mixed event. Haj Matsukata, the Ninja from HFC, defeated
Mario "Antonio Banderas Jr/Maestro Capin Jr" Martinez to capture the first
Open Men's Sabre event. On a positive note, this sabre Bay Cup, as well
as the foil Bay Cup in San Francisco, were both officiated by assigned,
non-competing referees (excepting those who were taking the refereeing
seminar and practicing while fencing).

The epee event at CFA's home turf was also well-attended. The Category-2
event had 22 competitors, near the top-end of the expected range. The
Open event had 29. Bay Area epee has always been top-heavy with plenty
of A-rated fencers. This event did not disappoint, with 5 A's and 4 B's
in the Open Epee.

The scary thought to the number of fencers in these events is that a good
number of junior fencers were absent because they decided to attend the
Junior World Cup in Louisville, KY. Top fencers like Dmitri Kirk-Gordon,
Emerson DiNapoli, Josh Lepold, Max Williams, Martin Lee, Bruce Vail,
Charles Cox, Eric Hohmeister, and several others, might have bumped the
entry numbers in foil and epee above the 40-competitor marks.

This coming weekend will include junior epee and foil in Sans Francisco
and Jose, and women's events in all three weapons in Concord. Let's see
what the turn-out will be for these events.


New season starts in mid-September;
lots of new items this season.


The first Bay Cup event for the 2002-2003 season will be the Junior
Women's Foil and Epee at upstart club, Davis Fencing Academy. Marcos
Lucchetti and Simon Pitfield will both organize and host this event.

The 2002-2003 season will see several new changes. The first change
will be the incorporation of junior sabre into the Bay Cup family.
Junior sabre fencers will now expect to win prizes commensurate with
other junior Bay Cup event winners. Most of the junior sabre events
are still set in the Sacramento area, and will be run at the DFA club.

The second change is a complete series of six veteran events. Veterans
will have their separate registration fee ($40) and will also earn prizes
at the end of the season. The only negative consequence of having a
separate veterans event will be the lengthening of the awards picnic due
to ever more prizes handed out. On the other hand, Jerry DeRaad may
actually arrive to pick up his prizes before the picnic's over.

We have also split the Youth-12 mixed foil into a Youth-12 boys foil and
a Youth-12 girls foil. The number of boys now fencing in the Youth-12
category has grown so large that keeping a mixed foil event may prove
to be problematic.

The recent changes to the USFA classification chart should also be good
news for all veteran and junior/youth fencers. Age-restricted events will
now be rated events, as per the classification chart. Round up your
fifteen or so fencers and work on earning or upgrading your classification!

For example, given some of our previous Bay Cup tournaments, we can
expect to see several new C's generated from our Youth-12 events by the end
of the season. We may see some new C's from the veterans events, given
that a number of fairly strong fencers compete in the veterans.

Also, we are pleased to submit to you a sorted version of the schedule
with the dates and location for each event identified for your visual
convenience. Please check the sorted version page.

Because of the tremendous growth in fencing in the Bay Area, two FOC
members, Greg Massialas and Peter Burchard, will initiate and create
a series of refereeing seminars that will be held prior to some Bay Cup
events. Fencers wishing to earn a national level rating (5 or higher)
for refereeing are encouraged to attend the seminars, take part in the
practical during the events, and schedule a time to pass the written
portion of the test.

Lastly, we are instituting a new registration system utilizing Bay Cup
registration cards. These cards will be sold by the clubs to their
individual members (unattached fencers will buy them at the tournaments)
and will be used at Bay Cup events to help expedite registration and
check-in. A detailed description will be available on-line soon, and
at all clubs so that club officers and fencers will know how to use
(and how to avoid abuse of) the cards. The goal of the cards is primarily
to help speed up seeding the initial pools.

Hope to see you all at the next Bay Cup event!
