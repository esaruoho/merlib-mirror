1852 -- High Seas: 1,400-ton paddle-wheel steamer HMS Birkenhead runs aground on rocks near the Cape of Good Hope. As the ship began to sink, soldiers were ordered to stand in ranks on deck while women & children were loaded into lifeboats. Some 200 were saved by lifeboats & another 30-40 were pulled from the wreckage of boat, but 420 others died, almost all soldiers. This incident established the now traditional concepts of "women & children first" & "going down with the ship".

1882 -- B. Traven lives (1882?-1969), Pozna, Poland. Anarchist author/novelist, aka Ret Marut, Hal Croves, Bruno Traven, Traven Torsvan, Otto Feige. Spent a portion of his life hiding his tracks, changing identity, country, & jobs. Daily Bleed Saint November 13.
alt sp; Posnan, Posanie, Poznan, Posen

Marut/Traven published the anarchist Munich newspaper "Der Ziegelbrenner" between 1917 & 1921. With the proclomation of the Republic of Bavaria, April 7, 1919, Ret Marut worked with fellow anarchists Gustav Landauer & Eric Muhsam.

His first novel The Death Ship appeared under the name B. Traven. The majority of Traven's novels, on the surface adventure books, reveal the sources & conditions which spark revolt: The Treasure of the Sierra Madres; Revolt of the Hanged; White Rose, etc.

Extracts of "Der Ziegelbrenner" were published in French in 1994, as In the Freest State of the World.

1894 -- France: Henry Le Fvre lives, Courbevoie. (1894-1991) Vegetarian, pacifist, anarchist, & publisher of "Le No Naturien", revue des ides philosophiques etnaturiennes.



1898 -- US: After scheduled visits to Baltimore & Washington, D.C., Emma Goldman is invited to Pittsburgh & coal mining towns in western Pennsylvania by anarchists Carl Nold & Henry Bauer in association with the International Workingmen's Association (IWA).
Though the Pittsburgh region is heavily populated by Germans, most of Emma Goldman's speaking engagements, February 23-March 12, are purposely conducted in English.

Talks include "Patriotism," with specific reference to the miners shot by the police at Hazleton, Pa., in September, & the possibility of war between Spain & the US. She addresses the Monaca, Pa., local of the Glass Blowers' Union, one of the most conservative unions in the country. Lectures in western coal mining towns include McKeesport, Roscoe, West Newton, & Homestead; Emma is also scheduled to speak in Beaver Falls, Carnegie, Duquesne, Charleroi, & Tarentum. Her engagement in Allegheny is canceled when owners of the liberal Northside Turner Hall refuse to let her speak.

Emma suffers several "nervous attacks" from the strain of continuous lecturing.




1899 -- Erich Kstner (1899-1974) lives. German satirist/poet/novelist, whose military experiences made him pacifist & opponent of totalitarian systems. Best known for his children's books, but they were not popular among Nazis. Kstner wrote Emil & the Detectives (1929), The School of Dictators (1956), among others.
http://www.kirjasto.sci.fi/kastner.htm


1899 -- France: Emile Bauchet lives (1899-1973), Roissy (prs de Paris). Militant anarchist & pacifist.

Bauchet deserted from the Army in 1919, eluding military "justice" for 10 years.

In 1927, Bauchet begins collaborating with Alphonse Barb on "Le Semeur" & declares himself a conscientious objector. Arrested in 1929 for his earlier desertion, he was sent to prison, despite the support of Louis Lecoin, Han Ryner & George Pioch at his trial.

Released in April 1930, Bauchet works with Alphonse's brother, the anarchist Paul Barb. He joined the "Ligue Internationale des Combattants de la Paix", & became secretary of the national office for the fdration du Calvados.

Bauchet was extremely active in the post-war period, working with various organizations & pacifist groups.




1901 -- Ivar Lo-Johansson (1901-1990) lives. Swedish author/social critic, recognized for his working class & landless peasant novels & short stories. His books include The Share Croppers (1936-36), Proletarians of the Earth (1941).
http://www.kirjasto.sci.fi/ivarlo.htm



1903 -- Jean-Baptiste Clement (1836-1903) dies, Paris. Communard, poet, singer & author of the famous song "The Time of Cherries".

Clement was several times sent to prison for his writings & lampoons. During the Paris Commune he ended up, on May 28, as one of the last on the barricades, along with Varlin & Ferr. He was forced into hiding, taking refuge in England.

Condemned to death in absentia, he returned to France following the Amnesty of 1879. Clement became a socialist & trade union militant, active particularly in the Ardennes.

Clement's "The Time of Cherries," written in 1866, became the song of the Paris Commune & was dedicated to the anarchist Louise Michel, who was also on that last barricade with Clement.

Now has a street named for him. http://www.paris-france.org/CARTO/NOMENCLATURE/4849.nom.html
