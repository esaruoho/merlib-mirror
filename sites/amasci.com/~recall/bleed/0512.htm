1310 -- Fifty-four Knights Templars burned at the stake as heretics in France.


1738 -- "London," a poem, is published anonymously. Samuel Johnson's first significant work. http://www.english.upenn.edu/~jlynch/Johnson/


1797 -- The Nore fleet mutineers declare themselves a "floating republic", under the Presidency of seaman Richard Parker. Too radical to get support from the Spithead mutineers (16 ships-of-the-line refused to sail.), they are unfortunately trashed by 14 June.
Source: 'Calendar Riots'


1812 -- Nonsense rhymer, artist, Edward Lear lives, London.

"There once was a man named Nation, Who worked for a radio station.

Although he was tall, His hands were too small, Wee paws for station identification."


http://www.accd.edu/sac/english/bailey/lear.htm



1820 -- Florence Nightingale (health activist, nurse: promoted the nursing profession, contributed to modern nursing procedures, founded Nightingale Training School for Nurses) lives. http://www.fordham.edu/halsall/mod/nightingale-rural.html


1828 -- Voice From the Grave?: Painter/poet Dante Gabriel Rossetti lives 1828-1882).
A founding member of Pre-Raphaelite Brotherhood, wanting to bring back into art a pre-Renaissance purity of style & aim. The movement was waning, but new disciples Edward Burne-Jones & William Morris brought fresh enthusiasm.

When his wife died Rossetti buried the only complete manuscript of his poems with her. Changed his mind & the manuscript was later recovered & published in 1870.

http://library.utoronto.ca/www/utel/rp/authors/rossettg.html
http://www.kirjasto.sci.fi/dgrosset.htm



1876 -- Louis Eugne Jakmin (aka Jacquemin) lives, Paris, France. Blacksmith, anarchist propagandist, antimilitarist, militant syndicalist.
Secretary of la fdration communiste anarchiste (1913), manager of "libertaire", & participant in the newspaper "Le rveil Anarchiste ouvrier", from 1912 to the end of 1913.

1888 -- US: Charles Sherrill of the Yale track team became the first runner to use the crouching start for a fast break in a foot race.


1898 -- US: Louisiana adopts new constitution with "grandfather clause" designed to eliminate black voters.

1898 -- Puerto Rico: After exchanging fire on the 10th between Spanish forces & the U.S.S. Yale, today a squadron of 12 U.S. peace-loving ships commanded by Rear Adm. William T. Sampson bombards San Juan without provocation or warning.

1907 -- Decadent Symbolist writer Joris-Karl Huysmans dies. Wrote rebours (Against the Grain), & L-bas (Down There). http://www.gatzke.org/ldm119.htm



1916 -- Connolly Ain't Nothing But a Train Station in Dublin?: Execution of James Connolly, IWW organizer & Irish freedom fighter.

James Connolly is executed for his part in this spring's Easter Rebellion, which challenged British rule in Dublin. Already wounded from the uprising, Connolly is shot as he sits in a chair. He is the 15th Easter Rebellion leader executed this last week. An Irish woman will describe the killing "It was like watching a stream of blood coming from under a closed door."

1939 -- Ron Ziegler, infamous Press Secretary for Beloved & Respected Comrade Leader President Dick ("I am a pacifist") M Nixon, lives.

1941 -- Juhani Peltonen lives (1941 - 1998). Finnish novelist, playwright & poet. http://www.kirjasto.sci.fi/jpelton.htm


1946 -- Founding Congress of the Anarchist Federation of Japan held in Tokyo.


1947 -- Convicted "trunk murderess" Winnie Ruth Judd escaped from the Arizona State Insane Hospital for the third time. She was recaptured 12 hours later (see Daily Bleed, 16 October). Judd escaped seven times from the mental hospital where she was sent. On one occasion she walked across the desert for seven days. The last time she escaped she eluded police for seven years.

1950 -- Bowled Over?: The American Bowling Congress abolishes its white males-only membership restriction after 34 years. The recalcitrant pins remain all-white though. http://www.miltonbowling.com/

1968 -- France: Paris, 1968. At least a thousand people have joined the few dozen students who had seized the Fine Arts School & turned it into a poster factory. Working in teams of 200, & submitting each design to the Sorbonne General Assembly, during the six-week occupation they were able to put out 350 different posters in print runs of tens of thousands. The imagination, impatience & forcefulness with which they mocked authority incited astonishment & delight below -- & grim horror above. http://www.physik.uni-regensburg.de/~sij17370/situ/mai68.html



1970 -- Nelly Sachs, dies, Stockholm, Sweden, her home since escaping Nazism in 1940. Wrote "O die Schornsteine" ("O the Chimneys"), in which Israel's body drifts upward as smoke from Nazi death camps. Gets Nobel in 1966 (co-recipient with S. Y. Agnon). http://nobel.sdsc.edu/literature/laureates/1966/sachs-autobio.html

1975 -- 39 members of "Mayaguez" crew seized by Cambodia.

1978 -- From the And You Thought We Had This Straightened Out By Now file: The National Oceanic & Atmospheric Administration announces it will alternate men's & women's names in the naming of hurricanes. It was seen as an attempt at fair play. Hurricanes were named for women, until NOAA succumbed to women's groups who were demanding that Atlantic storms be given unisex names. Typhoid Harry?

"It's not fair that women should get all the attention for causing damage & destruction," one women's activist claimed. David, Allen, Hugo & Andrew agreed.
