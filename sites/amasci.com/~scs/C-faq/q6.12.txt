 Question 6.12 
 Since array references decay into pointers,
if arr is an array,
what's
the difference between
 arr and &arr ?

The type.
 In
Standard C,
 &arr yields a pointer,
of type pointer-to-array-of-T,
to the entire array.
(In
pre-ANSI C,
the & in &arr generally elicited a warning,
and was generally

ignored.)
Under all C compilers,
a
simple
reference
(without an explicit & )
to an array
yields a pointer,
of type pointer-to-T,
to the array's first element.
(See
also questions
 6.3 ,
 6.13 ,
and
 6.18 .)

 References:

ANSI Sec. 3.2.2.1, Sec. 3.3.3.2

ISO Sec. 6.2.2.1, Sec. 6.3.3.2
