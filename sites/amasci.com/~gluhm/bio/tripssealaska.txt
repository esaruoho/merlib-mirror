 May, 2002. Southeast Alaska. Charter yacht Ursa Major 
 motors into Saook Bay, Baranof Island, stopping 
 briefly to toss out shrimp pots. The water depth is 160 feet. 
 Crab pots are pitched at 60 feet; we set anchor at 30. The halibut
 fishers on board drop lines over the side. In the meadow at 
 the head of the bay, five brown bear mow down sedges. Were 
 surrounded by forested hills and snow-capped peaks. We launch 
 kayaks for a close look at the bears - and the sea ducks 
 like surf scoter, harlequin, and Barrows goldeneye. The 
 hysterical, ascending trill of the Swainsons thrush descends
 from the tree tops. 

 For three weeks in Southeast, we spent most nights in bays like this. 

 At Anan Creek, hundreds of migrating surf and white-winged scoters, Barrows and 
 common goldeneye and harlequin duck floated on the bay. At Red 
 Bluff bay on Baranof, a waterfall-generated down draft nearly
 knocked us over. At Lituya bay, up in Glacier Bay National 
 Park, we anchored before the stark palisade of the Fairweather range that rises - like a
 moonscape - faster and higher out of the sea than any mountain
 range on the planet. 

 We pitched no tent, cooked no meals, hung no food. 

 We paddled kayaks every day as far as we liked. We paddled 
 remote, pristine, protected bays. We saw breaching humpback
 whales and brown bear frolicking in spring meadows. 
 We ate 
 gourmet meals and took hot showers at night.

 It was wild side easy.
