August 16, 2001

The WB network has approved the production of a pilot for a potential new Oz television series, based on the books. The pilot is being written by David Hayter (X-Men). The show will be set sixty years after the adventures of Dorothy and her companions, and will feature a young woman from America leading a revolt against the Emerald City. Lawrence Bender, Kevin Brown, and Joel Smith will be co-executive producers with Hayter. (Please note that the approval of a pilot does not necessarily mean that the pilot will be made or broadcast, or lead into a series.)

July 14, 2001

Tonight at the International Wizard of Oz Club's annual Winkie Convention, a number of awards and other Club news were announced:

  • The L. Frank Baum Memorial Award, the Club's highest honor which is presented annually for outstanding achievement in Oz research or Club service, was presented to writers Willard Carroll and Michael O. Riley. Carroll was the co-author of the 1999 book 100 Years of Oz (the photographs in that book were of items from his extensive collection), and wrote the forthcoming book I, Toto, the "autobiography" of the dog who played Toto in the classic 1939 film version of The Wizard of Oz. Carroll also produced the Oz Kids series of videotapes. Co-winner Riley wrote the book Oz and Beyond: The Fantasy World of L. Frank Baum, an examination of how Baum created Oz and how it changed over the course of the series.
  • The Winkie Award, presented to a contributor to the Winkie Conventions, was given to Lynn Beltz.
  • The Board of Directors, at their annual meeting, accepted the resignation of Jane Albright as Club Vice-President, and appointed Michael Gessel to fulfill the remainder of the current term. Robert Baum, great-grandson of Oz creator L. Frank Baum, was appointed to fill Gessel's now vacant spot on the Board of Directors.

July 1, 2001

The production and mailing gremlins got in the works again, but the Spring 2001 edition of The Baum Bugle, the journal of the International Wizard of Oz Club, is now being sent out to Club members. This is an especially important issue, as it's not only the first issue of the new millennium and the second century of Oz, it's also the first issue under new editor Atticus Gannaway.

In this issue:

  • The wraparound cover reproduces the endpapers for Dot and Tot of Merryland, the L. Frank Baum/W. W. Denslow book of 1901.
  • The inside front cover features a display of Munchkin figures from the 2000 Oz Centennial Convention, used to decorate the dining hall.
  • New editor Gannaway writes his first "Letter from the Editor."
  • "Oz and Ends" catalogs a number of recent Oz references on telelvision, in film, and on radio, and also talks about the new LeapFrog interactive version of The Wizard of Oz and reviews a stage production of Christmas in the Land of Oz in Guntersville, Alabama.
  • "Dot and Tot of Merryland: An Appreciation" by Barbara S. Koelle talks about Baum's children's fantasy book of 1901, in celebration of its centennial, and finds it's not as bad as some of its latter-day critics would have us believe.
  • "A Collector's Oz Adventures in the 1950s and 1960s" by Club founder Justin G. Schiller talks about how collecting Oz books has changed over the last fifty years, and includes personal anecdotes of his collecting experiences.
  • "A Chairperson's Perspective: Behind the Scenes at the Oz Centennial Celebration" by Jane Albright gives just that: It's both an overview of the Club's 2000 convention celebrating one hundred years of Oz and an insider's view of how it all came together.
  • "Childish or Childlike?:The Emerald City of Oz and Beyond" by Kenneth J. Reckford examines the roles of children, especially Dorothy, in L. Frank Baum's Oz books and contrasts them with the adults in the books who still act like children.
  • An extra long edition of "The Oz Bookshelf" reviews Baum's Road to Oz: The Dakota Years, edited by Nancy Tystad Koupal; The Wizard of Oz: The Film Classic Comes to Life with Sound and Stunning Three-Dimension by Jay Scarfone and William Stillman; Oz: The Hundredth Anniversary Celebration, edited by Peter Glassman; Oz-Story 6, edited by David Maxine; American Fairy Tales: From Rip Van Winkle to the Rootabega Stories, an anthology that includes tales from L. Frank Baum and Ruth Plumly Thompson, and edited by Neil Philip; Abducted to Oz by Robert Evans and Chris Dulabone; and Golden Wizdom: Beyond the Emerald City by Ilene Kimsey.
  • "Oz in the News" covers a number of recent Oz articles, including many book reviews; the 2000 Oz festival in Chesterton, Indiana; the "Century of Oz" exhibit at the Los Angeles Public Library; comparisons of the recent film How the Grinch Stole Christmas to The Wizard of Oz; the television miniseries Me and My Shadows: Life with Judy Garland; the continuing saga of trying to construct a Land of Oz amusement park and resort in Kansas; and numerous Oz centennial celebrations from around the country.
  • The inside back cover reproduces Tod Machin's Oz centennial logo.
Also mailed with this issue of The Baum Bugle are the latest editions of the Club's two newsletters, The Oz Gazette (aimed at kids) and The Oz Observer (for all members). Both publications are also under the guidance of a new editor, Peter Schulenberg.

March 3, 2001

Some production and distribution delays kept it out of members' hands for a little while, but the Winter 2000 issue of The Baum Bugle, the journal of the International Wizard of Oz Club, has now been sent out, making a fine swansong for outgoing editor-in-chief William Stillman.

In this issue:

  • The front cover features Dorothy and her famous friends riding the sky-lift balloons at the Land of Oz park in Banner Elk, North Carolina.
  • The annual Bugle Oz quiz is presented (with answers elsewhere in the issue).
  • "Oz and Ends" includes a number of items:
    • The Online Computer Library Center in Dublin, Ohio, has created an Oz-themed video to teach about using the World Wide Web. (You can find out information, and download the video, at http://www.oclc.org/oclc/new/announce/wizard.)
    • An edition of The Wizard of Oz, signed by a number of the cast members of the famous 1939 MGM film version of story, was sold at auction on July 19, 2000 for $49,300.
    • USA Today ran a list of favorite quotes from movies. At number nine was, "There's no place like home." "Toto, I've got a feeling we're not in Kansas anymore" also made the top twenty.
    • An update on the fothcoming (we hope) television series Lost in Oz: The developers are currently in negotiation with Columbia Tristar.
    • The Franklin Mint has released a musical portrait doll of Judy Garland as Dorothy, which includes a sound chip with sixty seconds of "Over the Rainbow" recorded on it. Those interested in acquiring the figure can call 1-800-THE MINT, or see the website at http://www/franklinmint.com.
    • New stage productions of The Wizard of Oz were presented in Japan and Sweden.
    • Lion of Oz, the animated prequel to The Wizard of Oz based on a book by Roger S. Baum, was released on Sony home video and DVD.
    • Autograph dealers Profiles in History have a number of Oz-related signatures for sale. They can be found on the World Wide Web at http://www.profilesinhistory.com.
    • The Children's Room of the New York Public Library exhibited a number of Oz items from June through September 2000, in commemoration of the book's 100th anniversary.
    • Facemankers, Inc, creators of parade costumes, now has an Oz line. For details, see their website at http://www.facemakersincorporated.com.
  • "Ruth Plumly Thompson, Pen Pal" by Helen Didriksen examines the letters Thompson wrote as Perky Puppet in the magazine Jack and Jill from 1962 until 1969.
  • The passing of Royal Historian Eloise Jarvis McGraw on November 30, 2000, was observed.
  • "Mike and Ann in Oz" presents a series of pages from the Christmas 1958 Miles Kimball catalog in which the title characters go to Oz and meet several favorite old friends, who all confess they shop at Miles Kimball...
  • "The MGM Scrapbook" profiles Caren Marsh-Doll, a previously unknown double for Judy Garland during the filming of The Wizard of Oz; shows a new picture of Terry (AKA Toto) with some of her canine colleagues at a 1941 Valentine's Day celebration; and presents Judy Garland in a Halloween-themed photo from 1939, in which she's wearing a costume from Babes in Arms, but being spooked by a familiar looking witch.
  • Ads, sketches, and publicity material for the 1967-68 television series Off to See the Wizard are reproduced, including a color cel of the Scarecrow on the back cover.
  • "Apple Trees, Yellow Bricks, and Souvenirs -- Oh My! Banner Elk's Land of Oz" by Sean Barrett examines the history of the now-defunct Oz-themed amusement park in North Carolina. Barrett also contributes a checklist of souvenirs sold or given out at the park over the years.
  • "Selling Larry Semon's Oz" by William Stillman looks at the publicity and release of the 1929 silent film version of The Wizard of Oz. Several photos are reproduced, including a color center spread and one on the inside front cover.
  • "Ken McLellan's" Cartoon Dream" by Stillman looks at the attempt by animator McLellan to make a series of Oz cartoons, including sketches and character profiles.
  • "The Oz Bookshelf" reviews The Hidden Prince of Oz by Gina Wickwar; A Foolish Fable from Oz, The Ridiculous Rivals in Oz, I Want to Grow Up in Oz, and The Land Before Oz, all by Marin Xiques and Chris Dulabone; the British paperback release of Visitors from Oz by Martin Gardner; Der Zauberer von Oz, a new German translation by Freya Stephan-Kühn of The Wizard of Oz; La Mirinda Sorcisto de Oz, a translation of The Wizard of Oz into Esperanto by H. R. Dreyer; a new British edition of The Wizard of Oz, illustrated by Michael Foreman; Heart of the Tin Man: The Collected Writings of Jack Haley, edited by Mitchell Cohen; the anthology Dunkiton 8, edited by Ruth Berman; the 100th anniversary reissue of The Wizard of Oz illustrated by Charles Santore; and a new adaptation of The Wizard of Oz by Kimberly Morris, illustrated by Mauro Evangelista.
  • "Oz in the News" summarizes Oz-related news from around the world, including the book's centennial and the Oz Club's Centennial Convention; the screening of the movie on the Turner Classic Movies cable channel; the forthcoming Fox television movie The O. Z.; the Harry Potter books (those articles that mention Oz as well!); the annual Oz festivals in Aberdeen, South Dakota, Chesterton, Indiana, Liberal, Kansas, and Chicago; various stage productions of the story; the film Nurse Betty (many of which compared Kansas resident Betty to Dorothy); and comments and reviews of the revised edition of The Annotated Wizard of Oz and the new cookbook Cooking in Oz.
  • "An Oz Oddity" looks at the 1929 edition of Briarpatch, the yearbook of Sweet Briar College of Virginia. The annual's theme was The Patchwork Girl of Oz, and several illustrations from that book were used throughout the yearbook. The title page is reproduced in color on the inside front cover.

Rumor Control
(Because of the many questions I am asked about possible forthcoming Oz projects or other bits of pseudo-news, I have added this section to answer some of these inquiries.)
Alliance Atlantis is working with ABC television on a mini-series based on the novel Wicked, by Gregory Maguire. You can read their press release about the project here. (Demi Moore had originally bought the rights to the novel, but when her contact expired, Alliance Atlantis stepped in. Moore may still be involved with the mini-series, however.) Wicked is the life story of the Wicked Witch of the West, which shows her to be more misunderstood than evil. In a related development, producer Marc Platt is working to bring Wicked to the musical stage.
Another television project on the drawing board is The O. Z. from Fox. This will be a hip-hop retelling of The Wizard of Oz. There have been many casting rumors and who's in and out, but the current buzz has 'N Sync's Justin Timberlake as the Scarecrow, John Leguizamo as a wicked warlock, and Little Richard as the Wizard. Brandy was approached to play Dorothy, but she declined, so the current rumor is singer Mya. No firm casting decisions or broadcast date have been set.
In recent years, there have been proposals for other Oz projects, none of which now appear that they will get off the ground. Among them:
  • Surrender Dorothy. Drew Barrymore as Dorothy's great-granddaughter coming to Oz, and battling the Wicked Witch of the West's granddaughter.
  • Somewhere starring Elizabeth Taylor as Dorothy, now a grandmother, returning to Oz.
  • Pamela West, where the Wicked Witch is the innocent victim and Dorothy (complete with Toto as a pit bull) is the evil interloper.
  • The Land of Oz on NBC, produced by Hallmark.
  • Lost in Oz, a potential syndicated television series with Tim Burton as executive producer.
If progress is made on any of these projects, such as actually going into production or a release date announced, the news will be posted as quickly as possible on this page.
The plans for an Oz-themed amusement park and resort in the Kansas City area are now in limbo. The local government has repeatedly voted on the applications for the park, and each time the vote has been deadlocked at 2-2. An independent study as to the resort's viability is now underway. For more information, check out the project's website at http://www.worldofoz.com
