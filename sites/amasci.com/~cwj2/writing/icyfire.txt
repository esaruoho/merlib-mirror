 This is another of the early 1998 sappy love poems which I deemed
not so drippy as to be embarrassing. I was reading a lot of Pablo
Neruda and e.e. cummings at the time, and most of my love poetry is
cribbed from them, drawing from cummings mostly in structure and
diction, and from Neruda in imagery. Heather tells me that it sounds Rod
McKuen-ish, which she implies isn't a great thing to be, but she still
likes it. I swear I've never read anything by him anyway. Really. 

- CWJ, 6 December 1998

 If i quietly slip my hand into yours 

If i quietly slip my hand into yours 
and we watch the night drape over us 
then i think that maybe we may 
love each other for Just a moment, 
while dying stars in distant galaxies 
prick the sky with icy fire.
