THE MINISTER'S COLUMN

The Search for Happiness

Much of my recent reading and study has delt with and attempt to understand the art of happiness. I have found four different resources which have helped me in ths attempt, and have found many points of agreement among them. These four are:

  · Two organizational consultants named Rick Foster and Greg Hicks. They believe they have identified the basic characteristics of people with a strong sense of well-being and happiness.  
  · Buddhist writers who have written on happiness, including the recent book by the Dalai Lama.  
  · Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi, who has written extensively on the subject of optimal experience.  
  · Murray Bowen and the writers like Roberta Gilbert who have developed his ideas about self-differentiation and its effect on health and personal effectiveness.  

These authors believe that there are certain patterns of acting and being that foster a sense of well being, happiness and even joy. Anyone can understand and practice these patterns. Does knowing how happiness is created make someone happy? No. Does practicing these patterns seem to generate happiness? These authors believe the answer is yes.

In a world of ecological deterioration, senseless violence, injustice, and many other problems, talking about happiness may seem self-indulgent or in poor taste. However, persons with a strong sense of personal well being tend to have positive and constructive effects on their environment. Perhaps experiencing a sense of well-being is the indispensable starting point for helping our world be a better place.

I will draw on thses resources from time to time in the hopes of stimulate your thinking about a subject that touches us all
