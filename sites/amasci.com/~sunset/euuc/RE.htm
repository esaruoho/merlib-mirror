Strong emphasis is placed on religious education for both children and adults.

The purpose of the religious education program is to give direction, enrichment, and perspective to this everyday opportunity for religious experience.

We seek to encourage an individual's natural ability to respond creatively to the world, to stimulate a spirit of free inquiry, and to encourage a broad appreciation for the religious experience.

Religious Education for the young people is based on the conviction that each individual's response to the experience of life is the ground upon which religious faith is built. This implies that religious education goes on hourly, daily, in the home, the school, and on the playground.

We hope to help children, youth, and adults develop an ethical, moral, and open approach to life, along with a sense of respect for themselves and others.

We also endeavor to provide a background in other religious movements and traditions, as well as in Unitarian Universalist history and values.

What's happening in March
We're making a little shift in focus this month in our RE classes. We've been talking about God, how people perceive the concept, how God is different ideas about God than we do. And, while some people are comfortable with using God language to describe their belief systems, not everyone is. So, as we attempt to explore our own personal beliefs, we are going to be asking questions in even more open-ended ways. What are the big things that really matter to you? What do you believe in? Do you have faith in something? If so, what? We're going to attempt to find words and images that help us to express some of the things that we find worthy in life.

We'll start this week with a wonderful story, "The Spyglass." I'll leave the book out on the RE table for your perusal and I've asked the Youngs to see if they can order copies for sale at the bookstore. - Mary
