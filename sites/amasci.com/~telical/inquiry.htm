Scientific Inquiry

A trained scientist would use ParaMind like this. One sees a shiny fabric, or the kind they call "neon colored" and one thinks to oneself, "Well that is shiny but what would be even more so?" One takes that meaning and merges it with a Word Category of things that shine. We can merge fabric with light, or sun, and we get something we have never had before but conceivably more advanced civilizations than ours would have. That is, a fabric that would glow in broad daylight -- a light emitting fabric. Clearly this idea seems as complex as did the electric light to people 150 years ago. But now with the computer, we can discover new and un-named concepts. A new idea must first at least be named -- and that is a most important step.

We can choose sentences that would approximate the invention of a light emitting cloth, such as this one:

A light emitting cloth was invented by treating conductive fabric with electricity until the material held luminosity stored by the cloth or provided by the electricity source.

Then we break down the idea, bit by bit, sentence by sentence. We explore the notion until we can get more and more realistic possibilities. Custom Word Categories can organize our material in important group relationships, and new ideas can be parsed and examined in these relationships. We end up with perhaps a five-hundred page analysis of an idea in a forty-hour work week, and within that analysis is very likely to be the correct formula for a luminous cloth. The work, incidentally, must be done by someone who understands the technical language pertaining to what a luminous cloth would be).
