 Our Mission 
 The Northwest Lesbian and Gay History Museum Project (NWGLHMP, or
The History Project), founded in 1994, is an organization which
researches, interprets and communicates the history of lesbian, gay,
bisexual and transgender (LGBT) people in the Pacific Northwest for the
purposes of study, education and enjoyment. Recognizing that the history
of this vibrant community has been sparsely and inaccurately recorded, the
History Project seeks to: 

 collect oral histories; locate photographs, ephemera, objects and
documents; and work with archives to insure the preservation of these
materials; and
 create public programs such as exhibits, publications and
presentations to communicate the collective experience we have
uncovered. 

 Some of our services and
projects: 

 ORAL HISTORY PROJECT 

On February 1, NWLGHMP members gathered for an all-day oral history
training led by Lorraine McConaghy, Historian at the Museum of
History and Industry in Seattle. Lorraine shared her years of experience,
discussing the power of oral history as a research tool, and coaching us
on interviewing techniques, recording equipment, legal and ethical issues,
and managing an oral history project. 

With 23 members now trained and ready to conduct interviews, NWLGHMP is
gearing up for a major oral history project in 1997. To get a more
complete picture of local GLBT history, we need to hear from a broad range
of people on topics such as: 
 coming out; the role of
race, ethnicity, and class in the GLBT community; experiences with
family, both biological and created; relationships between lesbians
and gay men over time; butch/femme roles; political activities; 
and the role of subcommunities (i.e., drag, S/M) in gay
culture. 
We will also be looking for photos, documents and
other materials which both help to illustrate local GLBT history, and
provide an important source of visuals for our exhibits and other public
programs. NWLGHMP already has a collection of over 25 interviews,
conducted while doing research for "Queen City Comes Out" and the "Family
Circles" exhibit preview. Read excerpts from some
of the oral histories we've collected here. 

We are excited about gathering more stories,
but we need your help! If you or someone you know has information,
stories, or materials to
share, please contact us . You may
be part of the Community Archive. Somewhere in your attic there's a pile
of Lavendar Panther newsletters, minutes from Queer Nation meetings,
photos of Radical Faeries at Elwa, or programs from Imperial Court Balls
-- call us! Even if you're not ready to give it up, we want to know
 what's out there so we can prioritize our preservation efforts and
plan for the future. Don't let this history slip through the cracks! If
you don't tell us what life was
like in a lesbian collective household in the '70s, or how you got the
courage to enter your first gay bar in the '50s, or about the queen who
taught you how to dress in drag in the '30s, who will??? 

 Queen City Comes Out: Exploring Seattle's Lesbian and Gay
History 

We presented our first exhibit in August of 1995, with funding
from the Pride Foundation. It charts the growth of Seattle's lesbian and
gay community from World War II through the 1970s. The exhibit focuses
on gay bars and their role in community development, pre-Stonewall
organizations and the flourishing of the lesbian and gay community in the
'70s, and the divergent strains of lesbian and gay political activism in
Seattle. The debut of the exhibit marked the first time that Seattle's
lesbian and gay history was assembled and publicly presented. Queen
City Comes Out is available for display (it can be installed for
periods of two weeks or more; an abbreviated version can be mounted
as a table-top display for briefer periods or one-time events) or as a
45-minute slide presentation. Please call or write gayhistnw@aol.com for details.

 Walking Tours of Historic Gay Seattle 

On October 26 and 27, 1996, we conducted two very successful
inaugural walking tours of historic gay Seattle in Pioneer Square.
Highlights included gay bars of the 1940s and '50s; the route of
Seattle's first Pride Parade; the Smith Tower's role in early gay
politics; and Shelley's Leg, Seattle's first disco. Moreover, we were
honored to hear stories of the old times from those who lived through
them from six different speakers over the two days. Over twenty people
attended each tour, and many others had to be put on a waiting list.
The success of these tours encouraged us to do more in the spring of
1997. Are you interested? Please call or write gayhistnw@aol.com !

 Claiming Space: a Historical Map of Lesbian and Gay Seattle 

Our map, which made its debut at Seattle Pride 1996, locates historically
significant places such as gay bars and other businesses; political,
social, religious, and service organizations; and important events in
Seattle's gay history (1920s-1990s). (This edition of the map was made
possible by generous contributions from Ellen Furgeson and Arlis Stewart.
Thanks!) A donation of $1.00 per map is requested. Please call or write
 gayhistnw@aol.com if you are
interested in obtaining a map; they are also available in certain Seattle
bookstores (try Bailey Coy, Beyond the Closet or Red & Black) and will be
in the Museum of History and Industry Store in June 1997!

 Queer Family Exhibit 

Our exhibit had a sneak preview in Seattle's Broadway Market from October
19 to November 16, 1996. It focuses on the ways queer people have
experienced family, giving historical perspective to an important
contemporary issue, including: 

 Families of Origin : experiences with birth families --
rejection, acceptance, avoidance, abuse
 Created Families : queer people's chosen "family" groups and
support networks -- long-term relationships, non-traditional parenting
arrangements, collective group households, circles of friends, support
services for queer youth, PWA care committees, and social organizations
such as the Imperial Court
 The Public Arena: recognition of gay family relationships from
the larger society, social and legal equality for gay families -- domestic
partnerships, religious commitment ceremonies, same-sex marriage, gay
adoption 

"Family Circles," a table-top display focusing on local GLBTs experiences
with families, in an historical context, is available for display.

While we have decided to put our Family Exhibit on hold, if you or anyone
you know has a story to share about queer family, please call or write gayhistnw@aol.com . We are also
looking for photographs, flyers, and other mateirals for the display. We
are especially seeking people of color and older people to interview for
this project. Call or write us , please!

 Our Future 
Our research has revealed an enormous variety of experiences, each
contributing to the vital history of this community. We have been moved
by the courage and humor of those who came before us and struck by the
importance of ordinary lives -- the kind of history that often slips
through the cracks and isn't found in history books.

We realize that many stories have yet to be heard, and we are committed
to continuing our research. We welcome your input and participation in
our future projects. Areas of future research include: 
 personal histories, individual stories
 long-term relationships and families
 pre-Stonewall, pre-World War II
 other cities and non-urban areas
 people of color
 gay experiences with military, religious, educational and mental
health institutions
 community responses to the AIDS crisis
 social, political and service organizations
 gay civil rights at the city, state and federal levels

Ultimately, we hope to establish a Museum of Northwest Lesbian and Gay
History . But in the meantime, we are currently unable to provide
safe, accessible storage for the long term. (Anyone have a couple million
dollars to spare?)

We do want to make sure that the valuable materials
we uncover will be preserved for future generations, however -- so if you
are planning to throw your treasures away, please call us or write gayhistnw@aol.com first!

We depend on private collections and unpublished materials for our
research, and we have learned a great deal from papers, flyers,
photographs, and objects that people have kept for years in their
closets. We can provide you with information about safe
repositories , and help you donate your "stuff" to them.

 The Archive Issue : Alas, with no gay community archive in the
Pacific Northwest, we are torn between lesbian and gay archives in other
regions (there are several), and local archives that are not specifically
devoted to queer materials
(the University of Washington, the Museum of History and Industry, etc.).
Following the example of the Gay and Lesbian Archives of the Pacific
Northwest, based in Portland, OR, we have begun to explore local
archives, and recently toured the University of Washington's Archives and
Special Collections facilities, with an eye toward developing some kind
of collecting relationship with them. The UW is very interested in
expanding its gay-related materials, and in working with us to organize
and catalogue new gay-related collections. We welcome your ideas,
concerns, and questions about this issue. Please call or
write gayhistnw@aol.com

 How about celebrating Pride Month (or any month) with a House
Party ?

What better way to celebrate pride in our community than to celebrate the
community's past in the presence of friends and family? All you have to
do is invite your friends and provide the space. We will happily stop by
with a short presentation (and bring our Family Photo Album of images and
quotes gathered from our research). The goals of the house party are: 

 spreading the excitement of discovering and preserving our local GLBT
heritage;
 furthering our research (if you know someone with stories or a box of
memorabilia from the old days, be sure to invite them);
 and, yes, raising some money for the organization (warn your friends
that a plea will accompany their snacks and good times). 

We know June can be a busy month. Often, though, the festivities, while
celebrating a vibrant community, overlook a foundation on which that
community is built: the strong and intimate bonds that we share with each
other everyday. Hosting a house party is an easy way to support NWLGHMP,
while reminding ourselves of what it is we are really celebrating.

 March 13-15: Second National Women in Historic Preservation
Conference , Phoenix, AZ

Our own Angie McCarrel was on the panel "Lavendar Landmarks: 
Historical Sites and Properties Related to Gays and Lesbians" at the
Second National Women in Historic Preservation Conference, March 13-15 in
Phoenix, Arizona. The conference is sponsored by the Women's Studies
Program and the Graduate Program in Public History at Arizona State
University, and the National Parks Service. Angie's presentation, "The
Interpretation of Historic Sites Associated with Gays, Lesbians, and
Bisexuals in Seattle," explored opportunities for preservation and
interpretation, and highlighted the potential links between existing
community based history efforts such as NWLGHMP, and historic preservation
activities. Images from NWLGHMP's "Queen City" exhibit provided a
brief historical overview. "Claiming Space," NWLGHMP's historical map,
provides an initial understanding of the varied building types and
locations associated with Seattle's lesbian and gay history. Angie
evaluated several sites on "Claiming Space," and proposed protection
strategies for them. Go, Angie! 

 In other news: 

 FEBRUARY RETREAT: NEW STRUCTURE, NEW PLANS FOR 1997 

At NWLGHMP's second organizational retreat, United Way consultant Susan
Burgess facilitated a serious discussion about where we are going and how
we can get there. We made lots of progress and emerged with new plans and
renewed energy. 

Our vision for NWLGHMP's future includes a space of our own for
exhibits, offices, and a collection; a stable budget; and a paid staff. We
want to have a presence in schools, libraries, and the media, and to serve
as a resource for both the gay community and the history/preservation
community. 

To make this a reality, NWLGHMP must have the stability and momentum to
survive when current members leave or cut back on their involvement. We
are developing a more formal structure for NWLGHMP, including an
Advisory Board to help us with long-term issues, and Subcommittees to
handle ongoing governance tasks. Our research, oral histories, exhibits,
walking tours, and other projects will continue to be planned and executed
by the whole organization, with ad hoc Task Forces for smaller projects. 

 NWLGHMP will now meet twice a month (2nd & 4th Wednesdays at 7pm) ,
with Subcommittees and Task Forces meeting as needed. The success of this
new structure depends on expanding our active membership: to put it
bluntly, we need you . We are ready and eager to plug new
volunteers into our Subcommittees and Task Forces, so if you want to get
involved, call now! Or write us at gayhistnw@aol.com . 

Also at the retreat, we vowed to increase diversity in our
membership, research, and public programs. We are planning an aggressive
outreach program in 1997 to bring in members, interviewees, and supporters
from a wide range of ethnic, class, and religious backgrounds. 

 Our UW Library School interns , Renee and Irene, have been with
us since September '96 -- and they've accomplished a ton. They have
inventoried three collections we have in temporary custody, including
ACT-UP and Dorian Group papers. These materials may go to the University
of Washington Archives. Our oral histories are now inventoried, and we
are making copies for researchers use. They are now compiling a directory
of GLBT archives in the region, and a guide to Seattles so called
Community Archive -- all the historic photo albums, scrapbooks, papers,
and other materials that are out there in peoples closets and basements.
 Three cheers for Renee and Irene! We really appreciate their time,
expertise, and commitment to GLBT history. We hope there are more like
these two back at the Library School!

 Harbaugh Papers at UW 

Last year Chuck Harbaugh's family contacted us about donating his
collection of papers, magazines, and books. We directed them to the UW
Archives, which recently accessioned 31 boxes of Chuck's material. 
NWLGHMP member Mikala Woodward has been processing this accession for the
Archives, and is excited about its research potential. Chuck was active
in many local organizations, and he collected material on religion and
homosexuality, national gay rights groups, AIDS, and much, much more.
Mikala will be writing a guide to the Harbaugh Papers and invites
those of you who knew Chuck to share any information you might have about
him.
