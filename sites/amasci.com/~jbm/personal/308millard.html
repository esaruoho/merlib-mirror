JBM - Personal - 0.308 Millard

Specifications

Caliber: 0.308 Millard (wildcat belted magnum)
Action: Sleeved Remington 700, converted to single shot.
Barrel: Hart 29x1.5", no taper.
Stock: Bruce Baer
Trigger: Arnold Jewel
Scope: 24x Unertl, 2" objective.
Weight: in the neighborhood of 45 pounds.

Comments

0.308 Millard Wildcat is nothing more than another blown out .300 H & H. It was born out of a need to clean up a poor chambering job in 30x8mm Remington Magnum and the desire to have my own reamer. Basic specs: 40 degree shoulder, minimum body taper, .333" neck diameter. The neck length is minimum. The reamer was made by JGS.

The action is glued into the sleeve which is bolted to a 3/4" block of aluminum glued (DevCon) into the stock. The sleeve is bedded onto the block with DevCon. The action, sleeve and barrel to not touch stock anywhere (the aluminum block also acts as a pillar system).

Bolt handle was extended by Hart. It's not possible to reach it otherwise.

The Jewel trigger is very nice. I currently have it set for the lightest touch of about 1-2 oz. (I've never really measured it...) The trigger has only one flaw that I know of -- it makes it very hard to use any other trigger!

The stock is pretty much in the same general shape as delivered. I did add about 10 pounds of lead shot mixed with cheap bedding compound to the butt. The butt plate is solid ebony. There is about .25" of clearance completely around the barrel.

Miscellaneous: Bolt release by Lilja. Titanium firing pin (it sounds neat, but I'm still not convinced that it is worth it) and heavier spring. Stainless steel rails on bottom of stock (also from Baer).

Reloading

Brass fireformed from 300 H & H Winchester -- twice. Bases turned flat, flash hole reamed, neck turned, annealed trimmed and cleaned. Sizing is done with Neil Jones hand dies. I typically size the neck only a couple thousandths of an inch resulting in minimum neck tension (you can pull the bullets out with your fingers).

Usual load is Federal 215 primer, H-1000 powder (measure set at 81.5) and 211 grain VLD from Berger. Any more powder and this would be a compressed load. Velocity is about 3250 ft/sec. Brass life is greater than fifteen shots per case on average -- usually limited by the primer pocket enlarging. I have very few split cases. I can and have loaded it hotter, but accuracy suffers. I think it is from bullet seating variations -- it's really hard to compress a load with really light neck tension.
