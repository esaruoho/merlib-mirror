My Father-in-law treated his family to a Caribbean cruise in February, 1996. One of the ports-of-call was Cozumel, Mexico where my husband and I took an excursion to the site of an old Mayan city, Chichen Itza.

Our guide quickly took us through the gates, under the trees, past all the vendors to the most impressive temple in Chichen Itza - Kukulkan's Pyramid or El Castillo.

View from the top of Kukulkan Pyramid - the ball court, and Temple of the Jaguars.

Narrow stairs. Yikes! Some people had no problem climbing. The proper way to traverse the steps is sideways, as it was improper to turn your back to the Gods. The Mayan people were short in stature (about 5 feet average height for men).

View of the market (all those columns) the Temple of the Warriors and the Temple of the Tables (far left).

We got to climb to the top. Our guide told us that probably in 1998 climbing on the ruins would no longer be possible - the government would be roping off access.

There are 91 steps leading to the top, 4 stairways totaling 364 steps plus the top step shared by all stairways adding up to 365 (solar days in the year). During equinox, when the sun is setting, you can see shadows from the temple terraces that connect with a serpent head at the bottom of the west stairway. It all connects into one big snake! Kukulkan's pyramid is build over the top of another pyramid - Yup, one on top of the other. We weren't allowed to take cameras inside, so no pictures.

The market place.

Wall of skulls - Tzompantili

Our guide told us the heads of war victims were set on top of this wall. Human sacrifice in religious ceremony became more and more common during the Mayan-Toltec period.

Temple of the Jaguars.

From the ball court. Acoustics are supposed to be really good in here.

From inside the ball court. In religious ceremonies, the captain of the winning team would be decapitated. It was a great honor to offer your life to the gods on behalf of your people.

Venus Platform. Venus is a guiding star for travelers.

From the Platform of Tigers and Eagles

Before being uncovered, buildings would be a mere heap of stones with jungle growth on top.

At Chichen Itza hieroglyphs are common. There's a lot more to be uncovered here.

Chichen Itza translates to -by the mouth of the well of the Itza-

CHI- mouth

CHEN- well

ITZA- name of tribe

Cultural Development

Formative Period (500 B.C. - 325 A.D)

Classic Maya Period (325 - 925 A.D.)

--Florescence Period (625 - 800 A.D.)

--Collapse (800 - 925 A.D) - thought to be brought about by foreign influence and land exhaustion

Interregnun (925 - 975 A.D.)

Maya-Toltec Period (975 - 1200 A.D.)

Mexican Absorption Period (1200 - 1540 A.D.) - thought to have been a period of peasant revolt with overthrow of the theocracy.
