What is the goal of the National Collegiate Paintball Association?

The NCPA's goal is to have a paintball club and/or team at every college and high school in the country and to utilize these clubs to inform students about paintball, encourage their participation in the sport, and educate the general public about paintball at a local level.

Why is this goal important?

Most people choose their recreational activities early in life. By providing college and high school clubs, the NCPA makes sure that young people have the chance learn about and try paintball. These new players also inform the general public about paintball through the normal, day-to-day contact they have with their friends and family. By establishing participants, public acceptence and therefore an audience for paintball, the NCPA plays an essential role in paintball's success not only as a recreational buisiness but also a competitive, broadcastable sport. High school and college paintball clubs, teams and leagues are essential in creating new competitive players and giving them the training, coaching and experience necessary to turn them into top-caliber paintball athletes.

What contribution programs are available?

The NCPA offers two levels to the contribution program for the 2001-2002 school year, Silver and Gold, at $5 per team per event and $10 per team per event respectively.

How does the NCPA use my contribution?

The NCPA applies 100% of your contribution directly to the cost of participation for the teams at our events. Contributions are first spent reducing entry and air fees. Once those have been covered, remaining funds are used on the cost of paint, followed by team travel and lodging expenses.

Why doesn't the NCPA give the money away as part of a prize package? Why doesn't the NCPA solicit prizes?

Because recreational clubs provide additional resources for teams to offer fields and buisinesses as well as a recruiting ground for the teams themselves, pushing the development of college and high school competitive teams also pushes the development of recreational clubs. The most successful competative programs also have the largest recreational base. A prize package does very little to create new players and teams because new players are not likely to be attracted by a prize package they probably won't win, and existing teams and players already travel hundreds of miles by car and air to compete for guaranteed prize packages amounting to trophies. New players are, however, attracted to events that are inexpensive, so the NCPA strives to provide events at most reasonable cost possible. More new teams means more new recreational clubs means more students at colleges across the country trying paintball means faster growth and wider acceptance of the sport.

The NCPA also prefers reductions in event costs over larger prize packages because we stress a friendly, sportsmanly atmosphere. This atmosphere is more attractive to new players, referees, fields and existing players alike and is much easier to maintain when everyone benefits from low event costs and quality, friendly tournaments. Prize packages unfortunately encourage the behavior exhibitted by too many professional and amateur players today by forcing teams to compete to cover their expenses. The NCPA will not tolerate poor behavior and it is in our best interest to prohibit players with poor behavior from playing. Such players also tend not to be attracted to events with small prize packages. The NCPA strongly believes that by providing an environment where proper behavior is stressed that players play in for four to eight years that those players will carry that behavior with them into amateur and professional competition.

Why are contributions calculated on a per-team basis?

There are a couple reasons to do this. One is that it makes your contribution directly proportional to the participation in the events. Fifty teams at an event is a $250 contribution for Silver Contributors, while five teams is $25. This assures that your contribution is proportional to the effect of your contribution. Another is that it is very difficult for the NCPA to predict exact tournament attendance in advance. By basing contributions on a per-team basis, we know exactly how much funding we have available for each team, whether there are 10 of them or 100 of them at a particular event. Per-team contributions also allow us to award the "same" funding, on a per-team basis, to all of our affiliated events without having events receive disparate funding due to differences in size. Also keep in mind that one "team" at a college or high school event is not only the 5 players on the team, but also the 50 or more recreational players that team represents from back home.

Why should I support the NCPA?

You should support the NCPA both because it is good for paintball and because it is correspondingly good for your business. Remember, the NCPA is not a tournament league like the WPF or NPPL. We are a non-profit organization of college and high school clubs who bring thousands of students across the country to paintball fields every year and train hundreds more to play competative paintball as well. Because NCPA teams must find their players from within their schools, they must work to improve the abilities of the players available to them instead of being able to rely on using existing experienced players. As a course of our activities, we also have a tremendous effect on the familiarity the public has with paintball, both through local publications as well as by directly exposing the public to the sport through the participation of their children. We consequently have a tremendous impact on the acceptance of paintball in the eyes of the public. The NCPA is not unique only because it is designed from the ground up to promote the growth and acceptance of paintball as a sport, but also because it does not fall on either side of the "recreational paintball vs. tournament paintball" divide. College clubs bring new players into recreational paintball and their teams play competitive paintball. College recreational players know and care how their teams do, and college tournament players host and play recball with their recreational members. Instead of paintball being divided into one or the other, both aspects are unified in the same program.

Contributing to the NCPA supports college teams and clubs and promotes their continued efforts. As an example of some of the things college clubs do:

  • The United States Military Academy (West Point) Club has hosted a big game (and it is big) every semester - totalling fifteen times.

  • The Iowa State University Club successfully acquired $14,000 from their university to construct a paintball field *ON-CAMPUS*, within view of thousands of students daily.

  • The University of Illinois Club recently had an article published in The Buzz, a weekly segment in the university student newspaper, read by tens of thousands of college students.

  • College clubs bring THOUSANDS of new players to paintball fields each year, recruiting them from campus, having meetings to inform them about the sport, and getting them to the field and back.

  • College clubs not only participate in college tournaments and other events like Skyball, but also train and coach new players to become successful tournament players.

It is important to remember that you shouldn't contribute to the NCPA for the same reasons you would support a tournament because the NCPA is not a tournament. The NCPA is a complete paintball promotion program. A college paintball team is not merely 5 players at an event. A college paintball team is 5 players representing a club of up to 300 players back home. These are the same 5 players those 300 players turn to to ask which mask or marker they should buy. Those 300 players are buying equipment because of the efforts of the serious paintball players who took the time to organize events for other students. Contributing to the NCPA supports those efforts and makes you directly visible to those players who directly influence the purchasing decisions of their memebrs. Remember, a professional team does not go home and tell the 300 other players in the club about that really nifty marker they saw from that company supporting their event.

You should also support the NCPA because while many believe the measure of a sport's prominence is TV exposure, the true measure a sport's legitimacy is its prevolence in schools. Paintball currently has less than 100 recognized college clubs across the country and fewer than 20 official high school clubs. Those numbers are abysmal when compared to other sports, even less "mainstream" ones such as badmitton, bowling, rugby, lacrosse, and even fencing. Paintball will have a difficult time providing an audience for television programming until it is more successful at reaching potential audience members at an early age. The NCPA directly supports the formation and continued operation of college and high school clubs. College paintball also provides team names that are familiar to the public, and both college and high school paintball provide a family friendly environment that lets parents and other family members get involved.

And lastly, you should support the NCPA because it is TAX DEDUCTIBLE. The NCPA is a tax-exempt organization capable of accepting tax-deductible contributions under section 501(c)3 of the Internal Revenue Code as an organization devoted to the development of amateur level athletic competition and the creation of athletes for such competition. This means that a $1000 contribution is closer to $600 or $700 post-tax dollars.
