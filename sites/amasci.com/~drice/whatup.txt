 Happy Spring to you All! 
 March 30, 2002 

 My dear friends, family members and fellow Northwest-area 
 artists,

 Spring is Here! Oh, what a fun month this is, isn't it? The first day of Spring with the 
 Strange Spring Snows in Seattle, the Palm Sunday through Easter festivals going on,
 the Passover Seder parties! Oh, yeah! I love this time of year! 
 I hope you are all having a wonderful March and April, 
 and the themes of all these celebrations - renewal, 
 rebirth, release from the bonds of slavery, freedom from tyranny for everyone - 
 I hope these themes give new hope to everyone who participates in them. 

Yeah, Yeah, you guys know me. Forged from PollyAnna, that's me. 
 Optimizm Iz Us. I just think we can use a little hope and renewal 
 after this miserable winter of terrorism, layoffs,
 Enron muck and our leader's grammar and vocabulary! Ack! Poor guy!
 ::chuckle:: He is in serious need of some theatre improv classes! Don't they have those in Texas
 and DC...? 

Meanwhile, back to the mundane senses of the word "renewal", 
 we're doing the Spring yard work thang - 
 digging our errant bamboo and filling our raised beds and spreading
 beauty bark! We're offering a new yard workout plan to our friends and 
 neighbors in the Seattle - swapping work parties! We're in with our next-door
 neighbors to dig the bamboo. They don't have to buy it, we don't have to dig 
 it all alone... Not a bad deal at all! We're actually getting the stand back 
 to where it was when we moved in 9 years ago. And, we're going to put our 
 recent containment knowledge to work afterwards. Peace and happiness should ensue
 shortly thereafter, eh? The reclaimed yard space can then be put to better use,
 and we'll still have our beautiful little bamboo forest. Woo-hoo! :-} 

 Back to the Stage 
 Yes, I'm doing some new stage projects, my friends. I couldn't resist
 two opportunities that came up recently - auditioning for the chorus of the 
 Seattle Gilbert & Sullivan Society shows this summer, and doing a staged 
 reading with two amazing actors - Henry Hart and Peggy Hunt -
 of the show
 Painting Churches . Read on for details. There are shows
 to see for one in late April (other dates TBA) and one in July! -->

 Off-Stage, But Still Busymaking - Films! I'm preparing to shoot a short indie film in April-May (see more info below under
 Things Were Once Red - they're still casting if you know someone who'd
 be interested in playing my husband and imaginary friend Jackie Onassis... :-} ), 
 and working on a way cool project for a multi-pronged scifi 
 movie/website/game experience!
 I'll be linking to more info as soon as I have something to show for it. :-} -->

Meanwhile, you know my day job is as a website administrator for the City of Seattle's
 power utility, Seattle City Light. For those of you who are interested, SCL just
 published a comprehensive report on how Enron's antics last year jacked power market prices
 up and sent the City of Seattle spiraling into debt. See the left panel of this page for links to that
 report, and to the Seattle Times article about what this means about SCL's rates.
 Did you guys know that the City of Seattle owns Seattle City Light, and that the City
 Council is legally mandated to keep it on budget? Very interesting stuff.
 By the way, I'm not saying all this as a representative of the utility, I'm just the 
 technical support for the website, not a writer or press representative or 
 anything like that... (In other words, don't quote me, I'm just telling you 
 about this as my friends.) :-}

 Seattle City Light and the Energy Crisis of 2000-2001 
 At the CityOfSeattle.net, our fair city's
 municipal power utility tells its story of how Enron's antics led to our
 region's utilities being in massive amounts of debt. 

 CityofSeattle.net: Seattle City Light Newsroom 

 Related article at the Seattle Times site
 about how this affects our electric bills:

 Seattle Times: City Light says its options are borrow or raise rates 

Oh! By the way, "Dirt" has been finished! Its final edit was screened at the 911 Media
 Arts Center last fall, and director Tim Coulter is off to LA to market the film, 
 which is apparently being considered for several film festival-type
 venues. Hey, I like the film, and I'm only on screen for about two minutes, so I'm
 not just saying that because I'm in it. Tim did a great job! I'll let you know if
 I hear that it's going to screen in Seattle again. The movie has a website, here:

 http://www.dirtmovie.com/ And for those
 who got to see the screening at the Seattle Art Museum, hosted by Warren Etheridge, 
 Warren's website is here: 

 http://www.thewarrenreport.com/

 Anne of Green Gables 

 Lakewood Players 

 Anne of Green Gables 

 Lakewood Players 
 November 29-December 22, 2002 

 Yes, dear ones, I'm back to my favourite genre of character - 
 mother of an emotionally volatile teenager! Woo-hoo!! ::chuckle:: 
 I'm playing Marilla
 Cuthbert in Anne of Green Gables . What fun, eh? 
 Seriously, this is a great show with a fabulous cast - in the
 round at the Lakewood theatre just south of Tacoma. The show opens the 
 day after Thanksgiving and runs through December 22, 2002. 
 For more info on tickets, directions to the venue, etc., check
 out the Lakewood Players website: http://www.lakewoodplayers.com/ 
 For those that find this kind of role to be your fave me to play,
 I have to say, this one rivals The Cripple of Inishmaan or 
 Little Women or I Never Saw Another Butterfly . This show
 is an amazing project, an all-new adaptation by Scott Campbell, and 
 I'm playing in a venue that has seen many of my friends grace its stage. 
 Yes, it's so totally worth the commute! So, come on down to the show! 
 And, thanks again to everyone who made it down to the Bagley Wright this
 summer for Trial by Jury and The Sorcerer . You all so rock! 
 I love you guys! :: big bear hugs:: 
 As always, may you have as much peace, light, health, hugs, music and happiness as you can comfortably tolerate in your lives, dear ones.

 Singing Gig -
 Cabaret Night at the Mountaineers' Club 
 August 4, 2002 

 This is a series of short musical pieces done by a combination of the casts of the Seattle Gilbert & Sullivan Society and the visiting Derby Cast (performing Iolanthe this year, following our run in the Bagley Wright Theatre). I'm just learning about the history of this myself, but apparently there are Gilbert & Sullivan performance competitions in Buxton, England, and the Seattle G&S Society has attended several times over the years, and garnered awards and such. :-} Cool, eh? Anyway, to quote Linda Sue Welch, the organizer of the Cabaret event: 
 "The cabaret began in 1996 as after-competition entertainment for (the Seattle Gilbert & Sullivan Society's) first Buxton trip. The idea was to show off some of the other talents of the cast in non-G & S fashion. Our first show was a USO-style variety show, featuring Dave Ross as our "Bob Hope" host, vaudeville excerpts from our beloved Ted Pool, and even some tap dancing! ... The basic concept has always been to focus on ensemble work and to give the supporting actors in the main show a chance for more involvement. This year will feature several big chorus numbers -- operettas and vocal jazz arrangements of the old musicals. ... The final part of the program will be several G & S large chorus numbers that will be sung in combination with the Derby group." 
 More details to come as I get them. :-}
