 Inseparable Pieces: A Sequence of Power Plays 

 The play&#146;s purpose was to show ways of combatting sexism and anti-gay sentiment, and building unity among diverse peoples. To help thematically unify such diverse ends, a large silk scarf wove its way through the various scenes; becoming a mother&#146;s head scarf in one scene, tug-of-war rope in another, and so on. At play&#146;s end, each performer related how they had spoken out against various kinds of oppression at some time in their life. 

 Inseparable Pieces was created out of three-months of improvisational workshops. The improvs were based on participant&#146;s personal life experiences during which they had witnessed some form of oppression. Demian based the script on audio tape recordings of the improvisations.

 While all pictured above participated in the workshops, the play performers were Laurie Brandt, Demian, Roland Green and Deni Lee. 

Company members were from diverse racial, sexual orientation, economic and ethnic backgrounds, reflecting the play&#146;s and Sweet Corn &#146;s commitment to equality of all people. Inseparable Pieces played during the summer of 1979 and was utilized as a successful fundraiser for the Metropolitan Community Church of Boston .
