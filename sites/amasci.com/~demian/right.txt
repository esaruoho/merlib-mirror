 W e deserve the civil right of marriage. However, it will not be dropped in our laps. We expect a fight much more rancorous than the campaign for equal treatment in the military. 

There are some gay men and lesbians who don&#146;t believe legal marriage is worth fighting for, or that it is winnable. 

We have produced a video which promotes the civil right of legal marriage for same-sex couples. Called The Right to Marry , this timely video was created to prepare the gay and lesbian community for the national debate now taking place in the media, courts and state legislatures. 

In the video, written and directed by Demian, nationally prominent gay and lesbian leaders spell out why this right is so important to the community, and what we can do to win and preserve it. 

Winning this civil right will only be possible if we organize quickly with the compelling facts fully at our command. The video presents these facts along with the personal stories that make this struggle so vital and compelling. 

Although the video will be persuasive to a general audience, it is aimed squarely at the gay and lesbian community. 

While a growing number support the right to legal marriage, too few understand the magnitude of what&#146;s at stake. You can bet the radical right knows. In fact, same-sex marriage is lambasted in their latest anti-gay video, and with all of their political endeavors. 

Demian&#146;s video, The Right to Marry , runs 72 minutes and features interviews with Rev. Mel White, well known from his &#147;60 Minutes&#148; appearance and his book Stranger at the Gate: To be gay and Christian in America ; Phyllis Burke, author of Family Values: A lesbian mother&#146;s fight for her son ; Richard Mohr, author of A More Perfect Union ; and Kevin Cathcart, executive director of the Lambda Legal Defense & Education Fund . 

The video also highlights some of the same-sex couples who have sued for legal marriage, with personal recollections from Faygele benMiriam, who sued Washington state in 1971, and Benjamin Cable-McCarthy, who along with partner Marcial Cable-McCarthy sued California in 1993. 

As of 1996, there have been 16 unsuccessful (two are ongoing) suits for legal marriage in the United States since 1971. The case in Hawaii, which finally lost in December 1999, had the strongest chance of success. This case and its implications are vividly described in the video by Susan Reardon, co-director of the Hawaii Equal Rights Marriage Project , and Evan Wolfson, co-counsel in the Hawaii suit and director of The Marriage Project at Lambda Legal Defense & Education Fund . 

Personal stories provide a backdrop for the discussion, including the multiple weddings of Frances Fuchs and her partner Gayle Remick, and the Unitarian church wedding and reflections of Jacqui Johnston and Natalie Hanson. 

 The Right to Marry is not a &#147;balanced&#148; report presenting both sides of the issue. There is only one just side when it comes to civil rights. It wasn&#146;t too long ago that couples of mixed race could not marry in many states. Years from now, the nation will look back in disbelief that a entire class of citizens was unfairly excluded from so fundamental a social institution. 

All of our Partners work over the last decade has pointed to this issue and this video project. It has long been clear to us that same-sex couples were subject to extensive discrimination &#151; not just from being identified as gay or lesbian, but also because we are in same-sex relationships. 

The extent of that discrimination was made clear by the landmark National Survey of Gay and Lesbian Couples we conducted in 1990. Among the 1,266 couples responding, about 40 percent experienced discrimination in employment benefits and taxes, about 20 percent in insurance and membership, and about 13 percent in housing and credit or banking. 

Domestic partner provisions redress a few of the inequities at select jobs and in scattered localities, but they are &#147;crumbs&#148; compared to the whole marriage cake. 

Only legal marriage can correct the full range of discrimination that same-sex couples face over issues such as child custody, immigration for one&#146;s partner, or the right to claim a partner&#146;s Social Security survivor benefits. 

 The Right to Marry , two years in the making, was produced on a shoestring budget with the help of modest donations. 

An original arrangement of the song &#147;Everything Possible,&#148; written by singer and songwriter Fred Small, was utilized as background music, as well as sung by a quartet during the ceremony sequence. It contains the line &#147;You can be anybody you want to be, you can love whomever you will.&#148; 

 Partners Task Force has advocated for same-sex couples since 1986, and has been a legal marriage proponent since 1990. 

 Video Production Team 

 Demian , producer/writer/director 
Demian, a photographer for more than 35 years, has worked in film and video creating documentaries, animations and dramatic narratives. He produced the musical fantasy The Fight Before Christmas . As director of Sweet Corn Productions since 1971, Demian has written and directed liberation-oriented plays in Boston and Seattle. Demian produced and hosted radio programs Gaybreak and Gayway in Massachusetts (1973-79). He earned a Doctorate in Education, a Master of Education, as well as a Bachelor of Fine Arts degree in painting. His photography, illustration and writing has been widely published in mainstream and gay journals.
