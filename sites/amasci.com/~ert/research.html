Ert's research projects

Research, research, research. If they had told me when I was seven that research meant building cool things out of Java and nucleic acids (mmm...DNA-mocha blend!) I would have liked it then, too.

My second year at UHCL I have been working on a user interface for working with a rule-based system for composite object manufacturing specifications.

My first year at UHCL I worked with the NASA-funded Research Institute for Computing and Information Systems lab on an agent-based information search engine.

When I was at Baylor College of Medicine, I did most of my work in two particular labs:

    During my first placement I worked with Randy Smith's group on genome informatics. Check out their BCM Search Launcher.

    After that, I worked with the Computational Neurobiology Lab on their virtual bee model.

I spent two years at The Whitehead Institute / MIT Center for Genome Research doing extensive computational work on the dense SSLP genetic map of the mouse.

While at the Whitehead I also worked with David Page's group. I was involved with informatics work on a mouse hermaphroditism study,, developing software to catalogue the lab's mouse colony, generating maps of the human Y chromosome, and analyzing CpG islands.

During 1992 I worked in Chip Quinn's lab on a CREB transgene which blocks long-term memory in Drosophila

During Summer 1990 I worked with Colin Lock in McMaster University's Chemistry Department, examining toxic properties of gold sodium thiomalate, an arthritis drug. Dr. Lock passed away in May 1996.

During Summer 1988 I worked with Gilles Lajoie in the Chemistry Department of the University of Waterloo on the design of an elastase inhibitor.
