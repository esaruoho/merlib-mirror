Who are the Jehovah's Witnesses?

Some brief highlights...

The Jehovah's Witnesses are one of the fastest-growing religious movements in the world. The 1996 Yearbook Of Jehovah's Witnesses shows an active membership of 5,199,895 people divided among 78,620 congregations in 232 countries. Attendance at the annual "Memorial" (communion) celebration was 13,147,201 in 1995. That the outreach and influence of this organization greatly exceeds its membership becomes evident from the "Memorial" attendance figures and from the circulation figures of the publications of the Watchtower Bible and Tract Society, Brooklyn, New York.

The Witnesses are very active in door-to-door proselytizing, and have a worldwide missionary outreach to gain new converts. In 1995, they spent over one billion hours in door-to-door work. Most of their increase comes from winning nominal Christians from Christian denominations, especially persons who are not well versed in the Bible. Membership in the Watchtower organization frequently leads to confusion, heartache, and family breakups as the Society takes the place of the member's family. Association with persons outside the Watchtower is stongly discouraged, even among relatives. The Watchtower organization uses the fear tactics of "shunning" and "disfellowshipping" to maintain strict control over members and warns new followers that friends and relatives may try to dissuade them from remaining with the Jehovah's Witnesses.

This organization denies most of the major doctrines accepted by the historical orthodox Christian church. The Watchtower's distorted version of the Bible, the New World Translation of the Holy Scriptures, is already printed in whole or in part in 14 languages and is now being translated into 16 other languages of Europe, Africa, and the Orient.
