 SONGWRITING & COPYRIGHTS 

 Songwriting is an art that you can develop through years 
 of painstaking work, but the learning curve can also be shortened 
 if you have some help along the way. With this column, we 
 will attempt to help you learn the basics and some of the 
 more advanced skills. Whether you want to be the next Bowie, 
 or just sing an original ballad below women's balconies, there 
 is something for you. 

 Playing the 
 Guitar while Singing 
 Songwriting # 35 
 by A-J Charron (15 Feb 2002) 
 A lot of bands have a lead vocalist who 
 also plays an instrument. You listen to 
 a live album from them and you hear the 
 instrument performed to perfection and wonder 
 how they do it. You've tried to sing while 
 playing your guitar and have discovered 
 that it's not an easy thing to do. 

 Charts 
 Songwriting # 34 
 by A-J Charron (29 Jan 2002) 
 I found that when I bring a new song to my band 
 mates, it can be a long learning curve for the 
 other guys. Now I put everything into charts and 
 give these to the guys a week or so before meeting 
 them with our guns ready. Works a lot better. 

 Where To Find Great 
 Prices 
 Songwriting # 33 
 by A-J Charron (25 Jul 2001) 
 &#147;The pawn shop is a musician&#146;s best 
 friend.&#148; I&#146;ve heard that phrase many 
 times before. The worst part of it is, it&#146;s 
 true. Many musicians end up pawning old instruments 
 and old equipment there. They get out of the game 
 or they simply change their instruments or equipment 
 for something newer. This is where you can profit 
 from their losses. 
 Best-Kept Secret 
 Songwriting # 32 
 by A-J Charron (01 May 2001) 
 Following the last column, this week we&#146;ll 
 be looking at our last option of what to do with 
 your songs. This option applies to both performers 
 and non-performers alike. The best-kept secret 
 in the music business is The Publisher. 

 Where Do I Go From 
 Here? 
 Songwriting # 31 
 by A-J Charron (06 Apr 2001) 
 What do you do with your songs? That's 
 a common enough question among songwriters. There 
 are three avenues you can take. Today, we'll be 
 looking at the first two. 

 Arrangements 
 Songwriting # 30 
 by A-J Charron (23 Mar 2001) 
 Some songwriters get stuck at the &#147;unplugged&#148; 
 part of writing songs. They have no idea how to 
 arrange their songs. If that&#146;s your case, 
 then that&#146;s OK. It doesn&#146;t mean you 
 won&#146;t make it. It just means that you&#146;ll 
 need someone else to arrange your songs. If you 
 can&#146;t find a particular arrangement for a 
 song, it might be a good idea to try and enlist 
 someone else&#146;s aid.

 Home On 
 Your Range 
 by David Hodge (13 Feb 2001) 
 Whether or not you realize it, you yourself may 
 be one of your biggest obstacles when it comes 
 to songwriting. Think about how you go about writing 
 - especially if you&#146;re one of those &#147;let&#146;s 
 strum around and see what comes up&#148; sort 
 of folks. Chances are you pick up your guitar 
 and the first chord you play will be an E, A, 
 C, D or G. Unless, of course, you happen to be 
 in a mad, sad or bad frame of mind and then you 
 pound out an Em or an Am.
