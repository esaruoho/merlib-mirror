 Chapter 1 
 The Advertising Policy 

 O n November 6, 1996 the Seattle School Board passed (5-2)
 the Advertising and Corporate Sponsorship Policy, D35.00 , which would actively solicit
 corporate advertising and sponsorship in the public
 schools. The Seattle School District faces a $35 million
 budget shortfall over the next three years, and the school
 board hopes to generate 10 percent of the funds they need
 with this policy. The decision to allow more commercial
 presence in schools was made in a void of public input.
 As a result of the disregard for public process, two
 public forums were held in December 1996 and January 1997,
 which were attended by parents, students, teachers and
 citizens outraged at the new policy. The outcome of these
 forums led to the formation of the Citizens' Campaign for
 Commercial-Free Schools (CCCS). Our primary goal is to overthrow
 the Ad policy, as well as to eradicate other forms of
 corporate influence in the public schools.

 I n response to the strong
 public outcry against their decision, on February 19th the
 school board decided to back down and put a two week
 moratorium on any action in regards to the policy. There
 will be a meeting on March 5th to work on a new policy
 with imput from the PTSAs (Parent Teacher State
 Associations). CCCS has made recommendations 
 for a process to write a new policy in regards to
 advertising. In addition to giving input for a new Ad
 policy, the CSS will continue to fight against current
 commercialism in the Seattle Public Schools.
