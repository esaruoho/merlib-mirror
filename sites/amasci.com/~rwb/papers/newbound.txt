A {\sf critical set } in an $n \times n$ array is a set $C$ of given
entries, such that there exists a unique extension of  $C$ to  an
$n\times n$ Latin square and no proper subset of $C$ has this
property.  The cardinality of the largest critical set in any Latin
square of order $n$ is denoted by $\lcs{n}$.  In 1978 Curran and van
Rees proved that $\lcs{n} \leq n^2 - n$.  Here we show that $\lcs{n}
\leq n^2-3n+3$.

to Combinatorics'2000.
