<p><strong>ERRANTE's in-line VIRTUAL GROUND NODE GENERATOR</strong> : what it is, how it works and what it does.</p>
<p><strong>Universal R.F. VIRTUAL GROUND NODE GENERATOR</strong><br />
<br />
by <em>Francesco Errante</em></p>
<div class="doublespaced">
<p>The Errante's in-line virtual ground node generator is a radioelectric circuitry based on the same principals and schematics of his own original patented virtual ground monopole HF antenna design.<br />
<strong>The reader who's not already familiar with the virtual ground node mechanism is strongly adviced to read about the "<a href="antenna_errante.htm"><em>virtual ground monopole HF antenna</em></a> and its <a href="suppressor.htm">background</a> prior to proceeding to the reading of this page.</strong><br />
<br />
The in-line VIRTUAL GROUND NODE GENERATOR (in-line V.G.N.G. for short) is a symmetric radioelectric network which converts an unbalanced RF signal into two precisely identical signals and re-converts them back into a non-inverted single unbalanced RF signal. While doing this, a virtual ground node is generated at its center. It can be effectively described as being an "Un-Bal-Bal-Un" network.<br />
In practice the device, as presented in here, has both the input and output gates terminated onto a 50 Ohm impedance value and SO-239 type of connectors to match the most common co-axial line impedance standard and it is optimized for HF operations between 1 and 30 MHz. Within its specified range, this device appears to be "transparent" between the RF source and the load [SWR = 1:1] and exhibits an extremely low insertion loss [-0.01 dB] However, it can be made to suit any other input/output impedance values and frequencies spectrum.<br />
<br />
<strong>The in-line V.G.N.G. can generate a virtual ground node at any point of a co-axial line in un-balanced transmission line system. This is, infact, its function.</strong> This device is also available in <a href="bvgng.htm">a version for balanced lines</a>.<br />
<br />
<strong>Main applications:</strong><br />
<br />
</p>
</div>
<blockquote>
<div class="doublespaced">
<p><strong>1)</strong> It can be employed to stabilize a whole RTX un-balanced system, however complex it could be, by providing a reliable and centralized RF earth grounding system. As most people may have already experienced, RF earth grounding is a very difficult task and has nothing to do with the conventional safety earth grounding solutions;<br />
<strong><span class="small">N.B. For safety reasons, where required, all the equipment must also be earthed the conventional way and the chassis of the in-line VGNG can also be wired to the building safety earth grounding circuit. All the system equipment chassis should be wired to the provided earth bolts on the in-line VGNG chassis by the shortest route.</span></strong><br />
<br />
<strong>2)</strong> It can be employed to provide a reliable way to short-circuiting antennas who have not antistatic protection, without the need for modifications or even reaching them up on the roof. Since the in-line VGNG exhibits a solid short circuit to the DC current on both its gates, all the electrostatic charges collected by the aerial and/or the co-axial outer conductor will be discharged via common ground and into the building safety earth grounding circuit;</p>
<p><strong>3)</strong> It can be used to stabilize RF co-axial lines exposed to third party hertzian radiation;</p>
<p><strong>4)</strong> It can be employed to stabilize reactive loads at their feeding points.</p>
</div>
</blockquote>

<td style="text-align: center;" data-bgcolor="#FFFF00" width="100%" height="20" data-valign="top"><p><strong>F.A.Q.</strong>:</p>
<p><strong>Question:</strong> I run a virtual ground HF antenna, do I still need an in-line VGNG ?<br />
<strong>ANSWER: NO !</strong> Your virtual ground antenna system already provides all the R.F. earth grounding you need. However, while using a different aerial an in-line VGNG might be needed.</p>
<p><strong>Question:</strong> Where should I place it along my transmission line?<br />
<strong>ANSWER:</strong> Where the un-balanced RTX system comprises only of a TX/RTX-SWRmeter-coaxial-antenna, the in-line VGNG must be placed right onto the TX/RTX antenna socket.<br />
<strong>N.B.</strong> If an antenna tuner is used, the in-line VGNG must always be placed before it, right on its input socket.<br />
If a linear power amplifier is used the in-line VGNG must be placed on its output gate.<br />
Where the system comprises of TX/RTX, a linear power amplifier and an antenna tuner, the in-line VGNG must be placed between the amplifier and the antenna tuner.<br />
If a filter, such as an anti TVI, is used place the filter between the amplifier and the in-line VGNG. Moreover, the filtering should improve and the system will also benefit by the added VGNG's out-of-band own signal rejection.<br />
<strong>N.B.</strong> The in-line VGNG should be inserted in the transmission line by using a short but comfortable RG-213, or better, patch lead. Wherever possible use double male connectors to plug the in-line VGNG in.</p></td>
