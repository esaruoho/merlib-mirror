## The Handkerchief Dynamics Model

### Physical Description

In the IET barrel experiment, a plane pump at the bottom creates a self-organizing toroidal vortex. The water surface is:

1. **Pulled down** in a vortex at the centre
2. **Thrown out tangentially** at the bottom of the barrel
3. **Twisted together** with the surface as it goes

The authors liken this to pulling a handkerchief through a hole: seize it at the centre, twist it together, and pull. The complete procedure is:

**Pull -- Twist -- Spread**

This is not merely a metaphor. The handkerchief dynamics describes a topological transformation of a 2D surface (the water surface / "handkerchief") through a 3D flow field, where the surface is:

- **Contracted** radially (pulled toward the vortex axis)
- **Rotated/twisted** azimuthally (vortex spin)
- **Stretched** axially (pulled downward by subpressure)
- **Folded back** at the periphery (toroidal return flow)

### Connection to Chaos Theory: The Horseshoe Map

The authors explicitly identify this as close to the classic **Smale horseshoe** of chaos science -- the canonical **stretch-and-fold** operation. In the horseshoe map:

1. A region of phase space is **stretched** in one direction
2. **Folded** back on itself
3. Points initially adjacent are **separated** and eventually lose all correlation

The handkerchief dynamics achieves exactly this in physical 3D fluid flow:
- The surface is stretched along the vortex axis
- Folded back through the toroidal return flow
- Points initially close on the water surface are separated and mixed

This is the mechanism by which the self-organizing vortex achieves **efficient mixing** -- not through turbulent energy dissipation, but through organized topological deformation.

## Beltrami Flow Connection

### What is Beltrami Flow?

A **Beltrami flow** is a vector field where the vorticity is everywhere parallel to the velocity:

**curl(v) = lambda * v**

where lambda is a scalar (which may be spatially varying). This means the flow **twists along its own axis** -- it is maximally helical. Beltrami flows are special because they are:

- **Force-free** (in MHD context: J x B = 0)
- **Eigenfields of the curl operator**
- Naturally arising as **minimum-energy states** under the constraint of conserved helicity
- Solutions that appear in **self-organizing systems** as attracting states

### Beltrami Flows in the IET Vortex

The IET barrel experiment produces flow with strong Beltrami characteristics:

1. **Helical structure**: The flow spirals downward at the centre while rotating, then returns at the periphery -- the velocity and vorticity vectors are substantially aligned along the central axis.

2. **Self-organization to minimum energy**: The toroidal vortex represents the state the system spontaneously selects. Beltrami flows are known to be **attractors** of dissipative fluid systems -- the state of maximum helicity relative to energy, which dissipation naturally drives toward.

3. **Twisted scroll rings**: The toroidal vortex structure that emerges in the barrel resembles the twisted scroll ring solutions that appear across dynamical systems. These are essentially Beltrami-type structures -- topologically nontrivial flows where rotation and translation are locked together.

4. **The measuring tape effect**: The authors describe how outer fluid layers are pulled inward by inner layers (due to attractive van der Waals forces and hydrogen bonds), forming a structure "like a paper or measuring tape that has been rolled up." This rolling/twisting is characteristic of Beltrami organization -- the flow wraps itself into a helically organized state.

### The Beltrami-Handkerchief Connection

The handkerchief dynamics is effectively a **Beltrami flow acting on a material surface**. The pull-twist-spread operation maps a flat surface through a Beltrami-type flow field:

- The **twist** comes from the Beltrami property (vorticity aligned with velocity)
- The **pull** comes from the axial subpressure
- The **spread** comes from the toroidal return flow at the periphery

The result is that the material surface (the "handkerchief" / water surface) undergoes a stretch-and-fold transformation driven by the underlying Beltrami flow structure.

## Torricelli Geometry Connection

### Torricelli's Trumpet (Gabriel's Horn)

The funnel shape that appears at the vortex centre -- the air funnel -- has a direct connection to **Torricelli's trumpet** (also called Gabriel's Horn), the surface of revolution of y = 1/x for x >= 1 around the x-axis.

Properties of Torricelli's trumpet:
- **Infinite surface area** but **finite volume** (pi units)
- Narrowing hyperbolically toward infinity

### The Air Funnel as Torricelli Geometry

The IET report describes the air funnel undergoing a sequence of geometric transitions:

1. **Initial state**: Smooth, slight depression at centre
2. **First bifurcation**: Spear-shaped funnel suddenly appears -- approximately **hyperbolic** (Torricelli-like) geometry
3. **Trumpet widening**: The spear widens into a trumpet shape
4. **Hopf bifurcation**: The funnel begins to **twist around its own axis**, adding a new frequency -- the axisymmetric Torricelli geometry breaks into a **helical** (Beltrami-like) minimal surface
5. **Further bifurcations**: Increasingly complex minimal surfaces

The initial hyperbolic funnel is essentially a physical realization of Torricelli's trumpet, where:
- The narrowing radius follows approximately r ~ 1/z (where z is depth)
- The surface tension of the air-water interface plays the role of the constraining surface
- The rotating flow provides the centripetal force that maintains the funnel shape

### Minimal Surface Theory

The air column forms a **minimal surface** -- the surface of minimum area subject to boundary constraints. The authors draw the analogy to soap bubbles:

- In a soap bubble: inner over-pressure balances surface tension
- In the vortex funnel: **inertial forces from the rotating flow** (pulling outward) balance **surface tension** (contracting inward)

This is Torricelli geometry constrained by Beltrami dynamics -- the funnel shape is determined by the balance between:
- Surface tension (Torricelli-like contraction)
- Centripetal acceleration from the vortex flow (Beltrami-driven expansion)
- Axial subpressure (downward stretching)

## Mathematical Framework

### The Vortex Velocity Field

In cylindrical coordinates (r, theta, z), the IET vortex combines:

- **Tangential**: V_theta(r) transitioning from free vortex (k/r) at periphery to forced vortex (kr) near centre
- **Axial**: V_z(r) with reverse flow in centre (Type II) or complex three-region flow (Type III)
- **Radial**: V_r(r) inward at surface, outward at bottom (toroidal return)

For a Beltrami flow, these components satisfy curl(V) = lambda * V, which constrains the relationships between velocity components.

### Kinetic Energy of the Vortex

The report derives:

**W_k = (1/2) * integral(omega^2 * r^2 * dm) = k * m * R^2 * omega_R^2**

Where k depends on the vortex type:
- Rigid rotation: k = 1/4
- Quasi-free vortex: k = 1/2
- Free vortex: k = ln(R/r_core) ~ 2.3 (for r_core ~ R/10)

With R = 0.25 m and omega_R ~ pi rad/s, this gives 15, 30, and 140 Ws for the three types respectively -- showing the free vortex stores significantly more energy, consistent with the Beltrami property of maximizing helicity (and thus energy storage) relative to enstrophy dissipation.
