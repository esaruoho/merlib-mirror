 Magnets

 A spinning magnet produces electricity but first it must be manufactured. The
earliest method was to hammer a red-hot iron bar making sure it was in line with
the magnetic North and South poles of the earth. The iron bar only becomes
magnetised when pointing North to South, in line with flow of the earths
magnetic field. 
 The heavy hammer hitting the metal causes the atoms in the metal to vibrate. 
 For that fraction of a second they are weightless, and therefore at the
mercy of the earths electrical currents that flow between the poles. The
atoms in the red-hot vibrating metal strip are forced into alignment with the
flow of the earths magnetic field before settling down. The captured
electricity flowing all around the earth is now flowing fairly smoothly through
the new magnet. 
 A spinning magnet is
in effect an electrical pump. The natural electricity flowing through the magnet is
picked up by the centrifugal forces acting on the magnet and the energy
is thrown out into the grid system under pressure.

 The same principle applies to a cyclist pedalling along with the magnet
spinning inside the dynamo. Electricity is pumped from the immediate
surroundings into the wiring and along to the lamps
