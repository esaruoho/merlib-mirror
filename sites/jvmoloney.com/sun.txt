 The Sun

 As discovered by Copernicus, the sun is the centre of
the solar system and our
existence, whether we like it or not. Light
and heat are two of the obvious energies but there is a third energy,
electricity, and without it there would be no life. The earth, a giant spinning
electromagnet, is electrically linked to the sun by a circuit passing through
the poles. 
 Strangely enough this mother of
all electric circuits is not the only one in the solar system. 
 The giant gas planet Jupiter,
suspected to be a failed star, is electrically linked to Io, one of its many
moons. 
 Satellites have provided
photographic evidence of volcanic activity on Io, showing it to be more violent
than here on earth. Io, by all
accounts, is turning itself inside out. Gathering
scientific opinion believes that Jupiters electrical link with Io, and the
volcanic activity, are not a coincidence and gives us a glimpse of planet earth 4
billion years ago. 
 A second parallel for these electrical circuits
in nature was discovered by geological observations in old volcanic rocks. 
 The magnetic flow in rock strata changes direction periodically, first in
one direction and then the other. It appears that the earths electrical flow
changes direction every 200,000 years. It is believed however that the last
change was more than 800,000 years ago, so are we long overdue?
