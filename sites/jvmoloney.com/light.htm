Light

For at least 2,500 years, and probably before, men of learning have speculated on the composition of light.  Ancient civilisations worshipped the sun, and the fact that the sun is responsible for light had always been held in awe, but what is it?  The idea of tiny particles making up all nature remains true right through to the present day, and light is simply thought of as another form of matter.

My research shows that light is comprised of two particles not one.  Apart from the electron with its negative charge, the other particle in greatest abundance is the proton with its positive charge.  When the electrons flood through the electric bulb the magnetic effect causes the opposite particles to vibrate vigorously, thereby giving us a light field. Therefore, the proton has to be the prime candidate for light.  It also neatly fits into being one of the baryon (sub-atomic) particles of life. 

My electronic experiments clearly showed a continuous connection between sun, moon and electric light.  When the answer finally dawned on me about what was happening, I was immediately struck by the simplicity of it all.
